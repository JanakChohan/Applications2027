6. Drill-down: the International Private Bank, EMEA and the UK

6.1 Structure and leaders (September 2026)

The Global Private Bank was given a single global CEO for the first time on 3 July 2025, when David Frame (joined 1995; ran the US Private Bank from 2019; earlier ran alternatives and equity trading in New York and London) was appointed. Under him, the International Private Bank is run from London by Adam Tejpaul (26 years at the firm; formerly Latin America CEO and Asia investments head), and EMEA by Pablo Garnica from Madrid (joined 1996; sits on the boards of J.P. Morgan SE and J.P. Morgan (Suisse)). In 2025 EMEA was reorganised into three regions with, in Tejpaul's words, "three very senior, almost CEO-like people": the UK, Channel Islands and Ireland under Maricé Brown (from the third quarter of 2025, previously the Mexico market manager; she succeeded Oliver Gregson, who left to run Schroders Wealth Management); Continental Europe under Sarah Catania (Milan, from September 2025); and Switzerland, the Middle East and emerging markets under Karim Rekik. Andrew Cohen is Executive Chairman of the Global Private Bank and head of 23 Wall. Martin Marron runs Wealth Management Solutions.

6.5 The London office itself

60 Victoria Embankment is J.P. Morgan's EMEA headquarters on the north bank of the Thames by Blackfriars. The listed Victorian building on the corner was the City of London School (1880 to 1882, architects Davis and Emanuel), restored in 1991 as part of the J.P. Morgan development and now used as the firm's conference centre; the statues on its façade are of Shakespeare, Milton, Bacon, Newton and Thomas More. The Private Bank's London team leads listed on the firm's site include Diana Robinson (Investments and Advice UK), Maya Prabhu (UK Domestic and Crown Dependencies), Khayyam Jumani (UK North), Paul Ferry (Financial Institutions), Zeynep Ozturk Unlu (Institutional Wealth Group) and James Chilvers (UK Wealth Advisory). The firm dates its UK presence to 1838.
