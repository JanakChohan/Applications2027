5. The moves: acquisitions, investments and what the firm has chosen not to do

JPMorgan's deal-making since 2021 follows a pattern: many small technology and capability purchases, one opportunistic crisis acquisition, and a refusal to do large conventional M&A because the law and the CEO's preferences both point to organic growth. What the firm has not done, and why

  • No US bank acquisitions. Federal law bars a bank holding more than 10% of US deposits from buying another deposit-taker except in a failure. JPMorgan is over the cap, so Bear Stearns, Washington Mutual and First Republic are the only kind of bank deal it can do. This is why "excess capital" is deployed into branches, advisors, technology and now equity stakes instead.
  • No large asset or wealth management acquisition. Erdoes told the February 2026 Company Update that her finance head "signed a different NDA once every two weeks last year... about 25 of them. Most of the big deals... we had seen and turned down." Dimon: "I'd love Mary to buy something if it made sense." The bar is high because AWM's organic flows ($553bn in 2025) already exceed what any purchase would add.
  • Maybe something abroad. Dimon in May 2026: "There might be, in the next couple years, a chance to put $10 or $20 billion to work buying something... we are on the lookout." Speculation has centred on custody or international franchises. Nothing announced.
