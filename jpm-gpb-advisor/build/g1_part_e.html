6.2 Who the clients are

J.P. Morgan's EMEA site names five groups: individuals and families; family offices; "institutional families" (the 23 Wall segment); business owners, founders and entrepreneurs; athletes and entertainers. In practice the London book is a mix of domestic UK wealth (entrepreneurs, private equity and hedge fund partners, executives, landed and inherited wealth) and international families using London as a hub: Gulf, Indian, African, Greek, Italian, Nordic and Israeli families among them. Thresholds are not published; third-party guides put the US Private Bank floor at $10 million of investable assets and the UK at around £10 million, with the internal "UHNW" segment starting around $25 million and family-office coverage from around $100 million. The Private Bank's own family-office page says family offices usually have "at least a few hundred million".

Two facts about the client base explain the sales approach. First, J.P. Morgan's Global Family Office Report (2026 edition, 333 family offices, average net worth $1.6 billion) found that these families hold roughly 40% in public equities, 35% in alternatives, under 15% in bonds and 8% in cash, that 86% have no formal succession plan, and that their top worries are geopolitics (74%) and trade policy (60%). Second, four in five North American ultra-wealthy people are self-made. The typical new client is therefore not a dynasty; it is a founder who just sold something, is over-concentrated, under-planned, and being courted by five banks.

6.4 From sales to relationship: what the journey looks like in revenue terms

{{FIG:pb_revenue}}

AWM's income statement has four lines that map onto the client relationship: asset management fees ($15.5bn in 2025), commissions and other fees ($1.2bn), net interest income ($6.8bn) and other income ($0.6bn). Net interest income was about a quarter of AWM revenue in 2022, rose to 40% in 2024 as rates went up, and fell back to 28% in 2025 as loan spreads narrowed. In 2022 and 2023 clients moved deposits into money-market funds ("cash sorting"); Private Bank deposits fell from $282bn to $233bn while AWM's money-market assets rose to nearly $1tn. Management's framing: "They can move from one to the other, but they generally don't leave the bank." Deposits had recovered to $257bn by the end of 2025 and loans reached $266bn.

The "sales" phase (steps 1 to 3 of the journey) is measured in new clients and net new assets. The "relationship" phase (steps 4 to 7) is measured in share of wallet, revenue per client and retention. J.P. Morgan's disclosed facts: more than 95% of Private Bank clients stay; when a US Private Bank client also actively banks with the firm, assets and revenue are on average 65% higher; only 2% of clients are credit-only; revenue per producer runs around $3 million; and revenue per average banker rose 15% between 2020 and 2025 even as the number of bankers grew 64%. A junior banker's book is built by "seeding", not by buying: "This is where we don't buy books of business. We seed books of business" (Erdoes, 2022).

6.6 The London market you would be working in

London is the hub of J.P. Morgan's International Private Bank, which is "the largest international private bank of any of the US-domiciled banks" and, per Erdoes, "the largest U.S. bank in the high-net-worth-plus segment internationally, growing at double-digit rates". The UK business, led since 2025 by Maricé Brown, covers London, Manchester, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Bournemouth and the Crown Dependencies. The EMEA business had revenue growth of about 14%, asset growth of about 25% and advisor growth of 9% in the year to June 2025.

{{FIG:emea}}

Three market facts matter for a London applicant:

  • The UK is a large but leaking wealth pool. About 578,000 dollar-millionaires live in the UK (fifth in the world) and HMRC data show roughly 3,400 people with over £100 million. But the April 2025 abolition of the non-dom regime (replaced by a four-year "foreign income and gains" window and residence-based inheritance tax) has pushed some internationally mobile families to Dubai, Milan, Switzerland and Monaco. Estimates of how many left range from the OBR's 1,200 to Henley & Partners' contested 16,500. J.P. Morgan's EMEA head of wealth advisory, Chris Ottenritter, says the firm was "amazed at how many of our clients who were part of the UK non-domiciled regime have decided to stay. This is going to cost some of them hundreds of millions of pounds." Either way, every London banker is having residence conversations.
  • The Middle East is the growth engine. J.P. Morgan announced 100+ new AWM hires in the region in 2025, obtained a Saudi regional headquarters licence in October 2025, and has a dedicated Dubai private banking team. Gulf families are served jointly from London, Dubai and Riyadh.
  • Regulation is client-facing work. The FCA's Consumer Duty (2023) requires fair value and good outcomes for retail clients; a proposed opt-up for clients with around £10 million of investable assets (expected in the second half of 2026) would take much of the UHNW book outside it. Suitability rules under MiFID II govern every recommendation. A private banker in London spends real time documenting why advice was suitable.
