Appendix A. Glossary (the words you will hear in week one)

AUM / AUS / client assets
Assets under management are money the bank actively invests for clients. Assets under supervision (J.P. Morgan's older term) and "client assets" add money the bank holds or advises on but does not manage, plus deposits and custody. Client assets is the broadest number and the one J.P. Morgan now headlines.
Net new money / net flows
Money coming in minus money going out, excluding market movements. The cleanest measure of whether clients are choosing you. Mary Erdoes' phrase: "clients vote with their feet".
Basis point (bp)
One hundredth of one percent. Fees, spreads and returns are all quoted in bps.
Discretionary / advisory / brokerage
Three ways to run a client's money. Discretionary: the bank decides and executes within an agreed mandate. Advisory: the bank recommends, the client approves each move. Brokerage (execution-only): the client instructs, the bank executes. Fees are highest for discretionary.
Strategic vs tactical asset allocation
Strategic: the long-run mix of equities, bonds, alternatives and cash designed for the client's goals (J.P. Morgan bases it on its annual Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions). Tactical: short-term tilts around that mix based on the house view.
Alternatives
Anything not listed equities, bonds or cash: private equity, private credit, hedge funds, real estate, infrastructure, timber. Illiquid, higher fee, and a strategic priority for every private bank because they lock in assets and differentiate.
Open architecture
Offering external managers' funds, not only the bank's own. J.P. Morgan's Manager Selection team researches third-party managers.
Securities-based lending (SBL) / Lombard loan
A loan secured on the client's investment portfolio. Lets a client raise cash without selling. The bank sets a loan-to-value and can demand more collateral (a "margin call") if the portfolio falls.
Net interest income (NII)
Interest earned on loans and investments minus interest paid on deposits. Rose sharply for all banks in 2022 and 2023 as central banks raised rates.
KYC / AML / source of wealth
Know Your Customer and Anti-Money-Laundering checks. Before a client is onboarded the bank must document who they are and how they made their money. In private banking this is slow, intrusive and non-negotiable; Advisors spend real time on it.
Suitability / Consumer Duty
UK regulatory requirements that products recommended are appropriate for the client and that firms deliver good outcomes. The FCA's Consumer Duty (2023) applies to private banks' retail clients.
Non-dom
A UK tax status for residents domiciled elsewhere, abolished from April 2025 and replaced by a residence-based regime. A live topic for every London private banker because it changed where some wealthy clients choose to live.
Family office
A private company set up by a very wealthy family to manage its investments and affairs. Single-family offices serve one family; multi-family offices serve several. J.P. Morgan's "23 Wall" team covers the largest ones.
Liquidity event
The moment a founder's paper wealth becomes cash: a sale, an IPO, a secondary. The single most important moment for a private banker to be in the room.
Share of wallet
The proportion of a client's total wealth that sits with you. Most UHNW families use two to four banks; the game is to become the primary one.
CET1 / fortress balance sheet
Common Equity Tier 1 capital as a percentage of risk-weighted assets, the core regulatory measure of a bank's safety. "Fortress balance sheet" is Jamie Dimon's phrase for holding more capital and liquidity than required so that the bank is a safe haven in a crisis.
ROTCE
Return on tangible common equity, JPMorgan's headline profitability measure. The firm's stated through-the-cycle target has been 17%; it has run well above that recently.
10-K
The annual report every US-listed company files with the SEC. JPMorgan's runs to several hundred pages and is the primary source for all segment numbers in Guide 2.
