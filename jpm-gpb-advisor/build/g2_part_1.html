Executive read: what the filings say in one page

Read in sequence, JPMorgan Chase's five annual reports from 2021 to 2025, its four Investor Days and its February 2026 Company Update tell one story in three phases.

1
2021 to 2022: invest through the cycle, and take the criticism
Coming out of the pandemic with record profits, the firm announced it would raise investment spending to $15bn a year and warned it might miss its 17% return target. The shares fell and analysts called it undisciplined. Management held a special Investor Day to defend the plan: "good expenses" (branches, bankers, technology) versus "bad expenses" (bureaucracy). Chase UK launched; a string of small technology acquisitions followed. Then rates rose, Dimon warned of a "hurricane", and the firm's fortress balance sheet became the point.
2
2023 to 2024: the crisis buyer, record profits, and a fight with regulators
When First Republic failed in May 2023 the firm was the only buyer with the capital and the systems to take it. That deal created "effectively a new business" in affluent wealth. Revenue jumped 23% in 2023 and set records again in 2024. The firm reorganised its wholesale businesses into one Commercial & Investment Bank. Dimon spent his letters attacking the Basel III capital proposal, refusing to buy back stock at high prices ("excess capital is earnings in store") and warning about deficits, geopolitics and inflation.
3
2025 to 2026: turning outward, AI at scale, and a succession clock
With about $40bn of surplus capital and a new US administration easing regulation, the firm launched a $1.5tn "Security and Resiliency" initiative with up to $10bn (or $20bn) of direct equity, bought Apple's card portfolio, launched Chase in Germany, put a deposit token on a public blockchain, and told investors that AI had already cut headcount in "discrete areas" by 30 to 40% while front-office hiring continued. In June 2026 two co-presidents were named and Dimon said he would stay "a few years" more. AWM, the division you are applying to, is the cleanest expression of the whole strategy: targets set in 2020 and beaten every year, advisor headcount up 64%, and a stated "path to $10 trillion" of client assets.
The ten recurring themes (Section 3 gives the quotes)
Fortress balance sheet as licence to act · Invest through the cycle · Excess capital is earnings in store, buybacks last · Completeness and scale, "not a conglomerate" · Technology and AI as the moat and the cost · Moats are shallow: paranoia about fintech and shadow banks · International expansion in consumer and private banking · Relentless hiring of client-facing people · Regulatory pushback, then cautious embrace of deregulation · A standing list of risks the firm worries about.

1. How to read a 10-K, for someone who never has

A Form 10-K is the annual report a US-listed company must file with the Securities and Exchange Commission. JPMorgan's runs to several hundred pages. For a job applicant, five parts matter:

PartWhat it containsWhat to look for
Business overview (Item 1)How the firm describes its segments, what each does, who its clients are.The exact definitions. When the definition of a segment changes, the strategy changed.
Risk factors (Item 1A)Everything that could hurt the firm, from geopolitics to a cyber attack.New risks added year on year (AI, stablecoins, private credit appeared recently).
Management's Discussion and AnalysisThe narrative of the year's results, segment by segment, with the key metrics (revenue lines, AUM, client assets, advisors, loans, deposits, margins, ROE).The segment tables. AWM's table has revenue split between Asset Management and Global Private Bank, the number of client advisors, and pretax margin by half.
Financial statements and notesAudited numbers; notes on acquisitions (First Republic is Note 34 in the FY2023 filing), litigation, capital.Deal terms and contingent liabilities.
The CEO letter (in the annual report, not the 10-K)Jamie Dimon's essay, typically 50 to 60 pages, plus letters from each line-of-business CEO including Mary Erdoes for AWM.The letters are where strategy and values are stated in plain language. They are the primary source for Sections 2, 3 and 5 of this guide.

JPMorgan reports revenue on a "managed" basis (grossing up tax-exempt income so segments are comparable). Every figure in this guide uses the managed basis unless stated. "ROTCE" is return on tangible common equity, the firm's headline profitability measure; its stated through-the-cycle target is 17%, which management calls "not aspirational" but "a helpful backstop".
