3. What JPMorgan Chase is

3.1 One paragraph

JPMorgan Chase & Co. is the largest bank in the United States and, by market value, the largest in the world. It does three things at scale: it banks ordinary Americans and small businesses under the Chase brand; it advises, lends to, trades for and moves money for companies, governments and institutions worldwide under the J.P. Morgan brand; and it manages money and advises wealthy people through Asset & Wealth Management. In 2025 it earned $57 billion of profit on $186 billion of revenue, has around 320,000 employees in 66 countries, and is worth roughly $945 billion on the stock market, which is more than Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo and Goldman Sachs combined. Its chief executive since 2005, Jamie Dimon, is the most influential banker of his generation and is now in the last few years of his tenure.

3.2 Where the firm came from (the short version that matters)

The name carries two histories. J.P. Morgan descends from the merchant bank of Junius and J. Pierpont Morgan (Drexel, Morgan & Co., 1871; J.P. Morgan & Co. from 1895), which financed US railroads, US Steel and General Electric, and effectively acted as the American central bank in the Panic of 1907. Its London arm, Morgan Grenfell, and the J.S. Morgan business gave the firm UK roots that the Private Bank dates to 1838. Chase descends from Chase National Bank (1877) and Chemical Bank, retail and commercial lenders that merged repeatedly through the 20th century. The two combined in 2000 (Chase Manhattan bought J.P. Morgan & Co.), and the modern firm was shaped by the 2004 merger with Bank One, which brought Jamie Dimon and a management culture of cost discipline, a "fortress balance sheet" and detailed operating reviews. In 2008 the firm absorbed Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual during the financial crisis; in 2023 it absorbed First Republic. Each crisis acquisition was possible because the firm had more capital than its peers when it mattered, which is the practical meaning of "fortress balance sheet" and a story the firm tells about itself constantly.

The phrase every employee learns comes from J.P. "Jack" Morgan Jr., testifying to the US Senate in May 1933 during the Pecora hearings into Wall Street: "the idea of doing only first-class business, and that in a first-class way, has been before our minds." It is quoted in the Private Bank's letters to this day, and 23 Wall Street, the old headquarters opposite the New York Stock Exchange, gives its name to the unit that serves the very largest families.

Two things stand out from the numbers. First, the Commercial & Investment Bank, not Chase, is now the largest profit engine: about 42% of revenue and 49% of profit. 3.4 Each division in plain English, and why sectors sit where they do

Consumer & Community Banking: the deposit and data machine

Chase is the largest US retail bank by deposits (11.1% of national retail deposits) and the largest US credit card issuer (23.6% of card spending). Its economics are scale economics: 87 million customers generate cheap deposits that fund the firm, and card spending generates fees and interest. Its strategic logic is the "funnel": a customer opens a checking account, gets a card, buys a house, and eventually invests. Marianne Lake, who ran it until June 2026, said the "secret sauce" is that "90% of new advised clients were referred by bankers" in the branches. That is why J.P. Morgan Wealth Management sits in Chase rather than in AWM: its clients are acquired through the branch network, the model is scaled and digital, and the product shelf is supplied by AWM. Chase has 5.5 million affluent banking households but only about a fifth of them invest with the firm, which the firm sees as its biggest untapped wealth opportunity. The same logic explains why Chase UK sits here: it is a digital consumer bank built to be "a multi-country digital bank at the intersection of banking and investing", now in the UK (2.8 million customers) and Germany (launched May 2026).

Commercial & Investment Bank: the corporate lifecycle machine

The CIB serves companies and institutions at every stage. Commercial Banking lends to and banks mid-sized firms and start-ups ("innovation economy"); Global Corporate Banking covers the largest multinationals; Global Investment Banking advises on mergers and raises equity and debt; Markets makes prices in every asset class for institutional investors; Payments moves money and holds $760 billion of corporate operating deposits (a third of the firm's funding); Securities Services safeguards $41 trillion of assets for fund managers. In January 2024 the firm merged Commercial Banking into the CIB. Doug Petno's explanation: "Our clients are growing, scaling and expanding globally at an accelerated pace... a single wholesale operating entity, four business units tightly knit... to be our clients' most important financial partner." The logic is client-for-life coverage: the company that Commercial Banking lends to at $50 million of revenue is the company Investment Banking takes public at $5 billion. Payments sits in the CIB because it is a wholesale product (treasury services, merchant acquiring) whose deposits fund the Markets business and whose rails Chase and Securities Services also use.

Asset & Wealth Management: the fiduciary machine

AWM is where the firm acts as a fiduciary, managing other people's money for a fee rather than lending its own. It has two halves that share one engine. Asset Management ("J.P. Morgan Asset Management", run by George Gatch) manufactures products: it is the world's largest active ETF manager ($250 billion), the second-largest separately-managed-account provider ($434 billion), and runs $560 billion of alternatives and roughly $1 trillion of money-market funds. The Global Private Bank (run by David Frame) distributes advice, investments, lending, banking and trust services to wealthy families. Erdoes insists they be seen as "two different businesses", but they sit together because the Private Bank is Asset Management's most important distribution channel and Asset Management's research and products are the Private Bank's raw material. A shared unit, Wealth Management Solutions (Martin Marron), builds the advice platform used by both the Private Bank and Chase's advisors so that "no matter where you come into this institution, you get the best advice." Section 6 goes deep on this division.

Corporate: the balance sheet's balance sheet

Treasury and the Chief Investment Office manage the firm's liquidity ($1.5 trillion of cash and marketable securities), capital and interest-rate risk. It is where the "fortress" is engineered. It also carries the firm's central costs and one-off items (the 2024 Visa share gain landed here).

3.5 How the pieces link: the "one firm" engine

Dimon's phrasing in every annual letter is that the firm is "not a conglomerate": "we strive to build enduring businesses, which rely on and benefit from one another." CFO Jeremy Barnum in February 2026: "Our completeness, global presence, diversification and scale are not just attributes. They're our competitive advantages... our operating model enables us to support our clients and customers through their entire lifecycle and through multiple generations." The disclosed evidence for the Private Bank specifically:

  • "Over 95% of our top 50 AWM clients also work with our other lines of business." (Erdoes, 2025 letter)
  • "There are 2,000 private banking clients that walk into those [Chase] branches every day." (Erdoes, Investor Day 2025)
  • 42% of the world's venture-backed company cap tables sit in J.P. Morgan's "innovation economy" franchise, built after the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank and First Republic in 2023. Founders on those cap tables are future Private Bank clients.
  • 60% of major US sports team owners are Private Bank clients; the firm financed 10 of the last 15 major team transactions by combining Private Bank lending with the investment bank's municipal finance and the commercial bank's real estate expertise.
  • Global Shares (bought 2022, now "J.P. Morgan Workplace") administers equity plans for 1.8 million employees and $372 billion of assets; its explicit purpose is "to give leads... to the whole wealth management continuum".
  • Payments sweeps about $175 billion of corporate client balances into AWM's money-market funds.

The cultural rule that makes this work
Most banks have referral programmes that fail because each division fights over who gets credit for the revenue. Erdoes' answer at Investor Day 2025 is the one to remember: "that whole flywheel of people, they don't sit there and count the beans of who gets credit for what... We do not tolerate that here at J.P. Morgan. We do not have side accounting groups that think about where those revenues get counted for. We are trying to win for the client." Whether "OneJPM" is an official slogan is unclear; the phrases the firm actually uses in public are "one face", "client-for-life", "a single wholesale operating entity" and "we are not a conglomerate".

3.6 The wealth ladder: which client goes where, and why

{{FIG:wealth_continuum}}

The split of wealth clients between Chase (CCB) and the Private Bank (AWM) is deliberate and was formalised in late 2020 when certain wealth clients were moved from AWM to CCB. The logic is that below roughly $5 to 10 million the economics only work with a scaled, branch-and-app model, while above it they only work with a bespoke team. J.P. Morgan Private Client, launched in 2024 at $750,000 and up, was built on the First Republic model that the firm acquired in 2023: a dedicated banker, a single point of contact and concierge service, in converted First Republic offices. 23 Wall, at the very top, was created in 2019 and now covers "800+ families" with an average net worth around $5 billion, offering institutional-style access to the firm's deal flow, balance sheet and research. 3.7 Who runs it

{{FIG:firm_leadership}}

Succession is the live story of 2026. Dimon has said he expects to remain CEO for "a few years" and then become Executive Chairman. On 25 June 2026 the board named Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh co-presidents, with Petno as sole CEO of the CIB and Rohrbaugh as CEO of CCB, and Marianne Lake retired. Press reports say Erdoes is no longer considered a candidate for the top job; she received a $20 million retention award and remains CEO of AWM. For an applicant the point is not the horse race but the pattern: JPMorgan promotes people who have run more than one business, and it treats management change as continuity ("the board meets without me in the room at every meeting since 2000," Dimon said in February 2026).

3.8 Culture and values, stripped of marketing

JPMorgan Chase does not have a catchy mission statement. It has a booklet. How We Do Business lists twenty "Business Principles" under four headings, and they date from the "small blue book" Dimon's team sent to employees after the 2004 Bank One merger. They are worth reading in full because interviewers at the firm were trained on them:

Exceptional client service
  1. We focus on the customer
  2. We are field and client driven; we operate at the local level
  3. We build world-class franchises, investing for the long term, to serve our clients

What "one JPMorgan", "collaboration" and "teamwork" mean here, specifically
They do not mean niceness. They mean that the firm's economic model depends on referrals flowing between divisions without internal transfer pricing, and on client teams sharing one client without fighting over credit. Dimon's phrase in the 2025 letter is that the firm is "its own strong 'neural network', powerful and healthy connections between our people". The test of whether you understand this: can you explain, in one sentence, why a private banker should introduce a client's company to the commercial bank even though the banker earns nothing directly from it? (Because the company's growth and eventual sale is the source of the family's next liquidity event, and the bank that financed the company is the bank that gets the call.)
