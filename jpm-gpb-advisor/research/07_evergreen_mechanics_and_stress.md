# 07 — Evergreen / semi-liquid private credit: mechanics and the 2025–26 redemption stress

Notes compiled 16 September 2026; registry at the end. Snippet-only sources are marked NOT-READ (snippet), confidence capped at Medium; FT items come from headline mirrors and are PAYWALLED-NOT-READ.

## 1. What an evergreen / semi-liquid fund is

**Plain-English version.** A traditional private credit fund is *closed-end*: ~10-year life, capital called as loans are made, no redemptions, cash returned only as loans repay. An *evergreen* ("semi-liquid", "perpetual") fund has no end date, takes money continuously at a monthly NAV and offers a limited exit window, usually quarterly, even though it holds 5–7-year illiquid loans.

**The standard liquidity term.** Blue Owl Credit Income Corp (OCIC), the flagship wealth-channel fund at the centre of the 2026 story, "intends to repurchase once per quarter no more than 5% of our outstanding shares"; NAV is published monthly; distributions are monthly [S500]. Blackstone's BCRED applies an "early repurchase deduction" of 2% of NAV to shares tendered within 12 months of purchase [S501]. Quarterly repurchase offers "typically capped at 5% of shares outstanding" are the industry norm, and the offers are at the board's discretion [S502]. That is ~20% a year at best; tender-offer funds and non-traded BDCs may decline to offer at all, while interval funds must offer at least 5% (SEC Rule 23c-3).

**Wrappers.** US: non-traded BDC (OCIC, BCRED, Apollo ADS, Ares ASIF, HPS HLEND), interval fund, tender-offer fund, non-traded REIT (BREIT, SREIT). Europe: ELTIF 2.0 and Luxembourg Part II funds (Partners Group Global Value SICAV is Luxembourg-domiciled) [S528]. UK: the LTAF, which typically allows up to 5% of NAV per quarter with notice periods; 25 LTAFs, £7.3bn AUM (from £5bn in June 2025), ISA-eligible from April 2026 [S506].

**Fees, tickets, assets, leverage (OCIC as the exemplar).** Management fee 1.25% of net assets; incentive fee 12.5% of net investment income over a 5% hurdle plus 12.5% of realised gains; 87% first-lien senior secured, floating rate, middle-market; target leverage 0.9–1.25x debt/equity, regulatory cap 2.0x; $35.8bn of investments at fair value [S500]. Morningstar puts the average semiliquid-fund expense ratio at ~3% all-in [S503].

**Who buys and how big.** Semiliquid fund assets reached ~$600bn by March 2026, more than double 2022; private credit was the growth engine but recorded ~$1bn of net outflows in Q1 2026 while private equity took $14.5bn of inflows over 12 months; only 16% of advisers say they are "very familiar" with the structures [S503]. Preqin counted a record 123 evergreen launches in 2025 (49 private credit) and 30 in Jan–Feb 2026 [S504]. Scope puts ELTIF AUM at €34bn at end-2025 (+55%), across ~268 funds, forecasting ~€50bn by end-2026 [S505]. The IMF's April 2026 briefing sized direct lending at ~$2tn globally, with ~15% (~$300bn) in semi-liquid structures that can gate [S507]; the FSB sized private credit at $1.5–2.0tn at end-2024 [S508]. On the iCapital platform private credit fell from 41% of flows in Q1 2025 to 18% in Q1 2026 while private equity rose from 35% to 51% [S509].

## 2. Why the liquidity mismatch matters, and the BREIT template

When requests exceed the cap the fund pays *pro rata* (5/21.9 = ~23% of each request); the rest is not queued and must be re-tendered next quarter. NBER research (Fang, Goldstein, Zeng) finds cash buffers and loan repayments are insufficient to fund repeated 5% quarterly outflows, inflows fall exactly when outflows rise, and shortfalls are met by loan sales, borrowing and delayed payments, so redemptions impose costs on remaining investors and create run-like "strategic complementarity" [S502].

**BREIT precedent.** Blackstone Real Estate Income Trust hit its 2%-monthly / 5%-quarterly cap in November 2022 and prorated for 15 months; it returned >$15bn to redeeming investors before lifting the cap in March 2024 when February 2024 requests ($961m) were 82% below the January 2023 peak (implying ~$5.3bn) [S510]. Starwood's SREIT went further: cap cut to 0.33%/month in May 2024, back up to 1.5% quarterly by March 2026, then repurchases suspended for most investors from April 2026 and the distribution cut from 6.3% to 4.7%; Barry Sternlicht: "the status quo is no longer tenable for SREIT" [S511].

- **10 Sep 2025** Tricolor (subprime auto) files Chapter 7; **~28 Sep 2025** First Brands files Chapter 11 two weeks later, with hidden off-balance-sheet financing [S512].
- **14 Oct 2025** Jamie Dimon on JPMorgan's Q3 call: "When you see one cockroach, there are probably more"; JPM took ~$170m of Tricolor losses; First Brands' debt ~$10bn [S513].
- **5 Nov 2025** Blue Owl announces merger of non-traded OBDC II into listed OBDC; OBDC II's quarterly tenders stop; **16 Nov** FT reports a potential ~20% haircut because OBDC trades at a discount to NAV; **19 Nov** merger terminated; OBDC II had seen ~$150m of withdrawals in the first nine months of 2025; class action filed [S514].
- **Oct 2025–Feb 2026** software stocks fall ~30% on AI-disruption fears ("SaaSpocalypse"); SaaS loans had grown from ~$8bn (2015) to >$500bn, 19% of direct loans, by end-2025; BDCs with high SaaS exposure underperformed by ~5pp [S515].
- **19 Feb 2026** Blue Owl BDCs sell $1.4bn of loans at 99.7% of par ($600m from OBDC II, $400m OTIC); OBDC II "permanently halts" quarterly redemptions in favour of return-of-capital distributions (up to $2.35/share, ~30% of NAV) [S516]; the board will "prioritise delivering liquidity ratably to all shareholders" [S517].

- **29 Apr 2026** SREIT suspends most repurchases [S511].

## 5. Regulators and central banks

- **Bank of England, Dec 2025 SWES scenario:** stress "would be easier to manage for closed-ended funds... compared with open-ended or semi-liquid funds which may experience redemption requests"; "semi-liquid structures face increased redemption requests, for some exceeding offered redemption limits" [S537]. **FSR, 7 Jul 2026:** "Redemption requests have been elevated in several retail funds, with some limiting redemptions, underlining both liquidity mismatch and valuation concerns"; UK stability impact judged limited [S538].
- **IMF GFSR, 14 Apr 2026:** ~$300bn of the ~$2tn direct-lending universe is redeemable; gates limit run risk; "to date systemic risk is contained" [S507].
- **FSB, 6 May 2026:** the "growing popularity of funds offering redemption options to investors... may heighten the procyclicality of private credit"; bank credit lines ~$220bn (estimates to $500bn) [S508].
- **Federal Reserve FSR, 8 May 2026:** evergreen vehicles saw "notable increases in redemption requests in late 2025 and early 2026", most capped at 5%; outflows modestly exceeded inflows in Q1; risks "manageable" [S531].
- **ECB FSR, May 2026:** mismatches "could grow as new partly retail-oriented semi-open private credit vehicles... expand"; US BDCs have faced "sizeable redemption requests"; US private credit ~$1.4tn (4.7% of GDP); euro-area exposure limited (~€100bn AUM) [S539].
- **FCA:** LTAFs ISA-eligible from April 2026 [S506]. No SEC warning located; a May 2026 SEC notice on monthly interval-fund repurchases appeared only in a snippet (Low confidence, no ID).

## 6. Trust between the wealth channel and managers

Managers say the structure worked as designed: Lipschultz — ~1% of investors made up most tenders, 90% stayed [S522], Q1 was "peak redemption" [S521]; Gray — caps are "a feature, not a bug" [S519]; Marshall — never expect 100% liquidity [S518]; Rowan — a first-lien manager unable to meet 5% is "an idiot" [S527]. Critics: NBER's run-fragility finding [S502]; Swain's "run on a bank" [S519]; Kephart: the market "has begun to collide with questions about how these structures actually behave" [S503]; Sternlicht on SREIT [S511]. Defences: sponsor/employee buying (~$400m, BCRED) and a lifted 7% cap [S518]; loan sales at 99.7% of par and OBDC II's conversion to return-of-capital [S516]; $500m OCIC bond [S529]. Damage: OTIC/OCIC paid only 13–27% of requests two quarters running [S534]; private credit fell to 18% of iCapital flows [S509]; institutions now buy what wealth clients sell [S535]. Resilience: Goldman's BDC at 3.24% [S532]; Blue Owl requests easing [S533]; BCRED's $17bn liquidity [S536].
