## 1. Blackstone: what has actually been launched or announced

**Correction to the lead.** Private Equity Wire's "SEC approves Blackstone's evergreen multi-asset credit fund" is dated **11 March 2025, not 2026** [S580]. The fund is **BMACX (Blackstone Private Multi-Asset Credit & Income Fund)**: an SEC-registered interval fund, daily subscriptions, quarterly repurchases of up to 5% of NAV at board discretion, 0.75% management fee, 12.5% incentive on income over a 5% hurdle, $2,500 minimum, S-share expenses 7.04% gross / 5.84% net, investing across corporate, asset-based, real-estate, structured and liquid credit [S580]. Launched 1 May 2025 via select RIAs [S581]. It is a 2025 launch now being scaled, not the September 2026 news.

**Platform stats (Q2 2026 call, 23 July).** Private-wealth AUM a record **$324bn, +16% YoY**; wealth sales $8.6bn; Gray: April–May flows were hit by geopolitics, then "a strong recovery in June" that "has continued so far in Q3" [S582]. Wealth AUM was $300bn at end-2025 (tripled in five years), 2025 wealth fundraising **$43bn, +53%**, and management said "we expect 2026 to be our busiest year yet in terms of product launches" [S601]. Forbes (Feb 2026, not read) reports a ~450-person wealth team target and a **$500bn** individual-investor AUM goal en route to $1tn [S600] (Med confidence).

**BCRED redemptions, 2026, by quarter.**
- Q1: requests **7.9% (~$3.8bn)**, a record; tender upsized to 7%, Blackstone and employees bought a feeder "on the same terms as all other investors"; **100% met** [S585][S588].
- Q2: requests **~10% (~$4.5bn)**; **5% honoured**, ~half, leaving ~$2.3bn backlog; NAV $42.8bn; gross sales $1bn, net outflow ~$1.2bn [S585][S583][S586]. Blackstone: "BCRED's structure is a fundamental feature" [S587].
- Q3: requests **~$4.3bn, ~10%** (largely resubmitted Q2 backlog); 5% honoured pro rata; investors queued in Q2 and Q3 "will have received an estimated 75% of their requested capital within approximately 90 days"; Class I 9.0% annualised net; >$17bn liquidity; 0.8x leverage [S584]. Gray's July claim that requests were "down materially" [S582][S583] looks optimistic against the 3 Sept ~10% figure [S584].

**BREIT.** Q2: $1.2bn raised, requests -42% YoY / -33% QoQ, "best regular-way net flows in nearly four years", NAV $57bn, 9.4% net since 2017, data centres 27%; all requests met in full since Feb 2024 [S586][S582].

**BXPE / BXINFRA.** BXPE raised $2.4bn in Q2, NAV >$25bn after ten quarters, 20% net annualised, June a record $1.2bn month; BXINFRA raised ~$0.9bn, NAV $6bn after six quarters, 16% annualised net [S582].

**Europe/Asia.** ECRED (Lux SICAV, Oct 2022) ~€5.2bn total assets / €2.6bn NAV at end-June 2026 [S592]. **BXINFRA Lux**, Blackstone's first ELTIF, announced 10 Sept 2025 for eligible individuals in the EEA, UK, Switzerland, APAC and Middle East, perpetual-life, CSSF-supervised [S591]. No Blackstone UK LTAF found; 2026 LTAF launches are BlackRock, Apollo, M&G, L&G [S609][S610]. BXPE SICAV is sold via UBS globally and UniCredit in Italy (search-derived, unregistered).

**BXHF.** Blackstone Multi-Strategy Hedge Fund, its first hedge fund for individuals: perpetual private fund for accredited investors who are also qualified purchasers ($5m), 1.25% / 12.5% over a 5% hurdle, sleeves in credit, equities, special situations and trading, subscriptions from summer 2026 [S594][S593][S582].

**Wellington–Vanguard–Blackstone.** Two interval funds launched 22 July 2026: **WVB All Markets Fund** (public equity and bonds plus Blackstone privates) and **WVB Blackstone All Privates Fund** (PE, infra, real estate, credit in one allocation); quarterly tenders, no guarantee of repurchase; sold first through Merrill and BofA Private Bank, RIAs next; the alliance is "exploring" retirement products; Gray: "simple and comprehensive access to help build long-term wealth" [S590].

**Retirement / regulation.** After the Aug 2025 executive order Blackstone created a DC unit (von Zuben, Nides, Quinlan) in Dec 2025 [S595]. DOL proposed a six-factor safe-harbour rule on **30 March 2026**; comments closed 1 June; final rule possible by end-2026 [S596]. On **1 Sept 2026** the SEC sent OMB a proposal to widen retail access via registered funds and extend performance fees (Bloomberg, "SEC Preps Plan to Widen Investor Access to Private Markets") [S616].

**What the user probably read.** No new Blackstone-branded evergreen launch was found in Sept 2026 (press index not fetchable, so not certain). The likeliest source is the FT's late-August piece by Heal and Livsey, "Institutional investors back Blackstone and KKR funds for wealthy individuals", describing Blackstone's four large evergreens for individuals and Solotar saying institutional money is still "only a small proportion" of capital raised; KKR lifted its K-Series deal cap from 7.5% to up to 20% and added institutional share classes [S597][S599]; secondary coverage ran to 6 Sept [S598]. Alternatives: the WVB/Merrill launch [S590] or the SEC plan [S616].

## 2. Landscape

**Size and pace.** Preqin (27 Feb 2026): record **123 launches in 2025** (49 credit, 32 PE); 30 in Jan–Feb 2026, ahead of 2025's pace; 337 launched 2023–25 vs 299 in 2016–22; AUM **~$530bn** end-2025; ELTIFs marketed 21 → 49 → 87 (2023–25); European private-wealth pool ~€9.1tn [S602]. Morningstar/PitchBook: **$607bn across 567 funds at Q1 2026** (direct lending $236.5bn, real estate $115.5bn, PE $99.3bn); 12-month flows PE +$16.3bn, alt credit +$12.3bn, direct lending +$6.3bn, real estate -$0.3bn [S604]. Morningstar (16 June): private credit net *outflow* ~$1bn in Q1; average expense ratio ~3%; only 4 of 19 rated funds Bronze/Silver; 16% of advisers "very familiar"; Kephart: the market "has begun to collide with questions about how these structures actually behave in practice" [S603].

**The 2026 stress test.** Trigger: Sept 2025 bankruptcies (Tricolor, First Brands) plus AI fears about software borrowers; alt-manager shares fell 41–66% Sept 2025–Mar 2026 (Blackstone -46%, Blue Owl -66%); Houlihan Lokey: "It resembles a run on a bank" [S588]. Q2 requests: Apollo Debt Solutions ~16.8%, Ares ASIF 14.4% (34.7% filled), BCRED 10%, Oaktree 4.5% (fully met) [S589]; HPS HLEND 13.3% in Q2, 11.5% in Q3, with Reuters noting "the redemption backlog is now clearing" [S612]. Partners Group gated its Global Value SICAV after 9.8% requests, guided 1–2pp lower net-AUM growth [S611], and per the FT replaced its CEO "as fund redemptions weigh on shares" [S616]. FT/RA Stanger: retail evergreen PE/VC fundraising grew only marginally YoY in Q1 2026 (a secondary summary cites +2% vs +55%), private-credit retail inflows fell sharply; infrastructure and real-estate evergreens still grew mid-double-digits [S605].

**"Evergreen 2.0" responses.** Gates were not tightened (Partners Group: "no intention of altering any documented liquidity mechanisms") [S611]. Innovation is in sponsor co-investment [S585], institutional share classes and higher deal caps [S597], multi-asset bundles (BMACX, WVB) [S580][S590][S601], daily pricing (Apollo) and deal-level benchmarks (StepStone/PitchBook) [S605], and continuation vehicles selling retail exits to institutions [S588]. iCapital's counter-view: gates "protect investors from a 'run on the bank'", 2026 redemptions are "market-driven" while portfolios stay healthy; public BDCs at ~80% of NAV show the valuation doubt [S614].

**Institutions and gatekeepers.** Pensions and endowments are buying wealth evergreens because traditional funds "struggled to exit investments and distribute proceeds" [S597][S599]. JPM Private Bank: ~20% of its alternatives assets are in evergreens, 4x five years ago; "careful manager selection will be critical" [S613]. WVB launched exclusively on Merrill/BofA [S590]; CAIS added ~40 managers in Aug 2026 [S618]; Capital Group/KKR targets 220,000 US advisers [S610]. Model portfolios with private sleeves are offered by BlackRock, Fidelity and iCapital (search-derived).

**UK.** LTAFs became ISA-eligible on **6 April 2026**; ~25 LTAFs, £7.3bn AUM (from £5bn June 2025) plus £3.1bn committed; Morningstar: ISA access "doesn't remove the need for careful due diligence" [S608]. Mansion House pushes DC schemes into private markets; Apollo's first UK LTAF (March 2026, Carne as ACD) targets DC [S609][S610].

**Europe.** Scope: ELTIF AUM **€34bn at end-2025, +55%**, roughly a third each PE, infrastructure, private debt; ~100 launches and ~€50bn forecast for 2026, €65–70bn by 2027 [S607]. Ares added a second (infrastructure) ELTIF in Aug 2026 [S598]; Goldman is preparing a PE ELTIF; General Atlantic launched a wealth evergreen ahead of its IPO [S616]; EQT launched an APAC evergreen and Neuberger's $2bn evergreen joined a UK wealth platform [S617].

**Tokenisation.** Apollo's ACRED tokenised feeder (Securitize) reached HashKey in Hong Kong in July 2026: 50,000 USDC minimum, daily NAV, but still a quarterly 5% gate and non-transferable tokens [S615]. Aberdeen appointed a Hong Kong distributor for tokenised interests in Sept 2026 [S617].

**Competitor 2026 wealth launches (selected).** Oaktree/Brookfield OABIX interval fund, Jan 2026, $400m at launch, Brookfield's sixth wealth evergreen [S619]; KKR Infrastructure ELTIF (Jan 2026), K-Series AUM +70% YoY (search-derived); Capital Group KKR U.S. Equity+ (March 2026) [S610]; T. Rowe Price Goldman Sachs Private Markets Fund, $2,500 minimum, no accreditation (July 2026) [S606]; Apollo UK LTAF [S609]; BlackRock DC LTAF (May 2026) and BlackRock/Partners Group SMA (Jan 2026), Morgan Stanley NHPAF, Carlyle wealth PE, State Street/Apollo PRIV ETF (all search-derived); Fidelity's route is model portfolios rather than a fund.

## 4. So what

**Bull case.** Permanent capital: evergreens charge continuously with no fundraising gap, and Blackstone's wealth AUM grew 16% YoY through the worst redemption cycle since 2022 [S582]. The pool is huge and under-allocated: European private wealth alone ~€9.1tn, only 37% of those investors hold evergreens [S602]. Structures worked as designed: BCRED met 100% in Q1 (with sponsor money) and 5% thereafter, queued investors got ~75% of cash within ~90 days, liquidity >$17bn, leverage 0.8x [S584][S585]; BREIT, the 2022–23 test case, is at four-year-best net flows [S586]. The 5% cap protected remaining holders from forced sales, and iCapital argues 2026 redemptions were sentiment-driven while credit performance held [S614]. Regulatory tailwinds: DOL safe harbour, SEC access proposal, UK ISA eligibility, ELTIF 2.0 [S596][S616][S608][S607]. Institutions are now validating the products [S597].

**Bear case.** Liquidity mismatch is real: requests ran at 2–3x the cap at Ares, Apollo, HPS and BCRED for two to three quarters [S589][S612][S584]; Morningstar: "when, not if, a run of redemptions will hit" [S604]. Mis-selling risk: 16% of advisers "very familiar", average expenses ~3%, BMACX S-shares 7.04% gross [S603][S580]; retail demand proved fickle [S605]. Marks lag: public BDCs at ~80% of NAV [S614]; Apollo's move to daily pricing is a tacit admission [S605]. Adverse selection: paying redemptions from repayments and the most saleable assets leaves remaining holders with the weakest loans; continuation vehicles shift rather than remove the problem [S588]. Contagion via distribution: the same wirehouses and private banks hold clients across many sponsors, so one gate hits peers' gross sales, which Evercore called "a larger, and more prolonged issue" than the request level itself [S587]. Sponsor risk: Partners Group lost its CEO and cut guidance [S611][S616]; alt-manager equities fell 40–66% in six months [S588].

**Net.** Evergreens are now the default wealth wrapper (record launches, $600bn+, institutional adoption); the 2026 cycle showed the gates hold; the price is that "semi-liquid" means ~5% a quarter in stress, paid for with ~3% fees and valuation opacity. For an adviser, suitability, sizing (a 12–24 month liquidity budget) and sponsor selection matter more than product novelty.
