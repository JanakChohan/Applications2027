# Research stream 2: JPMorgan Chase strategic evolution 2021-2026 from the 10-Ks, Dimon letters and Investor Days
Compiled 8 Sep 2026.

Headline: three phases. (1) 2021-22: controversial step-up in investment spend defended as "investing through the cycle". (2) 2023-24: First Republic, record profits, "excess capital as earnings in store", regulatory fight. (3) 2025-26: $1.5tn Security and Resiliency Initiative, AI cutting headcount in "discrete areas", pan-European digital consumer, deposit tokens, formal succession clock.

## FY2021 / 2021 letter (Apr 2022) / May 2022 Investor Day
- 2021: net income $48.3bn on revenue $125.3bn; ex-$9.2bn reserve releases "still earned 18% on tangible equity". AWM revenue $16,957m, NI $4,737m, ROE 33%; headcount 271,025.
- Jan 2022: revealed ~$15bn of investments for 2022, expense guidance ~$77bn; said it might miss 17% ROTCE; shares fell; first Investor Day since 2020 held in response (May 2022).
- 2021 letter: "expenses related to investments would increase from $11.5 billion to $15 billion"; "Over the last 18 months, we spent nearly $5 billion on acquisitions"; "$2.2 billion building new, cloud-based data centers"; "Stock buybacks... will be lower"; G-SIB rules cost "$30 billion" of Tier 1 vs European peers; "the moats that protect this company are not particularly deep — and we face extraordinary competition"; "Persistent inflation will require rising interest rates and a massive but necessary shift from quantitative easing to quantitative tightening".
- ID 2022 Dimon: "We've always invested through the cycle... there are good expenses and bad expenses. Bad expenses are things which lead to bureaucracy, waste, corporate statism... Good expenses are new branches, new bankers... technology"; capital hierarchy: "Pay your dividend consistently, invest in the company, organic growth... acquisitions... and then stock buyback... that's the last thing". Barnum: "17% ROTCE through the cycle... this is not an aspirational target". NII guidance raised to >$56bn. Dimon 1 Jun 2022: "it's a hurricane".
- Chase UK launched Sep 2021: 8 months in "over 0.5 million customers... over $10 billion in deposits"; cash burn "$450 million" in 2022; "break even in the next five to six years". Nutmeg (2021). C6 Bank Brazil. Viva Wallet 49% (~$800m). 2021 letter: "build a consumer bank outside the United States that, over time, can become very competitive."
- Acquisitions: FY2021 goodwill from Nutmeg, OpenInvest, Frank, The Infatuation, Campbell Global; 55ip and cxLoyalty (2020). 2022: Global Shares ($730m), Viva Wallet stake, Renovite, Frosch. 2023: Aumni; C6 to 46%; CIFM China to 100%. Fortune: 80+ companies invested in since early 2021, ~13 acquisitions; Dimon told managers to be "scared shitless" about PayPal/Square/Stripe. Kraft Analytics: unverified.

## FY2022 / 2022 letter (Apr 2023)
- Revenue $132.3bn, NI $37.7bn, ROTCE 18%, "record revenue for the fifth year in a row". AWM rev $17,748m, NI $4,365m, ROE 25%. Headcount 293,723.
- "enough total loss-absorbing capacity to bear out peak CCAR losses... more than eight times over"; Basel III endgame: "Banks will play a smaller role in the global financial system"; "Regulatory arbitrage is already forcing many activities... outside the banking system".
- Risks: deficits "$3.1 trillion (2020), $2.8 trillion (2021) and $1.4 trillion (2022)"; "we may have gone from a savings glut to scarce capital and may be headed to higher inflation and higher interest rates"; "We have not had a major land war in Europe since 1945".
- "more than 300 AI use cases in production"; 900+ data scientists; "#1 on the Evident AI Index"; "migrated approximately 38% of our applications to the cloud"; 57,000 technologists. ID 2023: "$1.5 billion of value" from AI by end-2023.
- May 2023 ID: NII ex-Markets guidance to ~$84bn on ~$3bn from First Republic.

## FY2023 / 2023 letter (Apr 2024)
- Record NI $49.6bn, revenue $162.4bn, ROTCE 21%. AWM rev $19,827m, NI $5,227m, ROE 31%. Headcount 309,926.
- First Republic (1 May 2023): acquired from FDIC for $67.9bn consideration; bargain purchase gain $2.7bn; headline cash $10.6bn; ~$173bn loans, ~$30bn securities, ~$92bn deposits; FDIC $50bn five-year fixed-rate financing; loss-share 80% of credit losses (commercial 5 yrs + 3 recovery; single-family 7 yrs). Pinto: "Jamie always says that excess capital is just future earnings and I think this is a nice example of that... deploy that 40 basis points of excess capital... at extremely attractive returns".
- Dimon 2023 letter: "First Republic had done an outstanding job serving high-net-worth clients and venture capitalists, and we are developing what is effectively a new business for us following First Republic's servicing model"; earnings contribution "closer to $2 billion" vs $500m announced; ~7,600 employees on integration. Piepszak ID 2023: FRC Private Wealth Management "most similar to the J.P. Morgan Advisors model... adding about 200 advisors and $200 billion in assets" (went to CCB); private banking relationships to GPB; Erdoes: FRC "insurance and annuities, trusts and estates, and fund finance... will drastically help". FY2023 10-K: $144.6bn FRC client investment assets; ~4,500 FRC people joined 2 Jul 2023. Retention: exec said ~75% of FRC brokers and clients retained; 86+ brokers with ~$50bn left.
- AI: "more than 2,000 AI/ML experts and data scientists"; "over 400 use cases"; "possibly as transformational as... the printing press, the steam engine, electricity, computing and the Internet".
- Basel: "many of the rules are flawed and poorly calibrated"; "increase our firm's required capital by 25%".
- "We plan to build an additional 500 branches and hire 3,500 employees over the next three years"; rates "2% to 8% or even more". Succession: "up to [the Board]".

## FY2024 / 2024 letter (Apr 2025) / May 2024 ID
- NI $58.5bn, revenue $180.6bn (7th straight record), ROTCE 20% (22% ex-Visa gain), EPS $19.75. AWM rev $21,578m, NI $5,421m, ROE 34%. Headcount 317,233.
- Reorg announced 25 Jan 2024, effective 2Q24: CIB + Commercial Banking = Commercial & Investment Bank; Lake sole CEO CCB; Piepszak and Rohrbaugh co-CEOs CIB. Erdoes: "the new behemoth of the CIB and the CB".
- Excess capital: ID 2024 Barnum: "we still don't see particularly compelling opportunities to deploy capital"; illustrative "$78 billion of excess capital as of 1Q 2025". Dimon rejected more buybacks at current prices; ~$54bn excess. Plan to hire ~1,300 advisers over three years to push wealth assets past $1tn (CCB). 2024 letter: "we look at excess capital as earnings in store or reserve — waiting to be used".
- AI: Pinto: "$1 billion to $1.5 billion" value; "60,000 developers... 80,000 people [ops/call centres]... almost half of the company where this technology will be very, very impactful". LLM Suite 200k+ employees. Tech spend ~$17bn (2024) -> ~$18bn (2025).
- 500 branches over five years. Feb 2025: direct lending commitment $50bn + ~$15bn co-lenders after ">$10 billion across over 100 private credit deals since 2021". 2024 expense ~$92bn incl. $1bn Visa-stock gift.
- Succession May 2024: "The timetable isn't five years, anymore".
- 2024 letter: "the most perilous and complicated geopolitical and economic environment since World War II"; "The recent tariffs will likely increase inflation"; "constantly fighting complacency, arrogance and bureaucracy"; rules "written by academics with no pragmatic experience". RTO: 10 Jan 2025 memo, five days from March 2025; Feb 2025 town hall: "I don't care how many people sign that f***ing petition"; "We're going back to the office".

- No May 2026 Investor Day. Company Update 23 Feb 2026; next Investor Day Q1 2027.
- FY2025: NI $57.0bn ($20.02), managed revenue $185.6bn, ROE 17%, ROTCE 20%, CET1 14.5%, employees 318,512, market cap $868.8bn (end-2025); expense $95.6bn; $2.2bn reserve for Apple Card forward purchase; $46bn returned to shareholders. ## Ten strategic through-lines
1. Fortress balance sheet as licence to act (TCE $49bn 2005 -> $260bn 2024 -> $280bn 2025; "allowed us to do the First Republic transaction"; "we don't run the company hoping for good times").
2. Invest through the cycle; "good expenses vs bad expenses"; "We will never stop investing in new advisors"; but "not carte blanche for solving every problem by adding resources".
3. Excess capital = earnings in store; buybacks last; ~$40bn excess earning 4%; "$40-50 billion organically over the next five years".
4. Scale, completeness, one firm: "we are not a conglomerate — all our businesses rely on and benefit from each other"; "complete, global, diversified, and at scale"; no internal pricing for referrals; counterpoint "Size can often be a tremendous business disadvantage".
5. Technology and AI as moat and cost: 300 -> 400 -> ~1,000 use cases; "past peak modernization"; "30% or 40%" cuts in discrete areas; "you don't uniquely benefit from AI"; Erdoes "end game winner".
6. Moats are shallow: "the moats that protect this company are not particularly deep" (2021); "the walls that protect this company are not particularly high" (2025); Block, Citadel Securities, Revolut, Stripe; "We've got our asses kicked in certain parts".
7. International expansion: digital consumer (Chase UK/Germany, "hundreds of millions") plus private bank ("largest international private bank among US banks"; "others tend to be pausing or retreating here").
8. Relentless hiring of client-facing people: "more than 60,000 people... over 900 branches" in five years; first driver of 2026 expense growth "bankers, advisors and branches"; CIB bankers +1,000 since 2022.
9. Regulatory pushback then cautious embrace of deregulation.
10. "Risks we worry about" always listed: inflation, QT, Ukraine, deficits, "hurricane", rates 2-8%, "most perilous since WWII", tariffs, Iran, private credit "cockroaches", "tectonic plates".
Net read-through: deploy $40-50bn organically (branches, advisors, Apple Card, SRI equity), hold 17% ROTCE backstop, AI compresses ops while front office grows, AWM to $10tn client assets, Chase across Europe, CEO handover to Petno or Rohrbaugh within ~3 years.
