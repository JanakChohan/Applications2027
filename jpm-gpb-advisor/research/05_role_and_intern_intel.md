# Research stream 5: what a private banker does, industry context, application intelligence
Compiled 8 Sep 2026.

## Fundamentals
- Retail banking: products for everyone. Wealth management: advice on investing around goals; in the US often RIAs with no balance sheet. Private banking = wealth management plus a banking balance sheet (deposits, lending, investing under one roof). Asset management: pooled funds for institutions and individuals (JPMAM, George Gatch). Investment banking serves companies; the private bank serves the people who own or run them.
- Segments (Capgemini): mass affluent $100k-1m; $1-5m; $5-30m; UHNW $30m+ (~1% of millionaires, ~34% of their wealth). JPM ladder: Chase Private Client $150k; J.P. Morgan Private Client $750k ("dedicated banker at no fee"); Private Bank ~$10m (raised from $5m in 2016 per secondary source); UHNW unit ~$25m+ liquid per Pablo Garnica. JPM private-equity-professionals practice: 200+ sponsor firms, 1,000+ partners, $140bn+ AUS.
- JPM four lenses: Liquidity, Lifestyle, Legacy, Growth (goals-based planning).
- LTCMA 2026 expected returns: US large cap 6.7%, global equities 7.0%, EM 7.8%, US Treasuries 4.0-4.9%, IG credit 5.2%, HY 6.1%, private equity 10.2%, US core real estate 8.2%, infrastructure 6.5%. Adding 30% alternatives lifts 60/40 expected return from 6.4% to 6.9% with 25% better Sharpe.
- JPM alternatives guide: committed clients hold 15-30% of investable assets in alternatives, ramped over 3-5 years; mix 40-60% PE, 10-30% private credit, 10-30% real assets, 10-30% venture/growth. Drawdown vs evergreen funds. UHNW hold ~22%, HNW ~4%, large family offices 45-46%, institutions ~55%.
- Mandates: discretionary ("authority to make investment decisions on your behalf, according to investment parameters you set"); advisory ("you retain final decision-making authority"); execution-only.
- Fees: discretionary 0.75-1.5% p.a.; all-in ~1.0-1.5% on £1-5m falling to 0.25-0.5% above £50m; fund charges 0.5-1.5%; FX spreads 0.5-1.5%. JPM EMEA page notes it "prefers J.P. Morgan managed strategies" and earns more when in-house funds are used.
- Lombard/SBL LTVs: govt/IG bonds 80-95%, large-cap equities 60-70%, diversified portfolios 50-70%, concentrated stock 20-50%, private shares often excluded; priced at SONIA/SOFR/EURIBOR + margin; margin calls. Why borrow not sell: no CGT crystallisation; keep exposure; speed; suits lumpy-income entrepreneurs. Specialty lending: aircraft, art, GP commitments, co-investments, monetising illiquid stakes. JPM "largest lending business in the private banking space"; financed "10 of the last 15 major sports transactions"; 60% of major sports team owners are clients.
- Wealth planning: pre-liquidity planning starts 18-24 months before a sale (strategies "may only be available before the sale and not after"). Cerulli: 77% of HNW practices use grantor trusts, 81% run family meetings.
- KYC: source of wealth (how fortune built) vs source of funds (this deposit); PEPs and complex structures trigger enhanced due diligence; analysts do "document collection and account openings" and "due diligence on existing clients and key prospects".

## Revenue model and book metrics
- Lines: recurring fees on assets; NII (Q2 2026 AWM avg loans $284bn +18%, avg deposits $260bn); transaction revenue; placement/alternatives fees ("$2 billion to work every single month"; clients "so hungry for alternatives"). 73% of AWM revenue recurring. Q2 2026 AWM: pretax margin 38%, ROE 48%, $50bn LT net inflows; expenses +13% partly from "continued growth in private banking advisor teams".
- Julius Baer gross margin 82-87 bps on AUM; cost/income 62.6%. Rule of thumb: 70-100 bps revenue on client assets, 60-70% cost, remainder profit.
- Book metrics: client assets/AUS; NNM/NNA ("we are not number one in AUM, but we are number one in flows"); revenue on book; ROA in bps; share of wallet (exclusive single-firm relationships halved 39% -> 19% 2019-2025; 35-50% of PB relationships loss-making). Carly Pierpoint (JPM AWM recruitment): "You have the ability to capitalize on full wallet, which most other firms can't provide". Advisor productivity "up 3.4 times". RM KPIs: new clients, NNM, revenue, cross-sell, referrals.

## What the banker does
- Team (Garnica): banker/RM (captain), investor, capital advisor (cash, deposits, lending), wealth advisor (trusts, estates, structuring), client service specialist. Advisor internship "gain[s] exposure to every facet... investments, lending, banking, and trust and estate planning"; works "with the team of advisors and specialists to bring in new clients and to serve existing ones".
- Week: prospecting (referrals highest-converting; COIs; IB/CB; PE partners; liquidity events; disqualify fast; ask for referrals); discovery (map balance sheet, banks, tax residence, family, goals); investment proposal (allocation, holdings analysis, lending capacity, cash plan; "coordinating with global investment specialists and wealth advisors on client pitches and meeting agendas"; "preparing account reviews"); reviews and market calls (quarterly; Connect Coach surfaces "who doesn't have European stocks", "a sample e-mail or talking points"); events (NextGen programmes); compliance/CRM; travel (London, Paris, Milan, Dubai, Geneva, Zurich, Madrid, Bahrain, Frankfurt).
- Hours ~50/week (M&I), lighter than IB. Skills: "a unique blend of interpersonal and analytical skills", "genuine interest in financial markets, investing, and macro-level economics", integrity, discretion, presentation, multitasking. Garnica: "UHNW clients are sophisticated, complex and highly demanding." Downside: sales culture, junior years are support work, limited IB/PE exits.

## Application intelligence
- 9 weeks London; week 1 induction; top performers get FT offers; penultimate year; no .edu emails. FT programme lists London, Manchester, Edinburgh; regional orientation then global training in New York.
- Flow: online application -> (possibly pymetrics 12 games, unverified) -> HireVue (3-5 questions, 30s-2min prep, 90-120s answers, one retry; pool of 100-150) -> superday ~2 weeks later: three back-to-back ~30-min interviews, "very behavioral", STAR. PB: "no technicals, but required some working knowledge about the current market".
- Reported questions: why JPM; why Private Bank ("articulate well why you want to work in Private Banking and specifically why JPM"); team under tight deadline; difficult client; conflict; analysing info from multiple sources; building trust with a new client; balancing client needs with practical solutions; persistence; ownership; coachability; "How do you keep updated on financial news and what trends are you seeing?"; "How do banks make money? Specifically, how do IB, WM, lending, and sales and trading make money?"; how you would assess a client's goals and make recommendations. Stock pitch: possible, not verified.
- What they want: "relationship builder", "genuine interest in financial markets, investing, and macro-level economics", discretion, presentation. Banker seat tests interpersonal over quantitative. Advice: 2-3 market views with a "so what" for a wealthy client; know four lenses and five-role team; explain how the bank makes money on one client three ways; frame past experience as evidence of persistence and trust-building.
