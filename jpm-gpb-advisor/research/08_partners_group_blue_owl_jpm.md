## 1. Partners Group (SIX: PGHN) — evergreen pioneer under redemption pressure

**1 Sept 2026 announcement.** Release title: "Partners Group reports H1 results as management income rises in line with AuM; announces rotations to the Executive Team" [S540]. From 1 Jan 2027 Roberto Cagnati (Partner, joined 2004; Head of Portfolio Solutions and Chief Risk Officer) and Juri Jenkner (Partner and President, joined 2004; formerly Head of Infrastructure and Head of Private Credit) become Co-CEOs; David Layton becomes CIO and Chairman of the Global Investment Committee [S540]. Meister: "As we prepare our firm to gain speed in this next cycle and era of transformation, we are proud to announce Roberto and Juri as our new Co-CEOs" [S540]. Layton's quote covered fundraising only ("over USD 80 billion in client commitments... Our exit pipeline remains full"); no explicit reason is given beyond "rotations" for the "next cycle" [S540].

**H1 2026** [S540]: AuM USD 186bn (+7%); fundraising USD 16bn; management income CHF 905m (+12% constant currency); performance income CHF 216m vs 355m (−39% in CHF), 19% of revenues vs 29%; revenues CHF 1,121m (−7%); EBITDA CHF 706m (−9%), margin 63%; profit CHF 502m (−13%). Guidance: 2026 performance income 20–25% of revenues (25–40% mid-term kept); new client demand USD 26–32bn.

**Headlines.** Reuters, 1 Sept: "Partners Group replaces CEO as it works through evergreen fund redemptions" — profit −13% to CHF 502m (~USD 620m); shares "fallen more than 30% year to date amid redemption pressure in its evergreen products, which the firm expects to continue"; it had "capped withdrawals at its $8.6 billion European evergreen fund and moved to do the same at the now-$15 billion US equivalent" [S541]. Bloomberg, 1 Sept: "Partners Group Names New Co-CEOs as Performance Fees Slump" [S542]. Bloomberg via swissinfo: shares −8.6% intraday, ~−32% YTD; profit missed CHF 521m consensus; evergreens ~30% of assets (~USD 56bn); Meister on departures: "That's not PG style" [S543].

**Evergreen timeline.**
- Global Value SICAV (Luxembourg Part II) launched 2007, ~USD 7.9bn at 30 June 2026 (site data, unverified) [S551].
- 4 June 2026 release: GV SICAV Q2 requests ~9.8% of NAV, so the 5%/quarter limitation applies; the Delaware US PE evergreen had requests ~6%, finalised end-July; three other mature evergreens (USD 9.7bn, mostly institutional) 3.5–5%; AuM ~80% institutional / ~20% private wealth; net AuM growth to slow 1–2 pts in H2 2026 and 2027 [S544]. Layton: "Liquidity features are designed to protect long-term investors, and to ensure returns are driven by quality of underlying private assets rather than short-term flow dynamics" [S544].
- Bloomberg, 3 June: "Partners Caps Evergreen Fund Redemptions as Requests Rise" [S545]. Bloomberg/swissinfo, 4 June: shares −18.2% on 3 June; US fund Partners Group Private Equity (Master Fund) USD 15.8bn; "The firm is prepared to enact the respective liquidity limitation mechanism across other funds" [S546]. FT: firm "set to cap withdrawals" at the ~USD 16bn US fund [S547].
- SEC tender (28 Apr 2026): the US vehicle (now Partners Group Private Equity Fund, LLC) offered ~5% of net assets (~USD 788m on NAV USD 15.77bn), pro rata if oversubscribed [S548].
- Reuters, 16 July: H1 withdrawals USD 3.8bn, 79% from three mature evergreen strategies; evergreens 26% of H1 commitments; trends expected "for several quarters" [S549].
- Reuters, 25 June (Meister): "We clearly don't see the need to change our strategy"; "We might keep them slightly smaller in size going forward, more aligned to flow dynamics over time"; the firm "has no intention of altering any documented liquidity mechanisms and has no plans to freeze any of its evergreen vehicles"; insiders bought >CHF 60m of stock after 3 June [S550].

No evergreen quote from Cagnati or Jenkner was found in the release or coverage [S540][S543].

## 2. Blue Owl Capital (NYSE: OWL) — the saga in date order

- **21 Oct 2025 (context):** Meta–Blue Owl Hyperion JV: ~USD 27bn cost, Blue Owl funds 80%/Meta 20%, ~USD 7bn Blue Owl cash, ~USD 3bn distribution to Meta, PIMCO-led bonds, Meta residual value guarantee [S567].
- **5 Nov 2025:** OBDC II to merge into listed OBDC; tenders halted pending the deal (~USD 150m withdrawn in 9M25); the FT (16 Nov) flagged a ~20% haircut as OBDC traded ~20% below NAV. **19 Nov:** merger terminated citing "current market conditions"; a SDNY class action followed [S552].
- **19 Feb 2026:** USD 1.4bn of loans sold at 99.7% of par to four pension/insurance buyers (OBDC II ~USD 600m, 34% of its book; OBDC and OTIC USD 400m each). OBDC II permanently ended quarterly redemptions, replaced by return-of-capital distributions (up to USD 2.35/sh, ~30% of NAV); Packer cited "strong demand... at fair value" [S553]. This is the "no more early redemptions" episode — OBDC II, not OCIC.
- **5 Feb 2026:** Lipschultz: "AI can be massively disruptive and it can certainly hurt some of these software companies. But does it hurt so many of the right ones down to 30% of their value? It doesn't," over "the average three-year duration of the loans" [S554].
- **2 Apr 2026:** CNBC: "Blue Owl caps private credit funds redemptions at 5% after steep request levels" — Q1 requests 21.9% at OCIC (~USD 36bn), 40.7% at OTIC (15.4% prior quarter), blamed on "heightened market concerns around AI-related disruption to software companies" [S555]. OWL hit USD 7.95, 68.2% below the USD 25.02 peak (Jan 2025); OTIC ~USD 6.2bn; requests ~USD 5.4bn; Ares and Apollo had similar caps [S556].
- **30 Apr 2026 (Q1 call):** Lipschultz: net outflows "of roughly $170 million from OCIC and OTIC were less than 6 basis points of our beginning of period AUM"; "1% of investors representing the majority of tenders and approximately 90% of the investor base electing not to tender"; a "headline-driven, not fundamental-driven redemption environment"; "we are working down our exposure to software". Kirshenbaum: advisers "want the products to work as designed, 5% tenders per quarter, not more. The reason for the 5%... is so that shareholders benefit from the asset class, the illiquidity premium"; OCIC paydowns ~USD 3bn vs USD 1bn gross redemptions, "3x covered". Wealth raised ~USD 3bn of USD 11bn total [S557]. FRE USD 393.6m, DE USD 292.5m, AUM USD 314.9bn [S558]. The phrases "trust" and "we did what we said" do not appear in the Q1 transcript; the closest is "worked as designed" [S557].
- **8 June 2026:** Bloomberg: "Blue Owl Credit Income Launches $500 Million Bond Sale After Redemption Caps" — 5-year notes at T+255bp, reoffer 98.771, to repay debt [S559].
- **2 July 2026 (Q2 tenders):** OCIC 8-K: requests 18.8% (USD 3.6bn vs 4.2bn, −14%); 5% tender filled ~27% pro rata (22.8% in Q1); YTD inflows ~USD 1.2bn; liquidity USD 11.6bn, ~12x the USD 958m tender; non-accruals 0.2% [S560]. Semafor (Liz Hoffman): USD 3.6bn/19% and USD 1.1bn/38%; OTIC requests "largely from Asian family offices"; shares opened +10% [S561]. OTIC: 38.1% (40.4% in Q1), ~13% pro rata, portfolio USD 5.2bn, liquidity USD 1.3bn [S562].
- **30 July 2026 (Q2 call):** Kirshenbaum: "a modest reduction in redemption requests"; "90% of our OCIC fund investors [did] not request a single dollar"; 1 July evergreen inflows ">50%" above 1 May. Lipschultz: redemptions "much more narrow than I think anybody probably thought"; "not seeing compression in cap rates" on data centres [S563]. FRE USD 0.25/sh, DE USD 0.22/sh, dividend USD 0.23; Q2 raised USD 7.8bn, ~75% institutional; AUM USD 319bn; direct lending 35% of AUM vs ~50% two years ago [S564].
- **Q3 2026:** OCIC's tender runs 1–30 Sept 2026 (payment ~30 Oct); size and results not yet published [S565]. OWL ~USD 10.24 on 16 Sept 2026 (52-week 7.95–19.29), ~−59% from peak (quote page; verify) [S566].

**Why "AI disruption" hit Blue Owl:** OTIC (Blue Owl Technology Income Corp) lends to software companies; the early-2026 AI-driven software sell-off made investors doubt those borrowers, hence ~40%/38% requests versus ~22%/19% at diversified OCIC [S555][S561]; Blue Owl's defence is senior-secured, ~three-year loans to large sponsor-backed firms, with exposure being reduced [S554][S557].

## 3. The JPMorgan angle

**(a) Dimon.** Q3 2025 call (15 Oct 2025), after Tricolor (USD 170m JPM charge-off) and First Brands: "when you see one cockroach, there are probably more... everyone should be forewarned on this one" [S568]. Shareholder letter (Apr 2026): leveraged-lending losses in a cycle "will be higher than expected, relative to the environment"; "credit standards have been modestly weakening pretty much across the board"; some "assume it will never happen"; weaker covenants, more PIK [S569]. Q1 2026 call: "I don't think it's systemic"; "You have to have very large losses in private credit before banks are going to get hit"; ~USD 1.7tn, "large but contained" [S570]. On competing (CNBC, 2 Mar 2026): JPMorgan "obviously underwrite[s] credit and compete[s]" (snippet) [S571]. The exact phrase "we compete and we partner" was not found in a primary source.

**(b) JPM's push.** 24 Feb 2025: USD 50bn balance-sheet direct-lending commitment plus ~USD 15bn from co-lenders; >USD 10bn deployed in >100 deals since 2021 [S572]. Q2 2026 (14 July): AWM revenue USD 6.9bn (+19%), long-term net inflows USD 50bn, client assets USD 7.7tn; no alternatives/evergreen remarks on the call [S573]. Private Bank platform (Euromoney, Mar 2026): alternatives from ~USD 40bn (2010) to >USD 200bn (July 2025), goal to double in five years; ~50% of assets for the 200 largest clients; 18-person structuring team; evergreen shelf across PE, credit and real assets; "PIPPA" pacing/liquidity analytics; Kallergis Rowland: "no one... has anchored more solutions than us this year... in the rise of evergreens" [S574]. Erdoes (Investor Day, May 2025): "In the private bank, we already put $2 billion to work every single month"; "with these evergreen strategies, you see the green in the evergreen. That's really the retail side that's going to grow" [S575]. JPMAM launched two SICAV Part II evergreens for EMEA wealth on 15 Sept 2026: JPMorgan Private Markets Fund (JPMF, PE) and JPMorgan Credit Markets Fund (JCRED, private debt) [S579].

**(c) Cembalest.** "The Deep End" (2 Dec 2025): private credit earned ~300bp over leveraged loans; PIK share "barely moving"; but 15 years without a real recession leaves underwriting untested; covenants loosening; retail fundraising from USD 25bn to an annualised USD 175bn, and documentation "firewalls" will be stressed "if a sharp recession and liquidity crunch coincide" [S578].

**(d) Adviser implications.** JPM Private Bank (12 Mar 2026): requests "driven more by sentiment than by fundamentals"; "gates and redemption queues are prudent tools"; keep private credit ~15% of core private-markets allocation (~1.5–4.5% of portfolio); "ensure risk taken is intentional" [S576]. 16 Apr 2026: "gates are simply a feature of the vehicles' structure"; "manager selection matters more as dispersion rises"; diversify across direct lending, ABF, opportunistic credit [S577]. In practice: a 5% quarterly tender means a full exit can take five-plus quarters when oversubscribed (OCIC filled 27%, OTIC 13% in Q2) [S560][S562]; semi-liquid funds cannot be the client's emergency reserve; "know what you own" means sector concentration (software at OTIC), pro rata mechanics, and whether tenders are funded by asset sales or borrowing (OCIC's USD 500m bond) [S559][S562].
