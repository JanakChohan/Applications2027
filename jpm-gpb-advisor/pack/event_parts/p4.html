Part 4

Questions to ask, by who is in front of you, and how to introduce yourself

Each question comes with why it is a good one and what to listen for. Pick three. Write them in the notebook before you go in.

Your thirty-second introduction

Say this, adjusted to the person
"Janak Chohan, penultimate-year Economics at UEA. I spent this summer at Pictet Asset Management in client servicing and onboarding, mostly on due-diligence questionnaires from distributors, which is where I got interested in how private-markets funds are sold to wealthy clients and what happened to evergreens this year. Before that a placement year at Box in client operations and a spring week on Goldman's trading floor. I have applied for the Advisor internship here. What I am trying to understand today is [one thing: how a new banker builds a book / how the team decides who leads a client / what the scenario taught you when you did it]."

It names three firms, one real theme and one honest question in under thirty seconds, and it tells them you have applied. Vary the last sentence by host. Never ask a question whose answer is on the website; ask the question the website raises.

For a senior leader

QuestionWhy it lands, and what to listen for
"Adam Tejpaul described a shift from an offshore, London-centric model to onshore, multi-hub, with Manchester, Edinburgh and Glasgow. What does a client in Manchester get from a Manchester banker that they did not get from London?"Shows you read the Euromoney interview. Listen for: proximity to regional entrepreneurs, referrals from the commercial bank's regional offices, the cost of a London hire versus a regional one [S705][S758].
"The firm says advisor hiring will normalise later this decade after growing 11% a year. What changes in how a new banker is given a book when the hiring wave is over?"Shows you read the Erdoes letter and understood the economics of "seeded books". Listen for: internal referrals, team splits, the Investor track as the alternative route [S815][S901].
"With Europe named as the biggest share opportunity and the UK's tax regime changing, where is the next five years of growth in this region: UK onshore, the Continent, or the Middle East desks run from here?"Forces a real answer about allocation. Listen for which region they personally own, and whether the Budget comes up unprompted [S705][S739][S775].
"The Private Bank's strategists wrote that gates are a feature of evergreen structures. After this year, how has the bank changed how it sizes semi-liquid funds for a client?"The most current question in the room. Listen for: liquidity budgets, the pacing tool, manager concentration (the firm backs fewer than six direct-lending managers), and whether they mention the September launches [S576][S577][S833][S765].
"Mary Erdoes says this is the only private bank with a uniform global model. Where does the model have to bend for a UK client?"Lets them talk about what they know best. Listen for: sterling banking, IHT and trusts, the FCA's Consumer Duty [S815][S785].

For a banker

QuestionWhy it lands, and what to listen for
"Where did your last three new clients come from?"The honest version of "how do you prospect". Listen for the split between investment-bank and commercial-bank referrals, existing-client referrals and cold outreach; the firm's line is that referrals are everything [S575].
"When a founder six months post-exit sits down with you, what do you ask first, and what do you deliberately not talk about in the first meeting?"Shows you understand discovery. Listen for "purpose of the wealth", tax ring-fencing, and the refusal to pitch products early [S804][S810].
"How do you and the Investor on your team divide a client conversation? Who leads when the client wants to talk markets?"The team model in practice. Listen for how often the Investor is in the room, and the Entrust/Engage/Execute split [S803].
"What does Connect Coach actually change in your week? Which of its insights do you act on and which do you ignore?"Specific, current, and it invites candour. Listen for meeting prep and call summaries versus "next best action" [S831][S834].
"What did your first two years as an analyst teach you that the training did not?"The question junior hosts like most. Listen for KYC, pitch books, learning to listen in client meetings.

For an investor, a capital advisor or a wealth advisor

QuestionWhy it lands, and what to listen for
Investor: "The Q3 house view expects about two more Fed hikes and calls core bonds attractive. How does that translate into a conversation with a client who wants to stay in AI stocks that made them rich?"Shows you read the 1 October note. Listen for concentration, the 42-stock number, and how they use the house view without hiding behind it [S773][S770].
Investor: "Half of the largest clients' assets are in alternatives. How do you pace a client into private markets when they have a large tax bill in January and gilts yield 5.4%?"Combines the alternatives push with today's rates. Listen for the liquidity budget and the pacing tool [S833][S781].
Capital advisor: "After the First Brands and Tricolor collapses and Dimon's 'loose credit terms' remark on the Q3 call, has anything changed in how you lend against private company shares or fund interests?"Current and precise. Listen for advance rates, the "two ways out" rule, and specialty lending against GP commitments [S752][S903][S806].
Wealth advisor: "Since the non-dom regime ended, how many of your clients left, how many stayed, and what did the ones who stayed want from you?"The 2026 UK question. Listen for Milan, Dubai, Switzerland; the ten-year IHT tail; the Budget on 28 October [S778][S826][S775].
Wealth advisor: "What does a family-governance conversation look like when the children do not want the business?"Shows you see the human part. Listen for next-gen programmes and the Advice Lab [S805][S819].

For the recruiting team

QuestionWhy it lands
"The London summer runs nine weeks with a capstone. What did last year's strongest interns do in weeks two to five that set them apart?"Specific to the programme shape and asks for a behaviour, not a trait [S743].
"How do you decide between the Advisor track and Investment Solutions for someone who could do either?"Shows you know there are two tracks and have thought about fit [S746][S747].
"After today, what is the next step in the 2027 process and when should I expect to hear?"Practical, and it shows you have applied.
Questions not to ask
"What does a private banker do?" "What is the culture like?" "How much do analysts earn?" "Is the HireVue hard?" Anything about Jamie Dimon's succession, the Epstein litigation or the First Republic layoffs. Anything that starts "I read that J.P. Morgan is the biggest". Anything with a compound three-part structure; one question, then listen.

How to use the answers

Write the host's name and one line of what they said in the notebook before you move on. On Tuesday, send one short email to the recruiting address thanking them and naming one thing you learned from one named person. If a host offered a LinkedIn connection, send it the same evening with a one-line note that quotes their own point back to them. The answers you collect on Monday are also the material for the HireVue "why this bank" answer and for the final-round interviews: a candidate who can say "a banker on the UK North team told me that..." has evidence nobody else has.
