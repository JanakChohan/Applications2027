How the International Private Bank is organised

The Global Private Bank is run by David Frame from New York. Its international half is run by Adam Tejpaul from 60 Victoria Embankment, and within that, EMEA is run by Pablo Garnica from Madrid through three region heads: the UK, Channel Islands and Ireland; Continental Europe; and Switzerland, the Middle East and Emerging Markets. London is both the UK region's home and the International Private Bank's headquarters [S701][S702][S705].

{{FIG:org}}

The three-region design is new and deliberate. Tejpaul told Euromoney in March 2026 that Europe needs "three very senior, almost CEO-like" region heads, and that the UK has shifted "from a largely offshore, London-centric model to onshore, multi-hub", with offices in Manchester, Edinburgh and Glasgow and new or expanded offices in Munich, Hamburg and Berlin. The International Private Bank has more than 1,000 relationship managers and an ambition of about 1,900 "over time"; in the twelve months to June 2025 EMEA revenue grew about 14%, assets about 25% and adviser headcount 9% [S705][S758]. Europe is where share is lowest and therefore, in his words, where the bank can "scale that materially" [S704].

The building, and the one after it

60 Victoria Embankment was built in 1880 to 1882 for the City of London School, restored for the bank in 1991; the old school is now the conference centre and the complex is AWM's London home [S742][S740]. In November 2025 the firm announced a 3 million square foot tower on the Riverside South plot at Canary Wharf for up to 12,000 staff, more than £3bn, six years from planning consent, "subject to a continuing positive business environment in the UK"; the 265-metre height was agreed with London City Airport in February 2026 and the planning application was still pending in April [S740][S741][S787]. The firm employs about 23,000 people in the UK, 13,000 of them in London, with 5,300 in Bournemouth and 4,000 in Glasgow and Edinburgh [S740]. Dimon was in London on 6 October telling Bloomberg that a windfall tax on domestic banks "lacks principle" and that the UK has "the highest tax rate for banks probably in the world" [S786]. Budget day is Wednesday 28 October, nine days after your event [S775].

The client journey, and a day in the life

{{FIG:journey}}

How clients arrive. Mostly by referral from the rest of the firm. Erdoes at the 2025 Investor Day: "We are every day investing in the companies that are taken public by the investment bank... helping the CEOs on their personal balance sheets... or we're taking all the great things in the commercial bank and the leads that they have." More than 95% of AWM's top 50 clients use other parts of the firm. The rule that makes it work: "We do not have side accounting groups that think about where those revenues get counted for. We are trying to win for the client" [S575][S815]. In London the FIG team covers founders of funds, the Entrepreneurs & Executives team covers people selling or running companies, and the Institutional Wealth Group covers families that behave like institutions [S701][S752].

Discovery. First meetings are for asking. The bank's own framing question is "What's the purpose of your wealth?" and its planning page lists ten disciplines it can bring: tax strategy, retirement and asset location, wealth strategy, estate planning, philanthropy, family and family-office advisory, cyber advisory, executive advisory, private business advisory, behavioural science [S804]. The Private Business Advisory page tells owners to start 18 to 24 months before a sale because some strategies "may only be available before the sale and not after", and lists the deal team: accountant, M&A adviser, valuation expert, corporate lawyer, trusts and estates lawyer, "personal banker and investment manager" [S810].

Proposal and onboarding. An investment proposal (allocation, holdings analysis, lending capacity, cash plan), the Entrust/Engage/Execute split, KYC on source of wealth, then account opening. The firm says real-time KYC should cut onboarding rejections by about a third and that well over three-quarters of eligible EMEA trades now execute online [S830].

Reviews. Quarterly reviews and market calls, where Connect Coach now surfaces which clients lack an exposure and drafts the talking points: about 17,000 users, "taking things that take days and weeks and turning them into seconds", and a 20% lift in gross sales the firm attributes partly to the tool [S831][S834][S756].

A London analyst's week, from the postings and accounts
Monday: the global morning meeting every banker attends so that market views are consistent across the world (Tejpaul: the same meeting in every city) [S704]. Then: preparing account reviews and pitch decks; running KYC document collection and account openings; coordinating with investors, capital advisors and wealth advisors on client pitches and agendas; drafting investment proposals; logging the CRM; sitting in on client meetings and writing them up; prospect research for the banker's pipeline. The Program Analyst posting describes supporting Client Advisors "with the routine of daily business", with promotion to Associate through an assessment centre [S828]. Hours around 08:00 to 18:30, lighter than the investment bank, heavier on judgement about people.

The client scenario: how it works and how to be good in it

The invitation promises "an interactive client scenario designed to give you insight into the work of the Private Bank". In every documented version of this exercise at J.P. Morgan and its peers, a team gets a hypothetical family or founder with a balance sheet and a problem, and is judged on how it thinks rather than on the answer [S744][S821][S822][S823].

{{FIG:scenario}}

The structure to hold in your head. Listen first. Map both sides of the balance sheet. Sort the money by goal and horizon. Name the seats you would bring in. Agree one or two next steps. The pitfalls the bank's own pages warn about: owners who "focus only on maximizing deal value and neglect their personal finances", and planning that cannot be done after a sale because it was not done before [S810][S811].

Questions a banker asks a new clientWhy they are asked
"Tell me about your family." "What is something you are most proud of?"Opens wide; the client chooses what matters. Both spouses are engaged [S825].
"What has changed, and what prompted the conversation now?"Finds the liquidity event, the divorce, the inheritance, the move abroad.
"What is this money for? Which parts of it, and when?"The bank's own "purpose of your wealth"; sorts lifestyle from growth from legacy [S804].
"Where does your money sit today, and who else do you bank with?"Maps the balance sheet and the share of wallet.
"What keeps you up at night?" "If something could be different in two years, what would it be?"Risk, in the client's words, not the bank's [S825].

An investment request ("put it all in AI stocks" or "I want 10% income")Translate into goals and horizons, then Entrust/Engage/Execute; a diversified core; alternatives sized to a liquidity budget. The house view is a tool, not the answer. Seat: Investor [S803][S833].
In the room, do not
Open with a product. Talk about markets before the client has said what the money is for. Give tax advice (say "that is a Wealth Advisor question; here is what I would want them to look at"). Dominate the table: the assessors are watching whether you bring in the quiet person. Invent numbers: say "roughly" and give a range. Forget the human: a founder who has sold has lost a job as well as gained a fortune.
