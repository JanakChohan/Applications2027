What a private bank is, where this one sits, and the words on the floor

Everything a candidate must know before walking in. Read once, then test yourself with the glossary.

What a private bank does, in plain words

A private bank is a bank for wealthy families that does everything under one roof: it invests their money, lends to them, holds their cash, and plans how their wealth passes on. The thing it sells is not a product. It is one accountable relationship, run by a banker, that can bring the whole bank to one family.

Three neighbouring businesses are easy to confuse. Retail banking sells standard products to everyone. Wealth management advises on investments around goals, often without a balance sheet to lend from. Asset management runs pooled funds for institutions and individuals; at J.P. Morgan that is George Gatch's business, next door to the Private Bank inside Asset & Wealth Management. Investment banking advises companies; the Private Bank advises the people who own and run those companies, which is why the two feed each other [S900][S575].

The International Private Bank, run from London, targets clients with more than $20m of liquid assets; the wider firm quotes "$10 million" as the Private Bank minimum in the United States and third-party guides put the UK at around £10m, though no official UK figure is published [S704][S812][S813]. Below that, UK clients are served by J.P. Morgan Personal Investing inside the Chase UK app, with a dedicated relationship manager above £250,000 [S835]. Above it sits 23 Wall, the unit for about 700 of the world's largest families, worth more than $4.5tn in aggregate [S816][S817].

Where the Private Bank sits inside JPMorgan Chase

JPMorgan Chase is three businesses sharing one balance sheet: Consumer & Community Banking (Chase), the Commercial & Investment Bank, and Asset & Wealth Management. In 2025 the firm earned $185.6bn of revenue and $57.0bn of profit. AWM was $24.1bn of that revenue, split between Asset Management ($11.7bn) and the Global Private Bank ($12.4bn), at a 36% pretax margin and a 40% return on equity, the highest in the firm [S900]. The Private Bank is small inside the firm and unusually profitable because it earns fees and spread rather than putting large capital at risk.

The five seats around a client

Every J.P. Morgan Private Bank client is served by a team, not a person. The banker owns the relationship; four specialists own the disciplines. The firm's own services page organises what they do under four headings: Wealth Planning, Investing, Borrowing, Banking [S801].

The EMEA investment approach uses these instead of "discretionary, advisory, execution-only". Entrust: a fully managed portfolio. Engage: a collaborative portfolio with ongoing dialogue, the client deciding. Execute: self-directed. Clients mix all three across sleeves. Strategies come from J.P. How the bank makes money on one client

Four legs. A percentage fee on invested assets. The spread the bank earns on the client's deposits and loans. Fees and commissions on alternatives, brokerage and structured products. And fees for trusts, custody and planning. In 2025 AWM's revenue was 72% non-interest (mostly asset-management fees of $15.5bn) and 28% net interest income ($6.8bn), and the firm says 73% of the whole is recurring [S900][S831]. The private-bank client who uses all four legs is worth far more, and is far harder to lose, than one who uses one: when a US Private Bank client also actively banks with the firm, assets and revenue are on average 65% higher [S901].

Why lending matters so much here: wealthy people prefer to borrow against assets rather than sell them, because selling crystallises tax and gives up the exposure. J.P. Morgan calls its private-bank lending "the largest lending business in the private banking space", with $266bn of loans at end-2025 and AWM average loans of $302bn in the latest quarter, losses of a few basis points a year because every loan has "two ways out", the collateral and the client's cash flow [S901][S751]. It financed ten of the last fifteen major sports-team transactions and counts 60% of major team owners as clients [S812][S575].

The words on the floor

AUM / client assets / AUS
Assets under management are what the bank invests on a discretionary or advisory basis; client assets include custody, deposits and brokerage too. AWM: $5.2tn AUM, $7.9tn client assets at 30 September 2026 [S751].
Net new money (NNM) / inflows
Money clients bring minus money they take out. The number the business is run on: $553bn in 2025, $263bn in Q3 2026 alone [S754][S750]. "We are not number one in AUM, but we are number one in flows" is a house line [S901].
Book
A banker's set of client relationships and the revenue they produce. Books here are "seeded" by the firm, not bought from other banks [S901].
Primary bank
The bank a family uses for most of its needs; the whole model is built to become it.
Goals-based planning
Organising money by what it is for (lifestyle, growth, legacy, giving) and when it is needed, "with regular check-ins" [S801].
Balance sheet
Both sides of a family's finances: what they own and what they owe, liquid and illiquid. Bankers advise on "their overall balance sheet" [S808].
Liquidity event
A sale, IPO or inheritance that turns an illiquid asset into cash. The moment most new clients arrive. Pre-sale planning starts 18 to 24 months before [S810].
Concentrated position
Too much wealth in one stock, usually the founder's own. The bank's four verbs: Borrow, Hedge, Monetize, Give [S809].
Securities-based lending / Lombard loan
Borrowing against a portfolio. Advance rates roughly 50 to 70% on diversified portfolios, lower on a single stock; margin calls if values fall [S806][S904].
Alternatives / privates
Private equity, private credit, real assets, hedge funds. About half of the portfolio assets of the bank's 200 largest clients; evergreen funds now a fifth of its alternatives [S833][S613].
Evergreen / semi-liquid fund
A fund with no end date, monthly dealing and a quarterly exit capped at about 5%. The 2026 story of the year (Part 3).
Non-dom / FIG regime
UK tax status abolished 6 April 2025 and replaced by a residence test and a four-year foreign-income-and-gains regime; inheritance tax now follows residence with a tail of up to ten years after leaving [S826].
IHT
UK inheritance tax, 40% above the allowances; pensions come inside it from April 2027 [S775].
KYC / source of wealth
Know-your-client checks: how the fortune was built (source of wealth) and where this deposit came from (source of funds). Analysts do a lot of it.
Discovery
The first meetings, where the banker asks and listens. The bank's own question: "What's the purpose of your wealth?" [S804]
Advice Lab
Internal name for the planning specialists: Philanthropy Centre, Family Governance, Executive Compensation, Tax, Private Business Advisory, Family Office and Estate Disposition modelling [S805].
23 Wall
Coverage unit for about 700 of the largest families, a joint venture of the Investment Bank and the Private Bank run by Andy Cohen [S816].
Institutional Wealth Group
London team (Zeynep Ozturk Unlu) serving families and entities that behave like institutions [S701].
FIG team
Financial Institutions Group: bankers covering founders and partners of asset managers, hedge funds and private equity firms. London lead Paul Ferry, from Citi in November 2025 [S714].
Connect Coach
The advisor-facing AI assistant: 25 agents, a million personalised insights, about 17,000 users; meeting prep, portfolio analysis, call summaries [S815][S831].
Uniform global model
Erdoes' claim that this is "the only private bank in the industry with a uniform business model globally": the same team and platform in every city [S815].
Fortress balance sheet
Dimon's phrase for holding more capital than required so the bank is the safe place in a crisis. CET1 13.9% at Q3 2026 [S750][S905].
Investor / Capital Advisor / Wealth Advisor
The three specialist seats (above). Say "investor" not "fund manager", "capital advisor" not "loan officer".
Superday / final round
A day of back-to-back interviews after the HireVue; behavioural, with "some working knowledge about the current market" expected [S960].
