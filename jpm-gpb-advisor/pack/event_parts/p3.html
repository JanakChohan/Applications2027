The strategy, in the leaders' own words

AWM's stated ambition is "a path to $10 trillion" of client assets, built on seven investments: active management, active ETFs, alternatives, workplace, Private Bank advisors, personalisation, and technology and AI. The Private Bank's share of that plan is to go from about $3tn of client assets to $5tn by growing advisors, growing alternatives, and growing abroad [S831][S901].

The tensions a thoughtful candidate can see

A strategy is a set of bets, and every bet has a cost. These are the six the senior leaders are managing right now. Each is also a question (Part 4).

TensionThe two sides
Advisor growth against productivityHiring at 11% a year while revenue per banker still rises 15% is unusual; expenses were up 14% in Q3 on "continued growth in private banking advisor teams". The firm itself says hiring will normalise later this decade. The question is what a new banker's first book looks like when the firm has already hired 1,600 since 2020 [S751][S815].
Alternatives against liquidityHalf of the largest clients' assets are in private markets and evergreens are the fastest-growing wrapper. 2026 was the year the wrapper's 5% quarterly cap bound at Blue Owl (Q1 requests 22% and 41% at its two wealth funds; Q3 still 17% and 39%), Blackstone's BCRED (over the cap three quarters running), Apollo, Ares, HPS and Partners Group, which replaced its CEO. The bank's own strategists called gates "prudent tools" and "simply a feature of the vehicles' structure" and told clients to keep private credit at about 15% of a private-markets allocation. Then it launched two more evergreens in September [S798][S576][S577][S765].
AI against the relationshipCoach is meant to let a banker cover 50% more clients. The bank's own line is "digitally enabled, not digital only", and Tejpaul's headline is "AI won't replace relationships". The risk is subtle: if every banker's talking points come from the same engine, what is the banker's own judgement worth to the client? [S830][S704][S834]
The UK as home against the UK as tax riskLondon is the International Private Bank's headquarters and the firm plans a £3bn tower, "subject to a continuing positive business environment". The non-dom regime ended in April 2025; 30% of advisers think more than a quarter of former non-doms have left, to the Gulf and Italy; a third of £250k+ investors say they are considering emigrating; the Budget on 28 October is Chancellor Healey's first, with a £35bn gap, a mansion-tax threshold cut mooted and pensions entering IHT in April 2027. Dimon says the UK bank tax is the highest in the world. The bank's answer has been "onshore, multi-hub": Manchester, Edinburgh, Glasgow [S741][S778][S779][S775][S786][S758].
Europe's low share against Europe's costShare "below 1%" in some markets is a runway only if the cost of building share is paid for years before the fees arrive. Tejpaul's three region heads, the German offices and the 1,900-RM ambition are that bet; EMEA revenue growing 14% while advisers grow 9% says it is working so far [S815][S705].
Succession at the topDimon, 70, named Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh co-presidents in June 2026; Marianne Lake retired; Erdoes, CEO of AWM since 2009, was reported to be no longer a candidate; Frame has been Global CEO of the Private Bank for fifteen months. The business is run on long relationships, and its own leadership is in transition [S900][S702].

Three market views with a "so what" for a UK client
Rates. "Both central banks are hiking into an oil shock. For a client that means cash finally pays and the long end is dangerous: gilts at 2007 highs are an opportunity for a liquidity bucket and a reason to keep duration short." AI concentration. "Cembalest's number is that 42 stocks are two-thirds of the S&P's return. For a founder whose sale proceeds went into an index fund, that is a concentration they did not choose; the question is whether to diversify into the parts of the market the AI trade ignored." Private markets. "Evergreens worked as designed this year and still disappointed people, because 5% a quarter was sold as liquidity. The lesson for a client is sizing: nothing needed in the next two years belongs behind a gate, and the manager matters more than the yield." [S773][S770][S798][S577]
