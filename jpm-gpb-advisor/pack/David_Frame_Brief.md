# David Frame, Global CEO of J.P. Morgan Private Bank: what he has said about the advisor role

Prepared 14 September 2026. Public professional information only. Every quote is verbatim from a source read in full unless marked "paraphrase" or "snippet". Source IDs S400 to S429 are in the pack's registry.

## 1. Who he is (career path with dates)

| When | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1991 | B.A., Middlebury College (American Civilization) | S401, S402 |
| Early 1990s | Media buyer at J. Walter Thompson; about a year at CBS Television | S402 |

Two things about that path matter for you. He spent eleven years on institutional equity desks in New York and London before he ever met a private client, and he then ran alternatives, capital markets and client advice before running the bank. He is a markets-and-product person who became a relationship leader, which is exactly the blend the posting asks for ("genuine interest in financial markets" plus "relationship building and sales capacity"). He has worked in the London office (1999 to 2003), so the international bank is not abstract to him.

Erdoes on his appointment: "David's global acumen and strategic vision will be instrumental in elevating the Global Private Bank to new heights" (3 July 2025, S400). His own line the same day: "As I step into the role of Global CEO, I am energized by the opportunity to connect with our teams and clients worldwide" (S400).

## 2. What he thinks the advisor role is, in his words

**The advisor is the relationship, and the platform exists to make each advisor as good as the best one.**
- "Personalization is at the heart of our value proposition. We constantly strive to make every adviser operate like our best and to make every client feel like our only one." (Global Finance interview, 30 November 2025, S404)
- "The metric is client engagement and satisfaction. Technology should support advisers in deepening relationships... Scale is important, but never at the expense of relationships." (S404, S406)

**Technology serves judgment; it does not replace it.**
- "Innovation matters only if it supports better conversations and smarter decisions. The measure for us is engagement: Technology should make advisers more responsive and help them anticipate client needs, not replace judgment or dilute relationships." (Global Finance, 8 December 2025, S406)
- "Digital capabilities and AI help us simplify processes, reduce errors, and free up time so advisers can deepen conversations with clients. The goal is not scale for its own sake, but scale that enhances personalization." (S406)
- On the 2025 rollout of AI across the bank: "We deepened our integration of those technologies across the entire Private Bank" (New Year message, January 2026, S412); a year earlier: "we already know what clients want to talk about" and "our advisors are delivering the best insight they can to each and every client" (New Year video, 31 January 2025, S413, fragments).

**What he hires for.**
- "We are committed to hiring client-focused advisors, who are attracted to the firm by our strong brand reputation, breadth of our platform, and depth of our global resources." (statement on hiring a $2bn Citi team, InvestmentNews, 22 September 2023, S409)
- In May 2022 he posted a recruiting piece answering "five key questions about our firm... from our growth strategy to what we look for in potential new hires"; the article has since been removed from the site (S424). Its absence is a gap; the surviving statements above are the best evidence of his hiring lens.

**The scale of the advisor force under him.**
- "We are now 4,000 advisors strong, helping clients across 77 cities around the world." (January 2026, S412)
- Erdoes, presenting his business at Investor Day 2025: "On the right is the business that's run by Dave Frame, the U.S. Private Bank, as well as Adam Tejpaul, who runs our International Private Bank. And those numbers are really quite tremendous." Then the apprenticeship economics that define how his advisors are made: "You can hire these people, or you can grow these people. And in fact, now, half of our new advisors come from growing them from within"; "we take some of the best front-line advisors, and we say, you're going to go train them"; "we now have these 25-year-olds that are coming online that are just as productive as our 30- to 35-year-old mid-career hires... It's a formula that's now repeatable, scalable, and it works globally." (19 May 2025, S415)

Note: the 2018 "hire 1,000 private bankers" plan often attributed to him was Erdoes' (S410), and the 2022 "1,300 more advisers" plan was Jennifer Piepszak's for the whole wealth continuum (S411). What Frame owns is the U.S. growth from 2019 and the global structure since 2025.

## 3. What he thinks about clients

- On why the bank went to one global structure in 2025: "The wealthier you get, the more you feel you're a citizen of the world. If that doesn't happen with the first generation of a family, it starts to happen with the second and the third." (to the Wall Street Journal, reported by PYMNTS, 3 July 2025, S414; second-hand)
- On the $10m to $25m segment: they want "institutional-quality investments, holistic planning, and lifestyle services, delivered digitally and seamlessly"; younger clients seek "purpose-driven guidance" (S404). A search snippet of the same interview adds: "They're entrepreneurs, business owners, and rising family leaders, who want sophisticated advice quickly and efficiently" (snippet, not seen in the fetched text).
- On family offices, launching the U.S. Family Office Practice: the aim is to "ensure that we remain the bank of choice for the world's wealthiest families" (26 July 2023, S421).
- On 2026 themes to clients: "Resilience and security remain defining investment themes. With continued interest in infrastructure, energy and critical resources, we are helping clients diversify across regions and sectors." And: "It's about aligning wealth with knowledge and purpose so your family is positioned not just for today's opportunities, but tomorrow's legacy." (January 2026, S412)
- On markets, from his trading years: "equities and other markets are story driven" (2020 profile, S402, fragment).

## 4. What he thinks about alternatives

He ran the alternatives group from around 2009. He co-signed the 2025 Alternatives Outlook with Adam Tejpaul, which reported that 50% of the Private Bank's 2024 alternatives commitments went into evergreen funds, up from 33% the year before (S416). Under his tenure the platform grew from about $40bn (2010) to over $200bn (July 2025), and alternatives are close to half of the assets of the 200 largest clients (Euromoney, March 2026, S417; the quotes there are Kristin Kallergis Rowland's). The point for an interview: the man running the bank built the alternatives shelf, so "alternative investment teams" in your posting is not a brochure line.

## 5. What he says about leadership and careers

From the 2020 profile (S402): "Good leaders actively root for your success"; his advice is to identify the work you enjoy most and then pursue roles that value those skills; he names Mary Erdoes as his mentor. He welcomes each analyst class publicly (the 2024 class was "nearly 400 recent graduates from over 30 countries", LinkedIn snippet, not verified in full).

## 6. What it means for your application

1. **Use his framing, not the marketing.** "Every adviser operate like our best and every client feel like our only one" is his and Erdoes' shared line. It says the role is judged on client engagement, and that the platform (Connect Coach, the specialists, the research) exists to raise the floor for every advisor. Say that the Advisor seat is where that sentence gets tested.
2. **He is proof of the trader-to-banker route.** If your interest in markets is genuine, his career is the argument that it belongs in this seat. He spent eleven years on equity desks before advising a single client.
3. **He built alternatives.** A question on how the Private Bank explains evergreen liquidity to clients after the 2026 redemption gates would land with anyone who reports to him.
4. **He made the bank global in July 2025.** The London office is part of his structure, not a satellite; Tejpaul's "Europe is the biggest share opportunity" and Brown's "double the UK in five years" mandates sit under it (S418, S422).
5. **Do not overclaim.** He has given no long interview since becoming Global CEO; there is no "$5tn target" in his words; the 1,000-banker plan was not his. Attribute correctly.

## 7. Questions you could ask that come from his record

- "David Frame's line is that technology should 'make advisers more responsive... not replace judgment'. In practice, what does Connect Coach change in a London banker's morning, and what does it not?"
- "Half of new advisors are now grown internally and the firm says 25-year-olds match mid-career hires. What does a first-year analyst in London do that shortens that curve?"
- "Frame spent a decade on equity desks before advising clients. How much do the London teams want analysts to bring a markets instinct versus a planning instinct?"
- "Alternatives are half the assets of the largest clients and half of new commitments are evergreen. After the 2026 gates, how does the UK team explain liquidity to a client with a date on their money?"
