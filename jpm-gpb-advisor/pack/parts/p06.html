Part 6

The role's own industry and its regulation

The history that shaped UK private banking, the current regulatory direction with exact instruments and dates, financial crime and conduct, the tax context every London banker must know, qualifications, and cross-border rules.

6.1 The arc in one paragraph

Until 1986 the City largely regulated itself. The Financial Services Act 1986 created a statutory framework. The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA) swept it into one regulator, the FSA, from 1 December 2001, using the concept of "regulated activities": you need permission to advise on investments, manage them, deal, arrange or take deposits. After the 2008 crisis the Financial Services Act 2012 split the FSA into the FCA (conduct) and the PRA (prudential, inside the Bank of England) on 1 April 2013. Since then the layers a private banker lives under are: the Retail Distribution Review (2012: how you are paid and qualified), MiFID II (2018: how you assess, disclose, record and govern), the Senior Managers and Certification Regime (2016 and 2019: personal accountability), the Consumer Duty (2023 and 2024: outcomes for retail clients), and a 2025 to 2027 "growth" wave: targeted support, client re-categorisation, a new disclosure regime, a slimmed-down accountability regime and Basel 3.1 [S141][S144][S146][S120][S121][S126].

6.3 Financial crime and conduct

  • Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (in force 26 June 2017), amended by SI 2023/1371 from 10 January 2024 so that a domestic politically exposed person starts as lower risk than a foreign one. The FCA's PEP guidance FG25/3 (7 July 2025) replaced FG17/6: UK PEPs lower-risk by default; the money-laundering reporting officer keeps oversight of every PEP relationship [S130]. Source of wealth (how the total wealth was generated) and source of funds (where this transaction's money comes from) must be established for PEPs and high-risk situations; the FCA warns against "tick box compliance exercises" [S124].
  • Proceeds of Crime Act 2002: section 330 makes it an offence for a person in the regulated sector who knows or suspects money laundering not to report internally; section 333A makes tipping off an offence. Bribery Act 2010 (1 July 2011): gifts and hospitality registers, no facilitation payments. Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023: the corporate "failure to prevent fraud" offence in force from 1 September 2025 for large organisations, with a "reasonable procedures" defence [S129].
  • UK Market Abuse Regulation: insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and manipulation; being "wall-crossed" makes you an insider and restricts you until the information is public. Criminal insider dealing sits in Part V of the Criminal Justice Act 1993.
  • Enforcement to know. Barclays fined £42m in July 2025 for financial-crime failings, including opening an account for a wealth firm without checking the FCA register [S131]; Nationwide £44m (December 2025) and Monzo £21m (July 2025) [S132]; James Staley, the former JPMorgan private-banking head and Barclays CEO, fined £1.1m in July 2025 over his account of his Epstein relationship [S132]; two Dolfin wealth-management executives banned in August 2026 over a scheme to bypass investor-visa rules [S133][S134]. No FCA fine on any JPMorgan UK entity in 2023 to 2026; the last was £3.1m on the old J.P. Morgan International Bank in 2013 for wealth-management record-keeping [S370]. After the Coutts "debanking" episode of 2023, new rules from 28 April 2026 require 90 days' notice and reasons for account closures [S135].

6.5 Qualifications

To advise on retail investment products a UK adviser needs a qualification on the FCA's Training and Competence tables that "fully meets requirement from 31 December 2012": the CISI Level 4 Investment Advice Diploma is the standard route; the CFA UK Investment Management Certificate covers securities advice and managing investments but not, on its own, retail investment products [S141][S142]. The CISI Level 7 Diploma in Wealth Management is the Chartered Wealth Manager qualification [S143]. Retail investment advisers also need an annual Statement of Professional Standing and 35 hours of continuing professional development. J.P. Morgan's UK banker postings prefer a "certificate in Investment Management level 4" and treat the CFA as "a plus"; whether the analyst programme funds the CISI exams could not be confirmed from a primary source [S44][S40].

6.6 Cross-border: why a London banker cannot freely advise an EU client

The UK's MiFID passport ended on 31 December 2020. A UK firm is now a "third-country firm" and may serve an EU-resident client only under a member state's national regime or "reverse solicitation", where the client initiated the service "at its own exclusive initiative". ESMA warned on 13 January 2021 that "where a third-country firm solicits clients or promotes investment services in the Union, it should not be deemed a service provided at the client's exclusive initiative" [S148]. In practice, EU-resident clients are booked and advised through J.P. Morgan SE (Frankfurt, created 24 January 2022, with branches in Dublin, Luxembourg, Paris, Madrid and Milan and a London branch "to service certain aspects of the firm's private banking business"), and Swiss clients through J.P. Morgan (Suisse) SA under FINMA [S147][S70]. This is why the EMEA structure has three regions and why the firm stresses its "uniform global model".
