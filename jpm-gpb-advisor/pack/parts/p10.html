10.2 The two types, and how to read which you have

The practitioner (banker, investor, team head)

Signs: asks "what would you do if a client..."; follows up on a market view; mentions their own clients; is short on process questions. What they are testing: would I put this person in front of my client in year two? Are they curious about people and markets? Can they hold a frame under pressure? How to pitch: concrete, client-first, brief; show the priority order (rules, client, process, speed); have two market views with a "so what"; ask them about their book (question 3 or 5 in Part 9.3).

The recruiter or programme manager

Signs: structured competency questions ("tell me about a time..."); checks eligibility and motivation; takes notes against a grid; asks about other applications. What they are testing: the five stated qualities ("tenacious, critical thinkers, collaborative, compelling, high performers" [S267]); fit with the posting's disposition list; whether you understand which programme you applied to. How to pitch: STAR-shaped answers with a clear "I"; name the programme correctly (Advisor track, not Investment Solutions); show you read the how-we-hire pages; ask about the programme structure (question 9 or 10).

Adapting live. If a practitioner asks a competency question, answer it with a client-shaped example. If a recruiter asks a market question, keep it simple and end with what it means for a client. If you cannot tell, assume practitioner: the downside of being too concrete with a recruiter is small; the downside of being too generic with a banker is fatal. No manipulation; read, adapt, be the same person in both rooms.
