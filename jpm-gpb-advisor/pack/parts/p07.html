7.1 The twelve stories

1. JPMorgan's 2026 results: AWM client assets reach $7.7tn (14 April and 14 July 2026)

What. Q1: firm net income $16.5bn, ROTCE 23%; AWM revenue $6.4bn (+11%), client assets $7.1tn. Q2: net income $21.2bn ($16.9bn excluding a $4.6bn Visa gain), revenue $58.0bn (+27%); AWM revenue $6.85bn (+19%), net income $1.96bn (+33%), client assets $7.7tn, long-term inflows $50bn, expenses up 13% [S222][S220][S221]. "Revenue in each line of business hit a new record." Note that the "nearly 44,000 first-time investors" line belongs to Chase's wealth management, not the Private Bank [S220]. Firm. AWM is the steadiest, highest-margin fee engine (38% margin in Q2). Advisor. Inflows mean onboarding, proposals and demand for advisors; expense growth is partly "continued growth in private banking advisor teams" [S82]. Raise. "AWM expenses grew 13% against 19% revenue: how much of that is EMEA advisor hiring, and how is advisor productivity measured?"

2. Dimon's 2025 shareholder letter (6 April 2026): Iran, inflation, private credit, AI

What. The letter warned that the Iran conflict means oil and commodity shocks "for longer", that gradually rising inflation is underpriced, that private-credit losses are "a little higher than they should be" and that the asset class "does not tend to have great transparency or rigorous valuation marks", that parts of Basel are "frankly nonsensical", and that on AI the firm "cannot predict the ultimate winners and losers" [S223][S224]. Firm. Sets risk appetite and the case for the fortress ($291bn CET1, $1.5tn liquidity at Q1) [S222]. Advisor. Clients read it and ask "is Dimon right about inflation?" and "should I be in private credit?" Raise. "How does the Private Bank's alternatives platform choose private-credit managers and explain liquidity terms after the CEO's own warning?"

3. Succession: co-presidents named, Marianne Lake retires (25 June 2026)

What. Doug Petno (now sole CEO of the CIB) and Troy Rohrbaugh (CEO of Chase) became co-presidents; Lake retired after 25 years; Erdoes stays as CEO of AWM; each co-president received a $30m retention award [S226][S115]. Firm. The successor field narrows to two; AWM leadership is untouched. Advisor. Continuity for the EMEA build-out; the CIB-to-Private-Bank referral link now runs through Petno. Raise. "With the two co-presidents running CIB and Chase, how does the Private Bank make sure it captures liquidity events originated in the investment bank?"

4. Europe: Chase launches in Germany (20 May) and a possible $10 to 20bn acquisition (27 May)

What. Chase Germany opened with a savings account paying 4% for four months, a 150-person Berlin office under J.P. Morgan SE, and current accounts, investing and lending promised by 2028; the FT reported at least three more European countries by 2030 [S228][S229]. Dimon: "there might be, in the next couple years, a chance to put $10 [billion] or $20 billion to work" [S230]. Firm. Europe is the largest organic bet; a deal that size would be the largest of Dimon's tenure. Advisor. A European wealth manager is one plausible target given the Private Bank's stated sub-1% share in many markets. Raise. "If JPM did spend $20bn, would a European wealth manager make sense, or does the firm prefer to hire advisors thirty at a time?"

5. AI: "30% to 40%" of jobs cut in some units; Connect Coach in the Private Bank (14 July and 20 March 2026)

What. On the Q2 call Dimon said AI has removed 30 to 40% of headcount in some units, mostly redeployed, and that "you don't uniquely benefit from AI"; the firm spends about $20bn on technology and runs about 1,000 AI use cases [S232]. In the Private Bank, Connect Coach has 12,000 users and 25 agents; Euromoney's 2026 digital award cites 75%+ of eligible EMEA trades executed online [S236][S234]. Advisor. An intern will use these tools; the human skill left is judgment and relationships. Raise. "Erdoes says revenue per banker is up 15% since 2020. How much of the next 15% is Connect Coach, and what does an analyst do that the tool cannot?"

6. The EMEA and UK build-out: Euromoney's Europe award (20 March) and a UK head "in hiring mode" (May)

What. Euromoney's citation: EMEA revenue up about 14%, assets up about 25%, adviser headcount up 9% in the year to June 2025; offices opened or expanded in Munich, Hamburg, Berlin, Edinburgh and Glasgow; three regional heads under Garnica and Tejpaul; 1,000+ international relationship managers targeting about 1,900 [S233]. Citywire (snippet only) reports UK head Maricé Brown wants to add about 30 people a year and double the regional business in five years [S235]. Advisor. The London office is hiring; this internship is part of that plan. Raise. "The Euromoney citation describes JPM as a complementary partner to domestic banks. In the UK, with Coutts and Evelyn scaling up, what does 'complementary' mean for a £10m+ client?"

7. The house view: "Promise and Pressure" (11 May), Cembalest cautious on AI (22 July), a Fed hike expected (5 August)

What. The mid-year outlook keeps three themes (fragmentation, structural inflation, an AI super-cycle), calls the Strait of Hormuz closure "the largest oil supply shock since World War II", notes gold up 130% in three years, and pushes infrastructure, commodity-linked equities and macro hedge funds; Grace Peters: "Investors must walk a fine line" [S237]. Cembalest's Eye on the Market turned "cautious" on US equity technicals as semiconductor stocks surged (SOXX +90% in a year) while hyperscalers stalled [S238]. On 5 August J.P. Morgan Wealth Management moved to expecting a 25 basis point Fed hike at the 16 September meeting [S239]. Advisor. Every client call this summer is about oil, gold, AI concentration and locking in 5%+ gilt yields. Raise. "The outlook backs infrastructure and security themes while Cembalest warns AI technicals look like 1999. How does the team reconcile the two for a client with concentrated tech stock?"

8. The macro clients ask about: the Hormuz oil shock, the Bank of England at 3.75% with three votes to hike, gilts at 5.36%

What. Fighting with Iran began on 28 February 2026; the World Bank says the Hormuz disruption "triggered the largest oil market shock in history", with Brent up 65% in March [S225]. The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 30 July by six votes to three, with three members voting to hike; CPI 2.6% but "expected to rise later this year"; next decision 17 September [S258]. The UK ten-year gilt yield was 5.36% on 11 September, the highest since 2007, with markets pricing four hikes by mid-2027 [S227]. Advisor. Clients with sterling loans face higher costs; "why not just buy gilts?" is the most common objection to a diversified portfolio; savings income tax rises to 22/42/47% from April 2027. Raise. "With ten-year gilts above 5.3% and gilt gains exempt from capital gains tax, how does the UK team think about low-coupon gilts versus a bond fund for a top-rate taxpayer?"

9. UK tax on wealthy clients: the Budget measures biting, a quiet Spring Statement (3 March), the "exodus" debate (16 June)

What. Part 6.4 lists the measures. The Spring Statement announced no new tax rises but the OBR expects a "temporary boost" from the repatriation facility [S245]. Henley's June report dropped its contested millionaire headcounts and now scores the UK 68.3 against the UAE's 85.3; British citizens are about half of its applicants; its essayist argues inheritance tax is "the biggest single driver of relocation" [S243][S244]. No confirmed 2026 policy U-turn on non-dom inheritance tax was found. Advisor. The daily conversation: leave, use the facility, restructure trusts, or accept the £2m property surcharge. J.P. Morgan can win either way if departing clients keep it as their international bank. Raise. "Henley stopped publishing outflow numbers after methodological criticism. What data does the UK team use to judge whether relocation is a trend or a headline?"

10. Regulation and conduct: targeted support live (6 April), the £10m opt-up pending, an ECB fine, the Staley transcript (August)

What. Targeted support went live on 6 April 2026 [S247]; CP25/36's £10m opt-up awaits final rules [S248][S249]; FCA fines in 2026 total about £18m, all on individuals in wealth management, none on a UK private bank [S250]; the ECB fined J.P. Morgan SE €12.2m in February for misreporting risk-weighted assets, self-reported [S251]; in August a transcript showed former executive Jes Staley telling a House committee he had shared confidential information with Epstein, including "$44 billion in private bank inflows over two weeks" [S259]. Advisor. Client categorisation, fair-value evidence and financial-crime red flags are front-line work; the Epstein case is the cautionary tale of a profitable client overriding compliance. Raise. "If the £10m opt-up is finalised, would the UK Private Bank move most of its book to professional status, or keep retail protections as a service differentiator?"

11. Competitors: UBS record quarter amid a capital fight; Morgan Stanley passes $10tn; Coutts completes Evelyn (30 June)

What. UBS: Q2 net profit $2.8bn, wealth pre-tax profit $2.0bn (+38%), $36bn of net new assets, but "UBS continues to strongly disagree with the proposed package, which is extreme" on Swiss capital rules that could require about $20bn more capital [S252][S253]. Morgan Stanley Wealth: record $8.9bn revenue, record $148bn net new assets, $10tn of client assets [S254][S255]. NatWest completed the £2.7bn Evelyn Partners purchase into Coutts, creating a £127bn business [S256]. Advisor. Coutts and Evelyn become the domestic scale player for £1m to £10m; J.P. Morgan's UK positioning is UHNW and international; UBS's capital squeeze is an opening in Europe and the Gulf. Raise. "Does the Private Bank see UBS's capital problem as a hiring or client-acquisition opportunity in London and Dubai, and how does it avoid overpaying for teams?"

12. Private-credit stress: semi-liquid funds gate redemptions (25 March)

What. In Q1 2026 redemption requests at semi-liquid private-credit funds far exceeded caps: Blue Owl halted quarterly redemptions in one fund, Cliffwater received requests for 14% against a 7% cap, BlackRock TCP wrote down net asset value 19%; Carlyle's Harvey Schwartz: "We just should have called them 'sometimes not liquid at all.'" [S257]. J.P. Morgan marked down software-loan collateral and restricted lending to private-credit funds on 11 March [S112]. Advisor. Clients who bought evergreen private credit for yield ask why they cannot get out; explaining gates, marks and the seven-to-ten-year lock-up needed to earn the illiquidity premium is a core skill. Raise. "After the gates, has the Private Bank changed how it explains liquidity on semi-liquid funds, and does it favour drawdown funds for clients who can lock up capital?"
