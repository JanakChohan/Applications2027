How this pack is built

This is one document in sixteen parts. Parts 1 to 8 are knowledge: the role, the firm, the industry, the competition and the arguments on both sides. Parts 9 to 13 are performance: the process, the question bank, the interviewer briefing, the scenario playbook and the cheat sheet. Parts 14 to 16 are reference: glossary, sources and gaps. Every non-obvious factual claim carries a source marker like [S300] that resolves to the source table in Part 15, and a confidence tag where the claim is not from a primary source. Nothing is invented; where the evidence is thin the text says so, and Part 16 lists what could not be found.

Five kinds of box appear throughout:

Key term
A definition, in plain English, the first time a term appears. The glossary in Part 14 collects them.
Say this in the interview
A phrasing that turns a fact in the pack into something you can say aloud. Scaffolds, not scripts.
Don't say this
A common line that sounds fine and lands badly, with the reason.
Your opinion goes here
A place where the pack gives both sides and you must decide. Interviewers at this firm reward a defended view over a safe one.
Source uncertain
A claim that rests on a single or secondary source, a forum, or an estimate.

The 10-minute emergency version

1
The job
You are applying to become the client-facing relationship banker at J.P. Morgan's Private Bank, which serves families with roughly ten million pounds or dollars upward. J.P. Morgan calls the seat "Advisor". You would win families, understand their whole financial life, and bring in the firm's specialists (an Investor for the portfolio, a Capital Advisor for lending, a Wealth Advisor for trusts and tax) to serve them. It is a relationship and sales job with technical content, inside a team, not a solo investing job [S1][S13][S28].
2
The firm in one line
JPMorgan Chase is the largest bank in the world by market value: $185.6bn of revenue and $57.0bn of profit in 2025, about 320,000 people, 66 countries, run since 2005 by Jamie Dimon [S300]. Its three businesses are Chase (US consumers), the Commercial & Investment Bank (companies and institutions) and Asset & Wealth Management (AWM), where the Private Bank sits. AWM earned $24.1bn of revenue and a 40% return on equity in 2025; the Private Bank is half of it [S300].
3
The mechanism that makes it different
Four things, each with a number. The referral engine: the investment bank sells a founder's company, the founder becomes a Private Bank client; "2,000 private banking clients walk into the Chase branches every day"; over 95% of AWM's top 50 clients use other parts of the firm [S320][S316]. The balance sheet: $266bn of Private Bank loans, 97% collateralised, losses of one basis point a year [S300][S318][S319]. The apprenticeship: "we seed books of business, we don't buy them"; half of advisors are home-grown; advisors went from 2,500 to 4,101 in five years [S317][S320][S316]. The global model: "the only private bank in the industry with a uniform business model globally", run for the international business from London, with Europe named as the biggest opportunity to gain share [S316][S24].
4
What they are selecting for
The posting's own words: "relationship building and sales capacity", "genuine interest in financial markets and macro-level economic trends", "exceptional interpersonal, communication, logical thinking and problem-solving skills", "good judgment and discretion when working with confidential information" [S1]. Erdoes' words: the first lesson in training is "banking is not a commodity"; the goal is that "every advisor operate like our very best and every client feel like our only one" [S318][S316].
5
The process
Online application (closes 1 November 2026, rolling, apply early), a HireVue video interview of three to five recorded questions with about two minutes per answer, then interviews in London reported as three behavioural and commercial conversations. No technical tests are reported for the Private Bank; working knowledge of current markets is expected [S1][S353][S358].
6
Your thirty-second "why"
See Part 12. The shape: one mechanism you can explain (the referral engine, the apprenticeship or the balance sheet), one number, one thing about you that fits it, and one specific thing you want to work on in London (for example, the UK team's work with families reconsidering residence after the April 2025 non-dom changes [S30][S31]).
7
The one sentence for any scenario
Rules and the firm's name first; the client's outcome second; the process and the record third; speed last. "I'd rather be slow and right than fast and wrong, and I'd tell you that to your face." Part 11 gives the method.
