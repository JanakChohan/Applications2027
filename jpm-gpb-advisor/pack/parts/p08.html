Part 8

Forming your own view: both sides of every argument

Six live debates, each with the strongest case on both sides and the number that matters. The interviewers reward a defended position over a safe one.

8.1 Is the integrated team model better than the adviser-owns-the-client model?

For the team model

Clients get five specialists; retention survives banker departures ("over 95% don't leave" [S318]); no product-pushing incentives; the firm can seed young bankers rather than pay for books. Revenue per banker rose 15% while headcount rose 64% [S316].

Against

It is expensive (compensation is 56% of AWM cost [S82]); coordination is slow; the best bankers earn less than on a wirehouse grid and can leave for one; the client may feel handled by a committee. Morgan Stanley's adviser-owned model produced $148bn of net new assets in one quarter [S255].

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: the team model wins above £10m where complexity is the product; the grid model wins in the mass-affluent segment where speed and personality are. That is why J.P. Morgan runs both, in different divisions.

8.2 Is a bank-owned private bank a conflict of interest?

Yes, and it matters

The EMEA site states the firm "prefers J.P. Morgan managed strategies" and earns more when they are used [S195]; the investment bank's deals are one wall away; the Epstein record shows revenue overriding compliance [S108]. The FCA's 2023 letter warned wealth managers about "providing a product or service which does not align with the needs of consumers" [S124].

Yes, and it is managed

850+ external alternatives managers; "no fiduciary manager should dictate choice" [S315]; 83% of ten-year active assets beat peers, so the house product is defensible on performance [S316]; three lines of defence; suitability under MiFID II and the Consumer Duty [S82][S146]. A boutique with no product has its own conflict: it must recommend something and cannot lend.

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: every model has a conflict; the question is whether it is disclosed, governed and outperformed. Ask the interviewer how the team handles the in-house versus third-party choice when the fee differs, and listen for a process rather than a denial.

8.3 Is J.P. Morgan's Europe push a share gain or a hard slog?

Share gain

EMEA revenue +14%, assets +25%, advisers +9% [S233]; UBS distracted by capital rules [S253]; Julius Baer weakened by losses [S159]; Coutts digesting Evelyn [S256]; the referral engine and balance sheet travel well; "below 1%" share means a long runway [S316].

Hard slog

UBS's $4.8tn against about $750bn international; local heritage in every market; the UK tax changes push away the international families that are J.P. Morgan's core; new offices in Germany face Deutsche and the Swiss; hiring "30 a year" is small against the target of doubling [S155][S320][S66].

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: a share gain in the UHNW and founder segment, a slog in domestic mass affluent. The firm agrees: it named UHNW Europe, not retail Europe, as the Private Bank's target and put Chase UK in a different division.

8.4 Will AI replace the private banker?

Largely yes, over time

Connect Coach already drafts talking points and flags clients; advisers find information 95% faster; books can grow 50%; SpectrumIQ automated 75% of equity trading; 30 to 40% of jobs cut in some units [S213][S336][S316][S232]. Fewer bankers with bigger books is the direction.

No, it changes the job

"If a client says, 'I don't like fixed income'... that's not the right answer, but that's where AI will go" (Erdoes) [S322]; trust, discretion and disagreement are human; the Consumer Duty requires a person accountable for outcomes; clients pay for judgment at a liquidity event, not for information.

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: AI removes the preparation and the paperwork and raises the bar for the human part. The Advisor of 2030 has more clients, less admin, and is judged more on judgment.

8.5 Should a wealthy client be in private credit now?

Yes, carefully

The illiquidity premium is real for capital that can be locked up; J.P. Morgan backs fewer than six direct-lending managers out of 3,500+ funds and focuses on larger borrowers; spreads have narrowed but fundamentals are "very stable" per its alternatives head [S203]. The Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions still favour private markets [S193].

Not in semi-liquid form

Gates at Blue Owl and Cliffwater; a 19% write-down at BlackRock TCP; "sometimes not liquid at all"; Dimon on marks that lack rigour; J.P. Morgan itself restricting lending to private-credit funds [S257][S223][S112].

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: drawdown funds for the patient portion of a large portfolio; semi-liquid vehicles only where the client understands they may be illiquid; never for money with a date on it.

8.6 Is London still the place to build a private banking career?

Yes

The seat of J.P. Morgan's International Private Bank; English law and trust infrastructure; the time zone; the deepest capital markets outside New York; Gulf, African and European families still route through it; the firm is hiring in London, Manchester and Edinburgh [S65][S46].

Less than before

Non-dom abolition; a Henley score of 68.3 against the UAE's 85.3; British citizens half of Henley's applicants; a Saudi headquarters licence and 100+ Middle East hires; Dubai teams growing [S243][S351][S350].

Your opinion goes here
Suggested position: London is where the clients are advised even when they are no longer resident, which is the whole point of a uniform global model. Say that, and say you would want time in Dubai or Geneva later.
