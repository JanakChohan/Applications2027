"Why J.P. Morgan" in three lengths, and the counter-case

Mechanism, not adjectives. Each version could not be transplanted to a rival without changing the words.

12.1 Thirty seconds

Say this in the interview (adapt the bracketed part to you)
"Two things about this seat that I can't get elsewhere. First, the way clients arrive: the investment bank sells a founder's company and the founder becomes a Private Bank client, and the firm's rule is that nobody counts the credit, which is why over 95% of its top clients use other parts of the bank. Second, the way bankers are made: the firm seeds books rather than buying them, half its advisors are home-grown, and it has gone from 2,500 to 4,100 advisors in five years while saying Europe is where it wants share. I want to be trained in that model, in London, and [the specific thing: the work with families rethinking residence after the non-dom changes]."

12.2 Two minutes

Use this shape; the sentences are yours to rewrite.

  1. What the seat is. "I understand the Advisor is the banker who owns the relationship and brings in the Investor, the Capital Advisor and the Wealth Advisor. It is a relationship and sales job with technical content, inside a team, not a solo investing job. That is what I want, and here is why [one piece of evidence from your life: something you sold, someone whose trust you earned over time, a long run of no before a yes]."
  2. The first mechanism: where clients come from. "Most private banks compete for the same families with the same products. This one has a referral engine no rival has at scale: the number one investment bank by fees for fifteen years, a commercial bank with 60,000 mid-sized companies, Chase branches that Erdoes says two thousand private banking clients walk into every day, and a workplace equity platform with 1.8 million participants. The rule that makes it work is 'no side accounting groups': nobody fights over who booked the revenue."
  3. The second mechanism: the balance sheet. "The Private Bank has $266bn of loans with losses of one to four basis points a year and calls itself the largest lending business in private banking. For a founder who has just sold, or a family buying a house without selling investments, that is the product. A boutique cannot do it."
  4. The third mechanism: how bankers are made. "The firm says it seeds books, doesn't buy them; that productivity doubles after a year and break-even comes in year three; that home-grown 25-year-olds now match mid-career hires; and that hiring will normalise later this decade. So the 2027 class is being trained at the peak of the build. I want to be in it."
  5. Why London. "London runs the international bank. The International CEO told Euromoney the biggest share opportunity is in Europe; the UK head is reported to be hiring thirty people a year; and the April 2025 tax changes mean every international family in London is having a residence conversation. I want to learn on that."
  6. The honest caveat. "I know it is not the biggest wealth manager, that Europe is a long runway from a share below one percent, and that the conduct history includes Epstein. I'd rather join a firm that discloses its numbers and its mistakes than one that doesn't."
  7. The close. "The specific thing I want to work on is [X]."

12.3 The close: one specific thing

Pick one and make it yours. Each is real, current and specific to London.

  • The UK Wealth Advisory team's work with families deciding whether to stay after the non-dom abolition: the four-year FIG window, the 12 then 15 percent repatriation facility, the ten-of-twenty-years inheritance-tax test [S136][S31].
  • The new Financial Institutions Group under Paul Ferry: private equity and hedge fund partners with carried interest that is taxed as income from April 2026 [S377].
  • The London Middle East desk and the Saudi and Dubai build-out: Gulf families served from London, Dubai and Riyadh [S44][S351].
  • The Entrepreneurs and Executives team and pre-liquidity planning: the founder eighteen months before the sale [S43][S198].
  • The private-credit conversation after the 2026 redemption gates: explaining liquidity honestly to clients in evergreen funds [S257].

12.5 The honest counter-case list

  • It is not the largest wealth manager (Morgan Stanley, UBS) and did not win Euromoney's global title in 2025 or 2026 [S154][S155][S177][S242].
  • Europe is a long runway: about $750bn of international client assets against UBS's $4.8tn; share "below 1%" in several markets [S320][S316].
  • A bank-owned wealth manager has product conflicts, and the firm says it "prefers J.P. Morgan managed strategies" [S195].
  • The conduct history is real: London Whale, FX, spoofing, recordkeeping, trade surveillance, Epstein [S97][S100][S101][S102][S103][S108].
  • The job is a sales job with a slow start; the firm's own words are "12-hour days six days a week" early on and break-even in year three [S334][S317].
  • Size brings "complexity, bureaucracy and complacency", in the CEO's words [S311].

None of these is a reason not to apply. All of them are reasons to sound like someone who has read the firm rather than its adverts.
