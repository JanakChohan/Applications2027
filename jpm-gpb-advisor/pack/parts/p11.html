Part 11

The scenario playbook

The frame-holding method, the priority order, eight frame-breaking tactics and their counters, how to stay calm, three scenarios for every duty in the posting, a composure set with decision trees, and the CV grill.

11.1 The four-step protocol

{{FIG:protocol}}
StepTimeExact phrasesWhat it does
1. Steady0 to 10 s"Let me make sure I've understood." Restate in one sentence: "So the client wants X by Y and the issue is Z."Buys thinking time; proves listening; removes the emotional charge.
2. Sort10 to 25 s"First, is there anything here I can't do?" Then: "Second, what does the client actually need? Third, what's the right process? Then, how fast can I do it?"Applies the priority order out loud, so the interviewer hears the frame before the answer.
3. Act25 to 60 s"I'd [verb] first, then [verb], and I'd bring in [person] because [reason]." Concrete verbs: check, call, draft, escalate, document.Shows you know who does what in the team (Part 1).
4. Close60 to 75 s"And I'd log it and tell my team lead the same day." Then stop.Ends on the record and the escalation; silence invites the follow-up you want.

11.2 The priority order

{{FIG:priority}}

When good things collide in this role: (1) the rules and the firm's name; (2) the client's outcome and fair treatment of every internal client and colleague; (3) the process and the record; (4) speed. The one sentence that holds every frame: "I'd rather be slow and right than fast and wrong, and I'd tell you that to your face." It works because it concedes nothing on 1, promises 2 and 3, and treats 4 honestly.

11.3 Eight ways interviewers break a frame, and the counters

{{FIG:frame_tactics}}

How to stay calm physically. Breathe out before you speak (the out-breath slows the heart; the in-breath does not). Feet flat, hands visible and still. Slow the first sentence by half. If your mind blanks, say the restating sentence from step 1; it is always available. Drink water before, not during. In a video interview, look at the camera when you deliver the close.

11.4 The scenario ladder: three for every duty in the posting

{{FIG:ladder}}

Duty 1: "understand client needs and examine market activities to develop and execute strategies"

Easy: the morning brief

Setup. The banker asks you to summarise what happened overnight for a client call at ten. Trap. Reciting headlines. Say. "Three things: oil is back above $100 on Hormuz, gilts are at 5.36%, and the Bank of England vote was 6-3 with three for a hike. For this client the one that matters is the floating-rate loan; I'd ask the Capital Advisor whether we should discuss fixing part of it." Why it works. The house view plus the client lens [S227][S258]. Twist. "The client thinks rates will fall." Hold: "Then I'd show them what the market is pricing and let them decide with the numbers in front of them."

Medium: the concentrated position

Setup. A founder client holds 70% of their wealth in one listed stock and says AI will make it triple. Trap. Arguing about AI. Say. "I'd acknowledge the conviction, then show two numbers: what a 40% drawdown would do to the family's plan, and what Cembalest's own note says about semiconductor technicals. Then I'd bring the Investor to discuss a hedge or a phased sale, and the Wealth Advisor on the tax." Why. Suitability and the four lenses [S238][S32]. Twist. "The client says you're too junior to have a view." Hold: "Fair. It isn't my view; it's the house view, and I'd get the Investor on the call."

Hard: the house view disagrees with the client and the banker

Setup. The banker wants to keep a client happy by agreeing to an all-gilts portfolio; the house view says diversify. Trap. Siding with either loudly. Say. "I'd put the two cases side by side for the banker privately: the client's stated goal, the after-tax real return on gilts with savings tax going to 47% in 2027, and the house allocation. Then the banker decides what to present. My job is to make sure the record shows the client chose with both options in front of them." Why. Suitability and the Consumer Duty's "consumer understanding" outcome [S146][S138]. Twist. "The banker says don't put it in writing." Hold: "I'd say the note protects them as much as the client, and I'd write it."

Duty 2: "work with a team of bankers, investment specialists, wealth advisors, and lending specialists"

Easy: the wrong specialist

Setup. A client emails you about a trust question. Trap. Answering it. Say. "I'd reply within the hour that I've passed it to our wealth advisory team and copy James Chilvers' team, with the banker in copy. Then I'd read up so I understand the answer when it comes." Why. The five-seat model; you are not qualified to advise [S28]. Twist. "The client says they only want to deal with you." Hold: "Then I'd stay the single point of contact and bring the specialist to the client rather than the client to the specialist."

Medium: two specialists disagree

Setup. The Investor wants the client fully invested; the Capital Advisor wants more collateral headroom on the loan. Trap. Picking a side. Say. "Both are right about their piece. I'd lay out the loan-to-value at three market levels and the cash the client needs for lifestyle, and let the banker frame the trade-off with the client. The bank's rule is two ways out, so the collateral view sets the floor." Why. "We want two ways out" [S318]. Twist. "Decide now." Hold: "Then the floor is the credit rule; within it, the Investor's plan."

Hard: a specialist is not responding and the client is waiting

Setup. The Wealth Advisor has not answered for a week; the client's lawyer is chasing. Trap. Complaining about the colleague. Say. "I'd walk over or call rather than email again; if they're overloaded I'd ask what I can prepare to unblock them; if it's still stuck I'd tell the banker the same day so the client gets a date, not silence." Why. "No side accounting groups" is also a no-blame rule; the client's outcome is priority 2 [S320]. Twist. "The banker says go around them." Hold: "I'd rather bring them along; but the client gets an answer this week either way."

Duty 3: "collaborate with market strategy, equity, and alternative investment teams... as part of a Solutions Team"

Easy: the project brief

Setup. Your intern project is "should our clients add infrastructure?" Trap. Writing an essay. Say. "I'd start from the house view (8 to 12% expected returns and 80% of family offices have none), then the client segment it suits, the vehicles we offer, the liquidity terms, and one page of risks. Two slides a banker could use." Why. The Mid-Year Outlook and the Family Office Report [S237][S240]. Twist. "The alternatives team disagrees with your risk page." Hold: "Then their view goes in and mine goes in the appendix with the reason."

Medium: the number you cannot verify

Setup. A slide you inherited says a fund returned 14% a year; you cannot find the source. Trap. Leaving it in. Say. "I'd take it out or mark it unverified, tell the person who gave it to me, and replace it with the number I can source. A wrong figure in front of a client is a Consumer Duty problem, not a typo." Why. "Consumer understanding" outcome; Business Principle 12 [S146][S323]. Twist. "The meeting is in ten minutes." Hold: "Then it comes out; a missing number is better than a wrong one."

Hard: the private-credit pitch after the gates

Setup. You are asked to help pitch a semi-liquid private-credit fund to a client who needs the money in three years. Trap. Pitching it. Say. "I'd raise the mismatch before the meeting: after Blue Owl and Cliffwater gated redemptions, semi-liquid means sometimes not liquid, and this client has a date. I'd suggest either a smaller allocation from the patient part of the portfolio or a different product, and I'd document the suitability reasoning either way." Why. Suitability; the firm's own 2026 caution on private credit [S257][S223]. Twist. "The banker says the client asked for it." Hold: "Then we show them the liquidity terms in writing and let them choose."

Duty 4: "bring in new clients and serve existing ones"

Easy: the prospect list

Setup. Build a list of twenty prospects for the Entrepreneurs and Executives team. Trap. A list of famous people. Say. "I'd start inside the firm: founders whose companies the investment bank or commercial bank has just advised or lent to; then recent UK exits in the press; then the team's existing clients' co-founders. For each, one line on why now." Why. The referral engine; "42% of cap tables" [S320]. Twist. "Cold-call the top five." Hold: "I'd ask the banker for a warm route first; a referral converts far better than a cold call."

Medium: the first meeting

Setup. You join a first meeting with a founder who sold last month. Trap. Talking about products. Say. "My job is to listen and take the notes that matter: the four lenses, where the cash is now, who else they bank with, what they're worried about. Afterwards I'd draft the discovery summary and the three follow-ups within a day." Why. Discovery before proposal [S32]. Twist. "The client asks you directly what you'd do with £80m." Hold: "I'd say the honest answer is that it depends on what they want the money to do, and that's the conversation."

Hard: the client who wants to leave

Setup. A client is down 20% and says they are moving to a rival. Trap. Defending. Say. "Listen first, fully. Then review against the plan and the benchmark, not the headline number. Involve the Investor and the team lead the same day. Make no promises about performance. If they still go, make the exit clean; clients come back to banks that behaved well on the way out." Why. ">95% don't leave" is earned in these moments [S318]. Twist. "They say the rival offered lower fees." Hold: "Then I'd show what the fee bought, and I'd never match on price alone."

Duty 5: "good judgment and discretion when working with confidential information"

Easy: the friend

Setup. A friend asks whether a famous person is a client. Trap. A knowing smile. Say. "I don't confirm or deny anyone, ever, including people who aren't clients. That's the job." Twist. "It's already in the papers." Hold: "Then they can read the papers."

Medium: the document on the printer

Setup. You find another team's client file on a shared printer. Trap. Reading it. Say. "I'd turn it over, hand it to the team or to the CSA, and mention it to my team lead so the control gap is logged." Twist. "It's your own team's client." Hold: "Same answer; I'd still note that it was left there."

Hard: the inside information

Setup. A client says over lunch that their listed company is "about to be bought". Trap. Mentioning it to the Investor "so we can position". Say. "It might be material non-public information. I'd not act, not repeat it, tell compliance and my team lead as soon as I'm back, note the time and context, and let compliance decide about restrictions." Why. Market abuse rules; the information barrier [S82]. Twist. "The client says everyone knows." Hold: "If it isn't announced, I treat it as inside information."

11.5 The composure set, with decision trees

{{FIG:tree1}} {{FIG:tree2}}
Hard: two senior people want the same scarce resource

Setup. Two bankers both need the Investor's afternoon for proposals due tomorrow; both ask you to "sort it". Tree. Is there a rule that decides (a regulatory deadline, a client-harm risk)? If yes, that one first. If not: which client outcome is worse if missed? Then which was asked first? Then escalate to the team lead with the facts, not the politics. Say. "I'd find out what each proposal actually needs from the Investor (often one needs an hour, not an afternoon), propose a split, and if that fails, take it to the team lead with both deadlines written down. I wouldn't decide it alone." Twist. "One of them is your boss." Hold: "Then I'd tell my boss what I'd do and why, and let them overrule me if they want."

{{FIG:collision}}
Hard: a mistake you made

Setup. You sent a client a report with another client's name on it. Tree. Stop the harm (recall, call the client, apologise). Tell the banker and compliance immediately; a data breach may need reporting under UK GDPR within 72 hours. Document. Fix the process that let it happen. Say. "I'd call the client before they found it, tell my banker and compliance within the hour, and write down exactly what went out. Then I'd change how I check names before sending." Twist. "The client didn't notice." Hold: "They still get the call, and compliance still gets told."

Hard: incomplete information against a deadline

Setup. A proposal is due at nine; you have the client's investments but not their liabilities. Tree. Can the missing piece change the recommendation? If yes, say so on page one and give a range. If no, proceed and flag. Never fill the gap with a guess. Say. "I'd deliver on time with the assumption stated on the first page and the two cases it changes, and I'd have the question to the client ready for the banker." Twist. "Just assume they have no debt." Hold: "I'll assume it, label it, and show what changes if it's wrong."

11.6 The CV grill

How a three-levels-deep attack works. Level one: "Tell me about X on your CV." Level two: "What specifically did you do?" Level three: "What would have happened if you hadn't?" or "What number did that change?" Most candidates survive level one and collapse at level three because the bullet was a group's work or a description of activity rather than of a result.

AttackFrame-holding answer
1. "This bullet says 'led'. How many people, and what did you decide?"Give the number and one decision you made alone, with its consequence. If you did not lead, say "coordinated" and own that.
2. "What was the result, in a number?"One number, its baseline, and the period. If there is no number, say what changed that someone else could see.
3. "What would you do differently?"One specific thing, and what it would have improved. Never "nothing".
4. "Why did you leave / stop / not continue?"The true reason in one sentence, then what you took from it. No criticism of the previous organisation.
5. "This looks like a group project. What was yours?"Name your piece, its interface with others' pieces, and how you resolved one conflict in it.
6. "Your degree isn't finance. Why should we trust you with markets?""You shouldn't yet; that's what the training is for. What I bring is [curiosity, evidence]. And here is what I've read in the last month and what I think about it."
7. "You've applied to other banks.""Yes, to [n]. Here is what is specific about this seat that the others don't have" (one mechanism from Part 12).
8. "Tell me something not on your CV."Prepared: one thing that shows persistence or trust-building, with a number.

The four-line drill for every CV bullet. For each bullet write: (1) what I personally did (verbs); (2) the number that changed and its baseline; (3) the hardest moment and what I decided; (4) what I would do differently. If you cannot fill all four lines, rewrite the bullet or remove it.
