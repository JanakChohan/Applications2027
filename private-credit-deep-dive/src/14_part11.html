11.2 Why this seat is harder in private credit

In listed equities, the price is a fact and your job is to explain performance against it. In private credit there is no price, so the number you are explaining is one your own firm produced. That changes the job.

The challengeWhat it means day to day
There is no market priceYou are explaining a valuation, not reporting a fact. When a client asks "how do you know it is worth that?", the honest answer involves methodology, third-party valuation agents and board oversight. Part 5.5 is why this question is not hostile.
The performance language is differentNot a simple percentage return but IRR, MOIC, DPI, RVPI and TVPI. Explaining why IRR can be flattered by a subscription line, and why DPI is the number that cannot be flattered, is a core skill.
Capital calls confuse peopleInvestors commit money they have not yet paid. Explaining commitments, calls, the consequences of failing to fund a call, and why uncalled cash drags on returns is routine work.
The J-curve needs explainingLess severe in credit than private equity because income arrives early, but still real.
Liquidity terms are now the main eventAfter 2026, every conversation about a semi-liquid vehicle involves the cap, the queue and the notice period. Part 8.3.
Due diligence is adversarialA consultant's questionnaire on a credit fund asks about valuation governance, workout capability, PIK exposure and covenant packages. These are technical questions requiring technical answers.
Key terms · the performance vocabulary, defined properly
  • IRR — internal rate of return. Annualised, accounts for timing. Can be flattered by borrowing early so investor cash is called later.
  • MOIC — multiple on invested capital. How many times the money came back, ignoring time entirely.
  • DPI — distributions to paid-in. Cash actually returned, over cash put in. The number that cannot be manipulated by a valuation.
  • RVPI — residual value to paid-in. What is still held, at the manager's estimate.
  • TVPI — total value to paid-in. DPI plus RVPI. The headline multiple.
  • Vintage year — the year the fund began investing. Funds are compared within vintages because conditions dominate.

If you learn one relationship: TVPI = DPI + RVPI. A fund with a high TVPI and a low DPI is telling you most of its reported value is still unrealised and rests on the manager's own estimate. Asking for the DPI split is the single most revealing question an investor can ask, and knowing that is a genuine signal.

11.3 Qualifications and background

From the postings reviewed: the CFA and CAIA appear as "desired" or "a plus" rather than required. Junior client portfolio manager roles indicated roughly three to five years of experience; director-level product specialist roles eight to fifteen[P341] Reported. The CFA Institute offers a Private Markets Certificate and CAIA a private debt microcredential[P342].

Source uncertain — pay

Base salary only, excluding bonus, which in this industry is a large share of total pay. Treat as indicative and do not quote it as a market rate.

11.4 Fifteen questions that test whether you actually know this

If you are going to say you want to specialise in private credit, assume you will get several of these. Each note says what a good answer contains, not what to say.

  1. What is private credit? Should include: direct lending, not traded, usually senior secured and floating rate, held to maturity. Part 1.1.
  2. How is it different from private equity? Capped upside, protected downside, contractual income rather than exit-driven gains. Part 1.2.
  3. Why does it exist? Bank capital rules after 2008, and the 2013 leveraged lending guidance. Bonus for knowing that guidance was withdrawn in December 2025. Part 2.2.
  4. How big is it? A range with the reason for the range, not a single number. Part 2.3.
  5. Where does the return come from? Reference rate, plus spread, plus OID and fees, minus losses, fees and leverage cost. Part 5.2.
  6. What is a unitranche? One blended loan, split privately behind the scenes through an agreement among lenders. Part 4.2.
  7. How do you value a loan that does not trade? Mark to model, third-party agents, board oversight, and honest acknowledgement of the conflict. Part 5.5.
  8. IRR versus MOIC versus DPI? See the box above. Knowing why DPI is the hardest to flatter is the differentiator.
  9. What is PIK and why does it matter? Interest paid in more debt. A legitimate tool and a stress signal when it spreads. Part 4.4.
  10. What happens when a borrower breaches a covenant? Negotiation, waiver, amendment, possibly ownership. You cannot sell. Part 4.5.
  11. What is a gate and why do these funds have them? Part 8.3, plus the 2026 episodes.
  12. Are default rates a good measure of stress? No, and the reasons are amend-and-extend, PIK and waivers. Part 5.4.
  13. What is the biggest risk in private credit? A view, with a mechanism. Part 9.5 gives you the transmission chain.
  14. Why are institutions buying evergreen funds? Your FT article. Capital-call drag, the J-curve, slow distributions, and the 20% versus 11% arithmetic. Part 8.3.
  15. Why do you want to work on this rather than equities? The honest personal answer. If it is that the analysis is about downside and structure rather than forecasting upside, say that.

11.5 What to read, to keep building

  • Alternative Credit Council, its annual Financing the Economy report. The industry's own data, free.
  • KBRA and Lincoln International for default and valuation data; Cliffwater for the index; Proskauer for its default index.
  • ILPA for the standard definitions of the performance measures above.
  • Regulators: the FSB report of May 2026, the FCA valuation review of March 2025, the Bank of England's Financial Stability Reports. All free, all primary, and almost nobody your age reads them.
  • Trade press: Private Debt Investor, 9fin, Creditflux, Octus, Debtwire. Mostly subscription.
How to say you want this specialism without overclaiming

The weak version is "I'm really interested in private credit." The strong version names a specific thing you find interesting and shows you know the shape of the argument around it.

Examples of a genuine hook, each drawn from this document: that a loan can be marked at 50 and 77 cents by two funds in the same quarter; that the industry's defence rests on locked-up capital while its growth came from money that is not locked up; that the 2013 guidance which created the market was withdrawn in 2025; or that institutions are now buying the product built for individuals. Pick the one you actually find interesting. An interviewer can tell the difference, and the follow-up question will find you out if you cannot.
