Part 0

Start here

The ten things that actually matter, in the order they build. If you read only this page you will know more than most people who use the phrase "private credit" in conversation.

  1. It is lending, not owning. The upside is capped at being repaid with interest. The downside is protected by the owners' money underneath you. That asymmetry drives everything. Part 1.2.
  2. The defining feature is that there is no market price. The loan never trades. That is why it can be held calmly through a panic, and why its reported value is an estimate produced by the firm that is paid on the basis of it. Both consequences are real. Part 5.5.
  3. It exists because regulation moved it. After 2008, capital rules and the 2013 US leveraged lending guidance made these loans expensive for banks to hold. The loans went to funds instead. In December 2025 the regulators withdrew that guidance as "overly restrictive"[P6], which is a twist worth knowing. Part 2.2.
  4. Nobody agrees how big it is, and knowing why is a better answer than any single figure. Estimates run from roughly $1.5trn on a narrow definition to about $3.5trn on a broad one, because of scope, dry powder and dates. Part 2.3.
  5. The return is contractual income. Around 8.5% to 9.5% all-in on senior loans in 2026[P61]: a floating reference rate, plus a credit spread, plus fees. You collect interest; you are not waiting for an exit. Part 5.2.
  6. It is mostly senior and secured. Around 86% of documented deals are first lien or unitranche[P51]. The caricature of private credit as exotic is wrong. But US Federal Reserve research put recoveries after default at about 33%, below syndicated loans at 52%[P120], which complicates the standard pitch. Part 5.4.
  7. Headline default rates understate the strain, because amend-and-extend, PIK and covenant waivers keep loans out of the statistics. PIK-featured loans rose from 5.4% to 9.8% of BDC loans between 2022 and 2026[P69]. Part 5.4.
  8. The growth is no longer in corporate lending. Practitioners name asset-based finance and fund finance as the fastest-growing areas[P52]. If you describe private credit as "lending to companies", you are describing 2018. Part 3.
  9. The biggest structural story is insurance. Managers have bought annuity companies to obtain permanent capital, converting fee income into spread income on their own balance sheet. Part 8.1.
  10. The unresolved question is whether the money is as patient as the assets. The industry's best defence is that its capital is locked up. Its growth came from evergreen vehicles where it is not. That tension broke into the open in 2026. Part 8.3 and Part 9.5.
The one sentence that holds it together

Private credit is the business of matching patient money to loans that cannot be sold. Every argument in this document, historical, commercial or regulatory, is a version of the question "is the money actually patient enough?" Banks turned out not to be the right holder. Insurance companies are. Whether quarterly-redemption retail vehicles are is the live question of 2026.

A note on the numbers in this document

Private credit is badly served by data. Different providers report different market sizes, different default rates and different recovery rates, honestly, because they measure different things. Where this document gives a figure it names the source and marks the confidence. Where providers disagree it shows the disagreement rather than picking a winner. Any figure marked Reported rests on one source. Check before you use it somewhere it matters.
