7.2 What actually separates one manager from another

In a market where everyone lends at roughly the same spread against roughly the same companies, four things genuinely differentiate:

Origination

Whether you see the deal at all. Sponsor relationships are the currency, because 70% or more of direct lending is sponsor-backed[P70]. A firm that sees every deal can decline the bad ones; a firm that sees few must take what it is shown.

Scale

The ability to write a billion-dollar cheque alone is a genuine competitive weapon, because it lets a sponsor do a deal in one conversation. It is also the reason the industry has concentrated.

Workout capability

You cannot sell a private loan. When a borrower struggles, your only options are to negotiate or to take the keys. Firms with real restructuring teams recover more. This is under-appreciated and hard to copy.

Permanent capital

Money that never has to be given back, from an insurance balance sheet or an evergreen vehicle. It removes the pressure to deploy before a fund's investment period expires. See 8.1.

7.3 The consolidation wave

Traditional asset managers, facing fee compression in public markets, have been buying private credit capability rather than building it. BlackRock's purchase of HPS is the landmark, alongside Nuveen and Arcmont, Janus Henderson and Victory Park, T. Rowe Price and Oak Hill, Manulife and CQS, Franklin Templeton and Benefit Street, and Blue Owl's own run of acquisitions[P152] Reported.

The strategic logic is simple. A public equity fund earns perhaps 50 basis points and is losing share to index funds. A private credit fund earns 150 basis points plus incentive fees on capital that cannot leave. If you are a large traditional manager, you either build that capability, buy it, or watch your revenue shrink.

7.4 Where Fidelity International sits, and why it is different

This matters if you are going to say you want to work on private credit at Fidelity, because Fidelity's position is not the one most candidates would assume.

The confirmed facts

In May 2026 Fidelity International launched a Private Markets Partner Network of ten external managers feeding its Diversified Private Assets LTAF[P155] Confirmed. The named managers include Ares, Bridges, Crescent, Churchill, CVC, DigitalBridge, Haveli, Hg and Stonepeak, alongside Fidelity itself[P155]. Its FutureWise workplace default was reported at £25bn at that point[P155].

Set that beside the firm's own audited UK accounts, which state a decision to "cease manufacturing of non-core private asset classes in order to pivot resources to areas of unique strength... active ETFs and the distribution of third-party capabilities for both private assets and select public market investments"[P156] Confirmed.

How to use this in an interview, carefully

Saying "I want to work in private credit at Fidelity" invites the reply "we mostly don't originate it." Saying "I'm interested in private credit, and I noticed Fidelity's approach is to select external managers through the Partner Network rather than originate directly, which makes the client conversation about manager selection and liquidity terms rather than individual credits" demonstrates you actually looked.

It is also a genuinely interesting position to defend. An allocator avoids the conflict of marking its own loans, and gets breadth. It also adds a fee layer and gives up control. Both are true, and having a view is better than having a preference.
