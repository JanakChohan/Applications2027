What Millennium is (three sentences)

Millennium is a private multi-manager hedge fund founded in 1989 by Izzy Englander, with assets of over $92bn on its own site and about $97bn per Bloomberg, roughly 6,700 to 6,900 staff and over 350 independent investment teams [S71][S70][S400]. It allocates investor capital to those teams under hard loss limits, passes its costs through to investors, and sells them a steady return that barely moves with markets: about 14% a year with one losing year since 1989 [S111][S115]. It sold a first outside stake in November 2025 and on 1 October 2026 closes $22bn of new commitments, with Jain Global and Albar now exclusive to the platform [S82][S400][S407].

Why this desk exists (two sentences)

Hundreds of independent PM teams all want time with company management, and since 2014 in the UK and 2018 across the EU that time has been a priced, rationed, compliance-controlled service [S180][S185]. The teams compete and do not share, so one desk turns their confidential demand into one voice to the market and one fair allocation back, and proves with data which access was worth its cost.

13.2 The lines, the questions and the room

Say this

"Millennium is built as hundreds of independent, tightly-risked teams turned into one steady return by a centre that is bigger than the investment side. That structure creates the desk: the teams can't coordinate meetings themselves, so someone has to turn their confidential demand into one voice to the market and a fair allocation back."

"A no from the desk is never just a no. It is an allocation with an alternative attached: the rule in one line, the alternative already secured, and a path to re-open it that isn't me. The consumption report is what makes the no credible, because the PM can see what the desk did get him. Trust is built in the noes."

"The desk's client base is growing faster than the desk: 350-plus teams, $22bn closing on 1 October, external managers joining. That is a process and data problem, which is what an intern can help with."

"Flat August, eight per cent for the year against a market up more: the product is the drawdown profile, not the headline number."

Saying no to a PM: the five moves

  1. Acknowledge the cost to the book; do not apologise for the rule.
  2. The rule, in one line. Never who else got it, never why they ranked higher.
  3. The alternative, already secured: group slot, IR call, next conference, a note if permitted.
  4. Escalation path: "If you want it looked at again, I'll take it to the head of desk now."
  5. Stay in the relationship: same tone tomorrow; log it; show the consumption report.

Don't say this

  • A precise AUM, fee, pod threshold or the desk's rules as fact.
  • "Best hedge fund in the world." Three rivals beat it in 2025.
  • "MiFID II banned corporate access." It changed how it is paid for.
  • The canteen story first. Lead with the email and AI story.
  • "Talking to Anthropic about deals." Say the partnership as announced.
  • Athens, Geneva or any named PM. Anything about the interviewers' private lives.
  • Anything implying you plan to move to the investing side.

Questions to ask (two or three per hour)

Strategy-level (for a head of desk, or if she goes there):

  1. What does the desk have to solve in the next two years to serve many more teams without losing the record or the service?
  2. How is the allocation rule communicated to the team that loses, and how often is a no escalated to you?
  3. How has the balance between broker-sourced and originated meetings shifted in EMEA, and where do you want it?
  4. Payment optionality covers research but corporate access was ruled out of scope. Has that changed how brokers behave toward the desk?

Mechanics-level (for Joanna):

  1. What does a week in conference season look like on the desk, hour by hour?
  2. What in the portal or the consumption report would you change if you had an intern for ten weeks?
  3. How do you keep the record when requests arrive by chat, email and in person on the same day?
  4. What did the last intern do that made your job easier?
