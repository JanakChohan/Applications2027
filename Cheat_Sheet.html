What Millennium is (three sentences)

Millennium is a private multi-manager hedge fund founded in 1989 by Izzy Englander, managing about $92bn (its own website, Sep 2026) with roughly 6,700 to 6,900 staff and over 350 independent investment teams. It allocates investor capital to those teams under hard loss limits, passes its costs through to investors, and sells them a steady return that barely moves with markets: about 14% a year with one losing year since 1989. In November 2025 it sold its first outside stake, 15% for about $2bn at a $14bn valuation, and in 2026 it is raising $10 to 20bn more while Citadel returns capital.

Why this role exists (two sentences)

Hundreds of independent PM teams all want time with company management, and since 2014 in the UK and 2018 across the EU that time has been a priced, rationed, compliance-controlled service rather than a free perk of trading with a bank. A central desk that pools demand, works both the broker channel and the direct-to-company channel, allocates scarce slots, and proves with data which access was worth its cost beats 350 pods fending for themselves.

Five talking points

  1. Two channels. "Sell-side broker sourced" and "Millennium originated" are two supply lines. The Services desk centralises the broker channel; a sectorised Sector Specialist Origination team goes direct to company IR teams. The desk sits in Execution Services within Platform Solutions (PSEM) [S42][S40]. Which channel for which request is the desk's core judgement.
  2. Internal clients. The desk markets access to PMs who have no obligation to engage, allocates contested conference slots (bank booking windows open 4 to 6 weeks out [S30]), and reports consumption by PM, broker, sector and channel. That data feeds the semi-annual broker vote and justifies a cost that investors pay through pass-through [S43][S104].
  3. Compliance is the bright line. Meetings are logged because UK MAR treats analysts as "not the public" and private issuer calls as "dissemination" [S21][S22]. If a PM comes back with a number that is not in public releases, flag it to Compliance; do not forward. Never say "edge" next to "management meeting".
  4. Regulation, precisely. 2014: access cannot be bought with commission. 2018: MiFID II unbundled research. 2024 and 2025: the FCA re-allowed bundled research payments but ruled corporate access "out of scope" [S181]. So access is still a separately valued service, which is why the desk, its data and its origination channel exist. Adoption of the UK option is "slow" and Europe's research budgets have barely recovered [S256].
  5. Right now. Up 8.2% to August against an S&P 500 up about 19%: the low-volatility product working as designed, and fuel for the cash-hurdle debate [S220][S249]. London equities being built out (co-head of equities relocated; Schonfeld's EMEA recruiter hired May 2026 [S231]) means the desk's client base is growing. Central-bank week follows your interview.

Say this

"I read the JD as two supply channels and a desk whose clients are internal. The portal and consumption reports are how you prove the access is worth its cost and decide which brokers deserve the vote."

"Millennium's edge is a system, not a market view: hundreds of hedged, independent teams under hard loss limits, so the return is the average of uncorrelated bets. One losing year since 1989."

"Citadel returned $5bn and Millennium raised $10bn and absorbed Jain Global: two answers to the same capacity problem. Millennium's only works if new pods become productive fast, and shared services like corporate access are part of that."

Don't say this

  • A precise AUM, fee or expense ratio as fact. Say "press estimates".
  • "Best hedge fund in the world." It was 17th of 20 on Bloomberg's 2025 table.
  • "MiFID II banned corporate access." It changed how it is paid for.
  • Anything implying you plan to move to the investing side.
  • Anything you read about the interviewers.

Questions to ask (pick two or three)

  1. Roughly what share of EMEA meetings is broker-sourced versus originated today, and is that shifting?
  2. How do the consumption reports feed the strategy sessions: broker evaluation, conference planning, or both?
  3. When several teams request the same 1x1 at a conference, is there a written allocation rule or is it case by case?
  4. How does the London desk work with the Sector Specialist Origination team and Broker Relations day to day?
  5. Payment optionality covers research but corporate access was ruled out of scope. Has that changed how the desk values broker access, or how brokers behave?
  6. What does the proprietary portal do that off-the-shelf platforms do not, and could an intern improve it?
  7. What does a strong Business Services intern look like by week ten?
  8. What are the next steps and the timeline after this round?

Reading the room (first three minutes)

Practitioner signals: introduces by desk; asks how you would do something; uses "allocation", "1x1", "NDR", "portal". Adapt: short biography, concrete steps, their vocabulary, admit gaps, ask questions 1 to 6.
Recruiter signals: "campus" or "talent"; CV first; same competency questions in order. Adapt: STAR-R, map to the JD's competencies, ask 7 to 8.
If unsure: "Would it help if I framed this from the desk's day-to-day, or from my background?"
