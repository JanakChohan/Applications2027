How to use this PDF

This PDF is generated from the same files as the interactive study application (gsam_csg_command_center.html) and the Markdown master dossier. It contains everything the application contains, with every expandable panel opened and every interactive feature explained in print: the coverage matrix, the client lifecycle, the fee calculator's method and worked cases, and the interview practice bank.

New to all of this? Read the first three parts first. "Start here" explains what you are applying to from zero. "Relationships" explains who a client relationship is with, what starts it, what keeps it going, what builds it and what it looks like over decades. "Sales and relationship management" shows how a client is won and kept. All three use plain words and diagrams. Everything after them goes deeper.

Easier reading. This PDF uses a sans-serif typeface, larger text, wider letter and line spacing, left-aligned lines, a cream page, bold for emphasis in place of italics, and no words set only in capitals. Each diagram has an "In words" line underneath that says the same thing as the picture.

Appendix J · Fee calculator: method and worked cases

Illustrative only

Every rate below is an invented round number. Nothing here is a Goldman Sachs fee. Results are gross management fees before costs, rebates, distribution payments, performance fees and every other adjustment.

Method

  1. Flows land at the start of a month: initial funding in month 1, any later tranche, one-off additions or withdrawals, and recurring net flows. A withdrawal cannot take the balance below zero.
  2. The month's fee is the annual fee on that month's fee base, divided by twelve. The fee base is either the market value of the assets or, for some private-markets funds, the committed capital. With a tiered schedule each slice of the base is charged at its own rate.
  3. The market return is applied at the end of the month, compounded monthly: monthly rate = (1 + annual return)1/12 − 1.
  4. Fees are assumed to be invoiced separately, not deducted from the assets.

The base case every other case is measured against: £500 million × 0.20% = £1 million a year. One basis point is one hundredth of one per cent.
