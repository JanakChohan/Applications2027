Part 3 — The predicted questions, tier by tier

Tier 1 — plan for these (high confidence). Each with the wording variants most reported, what it probes, and your move.

  1. The warm-up / intro video. "Introduce yourself" / "Tell us about yourself" / "What's your biggest strength?" — Officially a tech check; treat it as scored (sources conflict) [2][4]. Your move: the Present→Path→Future intro from the playbook, with your one light humour line. This is the freebie — deliver it properly.
  2. The motivation video. "Why do you want to join this programme / HSBC / Corporate and Institutional Banking?" or "What interests you about this area?" — Probes: curiosity + motivation, the posting's first trait. Your move: your 15-second contents section + one named deal + the 62% number. This is where all your firm research cashes in — almost no insight-stage candidate can answer at your level.
  3. The new-team / working-with-others video. "You're joining a new team where you know nobody / senior colleagues you've never met — how do you introduce yourself and build relationships?" — The single most-reported job-sim video prompt [6]. Probes: team player + We succeed together. Your move: posture → listen-first actions → deliver-small-things-early, closing on learning from different perspectives.
  4. The research-task scenario (video and/or ranking). After reading the fictional news article: "How would you approach starting this research project?" / "What would you look at first and why?" — Probes: curious + analytical + problem-solver. Your move: a simple method, said aloud: goal first → what do we know / not know → prioritise the two things that matter most → who I'd ask → deadline check. Method beats content — they're marking how you think.
  5. Prioritisation / judgement rankings. Scenario with ~5 possible actions, rank 1–5 most-to-least likely. Expect: competing deadlines, a colleague needing help, a quality problem spotted late, unequal participation in a meeting. Probes: problem-solver + values. Your move: rank by the values — collaborative, responsible options high; ignoring, blaming, or going-over-heads options low; decisive beats fence-sitting. Answer as you genuinely would.
  6. The written email. "Reply to this email from your manager/colleague" — often which request do we handle first and why, or summarise what the data suggests (~2,000–3,000 characters, typed live) [1][3]. Probes: problem-solver + detail + communication. Your move: acknowledge → triage with a stated "why first" → recommend from the given data → own the next step.
  7. Personality / work-style scales. Slider pairs (e.g. "I like variety" vs "I see one thing through"), possibly asking both what you'd do and what you'd enjoy — the strengths signature [5]. Your move: honest and consistent. These calibrate the authenticity check that runs across everything else.

Tier 2 — likely (prepare, lighter).

  1. A data-interpretation item — a chart/table from the fictional company: "What does this data suggest?" / "Which option does the data support?" (mirrors "bring data to life" from the posting). Read units and dates twice; calculator on desk.
  2. A "different perspectives" scenario — a colleague disagrees or a quieter voice isn't heard (probes We value difference). Take the other view seriously; the outcome improves because of it.
  3. A strengths video — "What would you bring to the team?" / "What are you good at?" One strength → proof → fit.
  4. A pressure/adaptability item — plans change late, information is incomplete (probes We get it done). Calm triage; "make it reversible, then decide."

Tier 3 — possible (don't over-invest, have one line ready).

  1. Commercial awareness — "a news story you've followed" (rarer in the sim than in later interviews, but your two warm events make it free marks if it appears).
  2. Free time / what energises you — truth → driver → light bridge.
  3. Ethics-flavoured ranking — usually appears as an SJT option rather than a video; the "check facts → proper channel" ladder handles it.

What will NOT come up CORROBORATED — consistent candidate reports[2][6]: valuation or accounting technicals, brainteasers, coding, or a formal commercial-awareness exam. Do not spend your remaining days on those.

Part 4 — Timing tactics per item type

  • Video (~2 min prep / ~2 min record): spend prep jotting 4 bullets max (opener → example → what-it-shows → close). Start speaking within the first seconds of the recording, front-load the point, land the close by ~1:40 so the auto-stop never cuts you. If you finish at 1:30 with everything said — stop. A complete 90 seconds beats a padded 120.
  • Rankings: ~60–90 seconds each. First instinct on the best and worst options, then order the middle. Don't re-litigate; consistency across scenarios matters more than any single item.
  • Personality scales: quick and honest — overthinking these creates the inconsistency the system flags.
  • Email: ~8–10 minutes. One read of the materials, four-move structure, one proofread pass. Under the cap with room to spare.
  • Between tiles: breathe, sip water, reset. Within a tile: one continuous focused sitting [4].

Part 5 — Your countdown plan (deadline ≈ one week out)

  • Today / Day 1: fill the story-bank grid (playbook Part 1). Lock your intro, your motivation answer (contents section + deal + number), and the new-team answer. Say each out loud twice from bullets.
  • Day 2: run the drill — record intro, motivation, new-team, and one "how I'd start research" answer on your phone, one take each, no restarts. Review against: point-first? one story? smiled once? landed by 1:40?
  • Day 3: the email rehearsal (10-minute timer, four moves, typed) + 20 minutes of ranking-style judgement questions on the Preparation Plus portal. Re-check your two news events.
  • Day 4 or 5 — SIT IT, mid-morning, fresh: quiet room, front light, one device, home connection, notifications off, calculator + paper, warm-up done properly (re-record it until sound and framing are right — that's allowed [4]).
  • Keep 2 buffer days before the deadline for any technical failure (genuine tech issues reported promptly are the one thing that gets re-issued).
