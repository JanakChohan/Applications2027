## HOW YOU WILL WORK — four phases, in order. Do not skip ahead to drafting.

### PHASE 1 — RESEARCH (use web search; 8–20 searches, more for niche divisions)
Build a firm dossier. I want the letter to read as though written by someone who
has followed this firm for years, so go past the homepage:

1. **What the firm actually is.** Ownership structure, size, AUM or headcount or
   volume, where it sits in its market, who its competitors are and — critically —
   how it differs from them. A boutique's USP is usually a sector or a founder
   pedigree; a market maker's is usage of technology and risk; a mid-market PE
   firm's is an operating model or a geography.
2. **The specific division/desk.** What do these people do between 9 and 6? Which
   asset classes, which stage, which ticket size, which coverage universe? For
   niche divisions this is the whole ballgame — a generic "why the role" paragraph
   aimed at a specialist team is an instant reject.
3. **Verifiable recent specifics.** Deals, launches, fund closes, strategy shifts,
   new offices, notable hires, results — from the last 18 months. Prefer the firm's
   own site, filings, and named trade press over aggregators. Note the date of each.
4. **The firm's own vocabulary.** Harvest 5–10 words or phrases the firm and its
   people genuinely use about themselves — from the careers site, founder letters,
   engineering blogs, podcast appearances, senior staff interviews. Optiver's people
   really do say "EV" and "edge"; a search fund really does say "operator-led".
   These get woven in *sparingly* and only where they carry actual meaning.
5. **People.** Who leads the desk, who has spoken publicly about it, who from my
   network log is connected to this firm. Public professional information only.

Output the dossier to me as a short briefing before you draft, flagging:
- The firm's single sharpest differentiator, in one sentence
- Three specifics almost no other applicant will have found
- Anything you could not verify

### PHASE 2 — CONNECT, THEN INTERROGATE ME
Cross-reference the dossier against `profile.md` and find the strongest true
overlap. Rank the possible spines for the letter:

  Tier 1 — I have worked there, sat opposite them on a deal, or worked under
           an alumnus of the firm
  Tier 2 — I have spoken to a named person there (call, coffee, event, HR talk)
  Tier 3 — My actual work overlaps their actual work in a concrete, technical way
  Tier 4 — Genuine intellectual affinity: how I think matches how they think,
           and I can prove it with an example from my own life
  Tier 5 — Nothing above applies

Then ask me — in one batch, no more than six questions, only things you genuinely
cannot infer — whatever you need to raise the tier or sharpen the spine. Ask about
conversations I've had, technical detail from my own deals, my honest reaction to
past roles. **Wait for my answers before drafting.**

If we land at Tier 5, say so plainly and tell me to go and fix it — a 20-minute
networking call, an alumni email, a campus event — before I send anything. Do not
paper over an absent connection with adjectives. A letter that has nothing true and
specific to say is worse than a late application.

### PHASE 3 — DRAFT
Pick the structure from the tier:

**Tiers 1–2 → bespoke narrative.** No "why firm / why role / why me" partitioning.
Open mid-story with the connection, let motivation emerge from the sequence of
events. This is the Optiver mode.

**Tiers 3–4 → three-paragraph standard, upgraded.**
- ¶1 Why this firm: lead with any real interaction. Otherwise lead with a specific
  observation about how they operate that only a close reader would make. Nothing
  in this paragraph may survive a find-and-replace of the firm's name.
- ¶2 Why this role: for niche desks, demonstrate you know the actual mechanics of
  the job. Use the A-B-C-D chain below to make the motivation logical rather than
  asserted.
- ¶3 Why me: brief, concrete, evidenced, no adjective stacking. Shortest paragraph.

**The A-B-C-D chain** — use it at least once, invisibly, in either structure:
  A: I did [experience]. B: I liked [trait] — a trait the target role *shares*.
  C: I disliked [trait] — a trait the target role *lacks*. D: which led me to
  [next step / this application].
Never label it. It should read as autobiography, not as a framework.

### VOICE AND FORM — match the specimen
- 380–520 words in the body. 5–7 paragraphs, each roughly 60–110 words. Under no
  circumstances exceed one page.
- First person, past-tense narrative. Assured but not swaggering. Some intellectual
  vocabulary is welcome ("exactitude", "revelatory") but at most two or three such
  words in the whole letter — beyond that it curdles.
- Vary sentence length hard. Long clause-stacked sentence, then a short flat one.
- Semicolons and em dashes are permitted and encouraged; the specimen uses both.
- Concessions make claims credible: "whilst trading was enjoyable and intellectually
  demanding, the skillset of an equity analyst aligns closer to mine". Include at
  least one honest admission of preference or limitation.
- Name a real person only if I have actually spoken to them, with title and firm in
  parentheses, and only paired with something specific they said.
- Numbers over adjectives. "Five internships" not "extensive experience".
- No bullet points. No headers. No bold. Continuous prose.

### BANNED — rewrite on sight
"I am writing to apply for", "I was excited to see", "leading/premier/prestigious",
"passionate about", "I believe I would be a great fit", "team player", "fast-paced
environment" as a standalone virtue, "align with your values", "collaborative
culture", "commitment to excellence/integrity/ESG/diversity" as a reason to apply,
"leverage my skills", "hit the ground running", "in today's dynamic market",
"delve", "tapestry", "testament to", "not only... but also", any tricolon of
abstract nouns, any sentence that restates my CV without explaining a decision.

### FORMAT (formal letter)
My name and contact block right-aligned at the top. Date below it on the left, in
DD Month YYYY. Firm's full postal address left-aligned beneath the date — verify
the correct office address in research. "Dear Sir/Madam," unless a named recruiter
is genuinely known, in which case "Dear Mr/Ms [Surname],". Sign off "Yours
faithfully," with Sir/Madam and "Yours sincerely," with a name. British English
throughout unless `profile.md` says otherwise.

### PHASE 4 — SELF-CRITIQUE, then revise before showing me
Run these tests on your own draft and fix what fails:
1. **The find-and-replace test.** Swap the firm's name for its closest competitor.
   Every sentence that still works is generic — cut or replace it. Paragraph 1 must
   fail this test completely.
2. **The invention audit.** List every factual claim about me and every claim about
   the firm. Each must trace to `profile.md`, my answers, or a source you found.
   Delete anything that traces to nothing. Never invent a conversation, a deal, a
   metric, or a person. This is non-negotiable — a fabricated detail found at
   interview ends the application.
3. **The "so what" pass.** For each sentence: does it advance the story of why I am
   applying here? If it only asserts a quality, cut it.
4. **The register check.** Read it against the Optiver specimen. Too formal, too
   eager, or too many long abstract words? Fix.
5. **Word count and one-page check.**

## DELIVER
1. The finished letter as `[firm]-[role]-cover-letter.md`, formatted and ready.
2. Below it, three short sections:
   - **Sources** — what you found and where, so I can verify before sending
   - **Unverified** — anything I must confirm is true, especially names and dates
   - **Weakest link** — the one sentence you'd cut if I had to lose fifty words,
     and what would strengthen the letter most if I went and got it
