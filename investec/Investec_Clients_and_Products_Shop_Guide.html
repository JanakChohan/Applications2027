Start here

The shop analogy

One picture that explains who Investec serves and what it sells.

Think of the big high-street banks as supermarkets. They sell standard products off the shelf, very cheaply, to millions of people. At the checkout a scanner, the credit scorecard, decides in seconds whether you get a loan. It works well for people with a normal salary.

Investec is a bespoke tailor in the same street. It serves people whose financial "body shape" does not fit standard sizes: pay that arrives as big bonuses, shares, a cut of a fund's profits, dividends from their own company, or dollars and euros. The scanner flags these as "unexpected items". The tailor measures them properly and makes something that fits.

The tailor's shop has two floors. On the ground floor it dresses the person (the Private Bank). Upstairs it equips the person's business (Corporate and Investment Banking, or CIB). The same tailor, the client's relationship manager, knows both, so a customer who comes in for one thing tends to stay for years and use both floors.

Part 1

Who the target clients are

Not just "rich people". A very specific kind of rich person.

Investec's published entry bar for the UK Private Bank is roughly £300,000 a year of income and over £3m of net worth, for UK residents Confirmed [S8]. Mortgages usually start at £1m Reported [S9]. But the real filter is not size of wealth. It is shape of income. Investec's own client pages group them like this [R, search extracts] [S12, S13, S39]:

Client typeWhy their money is "awkward"What they typically need first
Private equity partners and fund managersSalary plus bonus plus carried interest: a share of the fund's profits, paid years later and unpredictably. In Investec's survey, 43.5% said lenders struggle to understand their income Reported [S12]A large mortgage that counts the carry
Hedge fund partners and ownersPartnership profit shares and bonuses that swing year to year Reported [S6]A mortgage or a loan against property to fund their firm
Investment bankers, lawyers at US firmsBig bonuses, deferred shares (RSUs), pay in dollars Reported [S3, S5]A high loan-to-value mortgage; currency planning
Founders and business ownersWealth locked in their company; pay taken as dividends Reported [S1, S13]Speed and a lender that values the business
Tech executivesShares that vest over time; lump sums on leaving Reported [S5]Currency hedging and investment advice
International families moving to the UKAssets in foreign trusts and companies Reported [S9]A UK mortgage that recognises overseas wealth
UK expats in Dubai or SwitzerlandLive abroad, own UK property Reported [S16]Buy-to-let mortgages from £1m
Key termLoan-to-value (LTV): the loan as a share of the property's value. A £3m home with a £2.7m loan is 90% LTV. Most lenders cap large loans well below that.
The key filterIn 2018 the head of the UK Private Bank said: "We are not always especially useful to high net worth individuals who are simply looking to preserve their wealth" Reported [S36]. Investec wants people who are making money and will keep needing things, not people who just want to sit on it.

Part 2

What private banking actually means

Ground floor of the shop: the products, and what problem each one solves.

Private banking is not a mysterious service. At Investec it is mainly lending, with everyday banking and investment advice built around it. The single most important product is the mortgage: a loan secured on a home Reported [S9]. Everything else hangs off it.

Part 3

Why Investec chooses these clients

The business logic. Investec does not spell all of this out, so most of it is reasoning from the evidence.

ReasonHow it makes moneyEvidence
1. Less competitionThe scanner banks say no or lend too little, so Investec competes with a handful of private banks, not the whole market. That supports better pricing InferredInvestec does "not use standard credit scorecards" Reported [S9]
2. Good credit risk despite the "awkward" incomeClients are wealthy, loans are paid down from bonuses, and LTVs fall over time InferredCase studies with planned paydowns, e.g. 90% to 65% Reported [S6, S1]
3. One sale leads to manyA mortgage leads to deposits, FX on foreign pay, accounts and advice InferredRM adverts target "wallet share across lending, FX, investments, wealth management" Confirmed [S33]
4. Hard to leaveA banker who understands your carried interest is hard to replace InferredLong-standing client in the tech-executive story Reported [S5]
5. Cheap, stable fundingWealthy clients leave large deposits, which fund Investec's lending InferredStrategy to become the "primary bank" Reported [S17, S37]
6. Tomorrow's business clientsToday's founder or PE partner brings their company or fund upstairs InferredCorporate RMs must partner "with Private Client teams when personal and business needs intersect" Confirmed [S32]
The catchThe trade-off: tailoring is expensive. Investec's UK cost-to-income ratio is 54% Confirmed [S34], far above lean, computer-led lenders. The bet is that deeper, longer relationships pay for the extra people.

How the two floors connect

The investment bank and the private bank serve the same people at different moments.

Connection 1: the private equity ecosystem

Investec lends to private equity funds (Fund Solutions), to the partners personally for their own stake in the fund (GP financing) and with mortgages that count their carried interest, and to the companies those funds buy Reported [S12, S14, S24]. A dated but clear example: in 2011 Investec lent £14m to the managers of Clyde Blowers Capital so they could invest more of their own money in their £350m fund Reported [S15].

How long does a relationship last?

  • Corporate broking has no end date. It is a retainer that runs for years and produces extra fees on each share sale or bid. Cohort shows one broking relationship producing a share sale and a takeover mandate in a single deal Confirmed [S19].
  • Lending repeats. AELF came back for a second aircraft deal Reported [S29]. Fund managers return for each new fund (Inferred).
  • Mortgages run 5 to 25 years, and many are interest-only with planned paydowns, so the banker stays in touch through each bonus round Reported [S1, S3, S4].
  • Investec holds most loans to maturity rather than selling them on, "thereby developing a 'hands-on' and long-standing relationship" Confirmed [S35].
  • No published figure for average client tenure exists.

What links the client to the product, and why

The single idea that ties the whole model together.

Across every product, the link is the same: a person at Investec understands the client's situation better than a computer or a generalist would, and that understanding is what lets Investec say yes, price the risk, and then offer the next thing.

Why they do it this way

  • It is where the margin is. Standard products are a price war. Complex clients pay for fit Inferred.
  • Their pay system rewards it. Bonus pools come from profit after a charge for the capital used, not from sales volume, and loans are approved by committees needing unanimous consent Confirmed [S35]. That favours careful, repeat business with known clients.
  • It is a strategy, not an accident. Investec says "We do not seek to be all things to all people" and focuses on "select market niches". Its 2026 plan pushes both floors further: a full private bank and a corporate bank that feels like private banking [C/R] [S34, S37].
Say this in an interview"Investec targets people whose wealth is real but awkward, like PE partners paid in carry or founders paid in dividends. It wins them with a mortgage a scoring machine would refuse, then adds accounts, currency and advice. Upstairs it lends to and advises their funds and companies, from fund finance to M&A. One banker knows both sides, so a single loan can turn into a relationship that lasts the client's whole career."
