## 1. FINDINGS

### 4A. UK banking from scratch

#### 4A.1 What a bank actually does (plain English)
- **It takes deposits** from savers and businesses. Deposits are the bank's main *liability* (money it owes).
- **It lends** that money out as mortgages, business loans and so on. Loans are the bank's main *assets*.
- **It earns the spread.** Interest received on loans minus interest paid on deposits and other funding is **net interest income (NII)**. Divide NII by average interest-earning assets and you get the **net interest margin (NIM)**. For most UK banks this is the biggest revenue line. [Background]
- **It earns fees and commissions** from advice (M&A, ECM), arranging loans, wealth management fees on assets under management, FX and hedging products for clients, and card or payment fees.
- **It manages risk.** The four main risks are:
  - **Credit risk**: borrowers don't repay.
  - **Liquidity risk**: depositors want money back faster than loans mature.
  - **Interest-rate risk**: rates move against the bank's mix of fixed and floating positions.
  - **Operational and conduct risk**: systems fail or customers are mis-treated (motor finance is a live example).
- **It holds capital (equity)** as a loss-absorbing buffer. Regulation says how much capital a bank needs per £ of *risk-weighted* assets. That is why "capital-light" fee income and "capital-efficient" lending matter so much. [Background]
- **Why rates matter.** When Bank Rate rises, banks usually earn more on assets faster than they pass rises on to savers, so NIM widens. When rates fall, NIM tends to compress. Big banks dampen this with **structural hedges**, which are swaps that lock in rates on stable deposits. At Barclays and Lloyds, structural-hedge income was reported as about 37% of NII in Q1 2025. [Reported, S205]

#### 4A.3 Private banking vs wealth management vs retail
- **Retail banking** is the mass market: current accounts, savings, mortgages, cards, with standardised products at scale.
- **Private banking** serves wealthier individuals with *balance-sheet* products: bespoke mortgages and lending (for example against property or a share portfolio), deposits, FX. It is relationship-managed. The bank earns NII.
- **Wealth management** manages and advises on investments (discretionary portfolios, financial planning). It is *fee*-based (a % of AUM) and capital-light.
- Many firms join the two up as "private bank + wealth". In Investec's UK model, private banking stays inside the bank and wealth sits mostly in Rathbones. [Inferred/Background]

#### 4A.4 Corporate & investment banking for the mid-market — product glossary
- **Specialist lending**: loans to particular sectors or structures that big banks avoid or price poorly (for example healthcare, technology, aviation, power and infrastructure).
- **Leveraged / acquisition finance**: debt used by private-equity sponsors to buy companies, with high debt relative to earnings (EBITDA). Private credit funds compete hardest here. In UK sponsor-backed mid-market deals (loans below about £500m), direct lenders reportedly financed 68% of PE-backed companies in 2023, compared with 30% in 2016. [Reported, S204 — treat as indicative]
- **Fund finance**: lending *to funds*, for example subscription lines secured on investors' commitments, or NAV loans secured on a fund's portfolio. This lets banks serve the private-markets boom rather than fight it. [Background/Inferred]
- **Real estate lending**: loans secured on commercial or residential investment property and development.
- **Asset finance**: leasing and hire-purchase so businesses can buy vehicles, machinery and equipment.
- **Advisory / M&A**: fee income for advising on buying or selling companies.
- **ECM (equity capital markets)**: helping companies raise equity through IPOs and placings.
- **Corporate broking**: a UK speciality. The bank is the listed company's ongoing link to investors, giving advice and running fundraisings.
- **Treasury / hedging solutions**: FX forwards and options, interest-rate swaps and caps sold to corporate and private clients to manage risk.
- **The bank's own Treasury** (a separate function): manages the bank's liquidity, funding (deposits, wholesale issuance), interest-rate risk in the banking book and capital.

#### 4A.6 Structural forces reshaping UK banking (2025–2026)
1. **Deposit competition.** Savers moved from current accounts into fixed-term and easy-access savings. Challengers and digital banks pay up for deposits. The FSCS limit rose to £120k on 1 December 2025, which makes it easier for smaller banks to attract larger balances [Inferred]. The cash ISA limit for under-65s falls to £12,000 from 6 April 2027 (the overall £20k ISA limit is unchanged). That nudges savings from cash into investments, which matters to deposit-funded banks [Reported, S198].
2. **Private credit taking share.** The FSB (6 May 2026) estimates nonbank direct lending to mid-sized firms at **$1.5–2tn globally (end-2024)**, with the UK the third-largest market after the US and the euro area. It also notes bank credit lines to private credit funds of **about $220bn** in member data, "could be more than twice as large" [Confirmed, S188]. The BoE estimates UK banks have **£173bn** of banking-book exposures to private-market funds and sponsor-backed leveraged corporates, about 8% of wholesale committed limits [Reported, S179 search snippet; not found on the FSR summary page fetched]. The BoE is running a **system-wide exploratory scenario (SWES) on private markets**, with the scenario published in June 2026 [Confirmed for the SWES, S178/S179; date Reported, S202].
3. **Consolidation of mid-tier banks.**
   - Nationwide completed its acquisition of Virgin Money on **1 October 2024** (about £2.9bn).
   - Barclays completed Tesco Bank on **1 November 2024**.
   - Coventry Building Society completed The Co-operative Bank with effect from **1 January 2025** (combined assets about £89bn).
   - Santander UK agreed to buy **TSB** from Sabadell for **£2.65bn (1 July 2025)**. Completion was reported around **1 May 2026** after PRA and ECB approval.
   [Reported, S193–S196; completion date for TSB Reported only.]
   - Shawbrook listed on the LSE on **30 October 2025** [Reported, S201].
   - Pollen Street approached Metro Bank in June 2025. **The outcome was not found**, see Gaps.
   - EY (July 2025): mid-tier "M&A across this segment is poised to accelerate, once we have some economic and geopolitical stability" [Confirmed, S187].
4. **Mid-tier profitability squeeze.** EY found the challenger/specialist cohort's ROE fell to **6.9% in 2024, from 8.8%**. Specialist banks were at 5.5%, and the cost-to-income ratio rose to 63.2% [Confirmed, S187]. S&P/ION: "The UK has a surprisingly large amount of smaller, challenger and mid-tier banks, with many struggling to differentiate themselves" [Confirmed quote in S192].
5. **Mid-tier banks matter for SMEs.** Small and mid-tier banks provide about **60% of SME lending** (a doubling over a decade) and £35bn of SME funding in 2023 [Confirmed, S186]. The British Business Bank reported the 60% share was unchanged in 2025, with gross SME bank lending of £68bn [Reported, S200].
6. **Fintech, digital and AI.** Digital banks grew loans 25.1% in 2024 [Confirmed, S187]. Deloitte's 2026 outlook says banks must scale AI from pilots to enterprise strategies, with "agentic AI" dependent on "AI-ready data". It also expects nonbank competition "especially in the middle-market segment" [Reported, S191].
7. **The wealth transfer.** Kings Court Trust and Cebr estimate about **£5.5tn** will pass between generations in the UK over 30 years [Reported, S199]. This drives demand for private banking and wealth advice.
8. **The UK growth agenda: Mansion House and the Leeds Reforms (15 July 2025).** The first **Financial Services Growth & Competitiveness Strategy** aims to make the UK "the number one destination for financial services companies by 2035". Its strands include regulatory streamlining, capital changes to unlock lending, a ring-fencing review, SM&CR reform, and boosting retail investment [Reported, S197]. On the same day the BoE/PRA announced Basel 3.1 dates, higher MREL thresholds and an IRB discussion paper to help mid-sized banks [Confirmed, S158].
9. **London listings and ECM.**
   - New UK Listing Rules took effect on **29 July 2024**. Premium and standard listing were merged into one **ESCC** category [Reported, S181; multiple law-firm sources consistent].
   - **PISCES**, a private-company share trading sandbox: SI 2025/583 came into force on 5 June 2025, the sandbox opened on **10 June 2025** (FCA PS25/6), and it runs to June 2030 [Reported, S182].
   - IPOs: only 2 UK IPOs in Q1 2026, but **7 in H1 2026 raising £577.2m**, up 215% on H1 2025's £183m [Reported, S183].
   - For a corporate broker or ECM team this is "green shoots after a drought". It matters directly to Investec's broking and ECM franchise [Inferred].
10. **Lending outlook.** EY ITEM Club (autumn 2026, reported 1 October 2026) forecasts total bank lending growth of 3.6% (2025), 2.9% (2026) and 2.2% (2027), "a three-year low". Corporate lending is forecast at 5.3%, 2.1% and 2.8% [Reported via The Intermediary, S185]. The February 2026 EY forecast had business lending at 6.9% in 2025, slowing to 3.5% in 2026 [Reported, S184]. The definitions differ between the two releases.

#### 4A.7 Where does UK mid-market / specialist banking go in 3–5 years? Competing views
- **View A: "Private credit eats the mid-market."** Post-crisis capital rules, Basel 3.1, plus faster and more flexible non-bank lenders keep pulling sponsor-backed and riskier lending off bank balance sheets.
  - Evidence: FSB (S188) cites "changes to post-crisis bank regulation" and borrower demand for "tailored financing solutions and fast execution". McKinsey names four asset classes where private credit could gain share: asset-backed finance, infrastructure/project finance, certain residential mortgages and higher-risk CRE (S190). Deloitte expects competition "especially in the middle-market segment" (S191).
  - Implication: banks shrink in leveraged lending and must win on relationships, speed and ancillary products.
- **View B: "Frenemies — banks become the plumbing and partners of private credit."** Banks lend *to* funds (fund finance, NAV and subscription lines), sell risk to them (significant risk transfers), and form origination partnerships.
  - Evidence: McKinsey describes banks "partnering" with private credit and using funds for risk transfer to free capital (S189/S190). The FSB records growing bank–asset manager partnerships and about $220bn+ of bank credit lines to funds (S188).
  - Implication: specialist banks with fund-finance and sponsor relationships (an Investec strength [Inferred]) can capture fee-rich, lower-risk-weight business.
  - The *risk side* of this view: the BoE FPC and FSB both flag private credit as "untested to a prolonged economic downturn" (S188), and the BoE launched a private-markets SWES (S178/S179). A credit event could quickly tighten bank appetite.
- **View C: "Regulatory tailwinds and consolidation favour scaled mid-tier specialists."**
  - Evidence:
    - Basel 3.1 SME and infrastructure adjustments, designed so that removing support factors "does not cause an increase in overall capital requirements" (S157).
    - MREL thresholds raised to £25–40bn of assets (S158/S164).
    - Strong & Simple for banks below £20bn (S159).
    - The FPC cut its system Tier 1 capital benchmark from about 14% to about 13% of RWAs (S178).
    - An IRB discussion paper to help mid-sized banks (S158).
    - EY argues mid-tier banks are already the main SME lenders (S186) and predicts M&A will accelerate (S187).
  - Implication: a mid-tier group that reaches scale, moves to IRB models (Investec says it is "on its journey" to IRB, S174) and diversifies into fees wins. Sub-scale players sell to or merge with others (TSB, Virgin, Co-op, Tesco Bank already gone).
- **View D (bear case for margins): "Lower-for-longer margins and cost inflation squeeze the middle."**
  - Evidence: EY data shows ROE falling and costs rising across the cohort (S187). Scope and S&P note margin pressure and deposit competition, while big banks have structural hedges and scale (S205, S192). EY ITEM sees lending growth slowing to 2.2% in 2027 (S185).
  - Counterpoint: the BoE's 2026 pause and possible hikes (S150) delay margin compression.
- **My synthesis for interview [Inferred].** The likeliest 3–5-year path combines B and C. Banks keep origination, relationships, hedging/FX and fund finance, and partner with or distribute to private credit for the riskiest tranches. Mid-tier banks consolidate. Winners are niche specialists with low-cost sticky deposits (private clients), IRB capital efficiency and fee streams.

#### 4A.8 What internal clients and the business value from a junior analyst today [Inferred from role norms + S203]
- **Credit (corporate or private bank lending)**
  - Spread financial statements into the bank's model and calculate leverage (debt/EBITDA), interest cover and loan-to-value.
  - Draft credit papers ("what is the deal, why lend, what can go wrong, how are we protected": security, covenants), and run downside cases.
  - Monitor covenants and portfolio watchlists.
  - What they value: accuracy, judgement about downside risk, clear writing, and speed under deal timetables.
- **Private banking**
  - Prepare client meeting packs and analyse a client's assets and income for a mortgage or lending request.
  - Support onboarding and KYC (know-your-customer, anti-money-laundering), and track deposit and pricing information.
  - What they value: client empathy, attention to detail on compliance, and Consumer Duty "good outcomes" thinking.
- **Treasury (bank's own) / client treasury solutions**
  - Monitor liquidity ratios (LCR/NSFR) and deposit flows, and support funding plans and interest-rate risk reports.
  - On the client side, price simple FX forwards and interest-rate hedges and explain them to clients.
  - What they value: numeracy, an understanding of how Bank Rate moves affect NII, and an understanding of regulatory metrics.
- **Across all three:** Excel and modelling, clear one-page summaries, curiosity about the macro picture (rates, private credit), and awareness of conduct (motor finance shows the cost of getting it wrong).
- Investec's UK graduate programme is reported as 18 months with rotations across Wealth & Investment, Private Bank and Corporate & Institutional Banking, including front-office and operations placements [Reported, S203; Investec careers page returned 403].

### 4B. Regulation relevant to a UK specialist bank

#### 4B.1 Who regulates: the "twin peaks" dual regulation
- **PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority)** is part of the Bank of England. It regulates *safety and soundness*: capital, liquidity and governance of banks, building societies and insurers.
- **FCA (Financial Conduct Authority)** regulates *conduct*: fair treatment of customers (Consumer Duty), market integrity, listing rules, consumer credit including motor finance.
- **BoE Financial Policy Committee (FPC)** handles system-wide (macroprudential) policy: countercyclical buffer, stress tests, the Tier 1 benchmark.
- **BoE Resolution** handles MREL and resolution plans.
- **HM Treasury** writes the primary and secondary legislation (FSMA 2000, FSMA 2023, ring-fencing orders).
- Dual regulation began on **1 April 2013**, under the Financial Services Act 2012 [Background].

#### 4B.2 Basel 3.1 — UK implementation [Confirmed]
- **PS1/26** "Implementation of Basel 3.1: Final rules" was published on **20 January 2026** [S157].
- **In force 1 January 2027**, except the **internal model approach (IMA) for market risk, which applies from 1 January 2028** [S157, S158]. The 2028 IMA date was set on 15 July 2025, citing "continued uncertainty over the timing of the implementation of the Basel 3.1 market risk framework in some other jurisdictions" [Reported wording, S158/S160].
- CP9/26 (June 2026) consults on adjustments to the market-risk IMA [Reported, S160].
- **Output floor:** IRB banks' RWAs cannot fall below 72.5% of standardised RWAs. It is phased in to 2030 [Reported, S157 search].
- SME and infrastructure adjustments ensure that "removal of existing Pillar 1 support factors does not cause an increase in overall capital requirements" [Confirmed paraphrase-quote, S157].
- With dates aligned, "the Interim Capital Regime is no longer required" [Confirmed, S157].
- Earlier history [Background/Reported]: near-final PS17/23 (December 2023) and PS9/24 (September 2024). The originally planned 1 January 2026 start was pushed to 1 January 2027 in an announcement in January 2025.
- **Relevance to Investec [Inferred].** Investec plc reports on the **standardised approach** (CET1 13.0% at FY2026) and is moving toward IRB [Confirmed, S174]. Basel 3.1's revised standardised risk weights, the output floor and IRB permission all feed directly into its capital efficiency.

#### 4B.3 Ring-fencing [Confirmed, S161]
- **Origin:** Vickers Independent Commission on Banking (2011), then the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013. In force 1 January 2019 [Background].
- **Threshold:** the core deposit threshold went from **£25bn to £35bn**, via SI 2025/30, **in force 4 February 2025**. HMT: "In February 2025, the government took a decisive step forward by raising the threshold from £25 billion to £35 billion as part of the Smarter Ring-Fencing Reforms."
- **Ring-Fencing Review "Safeguarding Stability, Enabling Growth" (HMT, May 2026; reported 18 May 2026):**
  - The threshold is kept at £35bn and reviewed every 3 years, first in **Q2 2028**.
  - New **Growth Allowance**: ring-fenced banks may do otherwise-prohibited activities up to **10% of Pillar 1 credit-risk RWAs**, potentially unlocking "up to £80 billion" of financing.
  - More sharing of operational resources across the fence.
  - Delivered via the forthcoming **Financial Services and Markets Bill** announced in the King's Speech.
  - Five groups have ring-fenced bodies: Barclays, HSBC, Santander, NatWest, Lloyds.
- **Relevance:** Investec is far below £35bn of core deposits, so it is not ring-fenced. Ring-fencing reforms help its big competitors lend more to corporates and SMEs [Inferred].

#### 4B.4 "Strong and Simple" regime for smaller banks [Confirmed, S159]
- It applies to **Small Domestic Deposit Takers (SDDTs)**. The main test is "Total assets on average over the past three years of no more than £20 billion". Other conditions: at least 75% UK credit exposures, no IRB models, limited trading book, no non-UK parent, and so on.
- Liquidity and disclosure simplifications came through PS15/23 (December 2023), with opt-in from 1 January 2024 and liquidity changes from 1 July 2024.
- Near-final capital regime PS20/25 (28 October 2025); final **PS4/26 (20 January 2026)**. **The simplified capital regime applies from 1 January 2027.**
- **Investec is too large and uses/aims for IRB, so it is not an SDDT** [Inferred]. Many specialist competitors could be SDDTs.

#### 4B.5 MREL (loss-absorbing debt for resolution) [Confirmed S158; detail Reported S164]
- BoE Statement of Policy published **15 July 2025**, **effective 1 January 2026**.
- Indicative thresholds raised from **£15–25bn to £25–40bn of total assets**. Below £25bn a bank is generally expected to go into modified insolvency, with no MREL above capital. Above £40bn a bank generally faces a preferred bail-in strategy.
- Thresholds will be updated every 3 years from 2028.
- The resolution assessment threshold was raised from **£50bn to £100bn of retail deposits**.

#### 4B.6 Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SM&CR) reforms
- SM&CR has applied to banks since **7 March 2016** [Background].
- **15 July 2025**: HMT, PRA and FCA consulted on reform, aiming to cut the burden by about 50%. Proposals included removing the Certification Regime from FSMA (sections 63E/63F) and having fewer pre-approved senior manager roles. Consultation closed 7 October 2025 [Reported, S166].
- **22 April 2026**: the FCA and PRA confirmed a "first wave" [Confirmed, S165]:
  - About 15% fewer certification roles.
  - Enhanced-firm thresholds raised 30%.
  - Longer timelines for applications, criminal record checks and directory updates.
  - A proposed 2-month approval deadline (99.7% of FCA cases already met the 3-month deadline).
  - HMT confirmed it will remove the Certification Regime from legislation [Reported, S166 snippet].
  - Exact commencement per measure: some sources say 24 April 2026 [Reported], see Gaps.

#### 4B.7 FCA Consumer Duty [Confirmed, S167, S168]
- PS22/9 published **27 July 2022**.
- In force **31 July 2023** for open products and services, and **31 July 2024** for closed products and services.
- Core rule: "A firm must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers."
- **Four outcomes:** products & services; price & value; consumer understanding; consumer support.
- **Three cross-cutting rules:** act in good faith; avoid foreseeable harm; enable and support customers to pursue their financial objectives.
- Relevance: Investec's private clients are retail customers under the Duty. Fair value of savings rates is a live topic [Inferred].

#### 4B.8 Motor finance discretionary commission saga
- **28 January 2021**: the FCA ban on discretionary commission arrangements (DCAs) took effect [Background].
- **11 January 2024**: the FCA paused complaint handling on DCAs [Background].
- **25 October 2024**: the Court of Appeal ruled in *Johnson v FirstRand* against the lenders [Background].
- **1 August 2025**: the **Supreme Court** (*Hopcraft v Close Brothers; Johnson v FirstRand*) ruled that dealers were not fiduciaries, so the bribery and fiduciary claims failed. **But** it upheld Mr Johnson's claim of an **unfair relationship under s.140A Consumer Credit Act 1974** [Reported, S173 — consistent across multiple law firms].
- **7 October 2025: CP25/27** [Reported, S171]:
  - Agreements from **6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024**.
  - About 14.2m agreements (44%).
  - £8.2bn redress plus £2.8bn costs, total about **£11bn**.
  - Average about £700. Responses due 18 November 2025.
- **30 March 2026: PS26/3 final rules** [Confirmed, S169]:
  - **12.1m** eligible agreements; redress **£7.5bn** (assuming 75% uptake); non-redress costs £1.6bn; **total £9.1bn**.
  - Average about **£829** per agreement (the policy statement gives about £830).
  - Implementation periods end **30 June 2026** (loans from 1 April 2014) and **31 August 2026** (earlier loans). Most compensation is expected by **end-2027**.
  - Full commission plus interest goes to about 90,000 "Johnson-like" high-commission cases. Others get a hybrid remedy [Reported, S170].
- **1–2 July 2026: the Upper Tribunal partially suspended the scheme** [Confirmed, S172]:
  - Challengers: Consumer Voice (via Courmacs Legal), Volkswagen FS, Mercedes-Benz FS, Crédit Agricole/CA Auto Finance.
  - Lenders need not calculate or pay redress until the challenges are decided. Other rules still apply.
  - **Hearing 14–18 December 2026 or 16–26 February 2027.**
- **Investec's exposure** [Confirmed, S174]: "Based on the scheme as currently proposed, the Group has concluded that its existing £30 million provision, covering both redress and associated operational costs, remains appropriate" (FY2026 results, May 2026). CEO Fani Titi said exposure is small and the provisions conservative [Reported, S175].

#### 4B.9 Bank of England stress tests and capital framework
- From 2025 the main **Bank Capital Stress Test is biennial (odd years)**, so the next is due in **2027** [Reported, S180].
- **2025 BCST** (results 2 December 2025) covered 7 banks: Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Nationwide, NatWest, Santander UK, Standard Chartered. Aggregate CET1 went from **14.5% to a low of 11.0%**, and no bank needed to strengthen capital [Confirmed, S178].
- **2 December 2025:** the FPC cut its benchmark for system-wide Tier 1 requirements from "around 14%" to "around 13%" of RWAs (CET1 about 11%). It was the first reduction since the post-crisis framework [Confirmed, S178].
- **UK CCyB held at 2%** (neutral) in both the December 2025 and July 2026 FSRs [Confirmed, S178, S179].
- **July 2026 FSR (7 July 2026):** "The UK banking system remains appropriately capitalised with high levels of liquidity", and the system "is able to absorb a severe energy price shock" [Confirmed, S179].
- Investec is **not** in the BCST set. It runs its own ICAAP stress tests under PRA supervision [Inferred/Background].

#### 4B.10 Deposit protection [Confirmed, S176]
- **From 1 December 2025, FSCS deposit protection rose from £85,000 to £120,000** per depositor per authorised firm.
- Temporary high balances are protected up to **£1.4m** for up to six months.
- The PRA had consulted on £110k and settled on £120k given inflation data and feedback; HMT approved [Reported, S177].

#### 4B.11 UK capital markets reforms
- **UK Listing Rules**: published 11 July 2024, **in force 29 July 2024**. A single ESCC category replaced premium/standard, with fewer shareholder votes on transactions [Reported, S181].
- **PISCES**: SI 2025/583 in force 5 June 2025, sandbox from 10 June 2025 (PS25/6), running to 2030. The first operator was approved around September 2025 [Reported, S182].
- **Stamp-duty (SDRT) exemption for newly listed companies**: cited as a driver of the 2026 IPO recovery [Reported, S183]. The exact Budget measure was not verified.

## 6. INTERVIEW ANGLES
1. **"Explain how a bank makes money."** Deposits in, loans out, NIM, fees, capital. Then link it: "with Bank Rate at 3.75% and the MPC now split towards hikes (6–3 in September), margin pressure from the 2024–25 cutting cycle has paused, but credit quality in leveraged and real-estate books is the thing to watch."
2. **"Why a specialist bank rather than a big bank?"** Not ring-fenced, so more flexible. Mid-market focus, where EY shows mid-tier banks provide ~60% of SME lending. Private-client deposits. The chance to see a deal end to end.
3. **"What's the biggest threat to Investec's corporate lending?"** Private credit ($1.5–2tn globally, FSB). Answer with the frenemy point: fund finance and partnerships. Add the BoE/FSB warning that private credit is "untested to a prolonged economic downturn".
4. **"Pick a regulatory change and its impact."** Basel 3.1 on 1 January 2027: standardised risk-weight changes, the output floor, and the SME/infrastructure adjustments. Investec reports on the standardised approach and is moving to IRB, so this is directly about capital efficiency and the ability to price competitively.
5. **"What is the motor finance issue?"** Supreme Court 1 August 2025 (unfair relationship under s.140A CCA), FCA scheme £9.1bn (PS26/3, 30 March 2026), Upper Tribunal partial suspension July 2026. Investec holds a £30m provision and its exposure is small. Lesson: conduct risk and the Consumer Duty.
6. **"What's happening in UK banking M&A?"** Nationwide–Virgin (Oct 2024), Barclays–Tesco Bank (Nov 2024), Coventry–Co-op (Jan 2025), Santander–TSB (£2.65bn). Scale is needed for tech and regulatory costs, and MREL threshold rises (£25–40bn) reduce the penalty for growing.
7. **"How is the government trying to grow UK finance?"** Leeds Reforms (15 July 2025): ring-fencing Growth Allowance (10% of RWAs, up to £80bn), SM&CR streamlining, listing rules and PISCES, cash ISA reform. The IPO rebound in H1 2026 matters to Investec's ECM and corporate broking.

## GLOSSARY (55 terms, plain English)
1. **Bank Rate**: the Bank of England's main interest rate (3.75% now). It drives what banks charge and pay.
2. **MPC (Monetary Policy Committee)**: the 9-person BoE committee that sets Bank Rate to hit 2% inflation.
3. **CPI inflation**: how fast consumer prices are rising. The target is 2%.
4. **Deposit**: money customers leave at the bank. The bank owes it back.
5. **Loan book**: all the loans a bank has made.
6. **Net interest income (NII)**: interest earned minus interest paid.
7. **Net interest margin (NIM)**: NII as a % of interest-earning assets. It measures the "spread".
8. **Non-interest / fee income**: revenue from fees (advice, wealth management, FX) rather than lending.
9. **Cost-to-income ratio**: costs ÷ revenue. Lower is more efficient.
10. **ROE / ROTE**: return on (tangible) equity, i.e. profit ÷ shareholders' capital. Investec UK Specialist Bank ROTE was 13.7% in FY2026.
11. **Impairments / expected credit loss (ECL)**: money set aside for loans that may not be repaid.
12. **Provision**: an amount set aside for a likely future cost, for example Investec's £30m for motor finance.
13. **Capital (equity)**: shareholders' money that absorbs losses before depositors suffer.
14. **CET1 (Common Equity Tier 1)**: the highest-quality capital, mainly shares and retained profits.
15. **CET1 ratio**: CET1 ÷ risk-weighted assets. Investec plc is at 13.0%.
16. **Tier 1 / Tier 2 / AT1**: layers of capital. AT1 bonds can convert to equity or be written down in stress.
17. **Risk-weighted assets (RWAs)**: assets adjusted for riskiness. A risky loan counts for more.
18. **Standardised approach**: regulator-set risk weights.
19. **IRB (Internal Ratings-Based) approach**: the bank uses its own approved models for risk weights. Often lower capital for good-quality books.
20. **Output floor**: under Basel 3.1, model-based RWAs cannot fall below 72.5% of standardised RWAs.
21. **Basel III / Basel 3.1**: international bank capital rules. "3.1" is the UK's final package, from 1 January 2027.
22. **Leverage ratio**: capital ÷ total (un-weighted) exposures. A backstop to RWAs.
23. **Countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB)**: extra capital the FPC can raise in good times and release in bad. The UK rate is 2%.
24. **Pillar 1 / Pillar 2A / Pillar 2B**: minimum capital, firm-specific add-ons, and a stress buffer.
25. **ICAAP**: a bank's own assessment of how much capital it needs.
26. **Liquidity**: having cash or easily-sold assets to pay depositors.
27. **LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)**: enough liquid assets to survive 30 days of stress.
28. **NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio)**: long-term assets funded by stable funding.
29. **Structural hedge**: swaps that lock in interest on stable deposits to smooth NII.
30. **Wholesale funding**: money borrowed from markets (bonds, interbank) rather than customer deposits.
31. **MREL**: extra loss-absorbing debt bigger banks must hold so they can be "bailed in" instead of bailed out.
32. **Bail-in**: converting a failing bank's debt into equity to recapitalise it.
33. **Resolution**: the BoE's process for handling a failing bank without taxpayer money.
34. **Ring-fencing**: big UK banks (core deposits above £35bn) must separate retail banking from riskier investment banking.
35. **SDDT (Small Domestic Deposit Taker)**: a small UK bank (at most £20bn assets) eligible for the simpler "Strong and Simple" rules.
36. **PRA**: Prudential Regulation Authority (part of the BoE). Keeps banks safe and sound.
37. **FCA**: Financial Conduct Authority. Polices conduct, consumer protection and markets.
38. **FPC**: the BoE's Financial Policy Committee, which looks after whole-system stability.
39. **Stress test**: a hypothetical severe-recession scenario to check banks keep enough capital.
40. **FSCS**: the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Protects deposits up to £120,000 per person per bank.
41. **Consumer Duty**: the FCA rule (2023/2024) that firms must deliver good outcomes for retail customers.
42. **SM&CR**: the Senior Managers & Certification Regime, which makes named senior people accountable.
43. **KYC / AML**: know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering checks.
44. **Discretionary commission arrangement (DCA)**: a car dealer could raise the customer's interest rate to earn more commission. Banned in January 2021.
45. **Redress**: compensation paid to customers who were treated unfairly.
46. **Private banking**: lending, deposits and services for wealthy individuals, relationship-managed.
47. **Wealth management**: managing and advising on clients' investments for a fee (% of AUM).
48. **AUM / FUM**: assets (funds) under management.
49. **Mid-market**: companies too big for "small business" banking but below large corporates. Roughly £10m–£500m+ turnover (definitions vary).
50. **Leveraged finance**: lending to highly indebted companies, often to fund private-equity buyouts.
51. **Private equity (PE) sponsor**: a fund that buys companies, usually with borrowed money.
52. **Private credit / direct lending**: loans made by investment funds rather than banks.
53. **Fund finance**: loans to investment funds, e.g. subscription lines (secured on investor commitments) and NAV loans (secured on fund assets).
54. **Real estate / CRE lending**: loans secured on commercial or investment property. LTV (loan-to-value) measures risk.
55. **Asset finance**: leasing or hire-purchase to fund equipment and vehicles.
56. **Covenant**: a promise in a loan agreement (e.g. debt below 4x EBITDA). Breaching it lets the lender act.
57. **EBITDA**: earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. A rough cash-profit measure used in lending.
58. **ECM (Equity Capital Markets)**: helping companies raise money by selling shares (IPOs, placings).
59. **IPO**: initial public offering, a company's first listing on a stock exchange.
60. **Corporate broker**: the adviser that links a listed company to its investors and runs its share issues.
61. **DCM (Debt Capital Markets)**: helping companies issue bonds.
62. **ESCC**: the single UK listing category for commercial companies since 29 July 2024.
63. **PISCES**: the UK sandbox for periodic trading of private-company shares (since June 2025).
64. **Challenger bank**: a newer or smaller bank competing with the big five on mainstream products.
65. **Building society**: a mutual owned by its members (savers and borrowers) rather than shareholders.
66. **Significant risk transfer (SRT)**: a bank sells the credit risk of a loan portfolio to investors to free capital.
