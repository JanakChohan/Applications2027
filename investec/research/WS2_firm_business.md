## 1. Findings (A–I)

### A. Founding insight, founders, design, purpose and values

- **Founding.** Founded in Johannesburg in 1974 (some sources say 1974/75) as "Investors, Technical and Executors", a small leasing and finance company [Reported, S83; S85].
  - Founder lists vary by source. BusinessTech names five founders: Ian Kantor, Bernard Kantor, Larry Nestadt, the late Errol Grolman and Stephen Koseff [S83]. Wikipedia names Nestadt, Grolman and Ian Kantor as the 1974 founders [S85].
  - The safe phrasing: *"founded in 1974 by a small group led by Ian Kantor; Bernard Kantor joined in 1978 and Stephen Koseff in 1980."*
- **What the founders did before.**
  - **Ian Kantor:** BSc Electrical Engineering (Pretoria); MBA at UCT GSB in 1969; worked at IBM; lectured in business finance at Wits. He came from **Lease Plan International**, and leasing experience sparked the idea. He was CEO until 1984, chair until 2002 and a non-executive director until 2020. He later led Bank Insinger de Beaufort in the Netherlands [Reported, S83].
  - **Bernard Kantor:** previously at CCAP Investments. Joined in 1978 and was managing director until he retired in 2018 [Reported, S83].
  - **Stephen Koseff:** chartered accountant (qualified 1975); professional assistant at Alex Baillie Koseff & Wheeler from 1976 to 1980. Joined in 1980, when the firm had about 8 people. He was group CEO until 2018 [Reported, search snippet S84 plus BusinessTech S83].
- **Key milestones.**
  - **Banking licence:** 1980 [Reported, S83; S85].
  - **JSE listing:** BusinessTech says 1988 [S83]. Wikipedia says 1986, after a merger with Metboard [S85]. *Discrepancy: say "mid-1980s" or check before quoting.*
  - **UK entry:** 1992 [Reported, S83]. Guinness Mahon and Hambros were bought in 1998 [Reported, S85].
  - **Dual-listed company (DLC):** created **July 2002**. Investec plc is listed on the LSE and Investec Limited on the JSE. Wikipedia describes it as the first South African company to list on both exchanges this way [Reported, S85; FY26 RNS confirms the DLC with primary listings in London and Johannesburg, S70].
  - **Asset management demerged:** March 2020, listed as **Ninety One** [Reported, S85; S83].
- **How the founders shaped the culture.** Ian Kantor describes a founding practice in which each founder had a veto. In his words: *"This thing of 'you can do anything you like as long as there is consensus' became the basis of Investec's culture."* [Reported quote via BusinessTech, 5 Feb 2025, S83.]
  - [Inferred] Three habits trace back to the start:
    - consensus-plus-challenge decision-making (the "dedicated partnership" value);
    - entrepreneurial, niche lending learned in leasing;
    - expanding offshore to diversify away from rand risk, which led to the London DLC.
- **Purpose.** Investec's site states its purpose as *"to create enduring worth"* and pairs it with the "Out of the Ordinary" brand promise [Reported, search snippet of investec.com, S107].
  - The FY26 RNS includes Titi saying the group is *"guided by our purpose to create enduring worth for our clients, colleagues and the communities in which we operate"* [Confirmed, S70; this is a model-extracted quote, so check it before using verbatim].
  - A third-party case study gives the longer form *"create enduring worth, living in, not off, society"* [Reported, S107 snippet; not verified on investec.com].
- **Values.** The four values are **Client focus, Cast-iron integrity, Distinctive performance, Dedicated partnership** [Reported, S106; investec.com fetch blocked].
  - Paraphrased fragments from snippets:
    - "cast-iron integrity in all dealings, consistently displaying moral strength";
    - "client service, not customer support";
    - dedicated partnership means "collaborating unselfishly in pursuit of group performance, through open and honest dialogue – using process to test decisions, seek challenge and accept responsibility".
  - **Wording not verified on investec.com.** Use the four value names with confidence, and paraphrase the descriptions.

### C. The "contracts": what the UK bank gives and gets from each client group

- **Private clients (high net worth and high income).**
  - *What Investec gives:* lending (mostly residential mortgages, which grew 9.6% annualised in H1 FY26 [S75]), savings, and a high-touch relationship model. From May 2026 the plan adds current accounts, **its first UK credit card**, a rewards programme and advice delivered with Rathbones.
    - Head of UK Private Banking Ryan Tholet: *"Our shift from mortgage banker to relationship manager enables a greater offering at the source of any lending, increasing our relevance."* [Reported, City AM, 21 May 2026, S89]
  - *What Investec gets:* sticky deposits, mortgage NII, and now the chance to be the client's primary bank and earn fees.
  - *Targets:* add about 5,000 clients to the base of about 8,200. Business Day says profit should rise from **£32m to £57m by March 2030** [Reported, S92; S93].
  - City AM gives different figures: a £30m investment, doubling to 16,000 clients, a £9bn loan book and a 15% CAGR [Reported, S89]. *These conflict with the other reports; confirm with the investec.com press release.*
  - **Rathbones:** the relationship moves from referrals to an integrated assets-under-advice model. Investec leads the advice relationship and Rathbones supplies the investment capability [Reported, S93; S91].
- **Corporate and mid-market clients.**
  - *What Investec gives:* specialist and structured lending, advisory, hedging (treasury and risk solutions), and now transactional banking.
  - *What Investec gets:* fees, which rose 28% in IBP H1 FY26 [S75], plus lending margin and, increasingly, operational deposits.
  - *Strategy (20 Nov 2025):* full-service banking for the UK mid-market. The aim is to reach **60,000+ UK companies** and hold **1,000 tailored mid-cap relationships by 2030**. At group level the mid-market push should add **≥200bps of ROE by FY2030** [Reported, S90; S94].
  - Andy Hart, Head of Corporate Banking at IBP: *"We see a clear strategic growth opportunity to extend our offering and bring a private client banking experience to UK mid-market corporates."* [Reported, City AM, S90]
- **Depositors and funding.**
  - IBP is mainly funded by customer deposits: £21.4bn at Sept 2025 against £17.4bn of loans, a loan-to-deposit ratio of about 0.8x [Inferred from S75].
  - Investec plc is very liquid: LCR 349% and NSFR 139% at March 2026 [S70]; LCR 354% at June 2026 [S77].
  - Retail savings are sold through Investec Save, directly and on platforms such as **Raisin**. Products include easy access, notice accounts (30, 32 and 90 days) and fixed-term deposits. The Raisin listing shows a £1,000 minimum and £120,000 maximum [Reported, S88 snippet].
  - [Inferred] The deal with savers: Investec pays above-high-street rates for a stable, branchless retail deposit base. That funds niche lending without a branch network, and the cost is a higher cost of funds than clearing banks pay.

### D. Risk and control architecture

- **Regulators.**
  - Investec Bank plc is **authorised by the PRA and regulated by the FCA and the PRA, FRN 172330** [Reported, S88 snippet; FCA register page did not render].
  - Investec Limited and Investec Bank Limited are regulated by the SARB Prudential Authority [Inferred/standard].
- **Stated framework.** Group annual reports describe:
  - a board-approved **risk appetite framework**;
  - a **three lines of defence** model;
  - lending to "clients we know and understand", with a diversified book;
  - single-name limits of **7.5% of CET1 for Investec plc** and 5% of tier 1 for Investec Limited (up to 10% with board-committee approval);
  - a Global Credit Committee chaired by the Group Risk and Finance Director that meets twice a week.

  [Reported, S111 snippet, which appears to come from an older (c.2017) integrated annual report. The page timed out (522). **Check that these limits are still current in the FY26 risk report.**]
- **Through-the-cycle credit loss ratio range: 25–45bps** at group level [Confirmed, S70; S71].
  - The UK runs higher: guidance in the 50–60bps area [Reported, S72 extraction]; actual 57bps for FY26 [Confirmed, S70].
  - South Africa guidance is 15–35bps.
- **Capital and liquidity, 30 June 2026** [Confirmed, S77]:
  - Investec plc: CET1 12.7%; tier 1 14.4%; total capital 17.9%; leverage 8.9%; RWAs £20,530m.
  - Investec Limited: CET1 14.2%; total capital 18.0%.
- [Inferred] Investec plc uses **standardised** risk weights while Investec Limited uses AIRB (advanced internal ratings-based models). The UK bank therefore holds more capital per £ of lending, which is part of why UK ROE trails South Africa.

### E. UK business mix and what has grown

- **Private Banking (Private Client).**
  - Today: mortgages and lending to high-net-worth clients, savings and transactional banking.
  - Moving to "primary bank" status from May 2026 [S89; S93].
  - Residential mortgages were the fastest-growing book in H1 FY26, at +9.6% annualised [S75].
- **Corporate & Investment Banking.**
  - **Specialist lending:** fund finance ("Fund Solutions"), direct lending, growth and leveraged finance, real estate, aviation, power and infrastructure (energy and infrastructure finance), asset finance, and corporate and acquisition finance.
  - **Treasury and risk solutions** (hedging, FX).
  - **Advisory, ECM and corporate broking:** Investec bought Evolution Group in 2011 for about £230m [S85].
  - Fee income grew 28% in H1 FY26, driven by lending fees and advisory [Confirmed, S75].
  - Growth areas cited: Fund Solutions, Direct Lending and Aviation [S75].
  - In November 2025 Investec launched transactional banking for the mid-market [S90].
- **Wealth.** Investec Wealth & Investment UK (and Channel Islands) merged into **Rathbones on 21 Sept 2023** in an all-share deal valued at about £839m [Confirmed, S78; S85].
  - Investec holds a **41.25% economic and 29.9% voting** stake (27,056,463 voting shares plus 17,481,868 non-voting convertible shares).
  - It may nominate 2 non-executive directors. The first two were Henrietta Baldock and Ruth Leas.
  - Lock-up: 2 years, then up to one-third of the shares may be sold in each of years 3 and 4 [Reported, S115].
  - The holding is now an equity-accounted line: £76.6m share of PBT in FY26 [S70].
- [Inferred] **What has grown** in the UK: mortgages, fund and direct lending, and fees. **What has shrunk:** NII, as rates fell, and UK profit (FY26 −2.1%; H1 FY27 guided −2% to −6%), because FY27 is the "peak investment year".

### I. Skeletons, told straight

1. **Kensington subprime (2007–2014).** Investec bought UK subprime mortgage lender Kensington on 8 Aug 2007 for about R4.2bn, just before the crisis.
   - Impairments ran into tens of £m a year, e.g. £69.9m and £81.2m in successive years [Reported, search snippet of RNS].
   - The share price went from about R104 (2007) to about R27 (2009).
   - Koseff (Feb 2014): *"the timing of the Kensington deal was very unfortunate"* [Reported, S84].
   - Kensington was sold to Blackstone and TPG in **Sept 2014 for £180m** [Reported, S102].
2. **2008 crisis, no bailout.** Koseff: *"seven of the 10 big UK banks had to get a government bailout. We survived that crises without any help from anyone."* [Reported, SAJR, 18 Feb 2014, S84]
3. **Investec Australia.** The bank posted losses (e.g. an R117m half-year loss reported in 2014). Investec Bank (Australia) was sold to **Bank of Queensland for A$440m**, completing on 31 July 2014. Together with the Kensington sale, Investec plc's CET1 was expected to rise from 8.8% to about 11.3% [Reported, S103; S102].
4. **UK motor finance.**
   - Investec entered UK motor finance in 2015 and had a £555m book (about 1% market share) by 2021.
   - It provided **£30m in FY24** (reported 23 May 2024). Ruth Leas: *"We feel that it is prudent to take a provision. We acknowledge significant uncertainty around this estimation…"* [Reported, S95].
   - The provision was reaffirmed on 5 Aug 2025 [S96] and again after the FCA's final scheme on 30 Mar 2026 [Confirmed, S70].
5. **Cum-ex (Germany).**
   - News24 (21 Oct 2021) reported that Investec's Dublin office allegedly financed and facilitated cum-ex trades in **2008–2012**, including for funds linked to Hanno Berger, with German criminal investigations running since March 2018.
   - Investec's response: no employee or the bank itself had been criminally charged, indicted or subpoenaed. Investec has disclosed it as a contingent liability since 2019 [Reported, S98].
   - **No information found on the outcome after 2021. This is a gap; check the FY26 annual report's contingent liabilities note.**
6. **South Africa anti-money-laundering (AML) fine.** On 8 Aug 2016 the SARB fined Investec Bank Limited **R20m**, the largest of five banks fined, for weak AML/CFT controls, including sanctions screening of customers' related parties. The SARB stated the banks had not facilitated money laundering [Reported, S99].
7. **Steinhoff (Dec 2017).** Investec first called its exposure "negligible" (under 0.25% of R464.8bn of credit exposure). It ultimately lost **about R220m**, mainly on derivatives and trading, reported 17 May 2018 [Reported, S100; S101].
8. **FSA unfair terms undertaking (2013).** IBP agreed to amend terms in its **FTSE 100 Enhanced Kick-Out Plan 21** structured product after the FSA said some terms may be unfair. New wording applied from 18 Feb 2013. It was an undertaking, not a fine [Confirmed, S87].
9. **Rathbones risk (2026).** Investec's largest associate has an FCA-prompted remediation under way (see H). [Inferred] This poses a reputational and earnings risk to the "Rathbones-powered" advice model at the centre of the May 2026 private client strategy.
10. **DLC complexity.**
    - There are two listed parents, two capital regimes (PRA standardised versus SARB AIRB) and dual currencies.
    - There are three reporting sets (combined group, plc and Limited), plus IBP.
    - [Inferred] Analysts often cite this complexity as a valuation discount.
- **Not found:** no FCA or PRA financial penalty on Investec Bank plc in the FCA fines lists (search only, not exhaustive) [Reported].

## 6. Interview angles

1. **"Why Investec?" built on the economics.**
   - Over 40% of revenue is non-interest income, fees grew 14.7% in FY26, and NII fell as rates came down.
   - Investec is deliberately moving from "specialist lender" to "primary bank": mid-market transactional banking and current accounts and cards for private clients. The aim is to win sticky deposits and fees that hold up better than NII when rates fall.
   - Mention that the UK mid-market service is due to be "fully functional" in H2 2027, which is the internship window.
2. **The UK return gap.**
   - Investec plc ROE is 10.8% against 18.2% in South Africa.
   - Good question to ask: *"How does the UK bank close the gap to the 13–17% group range: through operating leverage from the mid-market build, through capital (standardised versus AIRB models), or through mix?"*
3. **"Out of the Ordinary" plus consensus culture.** Kantor's veto and consensus story maps onto the "dedicated partnership" value: test decisions, seek challenge, accept responsibility. Have an example ready of your own work that was challenged and improved.
4. **Rathbones as strategic lever and risk.**
   - The 41.25% stake contributed £76.6m of PBT, and the May 2026 plan relies on Rathbones as the investment engine.
   - Rathbones' June 2026 FCA remediation is a fair, informed question to raise.
5. **Credit discipline.**
   - The UK credit loss ratio is 57bps against a group range of 25–45bps, which is higher-risk niche lending priced accordingly.
   - Know the group's three lines of defence and that the credit committee meets frequently. Talk about knowing the client and the collateral.
6. **Learning from mistakes.** Kensington (2007) shows the cost of buying into a market just before a crisis. Selling Kensington and Australia in 2014 and folding wealth into Rathbones in 2023 show capital discipline and a willingness to simplify. This is a good "what would you do differently" talking point.
7. **Motor finance.** Investec's £30m provision is small next to peers (Lloyds £450m in 2024 [S95]). This reflects its roughly 1% market share and shows the value of niche scale.
8. **Watch next:** H1 FY27 results in late November 2026. The pre-close already guides UK profit 2–6% lower, so expect "peak investment year" framing. New Chair Henrietta Baldock has been in post since Aug 2026, and Koseff has now left the board, so no founder-era figure remains on it.
