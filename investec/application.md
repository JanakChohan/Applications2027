## Written answers (draft)

The exact questions only show up once the form opens. These drafts cover the usual Investec prompts. Cut each one down to the word limit on the form.

### 1. Why Investec?

> Investec built its business by backing entrepreneurs, and it still runs like a firm that was founded by one. What draws me is that the Specialist Bank works with mid-market companies and private clients that larger banks often treat as an afterthought. That is a part of the market where one person's judgement can still change an outcome. I've seen the opposite at bigger institutions, where a process can take weeks to move because nobody owns it. At Pictet I found a 400-line Excel tracker that went round every quarter and built a portal that sent each due diligence question straight to the team that owned it. Investec says it wants interns to take a project and make it their own, and that is the kind of work I enjoy most.

### 2. Tell us about an entrepreneurial idea / a fresh idea you had

> As Retail Sector Head at Oakwood Fund Management I pitched a BUY on Urban Outfitters at $80. My argument was that the market was valuing it as a struggling apparel retailer and giving no value to Nuuly, its clothing rental subscription business, which was growing fast and works more like a subscription model than a shop. I valued the rental arm on its own at $1.8bn, then checked that against a DCF and apparel retail comparables so the idea held up beyond one method. What I took from it is that a good idea is usually something hiding in plain sight that nobody has bothered to separate out. In a bank I'd want to look for the same thing: a client, a process or a number that everyone treats as one thing when it is really two.

*(Check that the Nuuly detail matches what was actually in the pitch before using it. If it doesn't, keep the answer to "its rental arm".)*

### 3. Tell us about a time you took ownership of a project

> During my placement at Box, sales teams kept asking for client stories but had no reliable way to find them. I designed a Box Hub that indexed every completed client story by region, industry and product, so an account executive could pull a relevant case study in seconds instead of chasing colleagues. It is the same instinct behind the DDQ portal I built at Pictet: if a problem keeps coming round, fix the system rather than the symptom.

### 4. Be you: what are you passionate about outside work?

> I collect boarding passes. Each one is a record of a trip, and a few have turned into conversation starters with complete strangers. I also blend my own spices, which started as a way to cook better on a student budget and turned into a habit of tinkering with ratios until something works. Both habits say something about how I work: I notice small details, and I like improving something step by step. I'm also convinced the 1984 Karate Kid is the best training montage ever filmed, and I'm happy to defend that at length.

### 5. Why financial services / why this programme?

> Across Goldman Sachs, Box and Pictet I've seen financial services from the trading floor, from the client operations side and from asset management onboarding. What has stayed with me is that the most valuable people know the client and also understand the plumbing behind the product. An eight-week programme that places me in one Specialist Bank team, while showing me the rest of the business, is exactly the next step I want before graduating in June 2027.
