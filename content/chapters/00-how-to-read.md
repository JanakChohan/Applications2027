# How to read this dossier

This is a research and learning project for one application: the **2027 | EMEA | London | Asset Management, Client Solutions Group | Summer Analyst** programme at Goldman Sachs. It sets out to answer six questions. What is this business? What does the role involve? Why does the work create value? What are its limits? What would you be choosing by entering it? Why might you enjoy the work itself, as distinct from the title?

It is not a set of scripts. The interview material sits in the last chapter on purpose, because an answer you can defend comes from understanding the business, and a memorised answer falls apart at the second follow-up question.

## The three layers

Everything in the project belongs to one of three layers, and they are labelled wherever confusing them would matter.

| Layer | What it holds | How to treat it |
|---|---|---|
| {{L:A}} | What Goldman Sachs, clients, regulators and other credible sources establish | Quote it with its source and date |
| {{L:B}} | What those facts suggest about client needs, the work and the economics | An argument. Know its reasoning and where it could be wrong |
| {{L:C}} | What might appeal to you about the work | A hypothesis about yourself that only your own evidence can test |

A Layer B argument is never a disclosed Goldman Sachs fact. A Layer C statement is never proof of your personality. The chapters on the industry and the role stand on their own and are not tied to your background.

## Evidence statuses

Every material claim carries one of six statuses.

| Status | Meaning | How far to rely on it |
|---|---|---|
| {{V}} | The supporting passage was read on a primary or credible source | Usable, with the date |
| {{SI}} | Reasoned from verified facts or general industry practice, or seen only in a search summary | Usable as "this suggests", never as "Goldman says" |
| {{UA}} | Known only from one person's account | A lead to test. Do not repeat it as fact |
| {{IL}} | Invented to teach a mechanism | Never quote as real |
| {{CE}} | Credible sources disagree, or an account conflicts with a source | State both sides |
| {{NPE}} | Looked for and not found in public sources | Say "not public", and do not guess |

Three rules sit behind the labels. Another AI's draft is not a source. Several articles repeating one press release count once. Where a figure is not published, the dossier says "not publicly disclosed" and leaves the gap visible.

## Where the starting account came from, and what the research did to it

The project began with notes from one call with a former intern on the sales side of the UK Institutional team. His account is useful and specific, and it is one person's view of one summer. Every statement from it was treated as a lead and tested. The full audit is in the Sources view of the application and in Appendix L of the PDF. The findings that most change the starting picture are these.

| The starting account said | What the research found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Client Solutions Group" is the equivalent of saying "sales" | Goldman's own listings pair the name with "Sales", and one advertisement calls it the sales organisation. A London advertisement for a Client Solutions Group role also gives it work on existing relationships, so the name does not exclude relationship management [@R1-S04][@R1-S01] | {{V}} |
| "CCG" (Client Coverage Group) is the relationship-management side | "Client Coverage Group" is a real Goldman name, defined in one advertisement as the group for retaining, expanding and supporting existing relationships. How it sits relative to the Client Solutions Group in London is not published [@R1-S11] | {{V}} for the name, {{NPE}} for the structure |
| OCIO exists only in the UK | It is a global business. Goldman reported $480 billion of OCIO assets at 31 March 2026 and has announced large mandates in the United States and across Europe [@R7-S13][@R3-S09] | {{CE}} |

The other is not confirmed by any public source, and its schemes' published reports name a different fiduciary manager. Its name is left out of this project [@R3-S01] | {{V}} and {{NPE}} |

## Two teaching levels

Each topic is taught twice. **Level 1** explains it from first principles in plain English with one concrete example. **Level 2** adds the institutional mechanics: contracts, incentives, calculations, decision rights and exceptions. In the application you can show either level or both. Caveats sit outside both levels, so the beginner view never hides a limitation.

## What has been left out on purpose

- **Your own records.** No CV, email or personal document is used anywhere, and no experience, feeling or motive has been written for you.
- **The "why-tree" exercises and the motivation chapter.** Removed at your request. The Relationships page and the Sales and relationship management page now carry that ground in concrete terms.
- **One client name.** As explained above.
- **Private information about people.** The directory holds public professional information only: names, public titles, offices and public career history, each with a source and an evidence date.
- **Recruitment tactics built on rumour.** Unverified claims about how candidates are screened or allocated are audited, and nothing here recommends tailoring answers to them.

## How the research was done, and its limits

Research date and information cutoff: 14 November 2026. Nothing later is used.

Nine research streams covered the programme and recruitment process, Goldman's disclosures, the UK institutional and OCIO market, third-party wealth, the rest of the EMEA coverage map with liquidity and insurance, people, mandates, and competitors and trends. Each stream recorded its sources, the passage supporting each claim, its search queries and what it looked for without finding.

Four limits matter when you rely on the result.

1. **The search allowance ran out.** Several streams stopped searching before their plan was complete. The coverage register in chapter 11 separates "searched, not established" from "not investigated", topic by topic.
2. **Goldman's careers site could not be read directly.** Most job-advertisement evidence comes from mirrors of the listings or from search summaries, and is capped at "supported inference" unless the exact passage was seen.
3. **Public profiles are a biased sample.** Senior and externally visible people are over-represented. The directory is not a headcount and cannot support a ratio, a conversion rate or an estimate of hiring odds.
4. **Things change.** Titles, policies, rates and figures are correct to the evidence date shown beside them, and no later.
