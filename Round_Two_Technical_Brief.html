Put together, "more technical" for a corporate access intern most likely means four kinds of question, in roughly this order of likelihood:

1. Mechanics

"What is an NDR?" "How does a one-on-one get allocated?" "What is in a consumption report?" "How does the broker vote work?" Definitions and processes. Part 3, domains A, B and D.

2. Walk-throughs

"Walk me through what you would do if..." A request arrives; a seat is lost; a conference opens; a PM wants a company in closed period. Process questions with a compliance edge. Part 3, domains G, E and F.

3. Data and tools

"How would you build the tracker?" "Which Excel functions?" "Here is a table; what does it tell you?" Possibly a small numeric example read out or shared on screen. Part 3, domain C, with a worked table.

A fourth kind, commercial awareness ("what is going on in markets", "why does this desk exist at a multi-manager"), is possible in any Millennium round [S263] and is covered in domain H. What is unlikely, on the evidence: a case study, a live Excel test, a market quiz with numbers, or a brainteaser. If one appears, the answer shape in Part 4.2 still holds.

3.1 Domain A: formats and channels (five questions)

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
A1. What is corporate access, in your own words?"Arranging contact between the people who run listed companies and the investors who follow them: one-on-ones, group meetings, conferences, roadshows, site visits [S1]. On this desk it means two supply lines, the broker channel and the originated channel, serving internal clients, the pods. Since 2014 in the UK it is a service that cannot be bought with trading commission, which is why it is managed, recorded and allocated deliberately [S180]."Probe: "Why does it need a desk?" Because pods compete and cannot see each other's demand; the desk pools it. Trap: "networking" or "getting an edge".
A2. Broker-sourced versus Millennium-originated: what is the difference, and when would you use each?"Broker-sourced is access a bank provides: its conference slots, its roadshows, its IR introductions. Originated is access the firm secures directly from a company's IR team, which the origination team does sector by sector [S42]. Use the broker channel for conferences and post-results roadshows, where the bank owns the grid; use the direct channel for companies the pods need outside those windows, for under-covered names, and where a bank would just be a middleman. The buy side is far more likely to take a direct meeting [S4]; companies increasingly expect it [S35]. Direct takes years to build, and some sectors resist."Probe: "Which is cheaper?" Direct has no broker to reward, but it costs the desk's time and the firm's reputation with IR. Trap: saying origination replaces brokers; practitioners say the opposite [S10].
A3. Name the meeting formats and tell me which are hardest to get."One-on-one, group meeting or fireside, conference slot, non-deal roadshow, site visit or field trip, capital markets day, IR call, and each of those virtually. Hardest: the one-on-one with a CEO or CFO at a conference in season, because there is one management team and hundreds of investors; 82% of buy-siders rank one-on-ones as the top item in the broker vote [S39]. Easiest: an IR call, and a capital markets day, which is open to anyone who registers."Probe: "What is a fireside chat?" A moderated group session; the moderator asks the questions. Trap: confusing an NDR with a deal roadshow.
A4. What is an NDR, and how does the desk get onto one?"A non-deal roadshow: management touring investors' offices over a few days with no transaction attached, usually right after results; about two-thirds are broker-sponsored [S38]. The sponsoring bank and the company's IR decide the list of investors; the desk gets Millennium on it by asking early, ranking its request, and being the client the bank remembers as easy. The buy side can also ask a broker to build a bespoke 'reverse roadshow' [S467]."Probe: "When do NDRs happen?" Post-results for the numbers; mid-quarter for the equity story [S440]. Trap: thinking the desk chooses the cities.
A5. How much of this is virtual now?"Most meetings were virtual in late 2021, 93% by one survey; by 2023 virtual had fallen to about 13% of activity, and one broker reports about a quarter of its meetings virtual and stable [S460][S458][S459]. So in-person is the default again, which is why rooms, invites and hand-overs are back in the job description."Probe: "Does virtual change anything for the desk?" Same rules, easier logistics, and a multi-manager head asks for cameras on [S10]. Trap: guessing Millennium's own mix; not public.

3.2 Domain B: requests, allocation and conference mechanics (seven questions)

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
B1. A bank announces its November European industrials conference. Walk me through what the desk does."About two months out the bank confirms the companies and slot counts. Booking for one-on-ones opens four to six weeks before [S30]. I would load the event into the portal, tell the pods whose names are attending, collect requests with each requester's own ranking, pool duplicates across pods, and send the bank one ranked Millennium list per company with context that helps the allocator without revealing positions [S28]. When the allocation comes back, I would allocate internally by the desk's rule, attach an alternative to every no, and keep watching the last two or three days when banks release held-back slots [S30]. After the event, log who attended what."Probe: "How early is early?" The day the window opens. Trap: starting the story at the conference.
B2. How does a bank decide who gets the one-on-one?"By tiering its investor clients on size, commission relationship and relationship depth, by fit with what the company wants to see, and by the company's own must-see list; top-tier accounts get first pick and residual slots go last [S438][S436]. Some slots are held back for the final 48 to 72 hours [S30]. Broker events are often one seat per firm, which is what makes the internal allocation the hard part."Probe: "So how does Millennium get more?" Being a client that matters, and being early and clean. Trap: assuming size alone wins.
B3. One seat, three pods. How do you decide?"I wouldn't, as an intern; I'd apply the desk's rule and record it. The rule has to be one the losing team could accept as fair: coverage relevance, a live position or live work, when the team last met the company, and the requester's own ranking are the kinds of criteria I'd expect. Then the two teams that miss get an alternative I have already secured, a group slot or an IR call, and a route to escalate that isn't me. None of the three hears who else asked."Probe: "And if the rule is unclear?" Ask the associate before allocating, not after. Trap: inventing the rule and defending it.
B4. What does "internal sales and target marketing" actually mean?"The desk knows the forward calendar and the pods do not. Marketing means telling the right teams, the ones covering the names attending, about an event before the booking window closes, through the portal and by email [S40]; the New York posting calls it making 'pro-active corporate access suggestions based on investment team's coverage' [S431]. Target means the right pods, not all pods. Requests that arrive after the window are the ones that fail."Probe: "How do you know who covers what?" The portal's coverage data and the relationships the desk keeps with PMs and analysts [S431]. Trap: sounding like a newsletter.
B5. What is a "no with an alternative"?"The desk's standard answer when a seat is lost: the rule in one line, an alternative already secured, and an escalation path that is not me. Never who got the seat, never why they ranked higher. The AllianceBernstein head's version is 'no, not right now, and here's why' [S433]. The consumption report is what makes it credible, because the PM can see what the desk did get him."Probe: "What if there is no alternative?" Say so, and say when the next chance is. Trap: apologising for the rule.
B6. How do you rank requests when a PM says everything is priority one?"I'd ask the PM to rank them, because a list with no order gives the bank nothing to work with, and banks allocate ranked requests [S28][S438]. If he won't, I'd rank by the desk's criteria and tell him the order I submitted, so he can change it before the deadline rather than complain after."Probe: "What if two PMs' rankings conflict?" That is the pooling problem; the desk's rule decides, and both are told. Trap: submitting everything as priority one.
B7. A conference's booking closed yesterday and an analyst asks today. What do you do?"Ask the bank anyway, politely and once, for the waitlist and for the group session; banks release held-back slots late and cancellations happen [S30]. Tell the analyst the honest odds today, offer the IR call as the fallback, and log the request so the next window on that name is marketed to that pod early."Probe: "Would you push the bank hard?" No; you need them next month. Trap: promising the seat.

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
D1. What do brokers want from a desk like this?"To be remembered at the vote. A bank's corporate access desk allocates scarce slots among hundreds of investor clients [S29]; it wants clean, ranked, early requests, prepared attendees who turn up, feedback it can pass to the company, and a fair score at the end. D2. How does the broker vote work?"The buy side periodically scores each bank's services and uses the result to decide how it rewards them [S39]. At Millennium the corporate access vote is semi-annual and the desk runs it, collecting feedback on 'broker access quality, meeting quality, and overall effectiveness' and compiling the results [S432]."Probe: "What does the desk contribute?" The consumption data that turns opinion into evidence. Trap: saying the vote is how access is paid for.
D3. A broker calls: "why did you rank us lower this time?" What do you say?"Nothing about the ranking on my own; that is the associate's conversation. What I can do is have the evidence ready: our requests to them, what they gave, what was attended, the no-shows on our side. The right tone is the one the desk uses with PMs: the rule, the record, and the relationship intact for next quarter."Probe: "Is the vote confidential?" Treat it as such unless told otherwise. Trap: defending or leaking the score.
D4. What goes in a broker request email?"One list, ranked, per event; format and any flexibility, such as 'one-on-one preferred, group acceptable'; context that helps the allocator without revealing positions, like coverage; the deadline I need an answer by; and thanks. Nothing I would not want the bank to hold, because it will."Probe: "Would you tell them the pod?" Not the position; the team designation is normal [S28]. Trap: copying a PM on it.
D5. What is the UK corporate broker, and why does it matter here?"UK listed companies retain a house broker, and AIM companies must have a nominated adviser; the broker runs the company's roadshows and reports investor feedback to the board [S36][S37]. So for a UK plc the desk usually goes through IR and the house broker's calendar, and how the firm's analysts behave in the room gets reported back."Probe: "Does that exist in Germany?" The named preferred access providers there were Berenberg, ODDO BHF and Jefferies [S34]. Trap: confusing the house broker with the research analyst.

3.5 Domain E: compliance and regulation (seven questions)

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
E1. Why can't we pay a bank for a meeting through our trading?"Because the FCA found in 2012 that no firm could justify corporate access as research, and from 2 June 2014 it cannot be bought with dealing commission [S180]; MiFID II from January 2018 applied the same logic across the EU, calling it 'a discrete service' [S185]. When the FCA re-allowed bundled research payments from August 2024 it kept corporate access out of scope [S181]. So it is paid from the firm's own resources, accepted where the rules allow, or arranged directly."Probe: "What is the exception?" Companies under £200m market cap: an acceptable minor non-monetary benefit [S15]. Trap: "MiFID II banned corporate access."
E2. What is inside information, and why does the desk care?"Precise, non-public, price-sensitive information under MAR Article 7; trading on it or passing it on is a crime, and the FCA says analysts in a private meeting are 'not the public' and private shareholder calls are 'dissemination' [S21][S22]. The desk cares because it arranges exactly those private forums, and its log is the evidence of who was in which room on which day."Probe: "Give me an example." Einhorn, 2009: a broker-arranged call, an equity raise mentioned, a sale, a £7.3m fine, and declining the NDA was no defence [S19]. Trap: calling public information "MNPI".
E3. An analyst tells you after a meeting that the CFO said the quarter is "well ahead". What do you do?"Treat it as a possible unlawful disclosure. Tell my associate and Compliance now, with the log entry: company, broker, attendees, date. Forward nothing, summarise nothing, and do not go back to the broker. What happens next is Compliance's decision."Probe: "What if the analyst says it's fine?" It is not the analyst's call, and the FCA criticised firms that left that judgement to others [S20]. Trap: "checking" with the broker.
E4. What is a closed period, and what does it mean for a request?"Thirty calendar days before an interim or year-end report, when the company's directors may not deal in its shares [S447]. It is a dealing ban, not a gag, but the FCA tells issuers to avoid private investor calls in it and ESMA has warned about pre-close calls [S449][S448]. Companies also set their own quiet periods, typically the last two to four weeks of a quarter [S451]. For a request: expect a no, do not push, ask for the first post-results slot, and log the dates."Probe: "Is a meeting in a closed period illegal?" No; it is usually declined and unwise. Trap: saying it is illegal.
E5. What is a restricted list, and what does the desk do with it?"The names the firm may not trade or discuss because someone inside holds inside information. A request on a restricted name goes to Compliance, not to a broker [S20]. Checking it is a step in the request process, before submission."Probe: "Who maintains it?" Compliance. The desk checks; it does not decide. Trap: not knowing it exists.
E6. Why does the desk log every meeting?"Four reasons. To evidence what a PM did or did not hear on a given date [S20][S22]; to prove access was not bought with commission or subsidised by research payments [S181]; to attribute consumption for the vote [S39]; and because the FCA suggests documenting discussions [S22]. If it is not logged, the firm cannot defend a decision."Probe: "Isn't that just admin?" It is the substance; the no is only credible because of it. Trap: giving one reason.
E7. What is the difference between UK and US rules here?"In the UK and EU, MAR binds both the company that discloses and the investor who receives; in the US, Regulation FD binds the issuer to disclose publicly what it told investors selectively, at once if intentional and within a day if not [S17], and the fund's risk is insider-trading law. And research payment rules differ: the UK re-allowed joint payments in 2024, the EU followed in 2026, and the US never unbundled in the same way [S181][S199]."Probe: "Does Reg FD apply to a UK company?" Not unless it is a US issuer. Trap: mixing the two regimes.

3.6 Domain F: EMEA, the calendar and time zones (four questions)

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
F1. When is this desk busiest, and why?"The four to six weeks before each conference cluster, because that is when booking windows open [S30], and the days after results, which is the highest-demand window of the quarter [S441]. In 2026 the September cluster alone had nine named European conferences between 2 and 20 September [S446]; July is quiet. You have just come through the peak."Probe: "Name one." Goldman's European Medtech on 9 September; Bernstein's Pan European on 10 September [S446]. Trap: saying the conference days are the peak.
F2. What does "primarily across EMEA" mean for the day?"Europe, the Middle East and Africa: London to Dubai, three hours ahead in autumn, with pods in New York five hours behind who also want European companies. Every invite carries the right zone and the other zone in the body; a wrong one is a missed meeting with the desk's name on it. UK results land at 07:00, so the morning is the spike; US results arrive after 21:00 and are picked up next day."Probe: "Where do European companies report?" Full-year February to March, half-year July to August; many mid-caps half-yearly only. Trap: not knowing New York is five hours behind.
F3. A PM in Dubai wants a London meeting at 09:00 London. Any issue?"It is noon in Dubai, fine for him; the question is whether the company's people are in London or dialling in from Zurich, which is 10:00 there. I'd put all three zones in the invite and confirm the dial-in works from the Dubai office."Probe: "And a New York PM?" 04:00 for him; offer the recording or a follow-up call. Trap: assuming everyone is in London.
F4. What is the marketing-calendar report?"A report of each company's results dates, closed-period start (results minus thirty calendar days), its own quiet-period policy, and its upcoming events, used to plan originated access; the New York posting names exactly this report [S431]. It tells the pods who is dark and when the door opens."Probe: "How would you build it?" A company table, the results date, a formula for minus thirty days, and the IR page's quiet-period line. Trap: not knowing what it is for.

3.7 Domain G: process walk-throughs (five questions)

These are the most likely technical questions from a team member, because they are what the team member does all day. The scenario cards in the Complete Interview Pack give the pressure versions; these are the plain versions, answered as process.

G1. "An analyst emails asking for a call with a company's IR team next week. Walk me through it."Easy
Say

"Reply within the hour so she knows it is in hand, and ask two things: what she needs to cover, and whether a group call would do if a one-on-one is not available. Put it in the tracker with an ID, her priority and my name. Check the company's results date and closed period, and the restricted list. Then the channel: if a broker has the company on a calendar this month, ask the broker; if not, go to IR directly through the origination team's contact. Chase, confirm, send the invite with the zone and the dial-in, and log it. If the answer is 'not this week', she hears that today, with the next available window."

Probe

"Why check the results date?" Because a company in its closed period will say no, and asking wastes the IR relationship. "Why the restricted list?" Because a meeting on a restricted name is Compliance's call, not mine.

G2. "You have forty open requests and a conference deadline at noon. How do you order the morning?"Medium
Say

"Deadline first: everything for that conference gets pooled, ranked and submitted by eleven so there is slack. Then anything with a compliance flag, because those cannot wait. Then confirmations for meetings happening today or tomorrow, because a missed invite is a missed meeting. Then new requests, oldest first, each acknowledged even if not actioned. Colour-coded in the tracker so the associate can see the state at a glance. Anything I cannot get to by four, I say so by two."

Probe

"A senior PM's request is in the last group. Do you move it up?" Seniority changes how I communicate, not the order; I'd tell him when I will get to it, and if it is for the conference it is already in the first group.

G3. "A company is visiting the office next Tuesday: CFO and head of IR, two hours. What do you do this week?"Medium
Say

"Confirm the format with the host: how many slots, one-on-one or group, who from the company. Check the name against the restricted list and its closed period. Market the visit to the pods covering the name and collect requests; allocate the slots by the rule, on separate invites so no pod sees another. Book the room, reception and any dial-in. Reconfirm with the host the day before. On the day, be at the room, run the hand-overs to the minute, and record attendance. Afterwards, log the same day and route any feedback through the associate."

Probe

"Is the broker in the room?" Not my decision; AllianceBernstein keeps brokers out by design [S433], and I'd follow the desk's practice. "A pod asks who else is attending." I don't say.

G4. "The portal shows a conference on the wrong date and three requests were made against it. Fix it."Hard
Say

"Check the source first, the broker's invitation, so I am sure which date is right. Fix the date. Then the three requests: tell each requester the corrected date and confirm they still want it; if the new date clashes with something, offer the alternative now. Check whether the same broker's other entries came in wrong, because a wrong date rarely travels alone. Tell the associate what happened and what I changed. And look at how the date got in wrong: if it was typed, that is the field to validate."

Probe

"You caused the error. Do you still tell?" Yes, faster; a hidden mistake costs more than a visible one. "The bank moved it and did not tell you." Then I'd ask the broker for a confirmation email whenever a date changes, and add a weekly check of upcoming events against broker calendars.

G5. "A PM wants a company that reports in ten days. He says it's urgent."Hard
Say

"The company is inside its closed period and almost certainly its own quiet period, so IR will not book management and I would not press them; the FCA tells issuers to avoid private calls in that window [S449]. I'd tell the PM that today, not after trying, and ask what he needs to cover. Then I'd request the first post-results slot, the IR call the day after or a place on the post-results roadshow, and get the ask in before the spike, because that is the most contested window of the quarter. And I'd log the company's dates so the next request on that name is timed right."

Probe

"He says another firm got a meeting last week." Maybe before the closed period began, maybe an IR call on public information only; either way it does not change what IR will do today, and I'd say so politely and offer to ask IR for the earliest post-results slot now.

3.8 Domain H: commercial awareness (four questions)

QuestionAnswer, under ninety secondsProbe and trap
H1. Why does a multi-manager need this desk when a long-only fund might not?"Because the teams are independent and compete; they cannot see each other's demand and will not share it, so without a desk the firm sends a bank three hundred conflicting requests and looks disorganised. Long-only funds need 'one or two substantive annual one-on-one meetings' per company; hedge funds want more frequent contact and shorter feedback loops [S11]. Fewer than a quarter of buy-side firms had a dedicated desk in 2021 [S461]; the ones that do are the ones with many teams and many requests."Probe: "What does the desk sit inside?" Execution Services within Platform Solutions [S42]. Trap: "because PMs are busy".
H2. What is happening at Millennium right now that matters for the desk?"Capital and teams are growing: about $97bn of assets by Bloomberg's count, and $22bn of new commitments closing on 1 October under a five-year restriction, with Jain Global and Albar now exclusive to the platform [S400][S407]. That is more teams to serve and more requests to pool. Performance is steady rather than spectacular: flat in August, up 8.2% for the year [S402], which is the product doing its job. For the desk, the question is how to serve more teams without losing the record or the service."Probe: "Is 8% good?" Against a market up more, no; against the firm's promise of low volatility, yes. Trap: quoting AUM as fact.
H3. What is going on in markets that a PM would want a meeting about?"One view, with a date. The AI trade drove July's fall for the platforms, so pods in technology and its suppliers want to hear how companies describe demand now; and the September conference cluster has just given them a chance to. In the UK, enforcement on conduct is live: the Upper Tribunal upheld the FCA's ban on Crispin Odey on 10 September [S413]. Check Hedgeweek for the September numbers on the morning; the central bank decisions of mid-September post-date my sources."Probe: "Rates?" Give the last figure you verified, with its date, and say so. Trap: forecasting.
H4. Why this desk, and not the investing side?"Because the desk is the only seat that sees every team, and I want to learn the firm from the centre before I understand any one part of it. The work is judgement under scarcity, with a record behind it, and Round 1 confirmed that the hard part is saying no well, which is a skill I want. I'm not using it as a route to a pod."Probe: "Where does it lead?" Senior desk roles, broker relations, business management, IR. Trap: hinting at a move to the investing side.
How to drill this in four days

Do not memorise forty-five answers. Learn the ten mechanisms underneath them (two channels, the request lifecycle, the six-week arc, the desk's rule and the alternative, the consumption report, the vote, the three regulation dates, MAR and the log, closed and quiet periods, the answer shape) and let each question pull the mechanism it needs. Then say ten answers aloud a day, timed, in a different order each time.
