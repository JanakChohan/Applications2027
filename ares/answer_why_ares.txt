What drew me to Ares was a detail in the FY2025 10-K: 93% of management fees
came from perpetual capital or long-dated funds.

Most alternative managers run closer to a drawdown treadmill, where revenue
depends on closing the next successor vintage, so raising capital is episodic
and campaign-led. By weighting its fee base toward vehicles with no fixed end
date, Ares changes what a client-facing role is for. Success stops being the
size of the last close and becomes retention and expansion instead. The
evidence is that 79% of Ares' 2025 institutional fundraising came from existing
investors, either re-upping into a new vintage or taking a second product. When
four-fifths of new capital comes from people who already own something,
servicing well is selling.

I also like where the function sits. Client Solutions and Investor Relations
sit across all four investment groups rather than inside one, so the learning
curve covers credit, real assets and secondaries rather than a single sleeve.
That breadth is what I want early on, because I would like to be able to hold a
proper conversation with a sophisticated allocator, on non-accrual rates and
leverage, or on why an asset-light manager behaves differently to a peer that
owns an insurance balance sheet.

Joining while private credit is under real scrutiny seems like the point at
which you actually learn to make that case.
