## Q1. Why are you interested in a career in private banking and wealth management? (250 words)

From the age of 14, I worked in my local school uniform shop. I never pushed a sale. I found the right fit for each child as they grew and helped parents save money where I could. Year after year, families came back and asked for me by name. That is where I learned I enjoy earning someone's trust over years, not minutes.

To see client relationships in a corporate setting, I interned at Box, working with clients from Oxfam to large financial institutions. The relationships were transactional, without the lasting connection I had with those families.

I also wanted to understand how wealth is managed, so I joined the Global Client Group at Pictet Asset Management. Answering due diligence questionnaires and client queries taught me how institutional portfolios are run. What I missed was the person behind the money. My audience was an investment committee, and success meant beating a benchmark.

Private banking closes that gap. The client is the person across the table, and success means their goals: a first home, school fees, passing a farm or business to the next generation. That last goal got harder when inheritance tax relief on farms and family businesses was capped in April. I want to help families through that, alongside their lawyer and accountant.

It combines the investment discipline I learned at Pictet with the trust I earned at 14. That is why I want this career, starting at a bank where one banker knows the whole client.
