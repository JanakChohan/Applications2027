## Q1. Why are you interested in a career in private banking and wealth management? (250 words)

From the age of 14, I worked in my local school uniform shop. I never pushed a sale. I found the right fit for each child as they grew and helped parents save money where I could. Year after year, families came back and asked for me by name. That is where I learned I enjoy earning someone's trust over years, not minutes.

To see client relationships in a corporate setting, I interned at Box, working with clients from Oxfam to large financial institutions. The relationships were transactional, without the lasting connection I had with those families.

I also wanted to understand how wealth is managed, so I joined the Global Client Group at Pictet Asset Management. Answering due diligence questionnaires and client queries taught me how institutional portfolios are run. What I missed was the person behind the money. My audience was an investment committee, and success meant beating a benchmark.

Private banking closes that gap. The client is the person across the table, and success means their goals: a first home, school fees, passing a farm or business to the next generation. That last goal got harder when inheritance tax relief on farms and family businesses was capped in April. I want to help families through that, alongside their lawyer and accountant.

It combines the investment discipline I learned at Pictet with the trust I earned at 14. That is why I want this career, starting at a bank where one banker knows the whole client.


## Q2. Tell us about a time you built a strong relationship with someone. How did you do it? (300 words)

The strongest relationship I have built began while I was house-hunting in my second year of university. It has outlasted the man I built it with, and continues today with his twin brother.

As lead tenant for a group of six, I was competing with 21 other groups for one house. At the viewing, I stopped looking at the rooms and asked about him instead. He told me how he had gone from construction worker to owning more than ten properties. I was genuinely interested, and we found common ground in self-improvement and the gym. When he chose us, it felt like that connection, not the paperwork, had won it.

I then worked to keep his trust. As lead tenant, I made myself his single point of contact, so six students' questions reached him once and clearly. I kept him updated every [month]. When we broke the stove, I told him that day and [offered to arrange the repair]. He said that kind of honesty was rare.

Then, suddenly, he passed away. I learned he had already spoken about me to his twin brother, who co-ran the business. I contacted his brother to [offer my condolences and make sure the house was handed over smoothly], and stayed in touch through that difficult time.

Today we look out for each other. He contacts me when he needs tenants and I spread the word among students; [X] have since rented from him. When I need a home, I go to him.

Honesty, regular contact and showing up when it was hard built that relationship. It also taught me that trust can pass from one family member to the next. That is what a private bank does across generations, and what I want to do for clients.
