The application form

Three questions, 800 words in total. This part covers how they are probably weighted, what each one tests, what a stand-out answer does, and a draft frame for each with placeholders for your own story.

A1. Is there an official weighting?

No. Weatherbys does not publish how the form is scored. Its careers pages and the 2027 advert give no weighting or marking scheme [S9][S14] Confirmed. No UK private bank that I could find publishes its rubric [S173] Reported. Anyone who gives you an exact percentage is guessing. What follows is a reasoned estimate, labelled Inferred.

How UK employers usually mark forms like this

  • Each answer is scored against a behaviour. The best public example is the UK Civil Service scale, which runs from 1 ("not demonstrated") to 7 ("outstanding"). The usual pass mark is 4, which means "adequate positive evidence and any negative evidence would not cause concern" [S173]. Other employers use a 0 to 3 scale per competency [S174].
  • Markers look for three things. Evidence of the behaviour, your own part in it ("I", not "we"), and a result you can point to [S173][S175].
  • Screeners are wary of generic text. In the ISE 2025 survey, 48% of employers worried that students' AI use misrepresents their skills [S172]. Specific, personal detail is the best defence.
  • At a small firm, a person probably reads every form. Weatherbys hired five Associate Private Bankers in 2025 [S4], so the intern cohort is likely small. That favours answers with a real voice over keyword stuffing Inferred.

A2. Question 1: "Why are you interested in a career in private banking and wealth management?" (250 words)

What is really being tested

  • Do you know what the job is? Many applicants confuse private banking with investment banking or stock-picking. A private banker at Weatherbys manages a portfolio of wealthy clients (the Senior Private Banker advert says 100+) across "banking, lending and investments" [S24]. Most of the bank's income is interest from lending and deposits, not investment fees [S4].
  • Is your interest real and earned? Have you acted on it?
  • Have you done research that a casual applicant would not?
Key term: private banking versus wealth management
Private banking is personal banking for wealthy people: accounts, deposits, tailored loans and mortgages, all through one named banker. Wealth management is managing and planning their investments, usually for a fee based on the money managed. Weatherbys does both through one banker, with its in-house Investment & Wealth Advice (IWA) team behind them [S9][S163]. The question names both, so your answer should touch both.

The structure (about 250 words)

Stand-out moves for Q1

  1. Start with a moment, not a statement. Avoid "I have always been passionate about finance." Use a short scene with a person in it: a family member's mortgage, a farm's succession question, a manager at a part-time job, a client you served.
  2. Name the mechanism you find interesting. For example, judgement over automation. Weatherbys says lending decisions are made by "a person who understands complex HNW income structures" rather than by "automated credit scoring" [S17]. The CEO says: "I try and leave as much judgement and discretion with the front line as I possibly can, because that's actually what clients want" [S15].
  3. Use one live issue that affects Weatherbys' clients. The best one: since 6 April 2026, full agricultural and business property relief from inheritance tax covers only the first £2.5m per person (£5m per couple) [S152]. Farms, estates and family businesses are Weatherbys segments [S16][S19]. A second option: falling rates cut the bank's interest margin from 4.53% to 4.05%, which is why advice and investment income matter [S4].
  4. Show that you understand the business and not only the brand. One number is enough. Pick NPS 84 [S4], the "double in size" plan [S4], or the CEO's 10,000 to 15,000 relationship ceiling [S15].
  5. End on the long term. The APB route, a named qualification (CeMAP, IAD or PCIAM, all supported by the bank [S9]), and the idea of being a family's banker for decades. This fits a seven-generation family firm.
Don't say this in Q1
"I want to work with high-net-worth individuals" (sounds like you want proximity to money). "Fast-paced environment" (says nothing). "Prestigious" (a value judgement about the firm, not about you). Anything about trading or markets as your main motive. A list of five adjectives. Horse-racing enthusiasm you do not genuinely have: the Racing Bank is a real part of the firm and a marker who rides will spot it.

A draft frame you can build on (yellow = only you can write it)

[One concrete moment, 1-2 sentences: e.g. "When my family's farm had to refinance in 2024, the outcome turned on one banker who took the time to understand how the farm's income really worked."] It showed me that most people's financial lives do not fit a standard product, and that the most useful person in the room is the one who understands the whole picture.

That is what draws me to private banking and wealth management: the job is to know one client's lending, investments and plans well enough to make a judgement, and to keep earning their trust over years.

Weatherbys is built around that idea. One named banker covers each client's banking, lending and investments, and lending decisions are made by a person who understands complex income rather than by automated scoring. The timing interests me too. Since April 2026, inheritance tax relief on farms and family businesses has been capped at £2.5m, so many of the farming and landed families that Weatherbys serves now face new succession questions. I would like to learn how a banker helps a family through that alongside their lawyers and accountants.

[Proof, 1-2 sentences: something you already do that resembles the job, e.g. "In two years at X, I learned that customers come back to the person who remembers them."]

I also want to learn in a firm that is growing: Weatherbys plans to double in size, and its internship leads to the Associate Private Banker programme. In the long term I would like to qualify through [CeMAP / IAD] and become the banker a family keeps for decades.
About 245 words with placeholders filled. Rewrite it in your own voice: a marker who reads 100 forms will notice a template.

A3. Question 2: "Tell us about a time you built a strong relationship with someone. How did you do it?" (300 words)

What is really being tested

This is the job itself. A Weatherbys banker's career depends on whether clients trust them enough to bring the next loan, refer a friend, or stay through a bad year. The bank describes success in a banker's first year as "a strong internal network" and "a growing base of engaged clients" [S9]. Notice the second half of the question: "How did you do it?" More than half your words should go on method, not on the setting.

Choosing the right story

Strong choicesWeak choices
Someone different from you in age, background or outlook (an older customer, a client, a mentee, a colleague who was cold at first). Closest to a private banker's reality.Your best friend, a partner or a sibling. The relationship may be real but the skill is not professional.
A relationship that started badly or had a setback you repaired, such as a complaint, a missed deadline or a disagreement.A relationship that was easy from day one. There is nothing to show.
One where trust is visible: they came back, asked for you by name, gave you responsibility, introduced you to someone.One where the only evidence is "we got on well".
A long period (months or years) with repeated contact.A single event, such as a weekend group project.

Methods worth describing (pick two or three that you actually used)

  • Listening before acting: what you asked, what you learned that others had missed.
  • Remembering and following up: details you noted and used later. This is the core of private banking ("Banking that knows you" [S16]).
  • Being reliable in small things: doing exactly what you said, by when you said.
  • Telling an uncomfortable truth well: saying no, or giving bad news early and with an alternative. This maps to the bank's "Straightforward" and "Trusted" values [S4].
  • Adapting to how they communicate: phone instead of email, in person, simpler language.
  • Connecting them to someone else who could help: the banker's job is often to bring in the right specialist.
Frame: In [setting and dates], I [role] and was responsible for [the person: who they were, why the relationship mattered]. At first [the gap: e.g. "she had complained twice and asked not to deal with students"].

I did three things. First, I [listening action], because [why]. That showed me [what you learned that changed your approach]. Second, I [reliability action, with a specific detail: a note, a call, a deadline kept]. Third, when [a setback], I [how you handled honesty or a problem], rather than [the easier option].

[Proof: "Six months later she asked for me by name, and introduced her neighbour."]

I learned that trust is built in the small, repeated things, and that is the part of private banking I most want to learn.
Aim for 280 to 300 words. Put a number in the proof if you have one: months, visits, referrals, a result.
Sentence upgrades
Weak: "I built rapport by being friendly and approachable."
Strong: "I started writing down what he mentioned each week, his daughter's exams or his knee operation, and asked about it the next time. By the third visit he was waiting for me at the door."
Weak: "We worked well together and achieved a great result."
Strong: "When I realised I had given her the wrong date, I rang her that evening instead of emailing, explained, and offered two alternatives. She told my manager it was why she trusted us."

A4. Question 3: "What extracurricular activities, sports, societies or voluntary work are you involved in?" (250 words)

What is really being tested

  • Initiative and work ethic outside the timetable. What did you start, lead, sustain or improve?
  • Collaboration. How do you act in a team?
  • "Would a client enjoy an hour with this person?" Private banking is social. The firm's own staff profiles mention "a royal archer" and a "whisky blending banker" [S25]. Interesting, sustained interests count Inferred.
  • Values fit. The bank runs a volunteering and mentoring programme, "Creating The Future", with match-funded staff fundraising (£115k donated in 2025) and 35 mentors working with 80+ students [S4].

The structure: depth, breadth and a thread

Stand-out moves for Q3

  1. Show contribution, not membership. "Member of the finance society" scores low. "Ran the society's speaker series, booking six practitioners and raising attendance from 20 to 55" scores high.
  2. Include one activity with people outside university, such as volunteering, coaching juniors, a part-time job or a community role. Private clients are mostly older than you and outside your world. Showing you can relate to them is valuable.
  3. Count sustained commitment. "Every Saturday for three years" beats a list of ten one-off events.
  4. Link lightly to Weatherbys only where it is true. For example: riding, polo, rural or farming life, mentoring (their Creating The Future programme), a charity that matches one of their 2026 partners (Alzheimer's Research UK, CALM, The Wildlife Trust BCN) [S4]. One genuine link is plenty. None is fine.
  5. Use the last two lines to name the pattern. For example: "Across all three, I end up being the person others rely on to organise and follow through, and I enjoy it."
Frame: My main commitment is [activity], which I have done for [time]. As [role], I [two specific things you did beyond attending]. The result was [a number or visible change]. The hardest part was [a real difficulty], which I handled by [action].

I also [second activity, one line with one detail] and [third, one line].

What joins them is [the trait]. It is also why I am drawn to a relationship-led bank, where [link in one clause].
If you have a striking personal interest (an instrument to a high grade, a sport at county level, an unusual hobby), give it one line. It is a conversation starter at the assessment centre.
