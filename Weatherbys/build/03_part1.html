1.3 The private banker's job, which is what you are being assessed for

  • Portfolio. A banker covers "a portfolio of 100+ private banking clients" [S24] Reported, as their "trusted point of contact across banking, lending and investments".
  • Support. Each client gets a "named private banker and assistant from day one" [S16]. Investment advice comes from the in-house IWA team; tax questions can go to a Senior Tax Adviser [S9][S13].
  • Lending. Residential mortgages, bridging loans, secured loans and Lombard loans (borrowing against an investment portfolio, up to 50% loan-to-value) [S17][S18]. Underwriting is "flexible" rather than "fixed criteria and automated credit scoring" [S17].
  • Winning clients. Business development is "very" important "but in a relationship-led way" [S9]. Referrals come from existing clients and professional introducers such as lawyers and accountants [S164].
  • What success looks like in year one: "a strong internal network, a growing base of engaged clients, sound judgement, confidence in lending and investment conversations, contribution to team culture" [S9].
Key terms
Loan-to-value (LTV): loan size divided by the value of the security. A £340k loan on a £1m house is 34% LTV. Lower means safer for the bank.
Lombard loan: a loan secured on an investment portfolio rather than property.
Bridging loan: a short loan to cover a gap, for example buying a new house before the old one sells.
KYC / AML: Know Your Customer and Anti-Money Laundering. The checks a bank must do on who a client is and where their money comes from.
