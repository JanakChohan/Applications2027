Why the firm is built this way

Four questions: why the structure, how the teams work together, what clients are really buying, and what the rest of the group does. This is the material that makes "why Weatherbys" more than adjectives.

3.1 One banker, the whole client: why that design?

Most large banks split a wealthy client across specialists: a mortgage team, an investment manager, a call centre for daily banking. Weatherbys gives one banker the whole relationship, with specialists behind them rather than in front of the client [S9][S16].

Sources: "Named private banker and assistant from day one" [S16]; holistic across lending, IWA investment and daily banking [S9]; human credit decisions [S17]. The left panel is a stylised alternative, not a description of any named bank [Inferred].

The theory behind it

  1. Wealthy clients' affairs are complex, and the complexity lives between products. A farm owner's mortgage, inheritance plan and business account all interact. Only someone who sees all three can spot the problem or opportunity Inferred.
  2. Human credit decisions win the clients that computers reject. The CEO's example is racing syndicates: "You can try and open an account for it with a High Street bank, but it would be close to impossible. The computer says: 'No!'" [S15]. Lending is "flexible underwriting rather than fixed criteria" with no upper age limit, and land and investment portfolios are accepted as security [S17].
  3. The relationship is the product. Board member Rory Tapner: "Pushing products won't work for the type of clients Weatherbys seeks to attract" [S4]. The careers page: "exceptional service, trust and holistic advice rather than product pushing" [S9].
  4. Low risk follows from knowing the client. The bank says it is conservative because it knows its borrowers, and its loans average about 34% loan-to-value [S4] Inferred link.

What the design costs

  • It does not scale infinitely. The CEO says "the upper limit for being able to be so flexible is 10,000 to 15,000 private banking relationships" [S15]. Growth means hiring bankers, which is expensive. That is one reason costs rose 19% in 2025 [S4].
  • Key-person risk. If a banker leaves, clients may follow. That is why the bank invests in culture and in growing its own bankers through the APB programme Inferred.
  • Judgement must be controlled. Discretion at the front line needs strong credit oversight, and the bank has a board Risk Committee chaired by Jane Millar [S4].

3.2 How the teams work together

The firm's words for its culture are "collaborative, high-trust and client-obsessed" [S9]. In practice collaboration lives in four places:

  • Banker and IWA: the investment team works behind the banker. Investment assets under advice grew 30% in 2025, 74% of it from new money [S4].
  • Banker and credit: every loan is a conversation between the banker, who knows the client, and the credit team, who protect the balance sheet.
  • Across segments: a racing owner may also be a farm owner and an entrepreneur. Segment teams are labels for expertise, not walls Inferred.
  • Across the group: referrals between the Private Bank, Racing Bank, Business Bank and Arkle Inferred.

The counter-case, with numbers
Clients also accept trade-offs. Weatherbys is small: £1.9bn of deposits against £6.4bn at Hoare's and £41.4bn in NatWest's wealth division, which includes Coutts [S4][S105][S103]. It has fewer branches, less in-house investment scale and a narrower digital offer than the large banks (the new digital bank was only due in 2026 [S4]). Minimum criteria are not published, so it is unclear how selective it is [S16].

3.4 The rest of the group: Racing Bank, Business Bank and Arkle

  • Racing Bank: the original community. It offers racing accounts, a Racehorse VAT Return Service, a Racing Payment Service and syndicate accounts [S21]. In 2025 it changed onboarding for multi-owner syndicates after industry feedback [S4].
  • Arkle Finance: asset finance (loans to buy equipment and vehicles), 32% of the group's loan book. 2025 profit before tax was £5.6m, up from £3.5m. The aim is to double to "£600 million of lending by 2030" [S4].
  • Business Bank: launched in Q4 2025 for owner-managed businesses and "specialist or complex sectors" such as charities and high-growth companies [S4].
  • Central functions: the Bank has 396 staff, most of them outside the front line [S4] Inferred. A Third Party Risk Management Specialist role in Wellingborough was live on 7 October 2026 [S35].

Don't overclaim
Weatherbys is not the oldest UK bank: Hoare's (1672) and Coutts (1692) are older, and Weatherbys' banking licence dates from 1994 [S5][S121][S120]. It is not the biggest or the most profitable. Its 2025 return on equity of 8.9% is "below our long-term aspirations" [S4]. Its digital offer is still being built [S4]. Say "one of the UK's few remaining family-owned private banks", not "the oldest" or "the leading".
