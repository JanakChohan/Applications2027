11.3 Eight ways interviewers test your frame, and the counter

TacticWhat it looks likeCounter
InterruptionCut off mid-answerStop, listen, answer the new point, then: "Coming back to what I was saying..."
Flat contradiction"That's wrong.""Can I ask which part? If I've missed something I'd like to understand it." Do not cave if your reasoning holds.

11.6 The CV grill

Interviewers often go three levels deep on one line: what did you do, why did you do it that way, and what would you do differently. The goal is to see whether the story is really yours.

AttackFrame-holding answer
"What exactly did you do?"Separate your actions from the team's, with one concrete detail.
"That doesn't sound very impressive.""It was a small role. What I learned was X, and I used it when..."
"Why did you stop?"An honest reason without blame, and what you moved on to.
"What went wrong?"A real problem, your part in it, the fix.
"Your grades dipped here."Facts, no excuses, what changed after.
"How does this relate to banking?"One transferable skill, linked to a banker's year-one success measures [S9].
"Your form answer said X; tell me more."Know every story in your form to three levels. Keep the same facts.
"Why should we pick you over someone with banking experience?""I don't have their experience. I've shown I build relationships that last, I've done the research, and I'm here to learn the Weatherbys way."
Four-line drill for every CV bullet
1. What happened, in one sentence. 2. My specific action and why. 3. The result, with a number if possible. 4. What I'd do differently, and what it taught me about working with people.

"Why Weatherbys" in three lengths

Mechanism, not adjectives. Each version would need rewriting to work for any other bank.

Thirty seconds

"Weatherbys is built so that the person who knows the client makes the decision: one banker covers lending, investments and banking, and credit decisions are made by people rather than scoring models. That's the job I want to learn. It's also growing while staying deliberately small enough to keep that flexibility, which means an intern is close to real work. And [personal link]."

Two minutes

Add to the thirty-second version: (1) one segment and a live issue, e.g. farms and estates and the April 2026 APR/BPR cap; (2) evidence that the model works, such as NPS 84 and Private Bank of the Year 2026; (3) evidence of momentum, such as experienced Coutts bankers joining, the Business Bank, and Lombard lending for external portfolios; (4) the APB route and the qualification you'd take.

The close: one specific thing you want to work on

"If I could pick one area, it would be [farms and estates / sport and entertainment / racing], because [reason linked to your own life], and because [the live issue: e.g. succession planning after the April 2026 changes] is where a banker who understands the whole family adds most."
