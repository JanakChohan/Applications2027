## 1. FINDINGS

### 1.1 What private banking is, and how it differs from other kinds of banking

| Type | Who it serves | What it does | How it mainly makes money |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retail banking** | The mass market | "Savings and current accounts, mortgages, personal loans, credit cards, and digital payments", with "standardized products, automated processes" [S163] | Interest margin earned at volume, plus fees |
| **Private banking** | High-net-worth (HNW) individuals, families and their businesses | Personal banking: current accounts, deposits, bespoke lending (large or complex mortgages and loans), payments, FX, all delivered by a named relationship manager. It "does not necessarily involve investing a client's assets" [S163] | Mostly net interest income on deposits and loans, plus some fees |
| **Wealth management** | Affluent and HNW clients | Investment management (discretionary or advisory), financial planning, estate and tax planning, succession [S163] | Fees, usually a percentage of assets under management (AUM) |
| **Investment banking** | Corporates, governments and institutions | M&A advice, raising capital, trading | Advisory fees, underwriting and trading |

The firms in the UK peer set mix these models. Some are **pure banks**. C. Hoare & Co calls itself "a pure banking service" that is "free to work with our customers' professional advisers" and makes "independent referrals" [S105]. Others are **integrated**, combining banking with in-house investment management: Coutts, Arbuthnot Latham, Barclays Private Bank, and Weatherbys through partners. Rathbones is mainly a **wealth manager** that also offers banking services [S131].

**How a private bank makes money (the basics)**
- **Net interest margin (NIM).** The bank takes client deposits and pays interest on them. It earns more than it pays, either by lending the money out (mortgages and loans to HNW clients and their businesses) or by placing it with the Bank of England (BoE). NIM is the gap between the two, measured against interest-earning assets.
- **Exposure to the base rate.** Much of a deposit-heavy private bank's money sits as liquidity, earning roughly the base rate. When the base rate falls, income falls too:
  - Hoare's: "While a fall in base rate reduces the bank's net interest margin, the bank is well positioned to offset further rate reductions with growth in the underlying business" [S105]. Its income fell from £271.7m to £249.4m in FY to March 2025, "due to a reduction in base rate (from its peak of 5.25%… to 4.50%) and a higher proportion of deposit customers moving to more stable, higher-paying products" [S105].
  - Arbuthnot: has £523m of "unhedged free cash". "The average base rate during the period was 3.75%, down from 4.48%" [S110].
  - NatWest's Private Banking & Wealth Management (PB&WM) segment shows the opposite effect of hedging: income rose 10.4% in H1 2026, "primarily reflecting deposit margin expansion from hedge income benefit" [S103].
- **Fees.** Investment management (AUM-based), advice, account and service fees, and FX. Rathbones agreed to stop "charging fees on the cash element of client portfolios", which cuts income by about £9m over six months. This shows how regulators scrutinise fees on cash [S112].
- **Low loan-to-deposit ratios.** Many private banks lend out far less than they hold in deposits. Weatherbys' loan-to-deposit ratio was 50% (2024) [S150]. Hoare's had deposits of £6,427m against lending of £2,352m, with 24% of deposits held at the BoE [S105]. That is why the base rate matters so much to their profits.

**What a relationship manager (private banker) does day to day** [S164, a generic job-description aggregation, so lower confidence]
- Manages a portfolio of HNW clients through "frequent client meetings and communications".
- Wins new clients and handles referrals from professional introducers (lawyers, accountants, wealth managers).
- Spots cross-sell opportunities, such as lending, investments and FX.
- Structures loans within the bank's "credit appetite", including term sheets and legal documents.
- Stays compliant with financial-crime (KYC/AML) and regulatory rules.
- A leading private bank RM "may serve as few as 20 clients" [S163]. Treat this as an illustrative US-sourced figure; UK RMs at smaller banks usually carry more.
- Weatherbys targets 10,000–15,000 private banking relationships as its "optimal scalable limit" [S150].

**What this means for smaller independent private banks.** The landscape now splits into two groups:
1. **Large groups consolidating:** NatWest/Coutts plus Evelyn, Rathbones plus Investec Wealth, UBP plus Kleinwort Hambros.
2. **A shrinking number of independent, mostly UK-owned banks:** Hoare's, Weatherbys, Arbuthnot, Hampden. These compete on service, flexible "common-sense" lending and continuity of relationship.

Legacy brands have been folded into their parents (Child & Co, Adam & Co), and some foreign owners have left (Butterfield 2016, SocGen 2025). That reduces competition for clients who want an independent relationship bank, and makes them available to firms like Weatherbys and Hampden. Hampden's 2026 results press explicitly credits Adam & Co's earlier changes [S165-snippet].

### 1.4 Structural trends

1. **The great wealth transfer.** King's Court Trust estimates **£5.5 trillion** will pass between generations in the UK from 2017 to 2055, peaking around 2035. 69% of advisers had no plan for it, and 46% fear losing AUM when a client dies [S158-snippet]. Combined with APR/BPR (2026) and pensions-in-IHT (2027), this makes **succession and next-generation relationships** central.
2. **Digitalisation.** Private banks now pitch "high-touch plus high-tech":
   - Weatherbys: "100 per cent human, 100 per cent digital", and appointed fintech NED Aileen Wallace in Jan 2026 [S150].
   - Hoare's: investing in "refreshing our technology" and app features [S105].
   - L.E.K. (Dec 2025): "around 70% of financial institutions still struggle to scale AI" [S160].
   - EY via WealthBriefing (Jul 2026): technology/AI was "the single most important driver of dealmaking decisions" [S159].
3. **HNW migration.**
   - Henley Private Wealth Migration Report 2025 projected a net loss of **16,500 millionaires** from the UK in 2025, the world's largest outflow [S161].
   - The 2026 edition is reported to project a further record outflow [S162-snippet; exact 2026 number unverified].
   - HMRC (reported 31 Jul 2026): non-dom taxpayers fell from 83,100 to 81,900 [S149].
   - Caveat: Henley's figures are projections and are widely debated.
4. **Consolidation.**
   - Per EY via WealthBriefing (7 Jul 2026): UK financial services M&A value rose from £4.2bn (H1 2025) to £33.7bn (H1 2026). Wealth and asset management deal value rose from £200m to £22.7bn [S159].
   - Deals: NatWest–Evelyn £2.7bn (completed 30 Jun 2026) [S103][S166]; UBP–Kleinwort Hambros (2025) [S125]; Rathbones–Investec Wealth (2023) [S131]; Canaccord–EFG Harris Allday (£3.1bn AUM, 19 Aug 2026) [S167]; Oaktree–Close Brothers Asset Management (2025) [S133].
5. **Competition for deposits.**
   - Hoare's: deposits grew 5% "despite the headwind of quantitative tightening and competition from tax-advantaged gilts" [S105].
   - Clients are moving to "more stable, higher-paying products" [S105].
   - NatWest PB&WM deposits fell £1.3bn in H1 2026 due to "seasonal client tax outflows" [S103].
   - Hampden grew deposits 36% in a year [S128], which shows that smaller banks can win deposits by paying for them.

**Three competing views on where UK private banking goes in 3–5 years** (my synthesis from the sources above, not a single source's forecast):
- **View A: scale wins.** Bank-owned "one-stop" franchises (NatWest/Coutts+Evelyn at £131bn AUMA; Barclays; UBP) combine banking, discretionary management and advice platforms. They spread technology and regulatory costs across more clients and squeeze subscale independents. Evidence: the M&A surge and £100m of NatWest–Evelyn synergies [S159][S166].
- **View B: boutique and relationship banks win.** As the big brands raise minimum thresholds and standardise service, entrepreneurs, landed families and professionals who want a named banker, flexible lending and quick decisions move to independents. Evidence: Hampden +36% deposits and NPS 75 [S128]; Weatherbys NPS 81 vs an industry average of 30, and an award [S150-snippet]; Hoare's NPS +74 [S105]; Investec targeting 5,000 extra UK clients [S113]. The SDDT regime (2027) lowers fixed regulatory costs for small banks [S143].
- **View C: tax and policy reshape demand more than competition does.** Non-dom abolition, APR/BPR caps and pensions-in-IHT shift the value of a private bank from deposit-taking towards **advice, succession, liquidity planning and lending around tax events**. Meanwhile, a rate path now debated in both directions (cuts in 2024–25, hike votes in Sep 2026) keeps NIM volatile. Winners will be those who can earn fees and lend against complex assets (land, businesses, bloodstock), not just hold cash [S152][S154][S102].

### 1.6 Graduate programmes and how applications are assessed

**Coutts (NatWest Group)**
- Offers a Relationship Management **summer internship** (search snippet says eight weeks): RM capability training, culture and values, workshops, "a business improvement project", and HNW product knowledge [S168-snippet].
- Interns are "assessed on what you've learned". Success can lead to an offer on the **Coutts Relationship Management graduate programme** [S168-snippet].
- Insight experiences, such as for Black-heritage talent in Coutts RM, lead to an **assessment centre** [S168-snippet].
- Make only one application: "if you make more than one they'll only accept your first" [S168-snippet].
- The Bright Network page for the 2025 internship returned 403, so I could not read it in full.

**C. Hoare & Co**
- A **24-month rotational** graduate scheme across "up to six areas". Each graduate gets a Buddy, a Mentor, a Line Manager for each rotation, and a People-team contact [S169-snippet].

**Arbuthnot Latham**
- **Two-year, department-specific** graduate programmes on **permanent contracts**, with "frequent exposure to senior leaders". 2025/26 listings were IT and COO functions [S170-snippet].

**How UK graduate recruiters assess applications**
- **Volume.** ISE Student Recruitment Survey 2025: about **140 applications per vacancy** (a record); graduate hiring fell 8% in 2024/25 [S171-snippet]. ISE Student Development Survey 2025 (published 9 May 2025): 48% of employers worry that AI use misrepresents graduate skills; 77% say interns arrive better prepared [S172].
- **Competency marking.** Answers are usually scored against a behavioural scale. The best published UK public-sector rubric is the Civil Service "Success Profiles" scale, which I read verbatim at COPFS [S173]: 1 Not demonstrated … 4 "Acceptable Demonstration: Adequate positive evidence and any negative evidence would not cause concern" … 7 "Outstanding Demonstration: The evidence provided wholly exceeds expectation at this level". The pass mark is typically 4+ in all behaviours. Other employers use 0–3 per competency [S174-snippet]. Private-sector banks do not publish their rubrics, but they typically score similarly: evidence of the behaviour, your personal role ("I" not "we"), and a measurable result.
- **STAR.** Situation, Task, Action, Result. Explain what *you* did, include team size and timescales, and quantify the outcome. Typical prompts are commercial awareness ("challenges facing our industry"), persuasion and teamwork [S175-snippet].

## 6. APPLICATION AND INTERVIEW ANGLES (Weatherbys private banking internship)

1. **Explain the business model simply.** "A private bank like Weatherbys takes deposits from wealthy clients and lends a portion back through bespoke mortgages and loans, earning the margin. It also earns fees. Because loan-to-deposit ratios are low (Weatherbys about 50% in 2024), profits are sensitive to Bank Rate. That's why the September 2026 6–3 hold, with three members voting to *raise* rates, matters."
2. **Lead on the tax changes for Weatherbys' client base.** Landed estates, farms, bloodstock and family businesses are hit by the APR/BPR cap from 6 April 2026 (£2.5m, or £5m per couple; 20% effective rate above that) and by pensions coming into IHT from April 2027. Show you understand why clients now need succession conversations, liquidity planning and lending to fund IHT. Also show you understand that an RM coordinates with the client's lawyers and accountants rather than giving tax advice.
3. **"Why a boutique, not Coutts?"** Use the consolidation evidence: NatWest–Evelyn, UBP–Kleinwort Hambros, Rathbones–Investec Wealth, Canaccord–EFG Harris Allday, and the eight-fold rise in M&A value. Then contrast with independents winning on service: Weatherbys NPS 81 and Private Bank of the Year 2026; Hampden NPS 75 and +36% deposits; Hoare's NPS +74. Mention "100 per cent human, 100 per cent digital".
4. **Talk about Consumer Duty in practical terms.** Cover vulnerable clients (Weatherbys hires dementia specialists), fair value, and the FCA's July 2026 review finding firms "describing processes… without demonstrating" adaptation. Add the Rathbones cash-fee review as an example of regulators testing value.
5. **Raise deposit protection and safety for HNW clients.** The FSCS limit rose to £120k (1 Dec 2025), and temporary high balances are covered up to £1.4m. Many private clients exceed these limits, so capital strength matters (Weatherbys: total capital 16.3%, LCR 1,079% in 2024).
6. **Mention small-bank regulation.** The SDDT "Strong and Simple" capital regime starts 1 Jan 2027 and Basel 3.1 starts the same day. Simpler rules could free resources at small banks. Ask the interviewer how it affects Weatherbys rather than asserting its status.
7. **Prepare a commercial-awareness answer with two sides.** Trends include wealth transfer (£5.5trn by 2055), HNW migration (non-dom taxpayers 83,100 → 81,900, with Henley projections contested), and rate uncertainty. A domestic, rural-rooted client base is less exposed to non-dom flight than London-international banks, but more exposed to APR/BPR.
8. **Competency answers.** Use STAR with "I" not "we", quantify the result, and aim for evidence that would score at least "Acceptable" (4/7) on every behaviour and "Strong/Outstanding" on one or two. Expect RM-relevant behaviours: relationship building and trust, communication, attention to detail and compliance, commercial judgement, resilience. ISE says employers worry that AI-written applications misrepresent skills, so use specific, personal detail.
9. **Questions to ask at interview.**
   - How has the APR/BPR change altered conversations with farming clients?
   - How does the business bank (launched autumn 2025) interact with private clients?
   - How does the bank balance growth towards 10–15k relationships with service quality?
