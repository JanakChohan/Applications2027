Chapter 2

What is an insight day?

In one line

An insight day is a short taster. A company invites students in to see what the work is really like, long before they apply for a job.

Think of it as a test drive

You would not buy a car without driving it. An insight day lets you “drive” a career for a day or two. You see the office, meet the staff and try a small version of the work.

It also works the other way. The company gets an early look at you.

How it compares

Insight dayInternshipGraduate job
How longOne or two daysSeveral weeksPermanent
What you doWorkshops and exercisesReal work, supervisedReal work, with targets
WhenEarly at universityUsually the summer before final yearAfter you graduate
Risk to youAlmost noneA summerA career choice
What it leads toA head start on later applicationsOften a job offerA career
Why this matters to you

An insight day is the lowest-risk way to explore a career. You give up two days. In return you learn whether to spend the next two years aiming for that job. Few decisions offer that much information for that little cost.

Chapter 5

Eight reasons to want it

In one line

Some reasons are about getting a job. The best ones are about learning: about the work, about business, and about yourself.

1. A head start on a graduate job

Graduate jobs at firms like this are competitive. Most applicants are strangers to the recruiters. After the Insight Days you are not. The team has met you, seen you work and put you on the fast-track list.

Why it matters: you would be applying in your final year with an advantage most candidates do not have.

2. You try the job before you choose it

The client brief simulation puts you in the Associate’s seat. A client asks for something. You work out who could help.

Why it matters: job adverts all sound good. Doing a small version of the work is the only honest test of whether you would enjoy it.

3. Skills you keep, wherever you end up

Negotiation, resilience and commercial confidence are useful in any career: law, banking, consulting, sales or starting a business. They are also hard to learn in a lecture hall.

Why it matters: even if you never work at AlphaSights, you leave better prepared for every interview and job that follows.

4. A window into how big decisions are made

AlphaSights’ clients are private equity firms, hedge funds and strategy consultancies. To serve them, you have to understand what they do and why they need to learn fast.

Why it matters: in two days you get a guided tour of three industries that are very hard to see inside as a student. That is real commercial awareness.

5. You meet the people

You hear the career stories of Associates and Managers, many only a few years older than you. You also meet other students with similar ambitions.

Why it matters: people tell you things a website never will. And the students in that room are your future network.

6. It costs you nothing but two days

Travel, accommodation, breakfast and lunch are all covered.

Why it matters: the event is open to you whatever your budget or wherever you live. The only thing you spend is time.

7. A strong name early on your CV

AlphaSights made $652.6 million in revenue in 2025 and is one of the two largest firms in its industry. Audited Industry estimate Being selected shows other employers that a demanding firm picked you.

Why it matters: it is evidence of commercial interest at a stage when most students have none to show.

8. It tells you whether this career suits you

The Associate job is fast, target-driven and involves a lot of rejection. Some people love that. Some do not.

Why it matters: finding out that you do not want a job is as valuable as finding out that you do. Either answer saves you time.

Chapter 7

The honest view

In one line

The Insight Days are a very good opportunity with almost no downside. The job they lead to is a bigger question, and the event is how you answer it.

What the Insight Days are not

  • Not a job offer. You still have to apply and interview in your final year.
  • Not a guarantee. “Fast-tracked” is a head start, not a promise.
  • Not an internship. Two days will not make you an expert, and the advert does not mention pay.
  • Not just a nice day out. You are being observed, especially in the simulation.

The job behind it: both sides

You should go in with open eyes. The Associate role has real attractions and real demands.

What people value
  • Responsibility for clients from the start
  • Pay linked to your results
  • Fast promotion on merit
  • Learning a new industry every day
  • A young, ambitious team
What people find hard
  • Monthly targets from the outset
  • Lots of outreach and many people who do not reply
  • Long, intense days tied to client demand
  • Plans that change without warning

Employee reviews are mixed. Glassdoor shows about 3.2 out of 5 for the Client Service Associate role. Some former staff praise the training and the doors it opens. Others say it depends heavily on your team, and views differ on how easy it is to move into other industries afterwards. Industry estimate

Why this makes the Insight Days more valuable, not less

A job that some people love and some people leave is exactly the kind you should test before committing. The Insight Days let you do that for free. Go in curious, ask Associates what a hard day looks like, and judge for yourself.

Questions worth asking while you are there

  • “What does a difficult day look like, and how do you handle it?”
  • “What surprised you most in your first three months?”
  • “What kind of person does well here, and who struggles?”
  • “Where have people you started with gone on to?”
Chapter 8

Getting the most from it

In one line

If you are invited, treat the two days as a long, friendly interview and a chance to learn. Prepare, take part and follow up.

March or June?

You may be asked which dates you can do. Check both against your university calendar before the call.

DatesThings to weigh
23 and 24 March 2027Earlier, so you are known to the firm sooner. Check it does not clash with end-of-term deadlines or exams.
30 June and 1 July 2027After most exam seasons, so you may have more time to prepare. Check it does not clash with a summer job or internship.

If both work, say so. Being flexible makes you easier to place.

Before, during and after

In the client brief simulation

From Part 3, the pattern that works in almost any client situation:

  1. Respond fast. Show you are on it.
  2. Ask before you assume. Find the real question behind the brief.
  3. Be honest. Say what you can and cannot deliver.
  4. Offer a way forward. Always suggest a next step.

In a group exercise, how you treat team-mates is noticed as much as your ideas. Bring quieter people in. Build on what others say.

Chapter 9

“Why did you apply?”

In one line

Give three reasons: one about the work, one about what you will learn, and one about AlphaSights. Tie at least one to something true about you.

Build your own answer

Pick one line from each row. Then add your own detail where you see the blank.

PartChoose one
1. The work “I want a commercial, client-facing career, and I would rather test that by doing it.”

“I enjoy persuading people and working to a goal. I saw that when I ______.”

“I like learning new subjects quickly, and the Associate role is built on that.”
2. The learning “The client brief simulation is the closest I can get to trying the real job.”

“The skills you focus on, like negotiation and resilience, are ones I want to build early.”

“I want to understand how investors and consultants make decisions.”
3. The firm “AlphaSights gives Associates real client responsibility from the start.”

“It is still led by its founders and has grown into one of the largest firms in its field.”

“I would like to meet the people and hear how their careers have developed.”

A worked example

About 40 seconds

“There are three reasons. The first is that I am drawn to client-facing, commercial work. In my role at ______ I found I enjoyed ______, and I want to test whether that is the right direction for a career. The second is the format. A client brief simulation is the closest I can get to actually trying the Associate job, which will tell me far more than reading about it. And the third is AlphaSights itself. I like that Associates are trusted with clients from the start, and I would value the chance to meet the team and understand what makes someone succeed here.”

Likely follow-up questions

“What do you hope to get out of the two days?”

“A real feel for the Associate role, some practice at the skills, and honest conversations with people who do the job.”

“Why AlphaSights and not another firm’s insight day?”

Use one specific point: Associates own client projects from the start, or the firm finds experts fresh for each project, or it is one of the two largest in its industry.

“What do you think the Associate role involves?”

“Taking a client’s request, working out who would know the answer, finding and speaking to those experts, and connecting them with the client quickly.”

“Are you considering the graduate role afterwards?”

Be honest and positive. “Yes, that is a big part of why I applied. I want to use the Insight Days to confirm it is the right fit.”

“Which dates suit you?”

Know your answer before the call.

Three things to avoid

  • Leading with the perks. Do not mention the free travel or the CV boost as reasons.
  • Making it only about the fast track. Show interest in the work, not just the shortcut.
  • Reciting. Speak in your own words, with a pause, as if you are thinking. Remember: no AI tools or notes on screen during the call.
One last thought before 2:30pm

You have done far more preparation than most candidates will. You do not need to show all of it. Be warm, be clear, and be curious. Good luck.
