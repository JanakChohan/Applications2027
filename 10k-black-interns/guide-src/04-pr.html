D1 · What PR is (and isn't)

PR is the strategic management of communication between an organisation and the people who matter to it (its stakeholders), to build understanding, trust and reputation.

The simplest distinction: advertising is what you say about yourself in space you paid for. PR is getting others, such as journalists, experts, customers and influencers, to say it for you. Because it's third-party, it's more credible. Because you don't control it, it's harder.
AdvertisingMarketingPublic relations
GoalDrive sales or awarenessDrive demand for productsBuild reputation, trust and understanding
AudienceCustomersCustomersAll stakeholders: media, investors, staff, government, communities, customers
ControlFull: you buy the spaceHighLow: journalists decide what to write
CredibilityLower ("they would say that")MediumHigher (third-party endorsement)
Includes bad news?NoRarelyYes: crisis, issues, results, job cuts

The PESO model: the four types of media

Paid
Adverts, sponsored content, paid social, advertorials
Earned
Press coverage, interviews, reviews, awards. The core of PR.
Shared
Social media, influencers, word of mouth, reposts
Owned
Website, blog, reports, newsletters, the company's own channels

Modern PR works across all four ("integrated communications"). A research report (owned) can generate a news story (earned), be amplified on LinkedIn (shared) and be promoted with a paid boost (paid).

Why reputation is worth money

Customers pay more and stay loyal to brands they trust
Investors value companies they understand and trust more highly, while scandals can wipe billions off share prices in a day
Employees, regulators and government: a good reputation attracts talent, eases regulation and earns a "licence to operate"
Warren Buffett's line is quoted constantly in PR: "It takes 20 years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it." Reputation is an intangible asset: you can't see it on a balance sheet, but it shows up in sales, share prices and hiring. PR people see themselves as its guardians. Framing PR as protecting and growing a valuable asset, not just "getting press", is the expert view.

Agency side

An agency serves multiple clients at once, so you get variety, fast learning and pace. Agencies range from global networks to small specialists. Most PR internships are agency-side.

In-house side

The communications team inside one organisation (a bank, a retailer, a charity, an asset manager), often called "Corporate Communications" or "Press Office". You go deeper on one organisation and get closer to senior decision-making.

D3 · How a PR campaign works

Good PR is strategic, not just "sending press releases". The standard planning model (often taught as RACE or ROSIE: research, objectives, strategy, implementation, evaluation) looks like this:

1 · ResearchSituation, audience, media landscape, competitors, current perceptions
›
2 · ObjectivesSMART goals: e.g. "raise awareness among UK DC pension decision-makers"
›
3 · AudiencesWho exactly must think, feel or do something different?
›
4 · MessagesThe 3–4 key messages and the proof points behind each
›
5 · TacticsPress releases, interviews, research, events, social, influencers
›
6 · ImplementTimeline, media pitching, spokespeople, content
›
7 · EvaluateOutputs → outcomes → business impact

Key messages and the message house

How PR is measured

LevelWhat's measuredExamples
OutputsWhat you produced and gotNumber of articles, reach, share of voice, key message pick-up
OuttakesWhat the audience took from itEngagement, recall, understanding
OutcomesChanges in attitude or behaviourTrust scores, consideration, sign-ups, enquiries
ImpactBusiness resultsSales, recruitment, share price stability, policy change
The Barcelona Principles (from AMEC, the measurement industry body) say PR should be measured by outcomes and impact, not just volume, and they reject AVEs (advertising value equivalents, "this coverage would have cost £X as an advert") as a measure of value. Knowing this shows you understand the profession's standards, not just its activities.

D4 · Media relations: how news works

To get coverage you must understand journalists: what they need, and what makes something news.

News values: what makes a story

New
Genuinely new information
Relevant
Matters to that outlet's readers
Timely
Linked to what's happening now
Data
Original numbers and research
Human
Real people and real stories
Conflict / surprise
Tension or the unexpected
Prominence
Well-known people or brands
Exclusivity
"First" or "only here"

The press release: inverted pyramid

Headline + first paragraph: who, what, when, where, why
Key details and supporting facts
Quote from a spokesperson
Background context
"Notes to editors", contacts

Journalists read the top and decide in seconds, and editors cut from the bottom. So the most important information goes first.

Notes to editors hold background facts and a short company description (the boilerplate).

An embargo means "you can't publish until this date and time". It's used to give journalists time to prepare.

Media relations vocabulary

Pitch: a short, tailored email selling a story idea to a specific journalist
Exclusive: giving one outlet a story first, in exchange for better coverage
On the record: everything can be quoted and attributed
Off the record / background: information not to be quoted or attributed (terms must be agreed beforehand)
Spokesperson: the person authorised to speak for the organisation
Media training: preparing spokespeople for interviews (staying on message, handling tough questions)
Bridging: moving from a difficult question to a key message ("What's important here is…")
Media list: a targeted list of relevant journalists and their interests
Newsjacking: linking your expert commentary to a breaking news story
Forward features: magazines' planned future topics, which create pitching opportunities
Wire service: a distribution service for press releases (e.g. PA Media, Business Wire)
Coverage report: a summary of what was published, where, reach and tone

Types of media

National (BBC, FT, The Times, Guardian, Sky), regional (Manchester Evening News), trade / B2B (specialist titles for one industry, e.g. Citywire, Investment Week and Professional Pensions for asset management; PRWeek for PR), broadcast (TV and radio), online-only and podcasts, and creators/influencers.

The relationship is give-and-take. Journalists are under enormous time pressure, with fewer of them covering more topics. PR people who reliably provide accurate information, good spokespeople, data and fast responses become trusted sources. Those who spam irrelevant press releases get ignored. "Thinking like a journalist" is the core PR skill.

D5 · Crisis communications

A crisis is an event that threatens an organisation's reputation, operations or finances and demands urgent communication. An issue is a slower-burning problem that could become a crisis if ignored.

1 · PrepareCrisis plan, scenarios, holding statements, a trained team, a "dark site"
›
2 · Respond fastFirst statement within the "golden hour": acknowledge, show concern, commit to action
›
3 · ManageRegular updates, one voice, all stakeholders (staff, customers, regulators, media)
›
4 · RecoverFix the cause, show change, rebuild trust over time
›
5 · LearnA review: what worked, what to change

The principles

Speed: a silence gets filled by others, often with speculation
Empathy first: show you care about the people affected before defending the organisation
Honesty: never lie or speculate. Say what you know, what you don't and when you'll update.
Responsibility: take ownership where appropriate; deflecting blame backfires
Consistency: one agreed message across every channel and spokesperson
Action: words must be backed by visible actions
Holding statement: a short, pre-prepared first response. "We are aware of [situation]. Our immediate priority is [the safety/wellbeing of those affected]. We are working urgently to understand what happened and will provide a further update by [time]."

Case studies every PR candidate should know

CaseWhat happenedLesson
KFC "FCK" (UK, 2018)A delivery-supplier change left hundreds of restaurants without chicken. KFC ran a full-page apology ad with its bucket spelling "FCK", humorous and humble.Owning a mistake with honesty and personality can improve reputation. Now a widely awarded campaign.
BP Deepwater Horizon (2010)A huge Gulf of Mexico oil spill. The CEO's remark "I'd like my life back" caused outrage.Empathy for victims must come first. One careless quote can define a crisis.
Woodford fund suspension (2019)A star UK fund manager's flagship fund froze withdrawals, trapping investors. Then the business collapsed.In finance, reputation is the product. Loss of trust becomes loss of assets almost immediately.
Johnson & Johnson Tylenol (1982)Poisoned capsules killed seven people in Chicago. J&J recalled 31 million bottles at once and introduced tamper-proof packaging.The textbook "public safety before profit" response, which restored trust.
In the social media era the "golden hour" is often minutes, and employees, customers and creators may break the story before the press does. Modern crisis teams monitor social channels 24/7 and coordinate legal, HR, operations and comms together. If you mention crisis work in your answer, emphasise calm judgement under pressure and clear, honest writing at speed.

D6 · Corporate & financial PR

This is the specialism closest to your asset-management experience, and one of the best-paid parts of the industry. Financial PR manages how listed companies, banks, investors and deals are seen by the City, the financial media and shareholders.

ActivityWhat happens
Results announcementsListed companies publish results (annual and half-year) via the RNS (the London Stock Exchange's Regulatory News Service) at 7:00am. Financial PR drafts the messages, briefs journalists, arranges CEO and CFO interviews, and monitors the reaction.
M&A (mergers & acquisitions)Handling communications for takeovers, including hostile bids. Timing is governed by the Takeover Code, and leaks can move share prices.
IPOsBuilding the story for a company listing on the stock exchange, and coordinating with banks and lawyers
Investor relations (IR) supportCommunicating strategy to shareholders and analysts; annual reports; capital markets days
Activism & special situationsDefending (or advising) when activist investors campaign for change at a company
Restructuring & crisisProfit warnings, debt problems, leadership exits, regulatory investigations
Regulation applies here too. Under UK MAR (Market Abuse Regulation), "inside information" that could move a share price must be announced properly via the RNS and not leaked selectively. Financial PR people work under strict confidentiality and are often put on "insider lists".

PR for asset managers: your bridge

What asset managers want from PR

  • Fund managers quoted as experts in the FT, Bloomberg, Reuters, The Times and on broadcast
  • Coverage in trade press: Citywire, Investment Week, Money Marketing, FT Adviser, Professional Pensions, Pensions & Investments, IPE
  • Thought leadership: market outlooks, research reports and commentary that build credibility with institutional clients
  • Fund launches, awards and senior hires
  • Crisis management: underperformance, fund closures, regulatory issues

Why it matters commercially

  • Consultants, fund selectors and trustees read the trade press, so media visibility supports distribution
  • Expert commentary builds the "brand" of individual fund managers
  • All marketing must comply with FCA financial promotions rules
  • Trust is the product: a reputational hit can trigger outflows, i.e. lost revenue
The UK financial and corporate PR firms to know (orientation only, not a 10K host list): Brunswick, FGS Global, Teneo, Headland, Lansons, Instinctif Partners, Citigate Dewe Rogerson, Powerscourt, Camarco, H/Advisors Maitland, Montfort (which provides PR support to the 10,000 Interns Foundation). Many asset managers also have strong in-house press teams.

D7 · How PR makes money, and careers

Agency business model

An agency sells people's time and expertise.

  • Retainer: a fixed monthly fee for ongoing work (from a few thousand pounds a month for a small client to £50k+ for large corporates)
  • Project fee: a one-off campaign, launch or deal
  • Hourly / day rates: especially for crisis and financial work, with senior advisers billing high rates
  • Success fees (sometimes): e.g. on completed deals

  • New business: agencies win clients through pitches, which is sales again (C3)
In-house: the communications team is a cost centre (A1) funded from the company's budget. It proves value through coverage quality, reputation and trust scores, crises avoided, and support for sales, recruitment and investor confidence. In-house teams often hire and manage agencies.

Professional bodies

CIPR (Chartered Institute of Public Relations) and PRCA (Public Relations and Communications Association) are the UK professional bodies. They run qualifications, ethics codes and the annual industry census. PRWeek is the industry's trade publication. Reading it for a few weeks before your application gives you current examples to mention.

Trends to know

1 · AI and "AI search". People increasingly get answers from AI chatbots. PR is asked how brands appear in AI answers (GEO, generative engine optimisation), and credible earned media matters more because AI draws on trusted sources.
2 · Misinformation and deepfakes. Fake images, audio and video can spark a crisis in minutes, so monitoring and rapid verification are essential.
3 · The creator economy. Influencers and creators (TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn) rival traditional media for reach, especially with younger audiences. ASA rules require clear labelling (#ad).
4 · Shrinking newsrooms. Fewer journalists covering more ground. PR must provide ready-to-use, accurate, data-led stories, and owned channels matter more.
5 · Greenwashing scrutiny. The CMA's Green Claims Code, ASA rulings and the FCA anti-greenwashing rule mean sustainability claims must be evidenced. PR teams now work closely with legal and compliance.
6 · Purpose and polarisation. Brands face pressure to take positions on social issues, and risk backlash from both sides. Knowing when to speak is a judgement call.
7 · Measurement and data. Clients demand proof of impact (D3). Analytics and social listening tools are standard.
8 · Executive visibility. CEOs and leaders building profiles on LinkedIn and podcasts. "Thought leadership" is a growing service, especially in B2B and finance.
A one-sentence synthesis for your answer: "As AI tools and social platforms reshape how people find information, and misinformation spreads faster than ever, credible, well-evidenced communication has become one of the most valuable things an organisation can invest in."

D10 · Expert check: can you answer these out loud?

What's the difference between PR, marketing and advertising?
Advertising is paid space you control. Marketing drives product demand. PR manages reputation with all stakeholders, mainly through earned, third-party coverage you don't control but which carries more credibility.
What is the PESO model?
Paid, Earned, Shared and Owned media. Modern campaigns integrate all four, with earned media at PR's core.
What makes a story newsworthy?
It's new, relevant to that audience, timely, backed by data or human stories, and has prominence, surprise or exclusivity.
How would you structure a press release?
An inverted pyramid: headline and a first paragraph answering who, what, when, where and why; then supporting details, a spokesperson quote, background, and notes to editors with the boilerplate and contacts.
What are the first steps in a crisis?
Establish the facts, issue a holding statement quickly showing empathy and action, align all spokespeople, update stakeholders regularly, and back words with action.
Give an example of good crisis handling.
E.g. KFC's 2018 "FCK" apology: honest, humble and on-brand, which turned a supply failure into a widely praised campaign.
How should PR be measured?
Beyond volume, by outcomes and business impact (the Barcelona Principles), such as message pick-up, share of voice, trust and behaviour change, not AVEs.
What does financial PR involve?
Communications for listed companies and financial firms: results via the RNS, M&A, IPOs, investor relations and crisis, all under market-abuse rules on inside information.
How does a PR agency make money?
Retainers, project fees and hourly rates for expert time. Profitability depends on utilisation, client retention and winning new business through pitches.
Name a trend changing PR.
E.g. AI-driven search and misinformation: credible earned media becomes more valuable because AI tools draw on trusted sources, and false content can spread in minutes.
