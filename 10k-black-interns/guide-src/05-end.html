E1 · How the three industries connect

Your choices aren't random. Investment management depends on both sales and PR. Seeing these links helps you understand each industry, but each answer must still stand alone, because each will be read by a different firm.

Your personal thread: "In every one of these industries, success depends on understanding an audience and earning its trust." Use that idea in your head to stay consistent across the three answers. But never mention your other choices in an answer, and make each one specific to its own industry's language and economics.

E2 · Turning knowledge into your 250 words

You don't win by showing all of this knowledge. You win by showing the right two or three pieces, used precisely. Over-stuffing jargon reads as a glossary, not understanding. Use the playbook's five-part structure (hook → understanding → evidence → contribution → close), and pick from these:

Investment ManagementSalesPublic Relations
Insight for your hookInflation erodes cash; asset managers decide whether millions retire comfortably (A2, B1)Buyers arrive informed, so a salesperson's value is diagnosis and trust (C1)Reputation is an intangible asset that takes years to build and minutes to lose (D1)
How it makes moneyFees on AUM; growth from performance plus net flows; retention (B10)Pipeline × conversion × deal size × retention; churn (C5)Agencies: retainers and utilisation. In-house: protecting value (D7)
A live trend (pick one)Fee compression; pension consolidation; private markets (B13)AI in sales; retention and NRR (C7)AI search and misinformation; greenwashing scrutiny (D8)
2–3 terms to use correctlyassets under management, distribution, client retention, institutional / wholesalediscovery, pipeline, account management, consultativeearned media, key messages, stakeholders, media relations
Your evidence angleWhat you did in your AM role and what it taught you about clientsThe AM sales cycle in sales language, plus a non-finance persuasion exampleHow carefully an investment firm communicates, plus a writing example

Before and after: what this knowledge does to a sentence

Before
"I am interested in investment management because I enjoy finance and markets."

After
"Because an asset manager's revenue rises and falls with the assets it keeps, I saw that client trust matters as much as returns."
Before
"I am a confident and persuasive person who would be good at sales."

After
"The best salespeople I've watched spent most of each meeting asking questions, because a client who articulates their own problem is halfway to buying."
Before
"I want to work in PR because I enjoy writing and social media."

After
"Earned coverage carries a credibility that advertising can't buy, which is why the skill of turning an organisation's goals into a story a journalist wants is so valuable."

Final self-test before you write

  • ☐ I can explain each industry's four parts in one breath
  • ☐ I can explain how each makes money in one sentence
  • ☐ I can name one live trend for each, and why it matters
  • ☐ I have answered every Expert check out loud
  • ☐ I know which 2–3 terms I'll use in each answer, and use them correctly
  • ☐ Each answer has a true, specific story from my own experience
