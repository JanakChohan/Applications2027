Sales

The function that turns a product into revenue by finding customers, understanding what they need, persuading them to buy and keeping them coming back. Around 1 in 6 UK employees work in sales-related roles. The Sales workstream can place you in technology, consumer goods, business services, media, telecoms or financial services.

C1 · What sales is, and the types of selling

Sales is the part of a business responsible for generating revenue: identifying who might buy, understanding their problem, showing how the product solves it, agreeing a price, closing the deal, and growing the relationship.

Sales vs marketing: marketing creates awareness and interest across many people at once (adverts, content, campaigns). Sales converts that interest into actual customers, usually through one-to-one conversations. Marketing fills the top of the funnel; sales closes it. In modern companies they work as one "revenue" team.

The customer journey. Revenue Operations ("RevOps") ties all four stages together with shared data.

The main types of selling

TypeWhat it meansExample

The modern view of sales: buyers now research online before talking to a salesperson. Studies often suggest B2B buyers are well over halfway through their decision before engaging. So the salesperson's value is no longer information; it is insight, diagnosis and trust. "Helping clients buy" rather than "pushing products" is the idea to show in your answer.

1 · Lead generation

  • SDRs / BDRs
  • Research target accounts
  • Cold calls, emails, LinkedIn
  • Qualify interest
  • Book first meetings
Goal: fill the pipeline with qualified opportunities.
Measured by: meetings booked, qualified leads

2 · New business (closing)

  • Account executives (AEs)
  • Discovery meetings
  • Demos & proposals
  • Negotiation
  • Closing
Goal: convert opportunities into customers.
Measured by: revenue closed vs quota, win rate

3 · Account management & customer success

  • Account managers
  • Customer success managers
  • Onboarding
  • Renewals
  • Upsell / cross-sell
Goal: keep and grow customers.
Measured by: retention, churn, net revenue retention

4 · Sales operations & enablement

  • CRM & data
  • Forecasting
  • Territories & pricing
  • Commission plans
  • Training
Goal: make the whole engine efficient.
Measured by: forecast accuracy, productivity

Sales leadership sits above all four: sales managers, regional heads, the VP of Sales and the Chief Revenue Officer (CRO). They set targets, hire, coach and forecast.

C3 · The sales process, step by step

Every structured sales team follows a version of this. Each stage has a clear exit criterion before a deal moves on in the CRM.

1 · ProspectIdentify target companies and people who fit the ideal customer profile (ICP)
›
2 · OutreachContact them by call, email, LinkedIn or referral
›
3 · QualifyDo they have a real need, budget, authority and timing?
›
4 · DiscoveryDeep questions: what problem, what impact, what success looks like
›
5 · SolutionDemo or presentation tailored to their problem
6 · ProposalWritten offer: scope, price, terms, business case
›
7 · ObjectionsAddress concerns: price, timing, risk, competitors
›
8 · NegotiateAgree on price, terms and contract length
›
9 · CloseSigned contract or purchase order
›
10 · Onboard & growSuccessful start, then renewal, upsell and referrals

Discovery: the most important stage

Great salespeople spend most of their time asking and listening, not presenting. Good discovery questions:

  • "What prompted you to look at this now?"
  • "How are you handling this today?"
  • "What does that cost you, in time, money or risk?"
  • "Who else is involved in this decision?"
  • "What would success look like in 12 months?"
  • "What's stopped you solving this before?"

Handling objections: a simple four-step technique

ListenLet them finish; don't interrupt or argue
›
Acknowledge"That's a fair concern…"
›
Explore"Can you tell me more about what's behind that?"
›
RespondEvidence, a case study or a reframe, then check: "Does that address it?"
Common objectionWhat it often really meansGood response direction
"It's too expensive."Value isn't clear yet, or the budget is genuinely limitedReconnect the price to the cost of the problem; explore options
"Now isn't a good time."Low urgency or other prioritiesUnderstand their timeline; agree on a next step
"We use a competitor."Switching feels riskyExplore satisfaction gaps; reduce switching risk
"Send me some information."A polite brush-offAsk what specifically would be useful, and book a follow-up
"Sell me this pen", the classic interview test. The weak answer launches into the pen's features. The strong answer asks questions first: "When did you last use a pen? What for? What frustrates you about the ones you have?" Then it sells the specific benefit that matters to that person. It demonstrates discovery, which is what interviewers are actually testing.

C4 · Sales methodologies

A methodology is a structured way of selling. You don't need to master them, but knowing the names and ideas immediately marks you as someone who has done their homework.

MethodThe core ideaHow it works
BANTA simple qualification check, originally from IBMBudget, Authority, Need, Timing. If one is missing, the deal is at risk.
MEDDIC / MEDDPICCRigorous qualification for complex B2B dealsMetrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, (Paper process), Identify pain, Champion, (Competition)
SPIN SellingNeil Rackham's research-based questioning modelSituation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff questions, leading the buyer to state the value themselves
Challenger SaleThe best salespeople teach, tailor and take controlBring a new insight that challenges how the customer thinks about their business
Solution sellingSell the solution to a diagnosed problem, not the productDiagnose pain → build a vision of the solution → prove value
Consultative sellingAct as a trusted adviserDeep listening, honest advice (even "we're not right for you") and long-term focus
Value sellingEverything is framed in the customer's business outcomesQuantify ROI: "this saves you £X / earns you £Y"

SPIN in action (an example)

Situation
"How does your team currently produce client reports?"
Problem
"Where does that process cause difficulties?"
Implication
"What happens when a report goes out late or wrong?"
Need-payoff
"How valuable would it be if that took half the time?"

The psychology of persuasion (Cialdini's principles)

Reciprocity: people return favours, so give value first (insight, help)
Commitment & consistency: small agreements lead to bigger ones
Social proof: "companies like yours use us" (case studies, references)
Authority: expertise and credentials build trust
Liking: we buy from people we like and relate to
Scarcity: limited availability increases value (used ethically)
Ethics matter. In regulated industries (financial services especially), pressure selling and misleading claims are illegal. Mis-selling scandals such as UK PPI cost banks tens of billions of pounds in compensation. The modern standard is selling only what genuinely helps the customer, which is also what builds repeat business.
If you can explain that SPIN and consultative selling are about helping the buyer discover the value themselves, and link that to your own experience of watching client conversations in asset management, you'll sound far more credible than someone who says "I'm persuasive and confident".

Improve any one of these and revenue rises. That's why sales is so data-driven.

The sales funnel (illustrative B2B)

1,000 prospects contacted
150 respond (15%)
60 qualified meetings (40%)
20 proposals (33%)
6 deals won (30%)

Each percentage is that stage's conversion rate. The overall conversion here is 0.6%.

Worked example: a software company

6 deals × £30,000 a year each = £180k of new annual recurring revenue (ARR).

If customers stay 4 years on average: lifetime value = £120k each, so £720k in total.

If it cost £90k (salaries, tools, marketing) to win them, the acquisition cost is £15k per customer.

LTV : CAC = 120 : 15 = 8 : 1. Very healthy (above 3:1 is generally considered good).

How salespeople are paid

Usually base salary + commission, adding up to "OTE" (on-target earnings). A typical split is 50/50 or 60/40 base/variable. Commission might be a percentage of revenue closed, with accelerators (higher rates) above 100% of quota. That's why sales attracts competitive, target-motivated people. In financial services, pay is more salary-plus-bonus, because regulators discourage incentives that could lead to mis-selling.

Pricing models you should recognise: one-off purchase; subscription (per user or per month); usage-based (pay per use); tiered (good / better / best); and percentage of value (like asset management's % of AUM). The pricing model shapes how a sales team sells and how it's rewarded.

Sales is one of the most transferable skill sets in business. Many CEOs started in sales, and the skills carry straight into asset management distribution, consulting, recruitment and entrepreneurship.

Trends to know

1 · AI across the sales cycle. AI drafts outreach, researches prospects, summarises calls and forecasts deals. Some firms use "AI SDRs" for first-touch outreach, so human value shifts to complex conversations, relationships and judgement.
2 · The informed buyer. Buyers research independently and talk to sales later. Salespeople must add insight beyond what's online, which explains the rise of consultative and "Challenger" approaches.
3 · Product-led growth (PLG). Users try a product free and upgrade themselves (e.g. Slack, Canva). Sales then focuses on expanding usage within companies.
4 · Customer success and retention. In subscription businesses, keeping and expanding customers drives most growth. Net revenue retention is now a headline metric.
5 · Social selling. Building credibility on LinkedIn through content and networks before ever making contact.
6 · Data-driven and RevOps. Marketing, sales and customer success are united under shared data and targets, so analytical skills are increasingly valued.
7 · Hybrid selling. A mix of video and in-person meetings. In-person is used deliberately for high-value moments.
8 · Ethics and trust. Regulation (e.g. Consumer Duty in finance) and buyer cynicism reward honest, long-term, value-based selling.
A strong one-sentence synthesis for your answer: "As AI takes on more routine outreach and buyers arrive better informed, the value of a salesperson increasingly lies in understanding a client's problem deeply and building trust over time."

C9 · Expert check: can you answer these out loud?

What's the difference between sales and marketing?
Marketing creates awareness and interest across many people at once. Sales converts that interest into customers through one-to-one conversations, then helps retain and grow them.
Walk me through a B2B sales process.
Prospect → outreach → qualify (e.g. BANT) → discovery → tailored solution → proposal → handle objections → negotiate → close → onboard → renew and expand.
Why is discovery so important?
Because you can't sell value until you understand the buyer's problem and its impact. Good discovery lets the buyer articulate the need, which makes the solution compelling and the price justified.
What does an SDR do vs an AE?
SDRs generate and qualify opportunities and book meetings. AEs run the sales process from discovery to close and own a revenue quota.
What is churn, and why does it matter?
The rate at which customers or revenue are lost. High churn means you must keep replacing customers just to stand still, so retention and account management protect growth.
What's pipeline coverage, and why aim for about 3×?
Pipeline value divided by target. Since only a fraction of deals close, you need several times your target in pipeline to hit it reliably.
How would you handle "it's too expensive"?
Acknowledge it, explore what's behind it, reconnect the price to the value or the cost of the problem, consider options (scope, terms), and check that the concern is resolved.
What is SPIN selling?
A questioning approach (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) that helps buyers recognise the impact of their problem and the value of solving it themselves.
How is AI changing sales?
It automates research, outreach and call analysis, so human value shifts towards complex conversations, judgement and long-term relationships.
How does asset management distribution resemble B2B sales?
Long cycles, many decision-makers (trustees, consultants), formal proposals (RFPs), pitches, and recurring fee revenue that depends on retention, all handled by sales and relationship teams.
