C1 · AI SDRs and the deliverability squeeze

From zero: an SDR (sales development rep) is the entry-level seller who finds prospects, contacts them cold and books meetings for senior sellers (AEs, account executives). An "AI SDR" is software built on a large language model that tries to do that whole loop itself: pull a target list, research each company, write personalised emails, send follow-ups, read replies and book meetings. "Agentic" means it plans and carries out several steps on its own, rather than just drafting text for a human.

How it works technically

Data layerCompany and contact data, buying "intent" signals, the CRM
›
LLMResearches the account and writes personalised copy
›
Sending engineRotates many mailboxes and domains to stay under per-inbox limits
›
Reply classifierDetects interest and books meetings or routes to humans

The catch is deliverability. AI SDR economics depend on cheap volume, and the email providers have shut that door. Since February 2024, Google and Yahoo require anyone sending 5,000+ emails a day to authenticate their domain (SPF, DKIM and DMARC, technical records proving the email really comes from you), offer one-click unsubscribe, and keep spam complaints below 0.3%. Microsoft announced the same rules for Outlook.com in April 2025, sending non-compliant mail to junk from 5 May 2025. Mass, low-quality outbound now damages the sender's reputation, and that drags down every email the company sends.

87%of sales organisations use AI (Salesforce State of Sales, Feb 2026)
10 : 1AI agents vs sellers by 2028, Gartner predicts…
< 40%…yet fewer than 40% of sellers will say agents improved productivity
3.4%average cold email reply rate (Instantly 2026 benchmark; others measure under 1%)

Real events

  • 11x (a heavily funded AI SDR start-up): TechCrunch reported in March 2025 that it listed customers that had only trialled the product, and that former staff spoke of losing 70–80% of customers. ZoomInfo said it "performed significantly worse than our SDR employees". The CEO stepped down in May 2025.
  • Salesforce: rebranded Sales Cloud as "Agentforce Sales" (Dreamforce, October 2025). At Dreamforce 2026 it launched named agents: Piper (an inbound SDR agent) and Hunter (outbound). Agentforce passed a $1.5bn annual run-rate by August 2026 (on a widened definition). HubSpot's "24/7 BDR" Prospecting Agent has 10,000+ activated customers.
Bull caseAgents can work every lead, including the millions nobody ever calls back, at a fraction of the cost. Salesforce's revenue from agents is real money, and high performers are 1.7× more likely to use prospecting agents (Salesforce).
Bear caseGartner's "value ceiling": "beyond a certain point, more AI does not mean more productivity". Bridge Group found 10 of 11 AI use cases rated "hit or miss". Mass AI outbound burns domains and buyer trust. The winners are AI-assisted humans, not autonomous spam.
1–3 years: agents shift from cold outbound to inbound response (answering people who already showed interest), research and deal follow-up. Email authentication becomes basic hygiene. 5+ years: buyers' AI agents negotiate with sellers' AI agents for routine purchases (Gartner expects agents to intermediate $15 trillion of B2B spending by 2028). Humans concentrate on complex, high-trust, many-stakeholder deals. Watch: agent retention data, Gartner's productivity survey, reply-rate benchmarks.
My take: the evidence points to augmentation beating automation in outbound sales. The firms getting value use AI to make a human far better prepared, not to replace the human with volume. Ask yourself: if every competitor can send 10,000 "personalised" emails, what becomes scarce? (Genuine relevance and trust.)
The cold-email volume game is closing. Your edge is judgement: choosing which accounts and signals matter, writing like a human, and checking what the agents produce. Learn SPF/DKIM/DMARC and CRM hygiene, and you become the person who runs the tools.

C2 · The rep-free buyer paradox

From zero: business buyers now research mostly on their own, increasingly by asking ChatGPT-style tools, and many say they'd rather not talk to a salesperson at all. Yet purchases involve more people than ever, and buyers turn to a human to check whether what the AI told them is true. Both statements are backed by data. That contradiction is the paradox.
61% → 67%of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience (Gartner surveys published 2025 and 2026)
69%turn to a sales rep to validate AI-generated insights (Gartner, May 2026)
7 → 14buying group size doubles for purchases with genAI features (Forrester 2026)
54%say AI chatbots are the top influence on their shortlist (G2, March 2026, secondary)

The mechanics of the new buying journey

1 · Ask AIAbout half of software buyers now start research in an AI chatbot (G2)
›
2 · Shortlist formsBefore any seller is contacted
›
3 · Committee13 internal stakeholders + 9 external influencers on average (Forrester)
›
4 · ValidateReps used to check AI claims, proof and ROI
›
5 · ConsensusProcurement decides in 53% of cycles
Digital sales room (DSR): a shared microsite for each deal holding the proposal, a joint action plan, security documents and recorded demos, with analytics on who viewed what. It's a tool to help a large buying group agree without the seller being in the room. Gartner published a market guide to DSRs in February 2025.
Answer engine optimisation (AEO): vendors now try to make sure AI chatbots mention and recommend them, because shortlists form before first contact. Gartner advises "modular, agent-ready content" that AI tools can read and quote.

The risk of going rep-free: older Gartner data found 43% of buyers reported high regret when buying through self-service, against 26% when a rep was involved. And 73% actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach, so badly targeted contact is worse than none.

"Rep-free" campThe preference keeps rising (61% → 67%). 94% of buyers use AI in the process (Forrester). Invest in self-serve, DSRs and AEO, and cut field sales.
"Reps matter more" camp69% validate with reps; self-serve buyers regret more; AI-feature purchases double buying groups. Complexity is rising, not falling.
The insider synthesis: fewer but higher-stakes human touches. The rep stops being an information source (AI does that) and becomes a validator and consensus builder, the person who reconciles conflicting information and gets 14 people to agree.
1–3 years: sellers are coached to handle buyers who arrive with an AI-generated view. Companies spend on AEO and DSRs. 5+ years: buyers' procurement agents query suppliers' agents directly, and the human seller handles exceptions, risk and executive agreement.
Which do you believe: will buyers need salespeople less, or need a different kind of salesperson? A strong position: "AI commoditises information, so the value of a seller shifts from knowing things to being trusted to verify them."
Practise "validation selling": evidence, case studies, ROI maths, and mapping 10+ stakeholders. In interviews, saying "buyers arrive already informed by AI, so my job is to add judgement and build consensus" shows you understand where the job is going.
Sources: Gartner, Mar 2026 · Gartner, May 2026 · Gartner, Jun 2025 · Forrester State of Business Buying 2026 · G2 via Demand Gen Report

C3 · The assembly line breaks, and the entry rung narrows

From zero: since around 2010, software sales has run like a factory line (the "Predictable Revenue" model). Marketing creates leads, SDRs qualify them, AEs close them, and customer success renews them. Each role is specialised, and juniors climb from SDR to AE in about 18 months. That line is now under strain: fewer SDRs, AEs prospecting for themselves ("full-cycle"), targets rising, and new technical roles appearing.

The key metrics (learn these formulas)

Quota attainment = bookings ÷ quota. "% at quota" is the share of reps at 100% or more.
Pipeline coverage = open pipeline ÷ remaining quota. The target is often 3× or more.
Sales velocity = (opportunities × win rate × average deal size) ÷ sales cycle in days
Ramp = months until a new rep is fully productive
Quota : OTE ratio = annual quota ÷ on-target earnings. Higher means a harder plan.
Promotion rate = the share of SDRs promoted to AE each year

Elsewhere: a survey of 560+ software companies found 36% cut SDR headcount in the past year and only 19% grew it (Emergence Capital via SaaStr, secondary). Stanford researchers (August 2025, payroll data) found a 13% relative employment decline for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations, while older workers were stable. Meanwhile, new hybrid roles are booming: GTM engineers (people who build automated, data-driven prospecting workflows with tools like Clay) and forward-deployed engineers (technical staff embedded with customers).

"The SDR is dead"AI does research and first-touch outreach. Full-cycle AEs plus agents are leaner (Jason Lemkin, Emergence data).
"Only this version of the SDR is dead"Pipeline generated per SDR is up (from $2.83m to $3.78m). Hiring is down across all jobs, not just because of AI. Falling attainment may reflect quota inflation rather than worse sellers.
1–3 years: smaller SDR teams working signal-led outbound; more full-cycle AEs; SDRs retraining as GTM engineers; AEs owning expansion. 5+ years: a "pod" model of AE + technical specialist + AI agents. The entry rung moves towards technical or operations roles and account management.
Bridge Group's 2027 SDR report · RepVue's quarterly quota-attainment index (around 43% across cloud companies in 2025) · GTM engineer job postings
Differentiate with technical go-to-market skills (CRM automation, data, prompt workflows) or deep knowledge of one sector. In interviews, ask: what share of reps hit quota? What is the ramp? How are renewals paid? Those questions signal you understand the business.

C4 · The efficiency regime: growth isn't enough any more

From zero: until 2021, cheap money meant investors rewarded software companies for growing fast, whatever it cost. When interest rates rose in 2022, that flipped. Now companies are judged on how much it costs to win £1 of revenue and how much revenue they keep. This decides how many salespeople get hired, what their targets are and how they're paid.

The investor's dashboard (formulas)

MetricFormulaGood benchmark / meaning
CAC paybackSales & marketing cost to acquire ÷ (new ARR × gross margin ÷ 12)Months to earn back the cost of winning a customer. Median about 16 months.
Magic number(Change in quarterly revenue × 4) ÷ prior-quarter S&M spendAbove ~0.75 is usually "efficient"
NRR(Starting ARR + expansion − contraction − churn) ÷ starting ARRAbove 100% means existing customers alone grow revenue
GRRAs NRR but without expansion (max 100%)Pure retention. The median fell from 88% to 84%.
Rule of 40Revenue growth % + profit margin %40 or more is "healthy"
ARR per employeeARR ÷ full-time staffUp 29% to about $193k: firms are doing more with fewer people
Worked example: a company spends £600k on sales and marketing to win £400k of new ARR at an 80% gross margin. Monthly gross profit from those customers = £400k × 0.8 ÷ 12 ≈ £26.7k. CAC payback = £600k ÷ £26.7k ≈ 22.5 months, worse than the ~16-month median. That company would cut expensive channels, raise prices or improve conversion.
20%median SaaS growth, down from 26% (Benchmarkit 2026)
25%median Rule of 40, up from 15%: more profitable, slower
87%of enterprises missed 2025 revenue targets despite record AI spend (Clari Labs, secondary)
~38%leaner go-to-market teams at high-AI-adoption small firms (ICONIQ 2025)
Bull caseAI enables "leaner, flatter" sales organisations with far more revenue per person (ICONIQ: about 2× net new revenue per go-to-market employee at AI-forward firms). Profitability has improved.
Bear caseThe efficiency came mostly from cuts (R&D fell from 35% to 27% of revenue; headcount down). Growth slowed, and retention (GRR) fell. AI spend isn't yet showing up in revenue (Clari's 87%).
1–3 years: boards track ARR per employee and CAC payback over headcount growth. Fewer, more expensive, more productive reps. 5+ years: if AI-native firms sustain about 2× revenue per employee, staffing ratios reset permanently, and "how many salespeople do we need?" gets a smaller answer.
Benchmarkit and KeyBanc annual medians · ARR per employee · NRR split by pricing model
Learn these formulas. Interviewers increasingly test whether candidates can reason about payback, NRR and pipeline coverage. Your value is measured in efficient pipeline, not activity counts.

C5 · Seats → usage → outcomes: the pricing revolution

From zero: software used to be priced "per user per month" (a seat). But if AI agents do the work, customers need fewer human users, so seat revenue shrinks. Vendors are moving to usage pricing (pay per action or credit), outcome pricing (pay per resolved ticket or qualified lead) and hybrid pricing (a base fee plus usage). This changes what a salesperson sells (a business result, not licences) and how they're paid (partly on whether the customer actually uses it).
CompanyWhat they didPricing unit
Salesforce AgentforceLaunched at $2 per conversation (2024). Switched to "Flex Credits" in May 2025, then added pay-as-you-go in August 2025. Pricing changed three times in about a year.$0.10 per agent action (20 credits)
Intercom FinAn AI customer service agent priced per resolution. Reportedly nearing $100m ARR (secondary).$0.99 per resolution; $9.99 per qualified sales lead
ZendeskClaimed the first outcome-based pricing in customer service software (August 2024)Per automated resolution
HubSpot (April 2026)Moved agents to outcome pricing. CEO: "there is a huge gap between AI output and AI outcomes."$0.50 per resolved conversation; $1 per recommended lead
21% → 15%companies using seat-based pricing over 12 months (Growth Unhinged 2025)
27% → 41%hybrid pricing over the same period
5% → 25%outcome pricing as primary model: today vs expected by 2028
108% vs 98%NRR for usage-based vs seat-based companies (Benchmarkit)

The mechanics that change a seller's job

  • Committed-spend contracts: customers pre-buy a pool of credits at a discount, and overage is charged at a higher rate.
  • Outcome definitions get contested. What counts as a "resolution"? Intercom counts a conversation as resolved if the customer doesn't reply within 24 hours. That's open to argument.
  • Revenue becomes variable after signing, so finance teams need consumption forecasting.
  • Commission changes: about 30% of companies set quota on forecast consumption, and 45–50% have clawbacks (commission returned if usage falls short) (ICONIQ, secondary).
Bull casePrice matches value. Customers pay for results, so usage-based firms keep and grow customers better (108% NRR). It's the natural model for AI agents.
Bear caseBills become unpredictable, and CFOs hate variable costs. "Outcome" definitions can be gamed. Seat compression threatens incumbents' revenue base. Constant re-pricing (Agentforce) confuses buyers.
1–3 years: hybrid (a platform fee plus credits) becomes the default. Sellers sell committed credit pools and are paid partly on consumption. 5+ years: pure outcome pricing for well-measured workflows (support, lead qualification), and value engineering, building the ROI case, becomes a core sales skill.
Next Growth Unhinged pricing survey · Salesforce and HubSpot pricing changes · share of commission plans paid on consumption
Asset management has always used outcome-linked pricing: a percentage of assets, plus performance fees. Is software converging on the asset management model? What lessons on fee pressure might transfer?
Be able to build an ROI case: "an AI resolution costs £0.80 against £6 for a human-handled ticket". Expect part of your pay to depend on whether customers use what you sold, which rewards honest selling.
Sources: Salesforce Flex Credits · Intercom pricing · Zendesk · HubSpot pricing · Growth Unhinged 2025

C6 · Signals, product-led sales and the RevOps stack

From zero: three connected ideas. Signal-based selling means prioritising accounts using live evidence of buying interest (website visits, hiring, funding rounds, product usage, "intent data") instead of static lists. Product-led sales (PLS) means users try a product free, and salespeople step in when usage data shows an account is ready to buy or expand. Revenue operations (RevOps) is the team that joins up the data, tools and processes across marketing, sales and customer success.
2.6×more likely to achieve growth if sellers get AI next-best actions (Gartner CSO survey, May 2026)
95%of seller research workflows to start with AI by 2027, up from <20% in 2024 (Gartner)
48%say their revenue data isn't "AI-ready" (Clari Labs)
>60%of buyers bought a trial, but only about a third planned to convert with that provider (Forrester 2026)

Market moves

  • Clari + Salesloft merged (announced August 2025; combined ARR around $450m per Forrester). This is the sales-tool stack consolidating.
  • Attio, an "AI-native CRM", raised a $52m Series B led by GV (August 2025): a challenger to Salesforce and HubSpot.
  • Salesforce found that 79% of high performers prioritise data hygiene, against 54% of underperformers.
Incumbents winSalesforce and HubSpot own the data ("data gravity"), and AI is only as good as the data. Bundles like Agentforce + Data 360 lock customers in.
Challengers winAI-native CRMs are built for agents from day one. Specialist tools stitched together by GTM engineers out-innovate the suites. Intent data is often noise.
1–3 years: AI "next-best action" built into CRMs; more mergers among sales-tool vendors; trials need human follow-up to convert. 5+ years: the CRM stops being a database reps update by hand and becomes a system of action run by agents, with humans supervising.
CRM fluency and clean data are now performance differentiators, not admin. Learn to read product usage data and turn it into a conversation: "I noticed your team added 40 users last month…"
Sources: Gartner CSO survey 2026 · Forrester on Clari–Salesloft · Attio Series B · Forrester 2026

C7 · Customer success becomes a revenue function

From zero: customer success (CS) teams were built to keep customers happy so they'd renew, and were usually measured on satisfaction rather than revenue. Under efficiency pressure, firms are making CS commercial: giving it quotas and expansion targets, moving renewals and upsells to AEs or account managers, and automating low-value accounts with AI. The metric everyone now watches is NRR (net revenue retention).
52%of revenue growth came from expanding existing customers (Ebsta × Pavilion 2025)
52 vs 91 daysexpansion deals close almost twice as fast as new-business deals
75%of software firms saw NRR decline even as ~60% raised CS spend (Bain 2024)
38%of AEs now own renewals or expansion, nearly double 2024 (Bridge Group)

Other signals: in one survey, 44% of companies cut CSM roles and 42% froze hiring (Customer Success Collective, secondary). Half of Salesforce's Agentforce and Data 360 bookings came from existing customers. New-customer acquisition cost is rising faster than blended cost, so growing existing customers is now the cheapest growth.

Merge itNo handoffs; one owner of NRR, the number investors watch most; expansion is faster and cheaper than new logos.
Keep it separateA quota turns the trusted adviser into a seller, and customers notice. Hunters neglect renewals. Gainsight argues merging suits small-business segments more than enterprise.
1–3 years: AI agents handle low-touch accounts; humans run commercial account management with NRR targets. 5+ years: a single "customer revenue" function, with pay linked to consumption (connecting to C5).
Share of AEs owning renewals · GRR and NRR trends · whether CS reports to the Chief Revenue Officer
This mirrors asset management exactly: keeping a mandate is cheaper than winning one, and client relationship managers protect recurring fees. If you're asked "hunter or farmer?", you can argue that the economics now favour farmers.
Account management and expansion roles may be a more stable entry point than pure new-business hunting. Learn renewal mechanics, account health scores and upsell plays.
Sources: Ebsta × Pavilion 2025 · Bain Tech Report 2024 · Bridge Group 2026 · Gainsight

C8 · Trust, authenticity and UK/EU regulation

From zero: as AI makes outreach cheap and generic, buyers trust it less, and regulators are tightening the rules. In the UK, marketing emails and calls are governed by PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations) and UK GDPR, both updated by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. The EU AI Act adds duties to disclose AI. And anyone selling financial products in the UK faces the FCA's Consumer Duty.
RuleWhat it means for selling
PECR: B2B emailMarketing emails to companies and LLPs don't need prior consent but must identify the sender and offer an opt-out. Sole traders and partnerships count as individuals, so they need consent. Calls must be screened against the TPS (Telephone Preference Service).
Data (Use and Access) Act 2025Royal Assent June 2025. It raises maximum PECR fines from £500,000 to UK GDPR levels: £17.5m or 4% of global turnover (exact start date to be confirmed).
ICO enforcement2025 examples include £90,000 (95,277 calls to TPS-registered numbers) and £50,000 (194,110 calls). Expect bigger fines under the new ceiling.
EU AI Act, Article 50From 2 August 2026, people interacting with an AI system must be told it's AI unless that's obvious. This directly affects AI chat and voice sales agents selling into the EU.
FCA Consumer DutyFinancial firms must evidence fair value and customer understanding. Sales conversations must show the product suits the customer.
73%of buyers avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach (Gartner)
51% / 49%expect misleading info from GenAI / from sales reps (Gartner, secondary)
26%say obviously AI-written outreach actively undermines their interest (Adience, secondary)
94%of buyers who researched with AI still fact-checked it (TrustRadius, vendor)

The "authenticity premium": as automated contact floods inboxes, trusted channels gain value: referrals, communities, events, human video. Event data is mixed, though. Bizzabo found 78% of organisers call in-person events their most impactful channel, while Forrester found 69% of events leaders had flat or falling budgets, shifting to smaller, invitation-only events.

Scale winsCompliant AI outreach at scale, properly disclosed and authenticated, still generates pipeline more cheaply than humans.
Trust winsRegulation, deliverability rules and buyer cynicism make volume self-defeating. Expertise, honesty and referrals are what AI can't fake.
1–3 years: ICO enforcement tests the new fine ceiling; AI disclosure becomes standard in agent scripts; referral, community and event channels gain share. 5+ years: provenance labelling of AI-generated content is common, and "human-verified" becomes a selling point.
Know the UK basics (corporate vs individual subscribers, TPS, AI disclosure). Compliance literacy is a hiring plus, especially in financial services, and integrity is the one thing buyers say they can't get from AI.

C9 · Selling funds: gatekeepers, models and "targeted support"

From zero: this is where Sales meets your first choice. Fund managers rarely sell to individual investors. They sell to gatekeepers: wealth managers, advice platforms and especially model portfolio service (MPS) providers. These firms build ready-made portfolios that thousands of financial advisers then use for their clients. One "yes" from a model provider can bring in hundreds of millions, and one "no" can take it away.
RecordUK retail gross fund sales in FY2025, yet net sales fell ~£10bn (ISS MI Pridham)
90%of platform gross sales come from adviser firms using at least one model (ISS MI)
$9.3tn → $18.6tnUS model portfolio assets, end-2025 to projected 2030 (Broadridge)
54%of US model assets are in ETFs (Broadridge)

The new UK channel: targeted support

The FCA confirmed "targeted support" rules on 26 February 2026, effective 6 April 2026. It's a new regulated activity: firms such as banks, platforms and pension providers can give suggestions to groups of consumers in similar circumstances (for example, "people like you with cash savings and no pension top-ups often consider…") without full personalised advice. It's designed to close the "advice gap" and could open new mass-market routes for funds.

Bull caseModel portfolios and targeted support bring millions of new savers into investing. Data-driven "digital distribution" lets sales teams target the right advisers precisely.
Bear caseGatekeeper concentration squeezes fees. Model providers favour cheap passive and ETFs. Relationship-led wholesale sellers are partly replaced by data and platform analytics.
1–3 years: more model portfolio partnerships and "big win, big loss" flows; banks and platforms launch targeted-support propositions. 5+ years: ETFs and active ETFs keep rising inside models, and fund sales becomes as much about data and evidence (performance, fees, Consumer Duty value assessments) as relationships.
In asset management sales, your customer is an investment committee. You need product, performance and fee-evidence fluency more than prospecting volume, which is a strong, specific insight for your Investment Management and Sales answers.
Sources: ISS MI Pridham Report · ISS MI model portfolios · Broadridge via WealthManagement · FCA advice-guidance boundary review

Five predictions to test against reality

By 2028, most outbound first-touches will be machine-generated, so human-written, highly relevant outreach and referrals will carry a premium.
Hybrid pricing (platform fee + usage) becomes the norm, and commission plans increasingly pay on consumption and retention, rewarding honest selling.
The seller's job shifts from information provider to validator and consensus builder for buying groups of 10–20 people.
Entry-level sales jobs become more technical (data, CRM, automation) or more account-management based, with fewer pure cold-calling roles.
In financial services, distribution concentrates on gatekeepers and models; data and Consumer Duty evidence become as important as relationships.
Pick one prediction and decide whether you agree. Then find one piece of evidence against it. That's how insiders form views: a position, held with reasons, and updated when the data changes.
One sentence for your Sales answer: "As AI makes information and outreach abundant, the scarce skill in sales is becoming judgement: understanding which customers genuinely need you and earning the trust of everyone involved in the decision."
