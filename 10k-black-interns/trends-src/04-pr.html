Public Relations: the trends

Earned media is becoming scarcer, as newsrooms shrink, and more valuable, because AI systems read and cite it. Trust has retreated inwards, regulation is turning communications claims into legal risk, and the agency model built on junior hours is being squeezed.

D1 · AI answer engines: earned media gets a second audience

From zero: people increasingly ask an AI (ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini) instead of clicking through search results. The AI writes one answer, sometimes with small source links. Whatever the AI "reads" shapes what it says about your company. It turns out that PR's traditional product, coverage in credible outlets, is one of the main things these systems read. Earned media now informs machines as well as people.

How AI answers are built: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

1 · Fan-outThe model rewrites your question into several search queries
›
2 · RetrievePulls candidate pages from a web index or licensed content
›
3 · RankScores them on relevance, authority and freshness
›
4 · GenerateWrites an answer from those passages, citing some sources

There are two routes into an AI's answer. Training memory (what the model learned) is slow to change. Live retrieval (what it fetches now) can be influenced. Retrieval favours well-structured, quotable, recent pages on trusted domains. GEO (generative engine optimisation) comes from a 2023 academic paper, which found content changes such as adding citations, quotations and statistics could boost visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. A new metric, "share of model", measures how often a brand appears across a fixed set of AI prompts compared with competitors.

~82–85%of AI citations come from earned media (Muck Rack "What Is AI Reading?", 2025)
5×growth in press-release citations by AI, July–Dec 2025 (Muck Rack)
8% vs 15%click-through when Google shows an AI summary vs when it doesn't (Pew, 2025)
900mChatGPT weekly users (Feb 2026)

Market signals: Adobe agreed to buy Semrush for $1.9bn (Nov 2025); "AI visibility" start-ups raised large rounds; Bing added GEO to its guidelines (Feb 2026). The UK competition regulator (CMA) gave Google Search "strategic market status" (Oct 2025), with AI Overviews in scope. Note the caveats: Muck Rack sells PR software, and its "earned" category is broad. Different AI models also favour different outlets: The Guardian is cited most by ChatGPT, Yahoo Finance by Claude.

Bull caseEarned media has been re-valued: credible third-party coverage feeds both humans and machines and is hard to fake. PR can claim the "AI reputation" budget ahead of SEO and ad agencies.
Bear case"GEO" is just SEO rebadged (Google says so). AI results are unstable: platforms disagree on 61.9% of recommendations. And if AI answers mean nobody clicks, the outlets that supply citations lose money and shrink, sawing off the branch PR sits on.
1–3 years: "AI visibility audits" become a standard agency deliverable; press releases rewritten as structured, fact-dense, machine-readable pages; "share of model" added beside share of voice. 5+ years: AI agents research and buy on people's behalf, and reputation becomes partly a data-supply problem.
Reuters Institute AI-news use (10% globally in 2026; 16% of under-35s) · Muck Rack's earned-media share · CMA interventions on Google · acquisitions of AI-visibility tools
Learn how RAG works and how to run a prompt audit: ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity the same 20 questions about a brand and record what they say and cite. That's a concrete, modern skill few graduates have.
Sources: GEO paper (Aggarwal et al.) · Muck Rack Dec 2025 · Muck Rack Jul 2025 · Pew Research · CMA on Google · Share of model

D2 · The shrinking news media: fewer journalists, more PR

From zero: news publishers made money from advertising and subscriptions, driven by traffic from Google and social media. When AI summaries answer the question on Google's own page ("zero-click"), fewer people visit news sites. Revenue falls, jobs are cut, and each remaining journalist faces more PR pitches with less time. Surviving outlets increasingly earn money from AI licensing deals, subscriptions and events.
−33%Google search traffic to 2,500+ news sites, Nov 2024 → Nov 2025 (Chartbeat, via Reuters Institute)
~3,440UK and US journalism job cuts tracked in 2025 (Press Gazette); BBC plans ~2,000 more
~6 : 1PR specialists per journalist in the US (BLS data; method-dependent)
50+pitches a week for most journalists; 72% find at most a quarter relevant (Cision 2026)

What journalists actually want (Cision State of the Media 2026, 1,899 journalists)

Finding%So what
Rely on PR-provided content as a top source of story ideas66%PR's leverage is rising as newsrooms shrink
Want more data and research47%Original data beats opinions
Want more embargoes / expert access45% / 42%Time to prepare and real experts
Want more press releasesonly 22%The classic release is the least wanted asset
Oppose AI-generated pitches53%Human, relevant pitching matters

Ownership and licensing: the Telegraph saga

A UAE-backed bid for the Telegraph was blocked (2024) under a new law on foreign-state influence over newspapers. After several failed deals, Germany's Axel Springer agreed £575m (March 2026), was approved in April and completed in June 2026. Meanwhile AI licensing deals multiplied: News Corp–OpenAI (reported $250m+ over 5 years), the FT with Google, Reach with Amazon. Press Gazette tracks 19 such deals since May 2025, but small publishers are largely shut out.

Bull for PRStretched newsrooms need ready-to-use assets: data, experts, embargoes. 66% already lean on PR content. Owned and creator channels fill the gaps.
Bear for PRFewer specialist reporters means shallower, harder-to-win coverage. "Churnalism" (reprinted press releases) erodes the credibility that makes earned media valuable to AI. At 6:1, the inbox is a lottery.
1–3 years: more newsroom cuts and a two-tier press: licensed giants vs starved small outlets. Pitching becomes asset-led (datasets, video, expert access). 5+ years: possibly a small core of "verification brands" (FT, Reuters, BBC) that AI systems and audiences lean on, plus a long tail of creators.
Press Gazette's job-cut tracker · Chartbeat referral data · AI licensing deal tracker · news sites' direct reach (down 12 points since 2021)
Build relationships with fewer reporters and know their beats precisely. Offer data, experts and embargoes, not press releases. Relevance is everything when 72% of pitches are judged irrelevant.
Sources: Cision State of the Media 2026 · Press Gazette job cuts · Chartbeat via Press Gazette · PR:journalist ratio · Axel Springer–Telegraph · AI licensing deals

D3 · Insularity, trust and the "hushing" retreat

From zero: the Edelman Trust Barometer (~34,000 people, 28 countries, every January) is the industry's most-quoted trust study. It says society moved from "grievance" (2025) to "insularity" (2026): people increasingly trust only those like them. Business, especially "my employer", is relatively trusted. But speaking out on contested issues (DEI, climate, Pride, tariffs) now invites attack from one side or the other. Many companies have gone quiet, and silence is itself a communications choice.
~7 in 10unwilling or hesitant to trust people with different values (Edelman 2026)
78%of employees trust their employer, the most trusted institution (Edelman 2026)
30%UK trust in news, down 5 points; global 37%, the lowest since 2015 (Reuters Institute 2026)
59%of S&P 500 firms changed or removed DEI wording in filings; only 19% removed it entirely (Orrick)

The vocabulary of retreat

Greenhushing
Doing climate work but saying less about it. About 70% of ~1,400 firms surveyed deliberately reduced climate communication while three-quarters spent more on decarbonisation (South Pole).
DEI hushing
"Reframe, not abandon". Use of the term "DEI" fell from 90% to 34% of S&P 500 filings, but 78% still disclose diversity information.
Sponsorship retreat
Three-quarters of 100+ UK Pride organisers saw corporate partnerships decline in 2025, and a quarter saw sponsorship fall by more than half.

Case studies insiders discuss

  • Cracker Barrel (Aug 2025): a new minimalist logo, buried in a press release about the autumn menu, triggered a culture-war backlash. The company reversed it within a week, and shares rose ~8% the next day. Lesson: even a logo is now a political event.
  • Amazon tariff display (Apr 2025): a report that Amazon's Haul store would show tariff costs drew a White House accusation of a "hostile and political act". Amazon said it "was never approved and is not going to happen" within hours.
The case for quietEmployees, not the public, are the trusted audience, so communicate inward, do the work and say less externally. Hushing reduces legal and political risk.
The case againstSilence reads as capitulation to staff and younger stakeholders. UK and EU rules still require disclosure. Hushing hides genuine progress, and Edelman argues CEOs are expected to be "trust brokers".
1–3 years: "values-quiet, operations-loud" becomes the default. Issue triage decides where a company has the standing and evidence to speak. US and UK diverge. 5+ years: a possible snap-back if politics shifts. Companies that kept their programmes but changed the labels will be best placed.
Should a company comment on a social issue? Insiders use a test: Does it affect our business, our people or our customers directly? Do we have the evidence and track record to speak credibly? What's the cost of silence? Practise applying it to a current news story.
Learn issue mapping and stakeholder materiality. Know the difference between "not saying" and "not doing". Expect interviewers to ask, "Should Company X have commented on Y?", and have a framework for answering.
Sources: Edelman 2026 · Reuters Institute DNR 2026 via Press Gazette · Orrick DEI disclosure · Greenhushing · UK Pride sponsorship · Amazon tariff episode

D4 · Deepfakes, synthetic media and proving what's real

From zero: AI can now convincingly fake a CEO's face and voice. Criminals use this to steal money, and bad actors use it to start crises. And when anything could be fake, real evidence can be dismissed as fake too: the "liar's dividend". Companies need ways to prove what's genuine, and crisis plans for when a fake goes viral.

The reference case: Arup (2024)

An employee at the UK engineering firm's Hong Kong office joined a video call where the "CFO" and several colleagues were all deepfakes. They made 15 transfers totalling about $25.6m. It's still the case every crisis team cites.

The scale (Resemble AI)

More than $1.28bn in deepfake fraud losses in 2025. In the corporate section: 41 incidents, $74.9m documented, the largest single loss $35m. Over 80% of incidents didn't disclose losses at all.

How content provenance works: C2PA "Content Credentials"

1 · CaptureCamera or app creates a "manifest": who made it, with what tool, what edits, whether AI was used
›
2 · HashA cryptographic fingerprint of the content is recorded
›
3 · SignThe manifest is signed with a private key, often in secure hardware
›
4 · VerifyAnyone can recompute the fingerprint. Any change breaks the match ("tamper-evident").

Limits: many platforms strip this metadata on upload; credentials prove origin, not truth; and adoption is patchy. Google's Pixel 10 reportedly signs all photos by default, while Apple's iOS 26 can view credentials but doesn't create them at capture. The EU AI Act's transparency duties apply from August 2026.

UK regulation in action: Ofcom opened a formal Online Safety Act investigation into X (January 2026) over its Grok AI being used to create sexualised "undressed" images. Potential fines reach 10% of global revenue.

Bull caseProvenance standards, verified owned channels and pre-registered "authentic" spokesperson content will make debunking fast. Technology plus speed can solve these crises.
Bear caseDetection is an arms race, and most people never check credentials. The faster threat may be AI systems confidently repeating falsehoods (D1) rather than fake videos.
1–3 years: crisis plans add deepfake scenarios, verification protocols (call-back codes, finance controls) and "pre-bunking" (warning audiences before fakes appear). Content credentials appear on official corporate imagery. 5+ years: corporate communications authenticated by default.
Ofcom's decision on X · EU AI Act enforcement · whether iPhones create Content Credentials at capture · annual deepfake loss data
Add verification to your crisis basics (holding statements, escalation trees, dark sites): reverse image search, checking Content Credentials, open-source intelligence. Know the Online Safety Act and Ofcom's role.
Sources: Arup case (CNN) · Resemble AI 2025 report · C2PA adoption 2026 · Ofcom vs X

D5 · Creators, platforms and executive visibility

From zero: younger audiences increasingly get news from TikTok, YouTube, podcasts and individual creators, not newspapers or TV. So PR now pitches creators, podcasters and newsletter writers as well as journalists, and helps executives build their own audiences, especially on LinkedIn.
52%of 18–24s say platforms or AI are their main news source (Reuters Institute 2026)
32% → 42%TikTok news use among 18–24s in one year
18% vs 32%weekly reach of news creators: UK vs US
70%of C-suite executives reconsidered a supplier after a competitor's thought leadership (Edelman–LinkedIn)

Journalists become creators

The top reason journalists use social media is to promote their own work (54%), and LinkedIn is their #1 platform in North America (Cision 2026). Many publishers are training journalists to behave like creators, and fear losing talent to Substack and YouTube.

Executive thought leadership

In B2B, 75% of decision-makers researched a product they hadn't considered because of thought leadership (Edelman–LinkedIn 2025). CEO and expert "personal brand" programmes are now a core agency service, especially in finance.
The rules: the UK ASA (Advertising Standards Authority) requires paid influencer posts to be clearly labelled as ads (e.g. "#ad"). "#aff" alone isn't enough, and the ASA publicly names non-compliant influencers. The CMA's new consumer-protection powers (D8) also cover hidden advertising. Once a creator is paid, the content is legally advertising, not earned media.
Bull caseCreators have trust and reach with young audiences that legacy media has lost. AI tools make finding and measuring creators scalable.
Bear caseAudiences rank influencers among the biggest misinformation threats. Only 6% of Brits say creators meet all their news needs. Paid creator content is advertising, and creators are cited less by AI than established outlets (D1).
1–3 years: creator relations merge with media relations, and journalist-creators on Substack and YouTube get pitched like outlets. The UK moves more slowly than the US. 5+ years: the line between earned, shared and paid creator content is regulated more tightly.
Here's a tension to think about: creators win young human audiences, but traditional outlets win AI citations (D1). If you had a limited budget for a financial services client, where would you put it, and why?
Be fluent in short video, newsletters and podcasts. Learn the ASA/CAP rules on disclosure. Know how to build an executive's LinkedIn presence: what to post, how often, and in what voice.
Sources: Reuters Institute DNR 2026 via Press Gazette · DNR 2025 · Cision 2026 · Edelman–LinkedIn · Ofcom news consumption

D6 · Measurement: from clippings to outcomes, and now AI visibility

From zero: PR has long been criticised for "counting clippings": measuring how much coverage it got rather than whether it changed anything. The industry standard says to measure whether people's awareness, attitudes or behaviour changed, and what that did for the business (Guide D3). The new problem: how do you measure appearing inside an AI answer that nobody clicks?

AMEC Barcelona Principles 4.0 (launched June 2025, Vienna)

#PrincipleWhat it means
1ObjectivesSet clear goals, and revisit them as things change
2StakeholdersListen to fragmented, polarised audiences
3ChannelsMeasure everywhere, now explicitly including newsletters, Discord and AI search outputs
4AnalysisMixed qualitative and quantitative methods, with human insight alongside AI
5Invalid measuresDon't use AVEs (advertising value equivalents, "this coverage was worth £X of adverts")
6Outputs → outcomes → impactReport all three levels, not just volume
7Ethics & transparencyBe open about data, methods and the use of AI

The new measurement dashboard

Share of voice
Your coverage vs competitors'
Share of model
Your mentions across AI answers vs competitors'
Sentiment
Tone in coverage and in AI answers
Business KPIs
Leads, trust scores, hiring, share price stability

About 40% of communicators already use AI for research and analytics (USC Annenberg 2025). AI-powered media intelligence tools (Cision, Muck Rack, Meltwater, CARMA) make outcome measurement cheaper. But AMEC hasn't yet standardised an AI-visibility metric.

Bull caseAI-powered monitoring plus AI-visibility trackers finally make proper outcome measurement cheap enough for every client.
Bear caseAVEs persist in practice. AI-visibility tools are unstable. Attribution (proving PR caused a sale) remains weak, and vendors define the metrics they sell.
1–3 years: dashboards combining share of voice, share of model, AI-answer sentiment and business KPIs. 5+ years: marketing-mix models that properly include earned media alongside paid advertising.
Be able to set SMART communications objectives, explain why AVEs are invalid, and describe outputs → outcomes → impact. In an interview, "I'd measure this by…" with a proper answer is a standout moment.
Sources: Barcelona Principles 4.0 · AMEC launch presentation · USC Annenberg 2025

D7 · The agency model under pressure: AI, mergers and pricing

From zero: agencies traditionally sold hours, many of them junior hours spent on media lists, monitoring, coverage reports and first drafts (Guide D7). AI now does much of that in minutes. Meanwhile the big advertising holding companies that own famous PR brands are merging, cutting costs or selling. Clients ask: why pay for hours AI saves?
+5–6%global PR agency fee growth in 2025; top 250 firms over $18bn (PRovoke)
$205krevenue per head, the first time above $200k: more output per person
76%of PR pros use generative AI, a plateau (Muck Rack 2026); only 12% use AI agents
"Run to stand still"PRWeek's verdict on UK agencies in its 2026 Top 150

Holding-company shake-up

GroupWhat happened
Omnicom + IPGMerger completed November 2025, creating the world's largest ad holding company. PR networks combined into Omnicom Public Relations; Golin and Ketchum merged (Feb 2026); Porter Novelli folded into FleishmanHillard. Famous brands disappear.
WPPA £500m savings plan to 2028; ~11,000 jobs lost since early 2025, and up to 1,000 more (FT, Sep 2026). Reportedly hired Goldman Sachs to explore selling Burson (not confirmed). Precedent: KKR bought a majority of FGS Global from WPP (~$1.7bn, 2024).
The ironyBurson won the 2026 Cannes PR Lions Grand Prix ("The KitKat Heist") and PR Agency of the Year while its owner considered selling it.

The pricing problem, in one calculation

The average US agency billing rate is about $278/hour (PR Council 2025). If AI compresses a 40-hour task into 4 hours and the client price stays the same, the implied rate exceeds $2,700/hour on the remaining time, and one commentator calculates $7,000+ on certain tasks. Either prices fall, or agencies switch to value-based or outcome pricing. This is the same shift happening in software sales (Part C5).

The junior squeeze: one UK dataset showed graduate vacancies down ~46% year on year (Adzuna, July 2026, unverified), and the Big Four cut graduate intakes. A CIPR Influence article (June 2026) warned "the bottom rungs of the career ladder have been kicked away". No PR-specific junior data was found.

Bull caseFees are still growing, and revenue per head is a record. Strategic advisory firms (Brunswick, FGS, Teneo) are thriving. AI frees people for strategy, and private equity money is circling the sector.
Bear caseUK firms "run to stand still". Holding companies treat PR as non-core and sell it. Storied brands are erased, the billable hour is deflating, and the junior pyramid that trained the profession is collapsing.
1–3 years: Burson's fate; agencies "productise" AI services (GEO audits, AI monitoring) and shift to retainers or outcome fees; fewer, more technical junior hires. 5+ years: a barbell of top-end strategic and financial advisory firms plus tech-enabled platforms, with a squeezed generalist middle. That's the same shape predicted for asset management (B3).
Entry roles will expect AI fluency from day one. Your value is judgement, writing, relationships and data, not list-building. Target firms by direction of travel: advisory boutiques, financial PR, or tech-enabled agencies.
Sources: PRovoke rankings · PRWeek UK Top 150 · Muck Rack State of AI in PR 2026 · Omnicom–IPG · WPP / Burson · $7,000/hour argument · CIPR Influence

D8 · Financial and corporate PR: IPOs, activists and legal claims

From zero: financial PR (IPOs, results, takeovers, activist fights) is the best-paid and most regulated corner of PR (Guide D6). Three big stories: London's listings drought and tentative revival, record shareholder activism (with the UK the most-targeted market in Europe), and new UK rules that turn marketing and green claims into legal risk, not just reputational risk.

London IPOs

2025 was the weakest year for London listings in more than 35 years: only £184m raised in nine months, against ~£17bn in 2021. CRH, Flutter and Wise moved their main listings to New York, and delistings have outnumbered listings every year since 2022. Signs of a thaw: H1 2026 saw 7 IPOs raising £577m, up 215% (EY), helped by a stamp-duty holiday on new listings and simpler FCA listing rules. The Iran war paused plans earlier in the year.

Record activism

255 global activist campaigns in 2025, a record (Barclays). Elliott was the most active, with 18 campaigns and ~$20bn deployed, including BP. 61% of Q4 campaigns pushed for M&A. The UK was Europe's most-targeted market, with record US activist pressure. See also Saba vs UK investment trusts (B12).

Claims become legal exposure

RuleWhat it means for communications
DMCC Act (CMA powers live April 2025)The CMA can now fine directly for misleading consumer practices, up to 10% of global turnover. First substantive fine: the AA, £4.2m for "drip pricing" (April 2026), plus refunds to 80,000+ customers. StubHub was fined £889k, and 100 firms received advisory letters.
Green claimsThe CMA's Green Claims Code and, for financial firms, the FCA anti-greenwashing rule. Every sustainability claim must be evidenced.
UK Sustainability Reporting StandardsFinal UK SRS (based on the global ISSB standards) published February 2026. The FCA consulted on requiring them for listed companies from 2027.
Market abuse (UK MAR)Inside information must be released properly via RNS. Leaks or selective briefing are illegal.

Tariff communications: "tariff" appeared on 238 S&P 500 earnings calls in Q3 2025, a third fewer than the previous quarter, as executives hedged their language to avoid political reaction. Some firms withdrew financial guidance altogether. Choosing how much to say about political risk is now a core financial-PR judgement.

Bull caseListing reforms and the 2026 pickup build an IPO pipeline. Record activism and M&A keep financial PR busy, and sustainability reporting creates new work.
Bear caseWeak post-IPO performance (2025's top five IPOs were down ~26% on average), New York's pull, and geopolitics keep closing IPO windows. Greenhushing becomes rational under the threat of fines.
1–3 years: late 2026–27 tests whether the London IPO rebound is real; UK SRS becomes mandatory for listed firms; more CMA fines, possibly the first green-claims fine. 5+ years: combined financial and sustainability narratives become audited territory, and "claims compliance" becomes a core PR skill.
Learn the basics of inside information, how an RNS works, takeover-code timing and how to substantiate a claim. This links directly to your asset management experience: the same discipline of accuracy applies.
Sources: EY UK IPO H1 2026 · Morningstar on delistings · Barclays activism · UK activism · CMA DMCC fines · UK SRS · Tariff mentions

D9 · Internal comms moves centre stage, and geopolitics arrives

From zero: two trends pulling communications teams in new directions. Inwards: employees are the most trusting audience (78% trust their employer), but they also leak, post and organise, and AI restructuring, layoffs and return-to-office rules all need careful messaging. Outwards: trade wars and real wars mean companies face political risk that communications must handle.

Internal communications

61%have no formal change-communication strategy (Gallagher 2026)
69%of comms teams have fewer than six people
73% vs 18%want to be strategic advisers vs say they actually are
~88,0002026 job cuts linked to AI (Challenger, secondary)

Return to office: Barclays delayed its three-day mandate to 2027 after a union-coordinated open letter; HSBC tied UK retail bonuses to office attendance; BlackRock requires managing directors in five days a week. Every internal memo is effectively public.

Geopolitics

$25bn+estimated cost of the 2026 Iran war to global companies (Reuters analysis, secondary)
279+firms cited the war when raising prices, cutting output or suspending buybacks
~2/3of tariff costs passed to consumers (Oxford Economics, secondary)
2/3of employees fear trade policy will hurt their employer (Edelman 2026)

"Corporate diplomacy" means communicating through tariffs, sanctions and conflicts where governments respond publicly to corporate decisions (see Amazon, D3). No neutral position exists.

Awards as signals: what the industry rewards

Cannes 2025 PR Grand Prix: "Lucky Yatra" (FCB India, Mumbai railways). Every train ticket became a lottery entry, to reduce fare-dodging. The jury rewarded redesigning a service to solve a business problem, not a stunt.
Cannes 2026 PR Grand Prix: "The KitKat Heist" (Burson London + VML). After a real theft of ~413,793 KitKat bars (about 12 tonnes) between Italy and Poland, Nestlé responded with humour around "have a break", reassured customers there were no safety or supply concerns, and invited the public to trace stolen batches. Fast, witty crisis judgement.
Bull caseInternal comms and public affairs gain a seat at the top table during AI transformation and geopolitical upheaval. Employees are the best ambassadors.
Bear caseTeams are tiny and unfunded, and information overload is rising. AI layoffs strain the "trusted employer" premium. Any geopolitical statement can trigger a government backlash.
1–3 years: AI-transformation change communication becomes the dominant internal brief, and internal and external messages must be identical (assume everything leaks). Scenario-based geopolitical risk communication becomes standard. 5+ years: internal and external comms converge into one "stakeholder communications" function.
Internal comms is an under-supplied, growing specialism and a strong entry route. Public affairs is another. Read the FT daily and learn to trace how a policy shock moves through costs, pricing and messaging. The KitKat Heist is a perfect case to discuss in an interview.
Sources: Gallagher 2026 · Barclays RTO · Iran war corporate costs (secondary) · Burson Cannes 2026 · Cannes 2025 Grand Prix

D10 · Synthesis: think like a PR insider

The five big shifts in one table

ShiftWhat it means
Earned media re-priced twiceScarcer (newsrooms shrinking) and more valuable (AI cites it). The risk: AI's zero-click behaviour starves the outlets it depends on.
Trust moved inwardEmployer trust 78%, news trust 37%. That elevates internal comms and makes external "hushing" tempting.
Claims became legal exposureDMCC fines, UK SRS, the Online Safety Act, EU AI Act, ASA/CMA. Communications people need compliance literacy.
Consolidating while growingFees up 5–6%, but UK firms "run to stand still", holding companies merge or sell, and junior roles shrink.
Verification is the new craftDeepfakes and AI falsehoods make proving what's real a core PR skill.

Five predictions to test

By 2028, "AI visibility" (how a brand appears in ChatGPT, Gemini and others) will be a standard line in every PR retainer, measured alongside share of voice.
The press release survives, but as a structured, fact-dense, machine-readable source document rather than a pitch.
Agencies move away from billable hours towards retainers, products and outcome fees, because AI makes hours meaningless.
Financial and corporate PR outgrows consumer PR in the UK as activism, M&A and regulated disclosure (UK SRS) expand.
Every major crisis plan includes a deepfake scenario and verification protocol within two years.
One sentence for your PR answer: "As AI systems increasingly decide what people read about an organisation, and draw heavily on credible earned coverage, the craft of building trusted, accurate stories has become more valuable, not less."
Choose one prediction you'd bet on and one you'd bet against. For each, name the evidence on this page. Then ask: what would change my mind? That last question is what separates a real opinion from a guess.
