A1 · Six habits of industry thinking

Industry professionals don't know the future either. What they have is a set of habits for turning news into a view. Learn these and every page that follows becomes easier, and you'll sound like an insider in interviews.

1 · Follow the money

For any trend, ask: who earns more, and who earns less, if this happens? Private markets grow because managers earn ~4× more profit per $bn there (B1). Outcome pricing spreads because AI shrinks seat revenue (C5). The money usually explains the motive.

2 · Think in second-order effects

First order: "AI writes emails faster." Second order: "everyone sends more emails, so inboxes flood, so providers tighten spam rules, so volume stops working, so human relevance becomes scarce" (C1). The interesting insights are usually two or three steps out.

3 · Separate structural from cyclical

Cyclical trends reverse when conditions change (e.g. a bad year for markets). Structural ones persist (e.g. passive's share rising for 20 years). The key question is always: "will this still be true in five years if conditions normalise?"

4 · Steelman both sides

Every theme here has a bull and a bear case. Before forming a view, be able to state the strongest version of the side you disagree with. Interviewers love "the bull case is X, but I lean towards Y because…"

5 · Watch leading indicators

Headlines are lagging. Insiders watch the numbers that move first: redemption requests before gates (B2), reply rates before pipeline (C1), click-through before newsroom cuts (D2). Each theme lists what to watch.

6 · Hold views loosely

A strong opinion is a position + evidence + what would change your mind. "I think X because of A and B; I'd change my mind if C happened." That's how professionals talk, and it sounds far more credible than certainty.

Recurring concepts you'll meet in all three industries

Disintermediation: cutting out the middleman (e.g. buyers skipping salespeople; AI answers replacing clicks to news sites)
Concentration: a market dominated by a few players (megafunds, model portfolio gatekeepers, holding companies)
Barbell: a market splitting into giants and specialists, with a squeezed middle
Commoditisation: a product becoming interchangeable, so price becomes the only differentiator (passive funds, basic outreach, press releases)
Unit economics: the revenue and cost of one unit (one client, one deal, one hour) that determines profitability
Operating leverage: when costs are mostly fixed, extra revenue is mostly profit, and lost revenue hurts badly
Regulatory arbitrage: structuring a product to fall under lighter rules (e.g. a 5% gate as convention rather than law)
Value capture: who in the chain keeps the profit: the manufacturer, the distributor or the platform

A2 · How to read a trend report (critically)

Most "trends" come from consultancies, vendors and surveys. They're useful, but every source has an angle. Professionals read them with five questions:

QuestionWhy it mattersExample from this briefing
Who published it, and what do they sell?Vendors publish research that supports buying their productSalesforce's AI survey (C1); Muck Rack's "earned media feeds AI" (D1)
Is it a measurement or an expectation?"Sellers expect AI to save 34% of research time" is not "AI saved 34%"Salesforce State of Sales (C1)
What's the sample and definition?Cold-email reply rates vary 7× by method; "evergreen AUM" may include insurance balance sheetsC1; B1
Is it a forecast?Forecasts from people selling the future are usually optimisticTokenisation: $715bn–$14tn by 2030 vs ~$12bn today (B8)
Does the opposite data exist?Most trends have contradicting evidence in the same dataset67% prefer rep-free vs 69% validate with reps (C2)
The source hierarchy: (1) Primary data: regulator statistics, company results, official filings. (2) Independent research: academic papers, Reuters Institute, Morningstar, BIS. (3) Consultancy reports: BCG, McKinsey, PwC (rigorous but selling advice). (4) Vendor surveys: useful signals with a commercial angle. (5) Commentary and opinion. Quote higher tiers when you can.
