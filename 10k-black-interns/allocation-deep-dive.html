Start here

The honest answer first

You cannot rank highly in the algorithm, because it doesn't rank. The employer FAQ says: "Candidates are randomly allocated to pledging organisations based on the selected workstream and location" and "Aside from the candidates' selected workstream, and location we do not filter candidates based on additional factors."

That splits the process into two separate games. You play them differently:

Game 1 Get allocated

A game of probability. The computer doesn't read your answers. You can't be "better" here. You can only put more tickets in bigger draws, through your workstream and location choices.

Run by: the Foundation's matching process
Decides: how many firms see you

Game 2 Rise to the top of the slate

A game of quality. A recruiter (and possibly an AI score) compares you with the other 25–50 people allocated to the same role. Here, ranking is real, and it is all about your CV and answers.

Run by: each firm, inside Pinpoint
Decides: whether you get an interview

Most applicants confuse the two. They polish answers hoping to "beat the algorithm" (impossible), then pick a location and workstreams without thinking (which is where Game 1 is actually won).

How to maximise your allocations

Because the draw is random, your expected number of allocations in each workstream behaves roughly like this:

Illustration only (made-up numbers). If IM × London has 60 roles, each drawing 35 candidates, and 1,500 applicants chose IM + London, then 60 × 35 ÷ 1,500 ≈ 1.4 expected allocations. If PR × Manchester has 3 roles × 35 and 200 applicants, that's ≈ 0.5. The real numbers aren't published, but the levers are clear: more roles in your cell and fewer people competing for them.

Things that do not help in Game 1: submitting first (applications are processed as one batch after 1 November, though you should still submit a few days early for safety), longer answers, keywords, or contacting 10K to ask for more allocations (the FAQ says such requests are not accepted).
One trade-off to think about: choosing a location only to increase your odds is pointless if you can't take the job. Internships last at least 6 weeks, paid at the Real Living Wage, and hosts may (but needn't) help with travel or accommodation. Pick locations you would actually accept.
Game 2

Inside Pinpoint: what the firm sees

When slates are released (23 Nov 2026), a recruiter at each firm opens a "job slate" in Pinpoint. Here is what is confirmed about that screen, and what Pinpoint is capable of. Each firm's settings are its own, so prepare for all of it.

Confirmed (Foundation FAQs)

  • Your profile carries your CV, cover letter and the 250-word answer for that workstream
  • Firms are told to review CVs and cover letters in Pinpoint before asking you for more information
  • Firms move you through statuses (e.g. Applied → in progress → hired / rejected). Statuses "do not negatively impact an applicant's profile", so a rejection from one firm doesn't follow you to another.
  • Rejection sends you an automated or personalised email
  • Firms see only candidates on their own slate

Pinpoint features a firm can switch on

  • AI Match Score (new in 2026): the recruiter answers questions about the role, Pinpoint generates up to 10 criteria marked must-have / preferred / nice-to-have, and every applicant gets a 1–10 score with a verdict, strengths, concerns and suggested interview topics. Pinpoint says it helps put "your strongest candidates… at the top".
  • Anonymised screening: names, contact details, location, dates and gender signals are removed for early review.
  • Scorecards: structured ratings by each reviewer.
  • Automations: e.g. auto-rejecting someone missing a core requirement.
Unknown: whether the 10K instance has AI Match Score or anonymised screening turned on, and for which firms. Pinpoint says its AI is opt-in and that "the goal isn't to make hiring decisions". A human makes the final call. The smart move is to write something that scores well with both, and the next page shows how.

The implication: you're ranked on what's visible in the first few seconds and on whether each likely criterion is clearly evidenced. That applies to the AI too, because it scores against criteria, not against how good the writing sounds.

Game 2

How to rank at the top of the slate

1. Reverse-engineer the criteria

A recruiter setting up an AI Match Score, or just mentally screening, for an "Investment Management summer intern" will land on criteria much like these. Make sure each one is explicitly evidenced somewhere they'll see it:

Likely criterionWeightWhere you evidence it
Genuine interest in investment management and marketsMustAnswer: opening insight + one live industry issue. CV: interests line (e.g. investment society, following markets).
Relevant experience / exposureMustCV: your asset-management role first, with results-focused bullets. Answer: the evidence paragraph.
Understanding of the industry / rolePreferredAnswer: AUM-based revenue, client retention, distribution (used correctly, not as buzzwords)
Analytical and numerical skillsPreferredCV: degree modules, Excel, a quantified bullet
Written and verbal communicationPreferredAnswer quality itself, plus a client-facing or presenting example
Teamwork, initiative, reliabilityNiceCV: society role, part-time job, a result you drove
Academic recordNiceCV: grade or predicted grade, clearly stated

Do the same exercise for Sales (target-driven, resilience, persuasion, relationship-building) and PR (writing, news awareness, organisation, research, media interest). Then check that each answer explicitly hits the must-haves.

2. Write for the skim and the scorer

Do

  • Front-load. Your first two sentences should contain the insight and the proof (your AM experience).
  • Name things plainly: "asset management internship", "client reporting", "RFP", "Excel". AI criteria and humans both look for explicit evidence.
  • Quantify wherever it's honest: numbers of clients, reports, £ raised, % improved.
  • Keep the CV, cover letter and answer consistent. The same story told from three angles reads as credible.
  • Use the workstream's own language. The 10K description of IM mentions "portfolios", "clients… pension funds, universities, insurance companies" and "long-term value".

Don't

  • Rely on your name, university or location to impress. Anonymised screening may strip them out, so the content must carry you.
  • Put key info in CV headers, footers, text boxes, tables or images. ATS parsers often skip them.
  • Keyword-stuff. Criteria-based AI looks for evidence of a criterion, not repeated words, and humans hate it.
  • Leave a must-have only implied ("I've always loved finance" is not evidence).
  • Upload a scanned or image-based PDF. Use a text-based PDF or Word file.

3. A CV that parses cleanly in Pinpoint

Layout
Single column, standard headings (Education, Experience, Skills, Interests), consistent dates (Jun 2025 – Aug 2025), 1 page.
Order
Education → Experience with your AM role first → Leadership/Activities → Skills. Bullets start with a verb and end with a result.
File
Text-based PDF named Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf. Open it, select the text and copy-paste it into Notepad. If it comes out jumbled, an ATS will see it jumbled too.

Your system

  • Gmail filter: from contains "pinpoint" OR subject contains "10,000" → star and label it
  • Check spam daily in late November and early December
  • A tracker with columns for firm, workstream, date contacted, next step, deadline and status
  • Have an updated CV ready. You can't edit the one on 10K, but you can send a new one to firms that contact you.

When a firm contacts you

  • Research that firm properly now. This is the first point where firm-specific tailoring pays off.
  • If they move you to their own ATS, complete it well before their deadline
  • Missed a deadline? Contact the firm directly. 10K can't reopen it.
  • Rejected? Ask once, politely, for feedback (most give it only after interview)

Myths vs facts

MythFact
"10K picks the best applicants and sends them on."No. It checks eligibility and allocates at random. Firms do all the selecting.
"Submitting early ranks you higher."No evidence. Applications are processed together after 1 Nov. Submit a few days early only to avoid last-minute problems.
"Better answers get more allocations."No. Allocations depend only on workstream + location. Better answers get more interviews.
"A rejection marks you down for other firms."No. Statuses "do not negatively impact an applicant's profile".
"I can ask 10K for more firms or a different one."No. Changes and extra allocations aren't accepted. Apply to other firms directly.
"The AI decides."Only if a firm switches it on, and even then Pinpoint positions it as decision support. A human reads your answer.

Summary: where to spend your effort

Game 1 (about 20% of your effort): choose your location(s) to maximise roles you'd accept, fill all three workstreams with IM first, and answer eligibility questions accurately. Game 2 (about 80%): a CV that parses cleanly with your AM role at the top, and three answers that clearly evidence every must-have in the first lines. After allocation: speed, organisation and firm-specific research.
