Goldman Sachs — AM Client Solutions Group, London Summer Analyst (req 171037)
Motivation statement — FINAL v3 — 288 words, three paragraphs (why Goldman / why role / why me), no dashes

Three Goldman insight events, across sales and trading, prime services and the buy side, taught me something I could not have read anywhere. On the equities panel, Jack McFarren described the salesperson as the conductor of the orchestra, drawing research, execution and the wider firm into one performance for a single client. The firm has a name for this: One Goldman Sachs. It matters to me because I want to build a career on the client side of finance, and the value of a client seat depends entirely on the platform behind it. Here, the whole firm is the product.

Client Solutions Group is where that idea becomes a job. Global Banking and Markets earned $41.5 billion last year; Asset and Wealth Management closer to $17 billion. The difference is texture. Deal fees must be re-won every January, while management fees accrue for every day a mandate stays funded, which is why durable fee revenue is the growth the firm's leadership is chasing, and why CSG, the group that wins, onboards and retains those mandates, exists. It owns the whole lifecycle: prospecting, RFPs, finals, then the reviews that decide whether a mandate endures or redeems.

My claim to the seat is that I have already worked one stage of that lifecycle. In Pictet's client onboarding team I brought on institutional clients, mostly other financial institutions, coordinating compliance, relationship managers and operations so that a client engaging several departments experienced one firm. I learnt that trust is won in the handoffs. But onboarding meets the client at chapter four of the story; I want the whole book, from first conversation to retained mandate, and this internship is where that craft is taught. Thank you for your time and consideration.
