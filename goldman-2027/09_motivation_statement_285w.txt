Goldman Sachs — Motivation statement (285 words, no dashes)

My introduction to Goldman came through three insight events, covering sales and trading, prime brokerage and the buy side. When Jack McFarren called the salesperson the conductor of the orchestra, pulling research, execution and the rest of the firm together to play the music the client wants, something clicked. I stopped seeing a bank as a set of separate desks and started seeing one instrument, with the client facing seat as the point where the whole firm comes together.

That integration is deliberate. I heard how Goldman brought once competing teams onto a single platform so a client meets one firm, and how much it matters that an analyst can attack a question from many angles in a way a smaller firm cannot. The product is really the whole platform, working for one client.

It is also the work I have enjoyed most. Onboarding new clients at Pictet, I coordinated compliance, the relationship managers and operations so the client felt they were joining one firm rather than being passed around. I learned to ask what a client actually wanted from a conversation, and how I could help with their goals rather than push my own.

What convinced me the sales seat belongs at Goldman is more technical. In European equities there are around a hundred venues since MiFID II, and a large order that touches the screen moves the price against itself. If a stock has 10% upside but buying it moves it 7%, only 3% is left. Protecting that spread takes real liquidity and balance sheet, and a stock borrow desk that sources what others cannot. That is not something a rival can copy quickly, and it is why I want to start here.
