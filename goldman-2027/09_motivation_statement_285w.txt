Goldman Sachs — Motivation statement (279 words, no dashes)

My introduction to Goldman came through three insight events, across sales and trading, prime brokerage and the buy side. I expected to leave talking about products and markets. Instead I left thinking about trust.

Every speaker, in their own words, kept returning to the same idea. Mini said the job is less about being the cleverest person in the room and more about understanding what makes your client look good to their own boss. Jack described relationships that started when he and a client were both juniors and grew over twenty years, until one became godfather to his child. What sat underneath all of it was simple. Products can be copied, but trust cannot, and trust is earned slowly, by making the person on the other side succeed before it ever pays you back.

That is a long game, and it is the one I want to play. It is also why the idea of the salesperson as the conductor of the orchestra stayed with me. The whole firm learns to play as one instrument so that a single client succeeds, and the client facing seat is where all of it meets.

I have felt a small version of this already. Onboarding new clients at Pictet, my job was to make someone feel they were joining one firm, not being passed between departments. I learned to ask what a client actually wanted before offering anything, because the trust you earn in the first week is what everything after it is built on.

I want to spend a career doing that, patiently, at the one firm whose whole model is built around it. Thank you for your time and consideration.
