# How To Write It Differently — A Client-Facing Masterclass
### Why everyone sounds the same, the four moves that fix it, and a live before→after clinic. Aimed at a client-facing role at Goldman.

---

## The one idea to hold onto

**Writing "differently" is not a vocabulary trick. It is a *substance* trick.**

People think standing out means fancier words — "leverage," "passionate," "dynamic." It doesn't. Two candidates using identical plain words can be worlds apart: one is generic, one is unforgettable. The difference is always the same thing —

> **How specific and how honest is the substance underneath the sentence?**

Generic = true of anyone, about any bank. Differentiated = could *only* have been written by you, about *only* Goldman. That's it. Everything below is just how to get there.

---

## Part 1 — Why everyone sounds the same

Almost every applicant makes the same three default moves without realising it. Learn to spot them — in your own draft — and you're already ahead:

1. **They claim traits.** "I have strong interpersonal skills, I'm a team player, I thrive under pressure." → Anyone can *type* these. Zero evidence. Invisible.
2. **They praise the firm.** "Goldman is a prestigious, world-leading firm with an amazing culture." → Flattery every applicant writes. Swap in any bank's name — still works. Dead.
3. **They restate the CV.** "During my internship at X I developed skills in Y." → The reader already has your CV. This adds nothing.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: **95% of applicants write some blend of these three, so the bar to stand out is genuinely low.** You don't need to be a genius writer. You need to *not* do these three things — and do the four moves instead.

---

## Part 2 — The four moves (this is the method)

Learn these four. Every strong sentence uses at least one. This is the whole craft.

### Move 1 — The Swap Test (kill the generic)
For every sentence, ask: **"If I swapped 'Goldman' for 'Morgan Stanley', or swapped *me* for any other applicant — would this still read perfectly?"** If yes, it's generic. Delete or rescue it.
- ❌ "I want to work at Goldman because of its world-class reputation." (true of any bank, written by anyone)
- The fix is always the same: **add substance until it can only be about you and only about Goldman.**

### Move 2 — Show, Don't Tell (claim → evidence)
Never state a quality. **Prove it with a specific thing you did**, and let the reader conclude the quality themselves. This is exactly what Sam teaches for CV bullets — "detail is how you demonstrate skill implicitly."
- ❌ "I'm great with people / a natural relationship-builder."
- ✅ [a specific 15-second story where you did something that *shows* it — see the clinic below]
- The reader thinking *"this person is good with people"* is 100× more powerful than you *saying* it.

### Move 3 — Concrete-First (open on a moment, not an abstraction)
Start sentences and paragraphs with a **specific image, moment, or detail** — not a general statement. Concrete is memorable; abstract is wallpaper.
- ❌ "I have always been fascinated by financial markets."
- ✅ "The £[X] I put into [specific stock] after [specific reason] taught me more than any lecture…"

### Move 4 — The Theme→Me Mirror (their DNA = how you already operate)
This is the highest-level move, and it's the engine of Sam's Optiver letter. Take something the firm genuinely values, then show **you already think/work that way** — with evidence. You're not saying "I admire your culture"; you're saying *"the way I already operate is the way you operate."*
- Structure: **[specific thing you observed the firm values] + [specific evidence you already live it].**

**Plus one rule that governs all four — go one level down.** Never write "Goldman's culture." Write about the *specific* thing (a desk, a client dynamic, a way of working) you actually saw. "Culture" is abstract; "the way the sales desk described conducting research, execution and prime as one instrument for the client" is concrete. Specific always beats general.

## Part 4 — THE CLINIC (before → after)

This is where you learn by watching the moves happen. Same idea, written the generic way, then rebuilt. Study *why* each one changes. (The "after" versions use **[bracketed placeholders]** — that's where *your* real facts go. I'm teaching the shape, not fabricating your life.)

---

**Clinic 1 — "Why I want a client-facing role"**

❌ **Generic:** *"I'm a strong communicator and a people person, so I'd thrive in a client-facing role at a top firm like Goldman."*
- **Diagnosis:** claims two traits (Move 2 fail), praises the firm generically (Move 1 fail), abstract (Move 3 fail). Swap-test: works for any candidate at any bank. Invisible.

✅ **Differentiated:** *"At the equities panel, Mini said the job isn't being liked — it's understanding what makes a client look smart to their own boss. That reframed something I'd felt but never named: [the moment you realised helping someone succeed to *their* boss/teacher/manager mattered more than being liked — e.g. how you handled a customer/teammate/committee]."*
- **Moves used:** concrete-first (opens on the specific panel moment), theme→me mirror (her insight → your lived evidence), and it's swap-proof (only true of you, only from this event). The reader concludes *"this person actually understands client work"* — you never had to claim it.

---

**Clinic 2 — "Why Goldman"**

❌ **Generic:** *"Goldman is a world-leading firm with an incredible culture and brand, and I'd be honoured to be part of it."*
- **Diagnosis:** pure flattery. Swap in "J.P. Morgan" — identical. This sentence is *actively* a red flag: it signals you have nothing specific.

✅ **Differentiated:** *"What struck me at the insight day was how the sales, research, execution and prime teams each described their job as playing one instrument for the client — Jack literally called the salesperson 'the conductor of the orchestra.' I hadn't understood before that Goldman's edge is the integration, not any single desk — and that's exactly the kind of connective role I'm drawn to, because [specific evidence you're the person who pulls pieces/people together]."*
- **Moves used:** one-level-down (the "conductor" specific, not "culture"), theme→me mirror, concrete-first. Un-swappable — it's Goldman's own language, from the room you were in.

---

**Clinic 3 — "Show don't tell" on relationship-building**

❌ **Generic:** *"I have excellent interpersonal skills and always build strong relationships."*

✅ **Differentiated (the trick is to *tell a 15-second story* and stop):** *"[The teammate/customer/regular who was difficult, and the specific small thing you did — remembered their name, solved the thing they actually needed, made them look good — and what it became over time.]"*
- **Why it works:** the reader *watches* you build a relationship and concludes the trait themselves. It also quietly mirrors Mini's "relationships that compound start early" and Shash's "remember names." You proved three things without claiming one.

---

**Clinic 4 — "Show don't tell" on genuine markets interest (the passion test)**

❌ **Generic:** *"I am deeply passionate about financial markets and follow them closely."*
- **Diagnosis:** the single most-written sentence in finance. Means nothing.

✅ **Differentiated:** *"[What you genuinely read/listen to] + [the £X you actually invested in [specific thing] and your real reasoning] — [and a specific market question you've been chewing on, e.g. one from the event: whether the market is really under-pricing the seismic-data bottleneck Jack described]."*
- **Why it works:** Sunil said he asks *every* candidate "what do you read?" and Guillaume asks "do you invest even £20?" — you're *passing the actual test* with evidence instead of claiming the trait. Un-fakeable because it's true.

## Part 5 — Your repeatable recipe (do this every time)

1. **Brain-dump ugly and honest first.** Write why you want this in plain, messy words — no trying to sound good. Truth first, polish later.
2. **Pick ONE client-facing theme** from Part 3 that is genuinely *you* (don't pick the one that sounds most impressive — pick the one you can back with real evidence).
3. **Find your evidence** — one specific 15-second story from your life that *shows* it (Move 2). This is the hardest and most important step. It doesn't have to be finance: a job, a team, a customer, a society role.
4. **Open concrete** (Move 3) — start on the event moment or the evidence, never on "I have always…".
5. **Run every sentence through the Swap Test** (Move 1). Cut anything that survives it. Then read it aloud — if it sounds like a robot or a brochure, rewrite it as you'd actually say it (GS screens for AI/generic — your natural voice is the goal).

## Part 6 — The red-flag self-edit checklist

Before you submit, delete or rewrite any sentence that:
- ☐ survives the Swap Test (works for another bank / another candidate)
- ☐ **claims** a trait instead of showing it ("I am hardworking/personable/driven")
- ☐ praises Goldman's "prestige / reputation / world-class culture"
- ☐ restates something already on your CV
- ☐ says "passionate about finance" (or any version of it)
- ☐ mentions money, prestige, or exit opportunities
- ☐ could have been written by someone who *didn't* attend your event
- ☐ sounds like ChatGPT rather than like you talking

If a sentence trips none of these, keep it. That's the whole test.

## Part 7 — Your blank scaffold (structure only — you write the words)

A ~300-word client-facing statement, built from the moves. I'm giving you the *frame and the job of each beat* — not the sentences.

- **Beat 1 — Concrete hook (2–3 sentences).** Open on the single client-facing moment from the event that genuinely landed for you (Mini's "make clients look smart," Jack's "conductor / pitch a conversation," Jessica's "listening"). *Move used: concrete-first.*
- **Beat 2 — Theme→Me (the core).** Name what that revealed about client work at Goldman, then prove *you already operate that way* with ONE specific story. *Move used: theme→me mirror + show-don't-tell.* ← spend the most words here.
- **Beat 3 — Why this desk (the tailored bit).** One division-specific line — a real Sales/Sales-Trading/Wealth dynamic or a deal/theme from the event — re-pointed per application. *Move used: one-level-down.*
- **Beat 4 — Close (1 sentence, your voice).** Why Goldman specifically, genuine, no adjectives, no prestige.

**Reflection prompts to fill it (be honest — the honesty is the differentiation):**
- When have you made someone *else* succeed — helped them look good to *their* boss/teacher/team — rather than seeking credit yourself? (Mini)
- When were you the "fixer" people came to when something went wrong? (Shash)
- When did *listening* — not talking — solve something? (Jessica)
- What relationship of yours grew because you invested in it early, before it was "useful"? (Mini/Jack)
- What do you genuinely read/follow, and what have you actually invested in? (the passion test)
