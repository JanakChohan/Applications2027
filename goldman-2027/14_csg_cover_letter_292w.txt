My three Goldman insight events, across sales and trading, prime services and the buy side, left me with one idea I could not put down. Jack McFarren called the salesperson the conductor of the orchestra, pulling research, execution and the wider firm together for one client. I later learnt the firm gives this a name, One Goldman Sachs, and it changed how I read the business itself: the advisory and trading desks earn their fees deal by deal, while Asset Management earns them for every day a client chooses to stay. Keeping that choice alive is a craft of its own.

At Pictet I lived one slice of that craft. Working in the client onboarding team, I brought on institutional clients, mostly other financial institutions, coordinating compliance, relationship managers and operations so that a client dealing with several departments still felt they were joining one firm. I loved owning the seams. My frustration was that I only ever met clients at chapter four of their story. The Client Solutions Group owns the whole book, from the first conversation to the RFP to the reviews that decide whether a mandate stays or is redeemed, and that full lifecycle is exactly the exposure I want next.

The deeper pull is time. One managing director on the equities panel told us her best client relationship today began when she and the client were both juniors booking trades at the end of the day. Jack has a client who is godfather to his youngest child. Relationships in this business compound the way the fees do, and I want to begin building mine now, in the part of Goldman where clients stay for decades. That is why this seat, and why now. Thank you for your consideration.
