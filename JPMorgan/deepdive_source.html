The one-sentence version of the firm's own theory of itself

The firm believes that holding more capital than it needs makes it the buyer in every crisis, that spending more than rivals in every downturn makes it the winner in every recovery, and that in wealth management the only measure that cannot be faked is whether clients keep sending new money. Everything else in this document is detail on those three beliefs.

Use this in an interview "What convinced me it was J.P. Morgan rather than a peer was three numbers: a 37% margin in the Private Bank against 25% to 29% at the closest rivals, $553bn of net new client money in 2025 against $356bn at the next best, and adviser headcount up 50% since 2021 while UBS shed advisers. Margin funds training, flows prove clients trust the advice, and a growing adviser base means the analyst programme is the pipeline, not a courtesy."

How to read those numbers like an insider

  • 2022 is the tell. Markets fell hard, AUM dropped by $350bn, and net flows almost stopped ($49bn versus $389bn the year before). Yet Private Bank revenue still rose 16%, because higher interest rates widened the margin on client deposits and loans. That is why the Private Bank is structurally more resilient than a pure asset manager: it has a bank inside it.
  • The adviser line is the strategy. Client advisors went from 2,738 to 4,101, a 50% increase in four years, and every 10-K from 2023 onwards attributes AWM's expense growth to "continued growth in private banking advisor teams". Revenue per Private Bank adviser stayed around $3m even as the headcount grew, which means the new advisers are not diluting productivity. Your internship exists because of this line.
  • Compensation is a third of revenue. AWM pays out roughly 36 cents of every revenue dollar in compensation, higher than the firmwide 29 cents, because this is a people business. Derived compensation per AWM employee reached about $291k in 2025.
  • EMEA is one sixth of the division and growing faster than the whole. EMEA client assets rose from $687bn to $1.03tn, a 51% increase, while EMEA revenue only rose 13%. Translation: the asset base has been built and the revenue is expected to follow. That is the gap the London hiring push is meant to close.
  • The loan book is the newest lever. Private Bank loans jumped from $236bn to $266bn in 2025 alone, the biggest one-year rise in the period. Lending to existing wealthy clients (mortgages, loans against portfolios) is where the growth in the "bank inside the private bank" now comes from.

What the 10-K says the Private Bank is, in its own words

The description has not changed in five years: the Global Private Bank "provides retirement products and services, brokerage, custody, estate planning, lending, deposits and investment management to high net worth clients". Notice what is absent. There is no dollar minimum anywhere in five filings. The firm never describes the Private Bank's strategy in the 10-K; it describes only its products. All of the strategy language lives in the shareholder letters and investor days (Part 3). The single most important sentence for a candidate is the one that appears every year: "The majority of AWM's client assets are in actively managed portfolios." The firm is betting that paying humans to pick investments beats index funds, and that its clients will keep paying for that.

Case study: First Republic, the deal that shows how the firm thinks

On 1 May 2023 First Republic, a San Francisco bank built around wealthy clients and their advisers, failed. J.P. Morgan bought it from the US deposit insurer the same weekend. The 10-K numbers: $192bn of assets acquired, including $153bn of loans, for $68bn of consideration, part of it a $50bn note to the insurer at 3.4% interest. Because the assets were worth more than the price, the firm booked a "bargain purchase gain" of $2.8bn, with further settlement gains of $103m in 2024 and $628m in 2025. The wealth angle: $145bn of First Republic client investment assets came across, First Republic deposits were moved into AWM in late 2023, and Private Bank revenue rose 20% that year partly on the acquired loans.

Why it matters to you: it is the third time in fifteen years (after Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual in 2008) that the firm has been the buyer of last resort. It could only do that because of the capital it holds, which only exists because it refuses to run the balance sheet hot. And it chose a wealth-focused bank, with $1.5bn of intangibles including wealth management client relationships, at exactly the moment it was doubling its adviser count. Strategy and opportunism are the same thing at this firm.

What the risk factors reveal about the competition it fears

  • The 10-K never names a wealth management competitor. It fears categories: "online automated algorithmic-based investment advice", "cryptocurrency, including stablecoins", "tokenized securities", and non-banks that lobby for "exemptions from regulatory requirements".
  • The 2025 filing adds a risk that "competitors are able to deploy AI more quickly". That is a firm spending nearly $20bn a year on technology worrying about being outrun.
  • Talent is a standing risk: "competition for qualified personnel is intense". From 2025 it also lists "workforce displacement" from automation and AI. Both sides of that sentence are relevant to someone joining in 2027.
  • Basel III "endgame" capital rules have been in limbo since 2023 in the US and delayed to 2027 in the UK. Uncertain capital rules are why the firm held a 15.7% CET1 ratio in 2024 and why lending to private bank clients, which uses capital, is managed carefully.

Part 3. The thinking: what the firm tells its owners, 2021 to 2026

In plain English Twice a year the firm explains itself to the people who own it. In spring, Jamie Dimon publishes a long letter to shareholders. In May, at Investor Day, each division head stands up with slides and is questioned by analysts. Mary Erdoes has done the Asset & Wealth Management session every year. These are the only documents where the firm says what it is trying to do and why. This section is built from the 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 Investor Day transcripts and decks and the 2021 to 2025 shareholder letters, read in order.

The operating model in four words

Every Investor Day since 2020 has opened with the same slide: the firm is "complete, global, diversified and at scale". Erdoes repeats it for her division: "We are complete. We are global. We are diversified. And we are at scale." Complete means the firm does every product a client might need, so it never has to send a client elsewhere. Global means it is in the room in 150-plus countries. Diversified means when trading is bad, wealth management or cards is good. At scale means the fixed costs (technology, research, compliance) are spread over more revenue than any rival's. The whole strategy is a set of consequences of those four words.

Bet 4 in detail: the international private bank, because that is your business

  • The argument. Daniel Pinto, then President, at the 2024 Investor Day: international private banking is "a substantially fragmented business everywhere". The firm's own share of the combined active asset management and private banking revenue pools is about 2% (2025 deck). Adam Tejpaul, who runs the International Private Bank: "in terms of where the biggest opportunity is to grow market share, it's very much within the European franchise". Nobody dominates, so an American bank with the largest balance sheet can take share by hiring.
  • The playbook. Onshore, multi-hub rather than London-only: offices added or expanded in Munich, Hamburg, Berlin, Edinburgh and Glasgow, Manchester in 2023, Leeds recruiting in 2025. Switzerland assets of $56bn to double by 2030 with staff up 30% in 2025. Middle East headcount from 370 to about 500 with revenue to double by 2030.
  • Why "while others retreat" is not just a boast. The years 2023 to 2026 saw Credit Suisse absorbed by UBS, Citi restructuring its wealth arm, HSBC reorganising, and several UK wealth managers sold or taken private (Part 7 has the detail). J.P. Morgan hired through all of it, including from Coutts, HSBC and Citi in the UK.
  • The London leadership change. Oliver Gregson, who ran the UK for nearly a decade, left in February 2025 to run Schroders' wealth business. Maricé Brown, who previously ran Mexico and before that a Silicon Valley team focused on founders and IPO planning, took over in June 2025. Her background is first-generation wealth and succession planning. That is a deliberate fit with a UK plan built on entrepreneurs in Manchester, Leeds and the Midlands rather than old money in Mayfair.

Part 5. The Private Bank's machinery, and where each track sits

In plain English The Private Bank is two machines bolted together. One machine faces the client: teams of bankers and specialists who win families and look after them. The other machine faces the markets: a large product organisation that decides what the bank thinks about markets, builds the portfolios and funds, negotiates with outside managers, and trades. The Advisor track trains you for the first machine. The Investment Solutions track trains you for the second. Most candidates do not know the second machine exists, which is why the roles feel confusing.

Why the London postings read the way they do

The firm's Asia careers page says full-time analysts "become an integral part of our investment solutions or integrated teams". That sentence is the key: "integrated teams" is the firm's name for the client-facing teams, and "investment solutions" is set against it. The full-time Investment Solutions posting says the same: you work "with product specialists on an investment solutions team or with a team of client advisors and bankers", and Solutions analysts "get involved with conducting research and analysis, using proprietary models, assist with product development and portfolio construction". Until the 2026 cycle this London track was simply called "Solutions"; the firm renamed it "Investment Solutions" globally. The Advisor posting, by contrast, says you "assist in client acquisition and relationship management across investment, credit, banking, and estate planning services". Acquisition is the word to notice.

The full-time programme both tracks feed into

  • Length: the current postings describe a two-year Analyst Program (older international postings said three). Top performers move to Associate; at Associate you choose the banker route or the investor route.
  • Training: a regional orientation, then "a five-week immersive global training program" in New York with incoming analysts from around the world, then continuous training. At the 2025 Investor Day the firm said internally grown advisers now get three times the training hours and four times the teaching time from top advisers compared with 2019.
  • The firm's own thesis about you: Erdoes told investors in May 2025 that "half of our new advisors come from growing them from within", up from a third in 2019, and that "25-year-olds that are coming online" are "just as productive as our 30- to 35-year-old mid-career hires". The median third-year Associate's flows productivity rose 3.4 times between 2019 and 2024. This is the clearest statement that the analyst programme is the main pipeline for the adviser growth in the 10-K, and it is why your internship is a serious hiring decision for them.
  • Ladder: Analyst, Associate, Vice President, Executive Director, Managing Director. Team Heads and Region Heads run markets. Forum accounts put Associate at three to four years and VP at about three.

How the money works in the seat

The Private Bank pays salary plus a discretionary bonus at every level. It does not use a "grid" (a percentage payout on the revenue an adviser personally generates), which is how brokerages and many US wealth firms pay. The firm's own compensation disclosure says bonuses "are not related directly to the sale of any specific product" and that managers use scorecards with "no prescribed relationship" to pay. In practice the scorecard for an adviser is dominated by flows, and the Investor Day literally labels adviser productivity as "Productivity (flows)". For you this means three things: the firm can afford to invest in training you for years before you produce; it can move you between teams without breaking a personal P&L; and your ceiling depends on the book you eventually build rather than on a payout formula.

Part 6. How a client is actually served: the process both tracks plug into

In plain English A family does not walk in and buy "a portfolio". They go through a process that the firm has industrialised: understand the whole balance sheet, sort the money into buckets by purpose, set a long-term mix, implement it with the firm's building blocks, layer borrowing on top, then review. The Advisor analyst lives in the first two steps and in packaging the rest. The Solutions analyst lives in the building blocks.

The house view right now, so you can talk about it

The Private Bank's 2026 Outlook is titled "Promise and Pressure" and is built on three themes: artificial intelligence, geopolitical fragmentation, and inflation. The monthly Global Investment Strategy View in 2026 has been overweight US equities, emerging markets, gold, defence and security, utilities and infrastructure, and short-dated bonds, neutral on Europe, and cautious on software and long-dated bonds, while pushing clients "beyond the 60/40" into infrastructure, hedge funds and structured products. Grace Peters, co-head of Global Investment Strategy, described the themes as "a fundamental shift in how economies operate"; Stephen Parker, her co-head, said investors "must navigate private markets with care, prioritizing manager selection and access". If an interviewer asks for your market view, you will do well to know the firm's view first and then say where you agree or disagree and why.

The intern's summer, reconstructed from people who did it

  • A training week (New York for many cohorts, London induction for the UK track) built around "immersive simulations and role-play scenarios focused on client engagement".
  • Advisor-side interns report prospect research, data entry into Connect, building presentations, sitting in on calls, and studying "how geopolitical events and market volatility affect investment portfolios". Client contact is limited until you are licensed.
  • Solutions-side interns (and their Asset Management equivalents) report daily morning strategy calls, note-taking on client calls, building decks, and pitches to the team.

Part 7. The competition: who else wants the same clients, and how they compare

UK domestic banks and wealth managers (Coutts, Barclays, HSBC, Rathbones, Evelyn, St James's Place) sell the brand on the high street and lower minimums. So why is J.P. Morgan the best place to learn this business? The evidence, not the brochure

  1. Highest margin in the peer set. 36% in AWM and 37% in the Private Bank, against 29% at Morgan Stanley, 28% at Bank of America, 25% at Goldman and UBS, and about 22% at Citi. Margin is what pays for training, technology and patience with a junior.
  2. Most new money, for longest. $553bn of net flows in 2025 versus $356bn at Morgan Stanley and $101bn at UBS, and 22 consecutive positive years. The scorecard the firm chose ("clients vote with their flows") is the one it leads.
  3. Growing advisers while rivals shed them. Private Bank advisers up 9% in 2025 and 50% since 2021; UBS down 383 in the year; Merrill with net departures. A firm adding seats is a firm that needs to fill them from the analyst programme.
  4. The only one with all three engines on one balance sheet. A top-ranked private bank, a top-five active asset manager that is number one in flows, and the world's largest investment bank. Erdoes: "there aren't any investment bankers that want to take a private banker to a meeting except for inside JPMorgan Chase." That is the founder-liquidity-event machine in one sentence.
  5. Alternatives leadership confirmed by outsiders. Best for alternatives at Euromoney in 2025 and 2026 and at PWM in 2025; $2bn deployed a month; 850 external managers. The product the whole industry is moving to is already the firm's strength.
  6. Technology at a scale no wealth-only firm can match. About $18bn a year firmwide, 63,000 technologists, LLM Suite on 200,000 desktops, Connect Coach rolled to private bankers first. Euromoney's best digital private bank in 2026.
  7. EMEA momentum with London as hub. Revenue up 14%, assets up 25%, advisers up 9% in the year to June 2025; Europe's Best International Private Bank 2026; a stated UK plan to double regional business by 2031.
And the weaknesses a good interviewer will respect you for knowing
  • The UK brand is thinner than Coutts, Barclays or HSBC. The 2025 and 2026 UK-specific awards went to Coutts, Barclays, Arbuthnot, EFG and Standard Chartered. Tejpaul concedes "low single-digit market shares" in several European countries. J.P. Morgan is the challenger in the UK, not the incumbent.
  • It is still a US firm. International assets were $751bn of roughly $3tn of Private Bank assets in 2024. UBS's EMEA book alone is about the size of J.P. Morgan's whole international private bank.
  • The £3m to £10m segment is left to others. The $10m floor means Coutts, Barclays, UBS and Julius Baer own the entry point where many entrepreneurs first choose a bank. Chase UK and J.P. Morgan Personal Investing (the renamed Nutmeg, which lost £40m in 2024 and over £200m cumulatively) are the firm's answer, and it is not yet working.
  • Conduct record. $348m and $200m regulatory penalties for trade surveillance failures (2024), a $151m SEC settlement including wealth-client fiduciary failures (2024), and the Epstein-related settlements (2023). A candidate who says "first-class business in a first-class way" without knowing these will sound naive.
  • Flows depend on markets and IPOs. Fourth-quarter 2025 flows were down 8% year on year; 2025 market gains of $633bn exceeded net flows. The adviser hiring push raises expenses before the new advisers produce.
  • The UK non-dom exodus cuts both ways. The firm can follow clients to Dubai or Geneva, but so can every Swiss rival, and a smaller UK ultra-wealthy pool makes the London growth target harder.

Glossary of technical terms used in this document

TermMeaning
10-KThe audited annual report a US-listed company must file with the SEC. Legally binding, no marketing. Sections: Business (Item 1), Risk Factors (Item 1A), Management's Discussion (Item 7), financial statements.
Investor DayAn annual presentation in May where each division head shows targets and results to analysts and takes questions. Transcripts and slides are public.
Managed basis (revenue)The firm's presentation of revenue that adds back tax-equivalent adjustments so segments can be compared. Slightly higher than reported revenue.
Pre-tax income / pre-tax marginProfit before tax; margin is that profit divided by revenue. The best single measure of a division's efficiency.
Return on equity (ROE) / ROTCEProfit divided by the capital shareholders have in the business. ROTCE strips out intangible assets. Banks target mid-teens; AWM makes 40%.
CET1 ratioCommon equity tier 1 capital as a share of risk-weighted assets. The core regulatory measure of how much loss a bank can absorb.
Net interest income (NII) / non-interest revenue (NIR)Interest earned minus interest paid; and everything else (fees, trading, commissions).
Assets under management (AUM) / client assets / assets under supervision (AUS)Money the firm invests on a client's behalf; the broader total including assets it holds in custody or advises on; and the firm's own term for that broader total.
Net flows / net new assets / net new moneyMoney clients add minus money they withdraw, excluding market movements. The key growth measure in wealth management.
Bargain purchase gainAn accounting gain booked when a buyer pays less than the fair value of what it acquires, as with First Republic.
Basel III endgameThe final round of international bank capital rules, still unfinalised in the US and delayed to 2027 in the UK.
Discretionary vs advisory (or brokerage)Discretionary: the bank manages the portfolio within agreed rules. Advisory/brokerage: the client decides each move on the bank's advice.
Strategic vs tactical asset allocationStrategic: the long-term target mix set from 10-to-30-year return assumptions. Tactical: short-term tilts around it based on the monthly house view.
Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions (LTCMA)J.P. Morgan Asset Management's annual forecasts of returns, risks and correlations for every asset class over 10 to 15 years. The input to strategic allocation.
Investment Policy StatementThe written document recording a client's objectives, target allocation and constraints.
Alternatives / private marketsInvestments outside listed shares, bonds and cash: private equity, private credit, real estate, infrastructure, hedge funds.
Feeder fund / evergreen fund / capital callA vehicle that pools many clients into one large fund; a fund with no fixed end date that clients can enter and exit periodically; a demand for a client to send money they committed to a fund.
Active ETF / SMAAn exchange-traded fund run by a manager rather than tracking an index; a separately managed account, a portfolio held directly in the client's name and customisable.
Structured noteA bond issued by the bank whose payout depends on a formula linked to markets, e.g. capital protection plus a share of equity upside.
Securities-based lendingA loan secured on an investment portfolio so the client gets cash without selling.
Single coverage vs partner coverageOne adviser acting as both banker and investor for a client, versus a banker and an investor working as a pair. The firm has extended single coverage from 8 to 18 international markets.
Grid (compensation)A payout formula giving an adviser a fixed percentage of the revenue they generate. Used by brokerages; not used in the Private Bank, which pays salary plus discretionary bonus.
Non-domA UK tax status for residents domiciled elsewhere that sheltered foreign income; abolished 6 April 2025 and replaced by a four-year foreign income and gains regime.
Liquidity eventThe moment an illiquid asset, usually a company, turns into cash. The point at which private banks compete hardest for a new client.
Wealth Management Solutions (WMS)J.P. Morgan's product and investment organisation (2,500-plus people) that serves the Private Bank and Chase's advisers: CIO, strategy, alternatives, brokerage, structured products.
Connect / Connect Coach / LLM Suite / SpectrumIQThe Private Bank's client-management workstation; the AI layer on it that suggests next actions; the firm's general-purpose generative AI platform; the AI system that analyses securities and automates trading in Asset Management.
