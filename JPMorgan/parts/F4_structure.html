Part 6. Structure follows culture: eight things about the Private Bank that only make sense once you know the belief behind them

In plain English When you first read the organisation chart in volume 2, several things look odd. Why is there a 2,500-person product organisation separate from the bankers? Why does the firm pay bankers a bonus rather than a cut of what they sell? Why open an office in Manchester rather than book northern clients from London? Why are the largest families served by a team that belongs to no division? Each has a belief behind it, and in most cases an episode where the belief was tested.

2. "Buyers of things, not distributors of things" produced Wealth Management Solutions, and the 2015 order proved it was needed

Figure 7. Independence of advice from distribution is a belief that has been tested twice by the regulator. The structure is the response.

Erdoes on the original intent, describing 2005: "We wanted to be the investment bank for people's personal balance sheets, to be buyers of things rather than a brokerage house that was a distributor of things." That is why the investor and the banker are different people on the team, and why the Investment Solutions track is a separate career path rather than a sales-support role.

What this means for you When an interviewer asks "what do you know about how we're organised", most candidates recite the divisions. You can explain why: the integrated team exists because nobody may say "my client"; Wealth Management Solutions exists because advice must not depend on what sells; the training programme is taught by producing advisers because the CEO fired the outside coaches; and Manchester exists because the firm believes you have to be where the clients are. That is the difference between reading the org chart and understanding it.
