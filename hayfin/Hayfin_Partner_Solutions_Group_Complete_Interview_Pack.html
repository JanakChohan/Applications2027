Duty 3. "Developing case studies on investments for use in marketing and investor communications."

This is the duty with the sharpest compliance edges. Know them unprompted:

  • Fair, clear and not misleading. FCA COBS 4.2.1R: "A firm must ensure that a communication or a financial promotion is fair, clear and not misleading" [S49]. Confirmed
  • Past performance. COBS 4.6.2R requires that past performance is not the most prominent feature, covers complete 12-month periods, states the source, warns that it "is not a reliable indicator of future results", and discloses the effect of fees if shown gross [S50]. These rules are written mainly for retail communications; Hayfin's investors are professional, so treat them as best practice and check with Compliance Inferred.
  • No cherry-picking. In the US, the SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1) bans gross performance without net performance shown with equal prominence. A 19 March 2025 SEC staff FAQ allows gross-only figures for a single extracted deal only if the whole portfolio's gross and net performance is shown with at least equal prominence [S53][S173]. Hayfin's own published strategy updates carry this language [S330]. Confirmed
  • Greenwashing. The FCA anti-greenwashing rule (ESG 4.3.1R) requires sustainability claims to be "consistent with the sustainability characteristics of the product or service" and "fair, clear and not misleading" [S52]. Hayfin trained its "Investor Relations and Partner Solutions teams" on this rule in 2024 [S25]. Confirmed
  • Confidentiality. Borrower names and numbers in a case study must be public or cleared. Loan terms are usually confidential under the facility agreement Inferred.

Duty 4. "Assisting with quarterly LP reporting, including updating performance data and proofreading reports."

Hayfin's Stewardship report lists exactly what goes to each fund and SMA every quarter [S305]: a Summary of Investments and Portfolio Update ("A line-by-line track record is provided for both realised and unrealised investments", with amount invested and called, carrying value, realised proceeds, expected gross IRR at deal and fund level, deployment and repayment profile); a Portfolio Breakdown with each company's LTM EBITDA, enterprise value and leverage; a CEO Letter sent through the administrator's portal; and audited financials within 90 days of 31 December. Key term · the return metrics you will touchIRR (internal rate of return): the annual return that makes all cash in and out net to zero. Gross = before fees and carry; net = after. MOIC: total value divided by money invested. DPI (distributions to paid-in): cash returned divided by cash called. RVPI: remaining value divided by cash called. TVPI = DPI + RVPI. Subscription line: a short-term bank loan to the fund that delays capital calls; it lifts IRR without changing the multiple. LTM EBITDA: a company's last twelve months of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Leverage: debt divided by EBITDA.

The 2026 change you should know. ILPA released an updated Reporting Template and a new Performance Template on 22 January 2025. The Reporting Template applies to funds "still in their investment period during Q1 2026" or starting on or after 1 January 2026 [S43]. The Performance Template's first delivery is due after the quarter ending 31 March 2027, according to Travers Smith [S45]. DLF V was still investing in 2026, so it is likely in scope if Hayfin adopts the templates Inferred. A summer 2027 intern may work on the first deliveries. Hayfin's adoption is not public [Gap].

What good proofreading looks like. Every number ties to its source (administrator report, monitoring system); dates and currencies are right; totals add; commentary matches the numbers; borrower names are spelled correctly and none are disclosed that should not be. A recruiter's description of IR work: "Materials must be prepared in a timely manner, but also with little to no errors" [S59].

QQp2bQQB. How Hayfin makes money

An asset manager like Hayfin makes money in two ways. First, a management fee: a percentage of the money it manages, paid every year whatever happens. Second, a performance fee or carried interest: a share of the profit above an agreed minimum return (the hurdle or preferred return), paid only if the fund does well. QQp3aQQ1. Why this structure: the theory, and what it costs

The theory in one sentence. A multi-strategy credit manager shares one origination network and one underwriting culture across several funds, so it can lend across the capital structure and across the cycle, with a workout team behind every loan. Each link below has evidence.

The evidence, link by link

This has allowed Hayfin to cast a wider net and engage a broader range of companies with the market now not only viewing Hayfin as a 15%+ lender but an 8%+ lender. Once engaged, Hayfin has a greater ability to obtain favorable pricing" [S307]. [Confirmed, primary LP document] In plain words: a company that meets Hayfin for a cheap senior loan is a future customer for a dearer, more complex one, and the other way round.
  2. Deploy at every point of the cycle. QQp5aQQWhat private credit is

A company that wants to borrow has three broad options. It can borrow from a bank. It can sell bonds or a syndicated loan to many investors through a bank, which are then traded. Or it can borrow directly from a private credit fund, which negotiates the loan one to one and usually holds it until it is repaid. Private credit means the third option [Inferred, standard definition].

Key termsDirect lending: a fund lends directly to a mid-sized company, usually PE-owned, often to pay for a buyout; floating rate, senior secured, 5 to 7 years. Senior secured: first claim on the company's assets. Unitranche: one loan blending senior and junior debt. Mezzanine: junior debt between senior and equity. Special situations / opportunities: flexible capital for complex or stressed cases. Distressed: buying debt of companies near default at a discount. Asset-based finance (ABF): loans secured on pools of assets, not company cash flow. NAV lending: loans to a PE fund secured on its portfolio. CLO: a vehicle that buys syndicated loans and funds itself with rated slices of debt. High yield: traded bonds rated below investment grade. Floating rate: interest that moves with a base rate such as Euribor. Covenant: a promise by the borrower, such as keeping leverage below a level. PIK (payment in kind): interest added to the loan instead of paid in cash.

QQp5bQQWho borrows, who invests, and how it differs from neighbours

Borrowers. Mostly PE-owned mid-market companies. The IMF said private credit borrowers "are often smaller and more indebted" than bond or syndicated loan issuers [S150]. The FSB said in May 2026 that borrowers "typically have lower credit quality and higher leverage", concentrated in technology, healthcare and services [S178]. Confirmed

Investors. Pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, endowments, family offices, funds of funds and, increasingly, wealthy individuals through wealth platforms. The Alternative Credit Council's 2025 survey found institutions supplied 76% of commitments and retail about 24% [S182]. In the euro area, insurers hold about €211bn of private credit exposure, pension funds about €52bn, banks €62.5bn (ECB, May 2026) [S177]. [Confirmed for ECB]
