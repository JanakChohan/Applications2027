# WS3: Hayfin's competitive landscape in European private credit

Prepared 2026-10-08. Source IDs S120-S149. Confidence tags: [Confirmed] = primary source or two credible outlets. [Reported] = one credible outlet. [Inferred] = my own reasoning from the sourced facts.

Method note. I ran about 30 web searches and fetched about 20 full pages and PDFs. The PDFs were the Tikehau H1 2026 results deck, the ICG FY26 results transcript, the Ares Q2 2026 SEC earnings exhibit and the 2014 L&G/Pemberton press release. Several pages were blocked or paywalled (Bloomberg, Business Wire, PE Insights, IPE). Those are marked in the source table, and I did not guess what they say.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Hayfin baseline (the reference point for every comparison)

- **Founded and history.** Hayfin was founded in 2009 [S127][S121] [Confirmed]. BCI (British Columbia Investment Management Corp) bought a majority stake in January 2017. Management agreed an Arctos-backed buyout of that stake on 30 July 2024 [S123] [Confirmed]. Financial terms were not disclosed [S123].
- **Ownership now.** After the MBO, Arctos sold minority stakes to Mubadala and AXA IM Prime (July 2025 partnership), then to Samsung Life Insurance (announced 10 September 2025, subject to regulatory approval) [S124] [Reported]. ACI reports that the Hayfin team are now "majority owners of the firm's common equity" [S124] [Reported]. A Korean outlet says Samsung Life's stake is 3.96% and completed in December 2025 [S128]. That outlet is an AI-generated summary, so treat it as [Reported, low confidence]. Bloomberg covered the Samsung deal, but the article is PAYWALLED-NOT-READ [S148].
- **AUM over time.** About €31bn at July 2024 [S123] [Confirmed]. €34bn at September 2025, across private debt, liquid credit and private equity [S124] [Reported]. About €40bn in the 2027 JD pack (brief) and in an October 2026 Korean report [S128] [Reported]. Growth from €31bn to about €40bn in roughly two years is mostly DLF V [Inferred].
- **Platform (from the Hayfin website).** Private Credit covers Direct Lending, Special Opportunities and Tactical Solutions, with Healthcare and Real Estate sector focuses. Liquid Credit covers High-Yield & Syndicated Loans and CLOs. There is also Private Equity Solutions and Real Assets (Maritime) [S127] [Confirmed]. The site says "With 13 global offices" [S127]. The JD and Workable page say ten offices, so the count depends on the source and date [Inferred].
- **Since inception.** Hayfin has invested over €55bn across more than 500 portfolio companies, per the July 2026 boilerplate [S121] [Reported]. The SEO page figure in the brief (over €30bn, 440+ companies) looks out of date [Inferred].
- **Flagship.** Direct Lending Fund V (DLF V) closed on 2 July 2026 at over €15bn, including related vehicles, "significantly exceeding" its target [S120][S121] [Confirmed]. DLF IV closed in August 2023 at over €6bn [S121] [Confirmed]. DLF V was more than 50% deployed across more than 35 companies at close [S121]. Hayfin made a record €7.1bn of direct lending investments in the prior 12 months. The strategy has deployed €38bn since inception across more than 350 investments [S120][S121] [Confirmed].
- **Investor base for DLF V.** Exclusively institutional: public and private pensions, financial institutions, insurers, SWFs, funds of funds, endowments, consultants and family offices [S121] [Confirmed]. A rated note feeder for insurers contributed about $600m [S122] [Confirmed]. Hayfin linked demand to strains in US semi-liquid retail funds, which "accelerated limited partner demand for conservative fund structures" [S120] [Reported].
- **Liquid credit.** The CLO platform had over €7.1bn AUM at 31 December 2025. Hayfin did six CLO resets in 2025, four European (Emerald series) and two US [S125] [Reported].
- **Special Opportunities.** Fund III closed above its €2bn target in December 2021, with related SMAs [S149] [Reported]. I could not confirm a Fund IV final close (see Gaps).
- **Asia push.** Hayfin opened a Seoul office on 1 October 2026, led by Director Sijin (Chris) Choi, to serve Korean institutions [S126] [Reported]. This follows the Samsung Life stake [S124][S126] [Inferred link].

### 1.2 The eight peers (plus HPS and CVC as context)

**Arcmont Asset Management (Nuveen/TIAA-owned).**
- Started in 2011 as BlueBay's private debt division. Spun out in 2019 with a Dyal minority stake. Nuveen closed its acquisition of a controlling interest on 1 March 2023 [S131] [Reported].
- Combined with Churchill, it forms Nuveen Private Capital. At closing that was over $66.5bn of committed capital [S131] [Reported]. The January 2024 Arcmont release put the combined Arcmont and Churchill figure at €73bn [S129] [Confirmed].
- Flagship: Direct Lending Fund IV closed at €10bn total investable capital in January 2024, at the top of its €8-10bn target. It was about 55% committed at close [S129] [Confirmed].
- Capital Solutions Fund II closed at about €1.5bn in February 2026, roughly double its predecessor [S130] [Confirmed].
- Arcmont says it has raised about €40bn of investable capital over 15 years across Direct Lending, Senior Loan, NAV Financing, Impact Lending and Capital Solutions [S130] [Confirmed]. That is cumulative capital raised, not current AUM.
- Model: mostly direct lending, plus capital solutions and NAV lending. It has no high-yield or CLO business and no sector verticals comparable to Hayfin's [S130][S127] [Inferred].
- IR and distribution: Arcmont can use Nuveen's global distribution and TIAA's insurance balance sheet. I saw no Arcmont evergreen wealth product in these sources (see Gaps).

**Intermediate Capital Group (ICG, LSE-listed).**
- AUM was $126bn at 31 March 2026. Fee-earning AUM was $86.5bn, up 11% year on year. FY26 fundraising was $17bn, of which Debt was $4.1bn [S133] [Confirmed]. ICG founded in 1989 (common knowledge, not verified here).
- Flagship DL: Senior Debt Partners 5 closed at $17bn (about €15.2bn) on 11 September 2024, including mandates, co-invest and sidecars. The initial target was $11-12bn and the fund was about 45% invested at close [S132] [Confirmed]. SDP 6 will likely launch later in FY27 [S134] [Confirmed].
- Model: multi-strategy. It covers structured capital, secondaries, real assets, private debt and liquid credit/CLOs [S133] [Confirmed].
- Positioning: the CEO says SDP is "100% ... senior secured, cash-pay" lending, with "no PIK or sub debt", "minimal software exposure", no US direct loans for two years, and "no open-ended or so-called semi-liquid structures in direct lending" [S134] [Confirmed].
- IR and fundraising: 870+ institutional LPs, with 83 new in FY26 [S133/S134 via search summary, Reported]. ICG has a wealth partnership with Amundi and has filed a Luxembourg SICAV umbrella. It is "broadening our insurance and North American coverage within our marketing teams" [S134] [Confirmed].
- Ranking: #6 in the 2026 PEI Private Credit 200, with $63.8bn raised from 2021 to 2025 [S144] [Confirmed].

**Ares Management (NYSE-listed; European DL arm).**
- Firm AUM was $671.3bn at 30 June 2026. Credit AUM was about $440bn [S136] [Confirmed]. Ares was founded in 1997 (common knowledge, not verified here).
- Flagship European DL: Ares Capital Europe VI closed at its €17.1bn hard cap in January 2025. The target was €15bn and LP commitments rose about 53% versus ACE V (€11.1bn). Total strategy capital including vehicles and leverage was about €30bn. Ares calls it "the largest institutional fund to date in the global direct lending market" [S135] [Confirmed, self-description].
- Target borrowers have EBITDA above €10m, which is upper mid-market [S135] [Reported].
- Wealth and insurance: an open-ended European direct lending fund (AESIF) passed €2.2bn in its first full year, raised mainly through the wealth channel [S137] [Reported]. It raised another $1.0bn in Q2 2026 alone [S136] [Confirmed]. Ares also reports "additional managed assets from our insurance platform" [S136] [Confirmed].
- Ranking: #1 in the 2026 PEI Private Credit 200, with $118.0bn raised from 2021 to 2025 [S144] [Confirmed].

**Pemberton Asset Management (Legal & General 40%).**
- L&G took a 40% equity stake and made an initial €250m commitment. The release is dated 17 July 2014; the document also carries an old 2012 template date [S140] [Confirmed].
- In May 2025 Pemberton closed its Mid-Market and Senior Loan direct lending funds at €6.1bn of commitments combined, and added 70 new clients from Asia, the Middle East and North America. Alternatives Watch put AUM at about $26bn [S138] [Reported].
- Strategic Credit Fund III closed at €2.3bn in 2024 [S138] [Reported].
- Model: core mid-market senior lending, plus asset-based finance (NAV, working capital, SRT, CLOs) and capital solutions [S139] [Confirmed].
- Awards: PDI "Lender of the Year – Europe" for 2025, announced 3 March 2026. That makes 12 PDI awards in 10 years by Pemberton's own count [S139] [Confirmed, self-reported].

**Tikehau Capital (Euronext-listed).**
- AUM was €53.5bn at 30 June 2026. Credit was €25.2bn of that, and fee-paying AUM was €43.5bn [S141] [Confirmed]. Tikehau was founded in 2004 (common knowledge, not verified here).
- Flagship DL: the 6th vintage closed at €5.2bn, "~60% larger vs. previous vintage" and "above the €4-5bn target" [S141] [Confirmed].
- The credit fee rate was 75bps LTM at June 2026 [S141] [Confirmed]. This is one of the few public peer fee data points.
- Model: multi-asset. It spans credit (direct and corporate lending, credit secondaries, CLOs, special opportunities), real assets, private equity and capital markets strategies [S141] [Confirmed].
- Distribution: Tikehau lists open-ended funds, SMAs and evergreen mandates alongside closed-end funds. It has a large HNWI, retail and private bank client segment [S141] [Confirmed]. Reading the client-type pie, that segment looks like about 31% of AUM against 56% institutional, but the chart labels do not align cleanly [Inferred].
- Net inflows were €5.7bn LTM, with 60% from Germany, Israel, South Korea and the US (search summary of results) [S141] [Reported].

**Permira Credit (part of Permira).**
- Permira Credit Solutions Fund V closed at €4.2bn in June 2023, above its €4bn target. It had 78 professionals and AUM had more than tripled in five years [S142] [Confirmed].
- Target companies have €10-75m EBITDA, which is core mid-market [search summary of S142] [Reported].
- Liquid credit (Providus Europe and Menlo US CLOs) was expected to reach €7.7bn AUM after its fifth 2026 deal [S143] [Reported]. That is close to Hayfin's €7.1bn CLO platform [Inferred].
- Model: direct lending plus CLOs. No special opportunities or high-yield funds were visible [Inferred].

**HPS Investment Partners (now part of BlackRock).**
- BlackRock completed its all-stock acquisition, valued at about $12bn, on 1 July 2025. It created Private Financing Solutions (PFS), which BlackRock says has about $190bn of private credit client assets [S146] [Reported].
- PEI ranks HPS #3 with $107.5bn raised over 2021-2025, listed separately from BlackRock (#10, $46.8bn) [S144] [Confirmed].
- HPS is a global, multi-strategy, large-cap-capable lender. I found no European-specific AUM figure (see Gaps).

**CVC Credit (context only).** EUDL IV closed at €10.4bn in October 2025 [S147] [Reported].

**Alcentra, Bridgepoint Credit and EQT.** I did not research these in depth. Data quality was weak within the time budget, so they are left out of the table (see Gaps).

### 1.6 Claims that appear distinctive to Hayfin (with evidence)

1. **Biggest step-up between vintages among peers reviewed.** DLF V was over €15bn, against DLF IV at over €6bn, so it more than doubled [S121]. Peer step-ups were Ares +53% [S135], Tikehau about +60% [S141] and Arcmont DLF IV at the top of its range [S129]. [Confirmed facts; Inferred comparison]
2. **One of the few top-three European DL raises from an independent, management-owned firm.** Ares and ICG are listed and Arcmont is Nuveen-owned [S135][S132][S131][S124]. [Inferred]
3. **Record deployment pace.** Hayfin invested €7.1bn in direct lending in 12 months and €38bn since inception [S120][S121]. [Confirmed]
4. **Insurer-tailored structure inside a flagship close.** A rated note feeder contributed about $600m [S122]. Peers likely do similar things, but Hayfin has disclosed it publicly. [Confirmed fact; uniqueness Inferred]
5. **Breadth with sector verticals.** Hayfin combines DL, special opps, HY/syndicated loans, a €7.1bn CLO platform, PE solutions and maritime/healthcare/real estate verticals at about €40bn [S127][S125]. No peer of similar size in the set has maritime lending [Inferred].
6. **Shareholder base doubles as an Asian LP bridge.** Samsung Life is a shareholder [S124] and the Seoul office opened on 1 October 2026 [S126]. [Reported]
7. **Explicit "conservative closed-end" message.** Hayfin pitched DLF V as the answer to the strain in semi-liquid funds [S120]. Note that ICG makes the same pitch [S134], so this is shared positioning, not unique. [Confirmed]

### 1.7 Where Hayfin does NOT lead

- **Not the largest European DL fund.** Ares ACE VI (€17.1bn) and ICG SDP 5 (about €15.2bn) are comparable or larger [S135][S132].
- **Not the largest manager.** Ares has $671bn [S136], ICG $126bn [S133] and Tikehau €53.5bn [S141], against Hayfin's roughly €40bn [S128].
- **Not in the PEI 200 top 10** for 2021-25 [S144].
- **No recent PDI Lender of the Year Europe award found.** Pemberton won for 2025 [S139].
- **No wealth or retail product.** This limits access to the fastest-growing capital pool. Ares raised $1.0bn into its EU evergreen fund in one quarter [S136]. It is a deliberate choice, but it is still a gap.
- **CLO platform is mid-sized.** It is similar to Permira's (about €7.7bn) [S143][S125].
- **No public Deloitte lender ranking** to back an "among the most active" claim [S145].

## 6. Interview angles for a PSG/IR intern candidate

1. **"Why Hayfin over ICG or Ares?"** Hayfin is independent and management-owned [S124], so LP alignment rests with the people running the money. It is credit-only but multi-strategy [S127], so an LP can get European DL, special opps, liquid credit and CLOs from one relationship. That is a cross-sell story, which is core PSG work. ICG's CEO says three quarters of ICG clients use only one strategy [S134], so cross-selling is industry-wide upside.
2. **Talk fluently about DLF V.** It closed at over €15bn against about €6bn for DLF IV [S121]. The raise was institutional-only, with a $600m rated note feeder for insurers [S122]. This shows PSG can build structures for specific LP types, such as insurers under Solvency II capital rules [Inferred].
3. **The semi-liquid debate.** Hayfin [S120] and ICG [S134] both market closed-end discipline. Ares is scaling a European evergreen DL fund [S136][S137]. A good candidate can argue both sides: wealth is a growth pool, but deployment timing and redemption risk are real.
4. **Asia as a fundraising frontier.** Samsung Life is a shareholder [S124] and Seoul opened on 1 October 2026 [S126]. Tikehau's inflows came 60% from markets including Korea [S141]. A PSG intern could support Korean LP DDQs and reporting.
5. **Be honest about scale.** Hayfin is not the biggest. Ares ACE VI is €17.1bn [S135] and ICG ranks #6 in the PEI 200 [S144]. The pitch is specialist depth, workout capability and growth trajectory, not size.
6. **Case-study material for marketing** (a JD task). Possible themes are record deployment (€7.1bn in 12 months) [S120], minimal software and AI-disruption exposure [S121], and an active CLO reset programme [S125].
7. **Questions to ask interviewers.** How does PSG split coverage between commingled funds and SMAs? Will Hayfin ever build a wealth product? How is AI used in DDQ and RFP responses? (The JD explicitly mentions AI tools.)
