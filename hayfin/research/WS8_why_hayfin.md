# WS8: Why Hayfin, built from mechanism

Workstream owner: research agent WS8. Source IDs S300-S399. Accessed 2026-10-08.
Scope: why Hayfin is built the way it is, how its teams work together, what its investors buy, how the non-investment side (including Partner Solutions) is wired, and a drafted "Why Hayfin" answer for a Partner Solutions Group (PSG) summer intern candidate.

Method note. The richest primary evidence came from four places: (1) Hayfin's SEC filings (one registered adviser brochure plus three Form ADV Part 1 filings, all dated 30 March 2026) [S301-S304]; (2) Hayfin's own UK Stewardship Code disclosure dated October 2025 [S305]; (3) two Pennsylvania PSERS board investment memos (2012, 2016) and a PSERS fee review (October 2025) [S306-S308]; (4) Hayfin press releases and job postings [S310-S314, S330-S334]. ## 1. Findings

### 1.2 Why a multi-strategy European credit platform? The mechanism, in Hayfin's own words

**The theory.** A multi-strategy credit manager shares one origination network and one credit underwriting culture across several funds. That lets it lend across the capital structure and across the cycle: performing senior loans in good times (Direct Lending), complex or stressed situations in bad times (Special Opportunities), gap financing in between (Tactical Solutions), plus liquid high yield and loans/CLOs. A workout capability protects LPs when loans go wrong. Local offices feed origination. The costs are complexity, conflicts between funds, and key-person concentration. Below is the evidence for each link.

**Link 1: one origination engine, several "doors".** The 2016 PSERS memo is the clearest third-party description. It says the Special Opportunities and Direct Lending strategies "source investments through two channels: primary lending and secondary markets" [S307]. It then makes the cross-strategy benefit explicit: "the continued evolution of Hayfin's Direct Lending strategy has served to benefit the Firm's Special Opportunities strategy. This has allowed Hayfin to cast a wider net and engage a broader range of companies with the market now not only viewing Hayfin as a 15%+ lender but an 8%+ lender. Once engaged, Hayfin has a greater ability to obtain favorable pricing" [S307]. [Confirmed, primary]
- In plain words: a borrower who meets Hayfin for a cheap senior loan is also a future customer for a more expensive, more complex loan, and vice versa. The same relationship can be monetised by whichever fund fits.
- The original 2012 memo shows the funnel: Hayfin "Generated more than 1,400 financing opportunities that led to 60 investments", and "83% of investments, by value, have been sourced on a proprietary basis" [S306]. That is roughly a 4% conversion rate. [Confirmed for the numbers; conversion rate is Inferred arithmetic]
- Hayfin's current website keeps the same logic. Private Credit offers "a full range of flexible funding solutions across the entire capital structure", works with "private equity sponsors and non-sponsor origination platforms", and sources "through the extensive local origination networks we have built" [S328]. Tactical Solutions sits "between our Direct Lending and Special Opportunities strategies" [S328]. [Confirmed, primary]

**Link 2: deploy at all points of the cycle.** The Special Opportunities page describes "a broad mandate with a flexible approach that leverages both direct origination and secondary purchases of illiquid assets" in order "to find attractive deployment at all points of the cycle" [S328]. The 2016 memo: "Secondary sourcing provides flexibility of deployment as cycles turn" [S307]. In 2020, when competitors were nursing portfolios, Hayfin invested a record €3.7bn in Direct Lending as bank competition eased [S314]. [Confirmed]

**Link 3: Europe forces breadth.** Tim Flynn, on Proskauer's Private Market Talks (April 2024), explains why Hayfin is not a narrow specialist: "We have to run extremely broad mandates compared to what we would do in the U.S." and "If we were in the U.S., we might run a very focused, cash flow only, sponsor-only business" [S324]. He describes Europe as "very, very shallow and inefficient", where "Small changes in absolute terms lead to very big changes in risk-return" [S324]. [Confirmed, primary audio/transcript page]
- Mechanism: a fragmented market (many countries, legal regimes, languages, bank-dominated lending) means no single niche is deep enough to deploy €15bn+ alone. Breadth across strategies and countries is how a European manager reaches scale. [Inferred]

**Link 4: workouts are a product feature, not an afterthought.** The careers blurb: "Most of our team are industry generalists, with our edge lying in our origination networks and proven expertise in managing complex restructurings, workouts and liquidations" [S331]. The About page cites a "dedicated legal execution and workouts team" [S327]. Flynn: "There is a far more focused mindset on workout capability" among LPs, and Hayfin "can add more value around successfully managing bankruptcies, or restructurings" [S324]. The 2016 memo notes Hayfin "utilizes internal legal resources, who are experienced in structuring and documenting debt investments at all levels of an investee company's capital structure and in each of the relevant creditor regimes in Europe" [S307]. A GlobalCapital interview (not readable; snippet only) reportedly had Flynn say about 25 direct lending investments needed major care during Covid and that the firm deployed about €1.2bn in special situations in that period [S325]. [Confirmed for site and podcast; Reported/SNIPPET for GlobalCapital]
- Mechanism: a Special Opportunities team that buys and restructures stressed debt is the same skill set a Direct Lending fund needs when a performing loan goes bad. Sharing it means each DL investor gets a distressed-debt toolkit it would not get from a pure lender. [Inferred]

**Link 5: local presence.** Stewardship disclosure: "We believe in being local in each of our markets, and that our diverse mix of nationalities, cultures and languages makes us more connected to these markets. While most of our team are industry generalists, we employ dedicated specialist teams where we believe focused expertise provides an advantage" [S305]. The 2016 memo: Hayfin "operates a local sourcing model with dedicated geographical coverage and focuses on managing key relationships and maintaining multiple contact points" [S307]. [Confirmed]

**Link 6: specialist teams where generalists lose.** The 2016 memo lists "specialized asset financing in niche healthcare, maritime, non-performing loans and alternative credit" [S307]. AUM by strategy at the stewardship date shows how small these are: Healthcare €0.6bn and Maritime €0.5bn of c.€32.5bn [S305]. Healthcare is pitched as "value protection through intellectual property, diversification and low correlation" [S328]. [Confirmed]

**Link 7: the liquid side is a separate muscle.** Stewardship disclosure: in private credit Hayfin "focuses on generally less liquid, 'off-the-run' investment opportunities where there is less competition to provide capital and does not rely on relationships with investment banks for allocations of broadly syndicated leveraged loan and high yield investment opportunities" [S305]. HY and syndicated loans are €9.2bn, the second-largest line [S305]. The US entity (formerly Kingsland Capital, acquired January 2018) manages US CLOs: regulatory AUM $1.61bn across 7 accounts at 31 December 2025, 33 employees of whom 32 do investment advisory work [S301, S302]. A 2025-era trade snippet reported a US CLO platform of $2.8bn across six vehicles [S340]. [Confirmed for ADV; SNIPPET for $2.8bn]

### 1.4 The costs of the model, and how Hayfin manages them (conflicts, allocation, barriers)

The stewardship disclosure is unusually specific [S305]:

- **Allocation.** Hayfin has "an Allocation Policy to ensure fair and balanced allocations are made to investors", reviewed annually [S305]. The US brochure says orders are allocated "in a fair and equitable manner" based on factors including "Hayfin Capital's allocation policy among Funds", "investment periods applicable to a Fund" and "constraints or needs applicable to a Fund" [S301]. [Confirmed]
- **Cross trades.** "All cross trades require prior approval of the Chief Compliance Officer and Chief Operating Officer", and the requester must explain in an email why each fund is a natural buyer or seller and the proposed price [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Refinancing one Hayfin fund with another.** If a fund or SMA refinances another, Hayfin assesses risk, pricing and potential "outsized gain or loss" and, if there is an apparent conflict, "calls upon a third-party to validate the pricing and terms", for example the fund Advisory Boards or a competing proposal [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Investing in different layers of the same company.** Rule: no material investment in a company where another Hayfin fund holds a material investment in a different class. "One of the strategies must be a non-blocking minority stake", expected to be "less than one-third of the relevant tranche". Hayfin also commits not to "initiate an investment on a loan-to-own basis in the debt of a portfolio company owned by a fund in which the funds strategy is a primary investor" [S305]. Exceptions go to an **Investments Conflicts Committee** made up of the CEO, CRO, COO, CCO and General Counsel [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Information barriers ("trees").** For competitive processes with multiple bidders, Hayfin sets up separate deal "trees". Tree members must not discuss the deal with other staff, calls must be behind closed doors, and documents must not be left on printers [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Public vs private side.** I did not find a document that explicitly describes a wall between the private credit teams and the public-side high yield/loan desk. The US brochure's Code of Ethics bans trading on material non-public information and requires pre-clearance [S301]. The careers material describes the liquid business separately. [Gap; Inferred that a wall exists because the firm both receives private lender information and trades public bonds/loans]
- **Fund governance.** "All our funds have an Advisory Board composed of representatives of selected investors", meeting twice a year to review performance, conflicts and fund expenses; LPA changes need 50%, 66.66% or 75% LP consent depending on materiality [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Incentives.** "Our investment professionals are rewarded to collaborate, rather than outperform peers" [S305]. Flynn: "Ours is a no superstar culture where we value the midfield general" [S324]. This is the cultural fix for cross-fund rivalry. [Confirmed]
- **Key-person risk.** The founding group is still central. Flynn and McCullagh have been members since 2009/2010; Tognolini was on the three-person management committee in 2016 [S303, S307]. The 2016 memo named a six-person investment committee needing a super-majority [S307]. Nashville's 2013 minutes show what LPs fear: in the same meeting, a different manager's departing principal triggered a "Key Man" provision and LPs appointed a liquidator [S309]. [Confirmed for facts; relevance Inferred]

### 1.5 How the teams work together in practice

- **Investment Committee and ESG Deal Committee.** Every private credit deal goes through an ESG Deal Committee before IC. Its members come from "investment, legal, ESG, and partner solutions teams" [S305]. An earlier passage says it includes "investment, ESG, investor relations and execution" members [S305]. So PSG sits inside the deal approval workflow, not just downstream of it. [Confirmed]
- **ESG Steering Committee.** Named members include Steve Bringardner from Partner Solutions alongside Legal and Investment members [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Quarterly reporting is joint.** "Hayfin's Investor Relations and Investment teams produce the following quarterly reports for each fund and Separately Managed Account": a line-by-line track record with "expected gross IRR at investment level and at fund level", a Portfolio Breakdown with LTM EBITDA, enterprise value and leverage for every investment, a CEO Letter, and audited financials within 90 days of year end [S305]. Borrower engagement case studies are "drafted by the ESG function and overseen by the Investor Relations team" [S305]. [Confirmed]
- **Finance feeds PSG.** The Fund Controller role reviews NAVs "before it goes to investors", supports "Investor Reporting with timely query resolution", supports "materials for the quarterly Advisory Board and Risk Committee meetings", "Solvency II reporting" (for insurer LPs) and "internal reporting on AUM and returns" [S333]. It says the role "reaches across all of Hayfin's Private Credit strategies, so you'll see how a multi-strategy manager works" [S333]. [Confirmed]
- **Portfolio monitoring.** The US brochure (CLO business) describes daily investment meetings, a weekly IC that sets risk budgets, and a Portfolio Administration team monitoring limits [S301]. Press calls Carlos Pla "head of portfolio management" [S320]. I could not confirm the internal name "Portfolio Management Group" from a primary document, though the brief lists a PMG intern role. [Confirmed for brochure; Gap for PMG structure]
- **Where collaboration actually lives.** Based on the above: (a) the shared origination network across DL, SO and Tactical; (b) the IC and ESG Deal Committee; (c) the workout/legal team that serves all funds; (d) the reporting chain from deal teams and Finance into PSG. [Inferred from Confirmed pieces]

### 1.7 Counter-case, with numbers

1. **Early net returns lagged targets.** SOCF I was at 8.1% net IRR against an 11-15% net target in 2015 [S306, S307]. Early-life IRRs are depressed by fees on undrawn capital, so this is not conclusive. But it is the only fund-level net figure I found in public. [Confirmed numbers; interpretation Inferred]
2. **Currency can wipe out returns for USD LPs.** 8.0% EUR became -2.6% USD for PSERS [S307]. [Confirmed]
3. **Carry reversal in 2024.** PSERS recorded -$3.9m total carried interest on its Hayfin funds in CY2024 [S308]. A negative accrual usually means fund value fell relative to the hurdle. It is small and one LP's view. [Confirmed number; meaning Inferred]
4. **Concentration in one strategy.** Direct Lending was about half of AUM in 2025 and DLF V is 2.5x DLF IV [S305, S310]. A European default cycle would hit the flagship hardest. [Inferred]
5. **Scale vs discipline.** More than doubling a flagship fund in one vintage raises the question of whether origination and workout capacity scaled too. 228 staff and 100 investment professionals in 2025 [S305] versus €15bn+ in one fund. [Inferred]
6. **Industry losses.** A Morgan Stanley piece cites an estimated annualised loss of 1.47% for the broad Cliffwater Direct Lending Index [S344]. Hayfin does not publish a current loss rate. [SNIPPET for benchmark; Gap for Hayfin]
7. **Ownership change and turnover.** Three ownership changes in eight years (BCI 2017, Arctos/management 2025, Mubadala/AXA IM Prime/Samsung Life 2025). A PDI snippet referred to "Two managing directors" leaving "post-recapitalisation" (undated) [S339]. A 2022 Creditflux headline reported a structured credit unit diluted when a senior pair left (PAYWALLED-NOT-READ) [S338]. [SNIPPET/PAYWALLED]
8. **Conflicts are real.** Hayfin's own rules exist because its funds can sit in different layers of the same company or refinance each other [S305]. [Confirmed]
9. **Competition.** Europe's direct lending market now includes very large US and European platforms. The PSERS fee review shows PSERS also uses Ares, ICG, HPS, Bain, Carlyle and others in private credit [S308]. [Confirmed for list]

## 6. Interview angles (for a PSG/IR intern candidate)

### 6.1 "Why Hayfin" in 30 seconds

"Hayfin is one platform with several doors. The same local origination network and the same underwriters can offer a company a senior loan from Direct Lending, gap capital from Tactical Solutions, or a rescue from Special Opportunities, and the workout team stands behind all of them. That is why LPs, half of them pension funds, just gave DLF V over €15 billion, two and a half times the last fund. For me, Partner Solutions is where that mechanism has to be explained, proven and reported to investors every quarter. I want to learn how you turn deal-level evidence into a story an LP can underwrite."

### 6.2 "Why Hayfin" in 2 minutes

"I looked at Hayfin from the investor's side, because that is the PSG seat.

First, the mechanism. Hayfin started in 2009 as a balance-sheet lender backed by OMERS, PSP and Australia's Future Fund, and it kept that lender's mindset. In 2016 a Pennsylvania pension memo described it as having two sourcing channels, primary lending and secondary purchases, shared by Direct Lending and Special Opportunities. The memo made a point I found striking: because the market sees Hayfin as both an 8% and a 15% lender, it sees more companies and gets better terms. That is the case for a multi-strategy firm. One relationship, several products, and a workout team that every fund can call on.

Second, the cost of that model, and how Hayfin handles it. If two Hayfin funds can sit in the same company, someone has to protect each fund's investors. Your stewardship disclosure sets clear rules: one fund must be a non-blocking minority, cross trades need CCO and COO sign-off, refinancings between funds get third-party validation, and there is an Investments Conflicts Committee. Those rules are exactly what an LP's due diligence questionnaire asks about. So for PSG they are not compliance detail. They are part of the product.

Third, the investor base. About 51% of AUM is from pensions, 20% from insurers and 12% from sovereign funds. Australia is almost a quarter of AUM, and you have just opened Seoul. Each group needs a different answer. Insurers needed a rated note feeder in DLF V. A US pension needs to think about the euro: PSERS's first Hayfin fund showed 8% net in euros and minus 2.6% in dollars in 2015, while Nashville hedged.

Fourth, the platform. Between 2016 and 2025 the team grew from 55 to 228, and the non-investment share went from about a third to over half. BCI said it helped build a partner solutions function. I would join a team that is still being built, with new product, coverage and regional roles added in the last year.

That is why Hayfin, and why PSG."

### 6.3 The close: one specific thing to work on

"One thing I would like to work on: a reusable case study library that links each published case study to its source data. For example, the deal team's memo, the PMG monitoring figures and the Finance-reviewed NAV, with the Marketing Rule disclaimers built in. Your PES update already shows the standard: gross and net TVPI defined, hypothetical net performance labelled under Rule 206(4)-1. If each DDQ answer and case study pulled from one checked source, PSG would answer requests faster and with fewer errors. I would happily build and test the first version with AI tools, as the JD suggests."

(Alternative close if the interviewer is in coverage: "I would like to build a one-page LP briefing note template: commitment history, consultant, currency, and the conflicts and workout questions they asked last time. That is what the US IR analyst role describes, and I would like to see how London does it.")
