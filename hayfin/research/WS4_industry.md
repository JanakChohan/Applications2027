## 1. Findings

### WS4A. Private credit from zero

#### 1.1 What private credit is

Private credit means loans made by non-bank lenders, usually investment funds, and held to maturity rather than syndicated and traded. The lender negotiates terms directly with the borrower. [Inferred, standard definition]

The main strategies, in plain terms:

- **Direct lending.** A fund lends directly to a mid-sized company, usually one owned by a private equity sponsor, often to finance a buyout. Floating-rate, senior secured, typically 5-7 year tenor. This is the largest strategy. PitchBook puts direct lending at USD 742.2bn of institutional drawdown-fund AUM as of Q2 2025, up from USD 90bn in 2014, a 22.2% CAGR versus 13.6% for private equity over the same span [S152] [Confirmed, primary data provider].
- **Senior secured / unitranche.** Senior secured means first claim on the company's assets. Unitranche blends senior and junior debt into one loan with one blended price and one lender group. It is the typical direct lending product. Proskauer's default index tracks "senior-secured and unitranche loans in the United States" [S155] [Confirmed].
- **Mezzanine.** Junior, subordinated debt sitting between senior debt and equity, often with PIK interest or equity kickers. Mezzanine was 12% of global credit capital raised in H1 2026 per Preqin data [S153] [Reported].
- **Special situations / opportunities.** Flexible capital for complex or stressed situations: rescue financing, carve-outs, structured equity-like debt. Hayfin runs a Special Opportunities strategy (per brief). Special situations funds took 13% of global credit capital raised in H1 2026, up from 6% in FY 2025 [S153] [Reported].
- **Distressed debt.** Buying debt of companies in or near default at a discount, often to drive a restructuring or take control. Oaktree Opportunities Fund XII closed at USD 16bn in February 2025, the second-largest private debt close of 2025 [S152] [Confirmed].
- **Asset-based finance (ABF).** Lending secured on pools of assets (consumer loans, equipment, receivables, royalties, real estate) rather than a company's cash flows. Cambridge Associates said it favours ABF funds for 2026 "due to higher entry barriers and stronger lender protections" while warning their complexity "requires more robust due diligence" [S158] [Reported]. The ACC estimates asset-backed lending, infrastructure debt and real estate together made up 40% of private credit investments, corporate lending 60% [S182] [Reported].
- **NAV lending.** Loans to a private equity fund secured on the net asset value of its portfolio. PitchBook lists NAV as a component of private debt AUM charts [S151] [Reported]. Detail not researched further. [Gap]
- **CLOs (collateralised loan obligations).** Securitisation vehicles that buy pools of broadly syndicated leveraged loans and fund them with rated tranches. Many CLOs took losses on First Brands' syndicated loans [S158] [Reported]. The March 2026 US Basel re-proposal lowers capital on senior CLO tranches, which could lower CLO funding costs [S180] [Reported].
- **Public comparators.** High-yield bonds and broadly syndicated leveraged loans (BSL) are the public market equivalents. They are rated, arranged by banks and traded. The IMF said US private credit's "market share is nearing that of syndicated loans and high-yield bonds" [S150] [Confirmed]. Hayfin runs a High Yield / syndicated loans strategy alongside its private strategies (per brief).

#### 1.2 Who borrows and who invests

- **Borrowers.** Mainly sponsor-backed mid-market companies. The IMF said private credit borrowers "are often smaller and more indebted" than leveraged loan or bond issuers, and that "more than one-third of borrowers now have interest costs exceeding their current earnings" (April 2024) [S150] [Confirmed]. The FSB said in May 2026 that borrowers "typically have lower credit quality and higher leverage" and that lending is concentrated in technology, healthcare and services [S178] [Confirmed]. Proskauer splits defaults by EBITDA band (under USD 25m, 25-49.9m, 50m+), which shows the borrower range [S155] [Confirmed].
- **Investors (LPs).** Pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, endowments and foundations, family offices, funds of funds, and increasingly wealthy individuals through wealth platforms. The ACC's 2025 survey found institutions supplied 76% of commitments and retail about 24%, "expected to rise" [S182] [Reported]. Euro area insurers hold about EUR 211bn of private credit exposure (2.3% of total assets) and pension funds about EUR 52bn (1.4%), versus only EUR 62.5bn for euro area banks (0.2% of assets) [S177] [Confirmed, ECB primary].
- **Insurers specifically.** PitchBook notes managers that own or partner with insurers use private debt to back long-dated liabilities, citing nearly USD 1.3tn of reported insurance AUM in the largest listed alternative managers [S152] [Reported]. The IMF in 2024 flagged that private-equity-controlled insurers hold more illiquid assets [S195 summary of 2024 report] [Reported].

#### 1.3 How private credit differs from neighbouring industries

| Compared with | Key difference | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Banks | Banks fund loans with deposits and hold regulatory capital. Private credit funds use committed investor capital locked for years, so there is less maturity mismatch. Banks often originate to distribute; private lenders originate to hold. AIFMD II now bans EU loan-origination funds whose strategy is "originate-to-distribute" [S163]. | [Confirmed for AIFMD II; rest Inferred] |
| Private equity | PE buys equity and aims for capital gain. Private credit lends and earns contractual interest and fees, with downside protection from seniority and covenants. The same sponsor-backed companies are the customers of private credit. | [Inferred] |
| Hedge funds | Hedge funds usually trade liquid securities with frequent redemption. Private credit is mostly illiquid, closed-ended, hold-to-maturity. Overlap exists in distressed and special situations. | [Inferred] |
| Traditional asset managers | Long-only managers buy public bonds. Private credit managers originate, structure, monitor and work out loans, which needs deal teams and restructuring skills. Hayfin's stated edge is origination networks plus restructuring and workout capability (per brief). | [Inferred] |

Bank and non-bank are increasingly linked rather than separate. The FSB counts about USD 220bn of bank credit lines to private credit funds across its members, while commercial estimates run from USD 270bn to 500bn [S178] [Confirmed]. A June 2026 letter from US Senate Democrats cited bank lending to non-bank financial companies rising from USD 56bn in Q1 2010 to USD 1.32tn [S181] [Reported, partisan source].

#### 1.4 Market size: why estimates differ so much

There is no single agreed definition, so numbers range from about USD 1.5tn to over USD 40tn. Knowing why is a good interview point.

- **IMF (April 2024 GFSR, Chapter 2 "The Rise and Risks of Private Credit"):** USD 2.1tn "in assets and committed capital" globally at end-2023, about three-quarters in the US [S150] [Confirmed].
- **FSB (May 2026):** USD 1.5-2.0tn at end-2024, "heavily concentrated in a few jurisdictions" [S178] [Confirmed].
- **PitchBook:** private debt AUM "nearly reached $2 trillion" by mid-2025 in institutional funds, and "surpassed $2.5 trillion when inclusive of perpetual capital vehicles in the wealth and insurance channels" [S152] [Confirmed].
- **Preqin (via Paul Weiss):** global credit AUM at a record USD 1.864tn in H2 2025; dry powder USD 521.9bn [S153] [Reported].
- **ACC / Houlihan Lokey (Dec 2025):** USD 3.5tn global AUM, up 17% in 2023-2024; US 65%; record USD 592.8bn deployed in 2024; fund-level leverage USD 398bn, about 32% of net AUM [S182] [Reported]. The IMF's April 2026 GFSR coverage also uses the USD 3.5tn figure [S195] [Reported].
- **ECB (May 2026):** US market about USD 1.4tn at end-2024 (4.7% of US GDP); euro area private credit funds managed from the region about EUR 100bn in 2025, growing about 14% a year since 2010. About 70% of private credit funding for euro area companies comes from non-euro-area funds [S177] [Confirmed].
- **Bank of England (Dec 2025):** private market funds overall (PE plus PC) hold "roughly $16 trillion" AUM globally [S175] [Confirmed].
- **Apollo:** Marc Rowan argues "Everything on a bank balance sheet is private credit" and "$1.5 trillion out of $42 trillion is levered lending" (March 2024). Apollo's own materials frame it as a ">$40 trillion market" [S179] [Confirmed as Apollo's view; promotional].

**Europe size.** BlackRock, using Preqin data, put European private credit AUM above USD 500bn at Q1 2025, about 23% of the global total, with direct lending 64% of European AUM [S187] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Apollo's broader estimate is about USD 800bn for Europe and the UK [S187 search summary] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Debtwire data shows European direct lending volume hit a half-year record of EUR 63.16bn in H1 2026 (Q1 EUR 34.8bn, Q2 EUR 28.4bn, Q2 down 25% year on year). Ares led with 31 deals (about 7.8% share), Arcmont second (23 deals, 5.8%), Apollo third (20 deals, 5%) [S186] [Reported]. Hayfin does not appear in the top three in that ranking. [Reported]

#### 1.5 Default rates: three measures that tell different stories

This is the most important nuance for an interview. Headline "default rates" diverge because of definitions.

- **Proskauer Private Credit Default Index (US senior secured and unitranche):** Q2 2025 1.76%; Q3 2025 1.84%; Q4 2025 2.46%; Q1 2026 2.73%; Q2 2026 2.51% (716 loans, USD 195.6bn original principal) [S155][S156] [Confirmed]. By EBITDA band in Q2 2026: under USD 25m 1.9%; USD 25-49.9m 3.4%; USD 50m+ 2.4% [S155] [Confirmed]. Proskauer's Stephen Boyko calls this evidence of "resilience" [S155].
- **Fitch US Private Credit Default Rate (PCDR):** much higher because it counts PIK switches, interest deferrals and distressed extensions as defaults. 5.8% trailing twelve months to January 2026, "the highest rate since its inception in August 2024" [S159] [Reported]. Interest deferrals and PIK introductions drove 60% of defaults in that period [S159]. Bloomberg Law reported a record 6.0% for the year to April 2026 [S160] [Reported, SNIPPET]. A search summary of a later Fitch release reported 6.3% for August 2026 [S161] [Reported, SNIPPET, not verified against Fitch directly].
- **Lincoln International (US private companies valued for lenders):** Q2 2026 covenant default rate 2.7% (Q1 3.1%, six-year average 3.9%). PIK present in 11.1% of loans and 11.3% of interest income. "Bad PIK" (no PIK at close, PIK now) equals 6.2% of all loans, "which may also be viewed as shadow default rate" (Q1 5.9%) [S162] [Confirmed].
- **Cambridge Associates (Nov 2025):** cited KBRA middle-market interest coverage of 2.3x at Q3 2025 (2.0x a year earlier) and a direct lending default forecast of 1.5% for 2025 versus a possible 3.8% for BSL [S158] [Reported].

Interpretation [Inferred]: the cash payment default rate in senior direct lending is low (2-3%). Stress shows up instead as PIK toggles, amend-and-extend and covenant resets. That is why valuation scrutiny and LP reporting quality matter so much now.

#### 1.6 Returns

- PitchBook: rolling one-year private debt IRRs "fell to 3.9%" through H1 2025, "the weakest reading since 2020" [S152] [Confirmed]. Its Q2 2026 report says "headline returns have compressed into the mid-single digits" and that "the metric most relevant to LPs is not absolute return, but the excess spread that managers can generate over public fixed-income products" [S151] [Confirmed].
- PitchBook expects "a period of cooler returns ahead" as rate cuts reduce floating-rate income [S152] [Confirmed]. Its Q2 2026 report notes Europe "has already delivered one hike this cycle" and calls higher-for-longer "a plausible base case" [S151] [Reported].

#### 1.7 Structural forces now (2025-2026)

**(a) Bank retreat and Basel III endgame.** Post-2008 capital rules pushed banks out of mid-market leveraged lending, creating the space private credit filled [Inferred]. In Europe, banks still provide roughly 70-76% of corporate credit versus 21-25% in the US, per Latham figures relayed in a search summary, which is the "headroom" argument for European private credit [S188] [Reported, SNIPPET]. The US story is now reversing slightly. On 19 March 2026 the Fed, FDIC and OCC rescinded the 2023 Basel III endgame proposals and re-proposed. Standardised corporate loan risk weights move from 100% to 95%; ERBA banks get 65% for investment-grade corporate loans; agencies estimate up to USD 441bn of extra lending capacity, which Simpson Thacher says "may empower banks to compete more effectively with non-bank lenders" though estimates "are likely to overstate" actual growth [S180] [Reported, law firm analysis of primary proposal]. Comments were due 18 June 2026 [S181] [Reported]. Final status as of October 2026 not confirmed [Gap].

**(b) Wealth / retail channel.** Evergreen private credit fund net AUM rose from about USD 167bn in 2022 to nearly USD 400bn by end-2025, driven by non-traded BDCs [S152] [Confirmed]. Largest non-traded BDCs in H2 2025: Blackstone Private Credit Fund (BCRED) USD 46.7bn offered, Blue Owl Credit Income USD 19.3bn, Apollo Debt Solutions USD 14.3bn, HPS Corporate Lending USD 11.7bn [S152] [Confirmed]. In Europe the wrappers are ELTIF 2.0 and, in the UK, the LTAF (see WS4B regulation section).

**(c) The 2026 retail redemption wave.** This is the biggest live story. In Q1 2026, investors asked to redeem 21.9% of Blue Owl Credit Income Corp (USD 36bn) and 40.7% of Blue Owl Technology Income Corp (USD 6bn); both capped at 5%. Apollo Debt Solutions saw 11.2% requests (paid about USD 730m); Ares Strategic Income Fund about 11.6% (paid about USD 525m); HPS Corporate Lending 9.3% of NAV (paid USD 620m); Blackstone's BCRED saw 7.9% and raised its cap from 5% to 7%, with USD 400m added by Blackstone and employees [S184] [Reported]. Blue Owl CEO Craig Packer called it "a period of heightened negative sentiment toward the asset class". Mohamed El-Erian asked whether it was a "canary-in-the-coalmine moment, similar to August 2007?" [S184]. A later report said BCRED Q2 requests reached about 10% and Blackstone reverted to a 5% cap, about USD 2.2bn [S185] [Reported, SNIPPET only, page blocked]. Trigger: AI-driven fears for software borrowers; BDCs average about 26% software exposure per one analysis [S185 search summary] [Reported, SNIPPET].

**(d) Insurers and ABF.** Large US managers (Apollo, Blackstone, KKR) pair credit origination with insurance balance sheets and push investment-grade private credit and ABF [S152][S179] [Reported]. Blackstone said its investment-grade private credit business grew 35% year on year to USD 107bn (April 2025) [S199] [Reported, SNIPPET].

**(e) Competition with the BSL market.** The syndicated market reopened in 2024-2025, so large borrowers can refinance away from private lenders. PitchBook cites "the return of bank competition" as likely to moderate deal flow [S152] [Confirmed]. [Inferred: this compresses spreads in upper mid-market unitranche.]

**(f) PIK usage.** See 1.5. The FSB lists "more payment-in-kind arrangements" as a sign of emerging stress [S178] [Confirmed].

**(g) Valuation scrutiny.** The IMF (2024) warned loans "are usually valued quarterly with models" which can produce stale marks [S150]. The FCA's March 2025 multi-firm review covered 36 firms (about GBP 3tn global private AUM) [S166]. The FSB (2026) warns of "valuation opacity and reliance on private ratings" [S178] [all Confirmed].

**(h) Fundraising concentration.** See WS4B section 1.10. 93% of 2025 private debt capital went to managers raising fund four or later [S152] [Confirmed].

**(i) US managers entering Europe.** In 2025 Europe took 35.9% of global private debt capital raised (USD 79.4bn vs USD 55.9bn in 2024), helped by US LPs diversifying after the April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariffs; excluding Ares Capital Europe VI (USD 17.7bn) and CVC Credit Partners European Direct Lending IV (USD 12.2bn), Europe's share would be 25.9% [S152] [Confirmed]. Ares tops European direct lending league tables in H1 2026 [S186]. White & Case describe US funds facing crowding at home and looking to Europe as "an attractive first port of call" [S188] [Reported, SNIPPET]. LPs in the 2026 Intralinks survey now prefer UK and Europe (54%) over North America (50%) as an investment region [S189] [Confirmed].

**(j) AI.** Two angles. First, AI as a credit risk: AI disruption to software borrowers drove the 2026 BDC sell-off [S151][S184] [Reported]. Fitch said software risks "will emerge primarily over the medium term" [S159]. The ECB flags private credit's "potential role in AI-related financing" as a monitoring point [S177] [Confirmed]. Second, AI as an IR tool (see WS4B 1.13).

#### 1.8 Systemic risk and the First Brands / Tricolor episode

- **Tricolor Holdings** (subprime auto lender and used car dealer) filed Chapter 7 on 10 September 2025 in the Northern District of Texas, amid alleged double-pledging of collateral [S197][S158] [Reported].
- **First Brands Group** (auto parts) filed Chapter 11 in the Southern District of Texas in late September 2025 (reports say 28 or 29 September) with liabilities over USD 10bn; issues included about USD 2bn of factoring and poorly disclosed off-balance-sheet finance [S196] [Reported]. Its debt was largely broadly syndicated, so CLOs took losses [S158].
- **Jamie Dimon (JPMorgan Q3 call, 14 October 2025):** "when you see one cockroach, there are probably more" [S157][S158] [Confirmed]. JPMorgan wrote off USD 170m on Tricolor [S157]. He also said "we don't know all the underwriting standards that all of these people did" [S157].
- **Pushback:** Cambridge Associates: "Both cases are idiosyncratic, driven by fraud and unique business practices rather than broad market weakness." It noted most high-quality managers spotted red flags and avoided both [S158] [Reported]. Goldman and Citi stressed their books were mostly investment grade and collateralised [S157].
- **Regulator follow-through:** the Bank of England launched its second system-wide exploratory scenario (SWES) on private markets on 4 December 2025, with a final report expected early 2027 [S175] [Confirmed]. A trade article said the scenario models equities down 35%, inflation 7%, UK GDP down 4% [S176] [Reported, SNIPPET]. The ECB's May 2026 special feature said First Brands and Tricolor show how weak underwriting and opacity spread losses, but "it unlikely that private credit in isolation could be a source of systemic financial instability at present" [S177] [Confirmed]. The FSB warned on 6 May 2026 that private credit "remains untested in a prolonged economic downturn" [S178] [Confirmed]. The IMF's April 2026 GFSR cautioned that more defaults could cascade into concerns about corporate credit generally [S195] [Reported, SNIPPET].

#### 1.9 Competing views on the next 3-5 years

**View 1. Structural bull: private credit is a USD 40tn opportunity.** Proponents: Marc Rowan (Apollo), Jon Gray (Blackstone). Rowan argues most private credit is investment grade and that bank balance sheets are the addressable market [S179]. Gray in April 2025 described "tremendous structural tailwinds" from the shift in lending [S199] and in June 2026 called Blackstone's USD 310bn wealth franchise "underestimated" despite "the war and all the noise on private credit" [S198] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Logic: banks keep shrinking risk, insurers need long-dated assets, retail wants yield. [Reported]

**View 2. Cyclical bear: hidden losses and liquidity mismatch.** Proponents: Jamie Dimon ("cockroach") [S157]; Mohamed El-Erian ("canary-in-the-coalmine") [S184]; Fitch default data at record highs [S159][S160]; the IMF and FSB warnings [S150][S178]. Logic: borrowers are weaker, defaults are disguised as PIK, marks are model-based, and evergreen vehicles promise liquidity on illiquid assets. The 2026 BDC gates are the evidence. [Reported]

**View 3. Monitored but not systemic; dispersion rises.** Proponents: ECB (May 2026) [S177], Bank of England (SWES as fact-finding, "not a test of the resilience of the firms") [S175][S176], Cambridge Associates [S158], PitchBook ("future performance is likely to depend more on manager selectivity than on macro tailwinds") [S152], Proskauer ("resilience") [S155]. Logic: direct exposures of banks are small; losses will be manager-specific; good underwriters and workout teams win. [Confirmed]

**View 4 (institutional LP middle ground). Calibrated retreat.** Coller Capital's Summer 2026 barometer (108 LPs, USD 2.045tn AUM): only 29% plan to grow private credit target allocations, down from 42% six months earlier, but 87% will maintain or grow exposure; 53% see "isolated pockets of elevated risk", 18% a systemic problem [S183] [Reported]. Preqin says investors "remain constructive" and H1 2026 closed-end credit fundraising set a first-half record [S154] [Confirmed].

[Inferred] Implication for a European, value-oriented manager with restructuring capability such as Hayfin: Views 3 and 4 favour established managers with workout skills and transparent reporting. The PSG pitch writes itself around selectivity, downside protection and reporting quality.

#### 1.11 The IR role, LP types and the allocation process

**History of IR in private markets** [Inferred, no specific source fetched]: in the 1990s-2000s, fundraising was led by founders and partners, with placement agents doing much of the legwork. As funds multiplied and LPs professionalised (consultants, ODD teams, ILPA standards from 2009 onwards), firms built dedicated IR / "client solutions" teams covering fundraising, reporting and servicing. The growth of separately managed accounts (SMAs), co-investment and wealth channels widened the job into product and client servicing. Hayfin's naming, "Partner Solutions", reflects that broader remit. This framing is my synthesis and should be checked with sources on IR history. [Gap]

**LP types.** The Intralinks 2026 LP survey sample (280+ LPs) included portfolio/asset managers, pension funds, wealth managers, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, family offices and funds of funds [S189] [Confirmed].

**The allocation process (typical)** [Reported, vendor sources S202; structure partly Inferred]:
1. Strategic asset allocation set by board/IC; private credit target weight.
2. Pacing plan: how much to commit per year to stay at target.
3. Sourcing: existing GP relationships (re-ups), consultant buy-lists, placement agent introductions, databases (Preqin, PitchBook, eVestment).
4. RFP (request for proposal) and DDQ (due diligence questionnaire), often on the ILPA DDQ format.
5. Investment due diligence (team, track record, strategy, loss history) and operational due diligence (ops, compliance, IT, admin, valuation).
6. Investment committee approval, legal negotiation of LPA and side letter (fees, MFN, co-invest rights, ESG reporting).
7. Ongoing monitoring: quarterly reports, capital call / distribution notices, annual meeting, ad hoc queries. Then the re-up decision for the next fund.
One vendor source says "for most institutional LPs, a consultant's recommendation is a prerequisite" and that the process typically spans six to eighteen months [S202] [Reported, vendor, low weight].

**Re-ups.** PitchBook's reading of 2026 step-ups is that LPs are re-upping with proven managers [S151] [Confirmed]. Exact private credit re-up rates were not found [Gap].

**What the PSG JD maps onto** [Inferred]: steps 3-4 (pitch materials, RFP and DDQ responses), step 7 (quarterly LP reporting, SMA reporting, ad hoc requests) and case studies used in steps 4-5.

#### 1.14 What LPs value today

From the SS&C Intralinks 2026 LP Survey (280+ LPs, with Reuters Events) [S189] [Confirmed]:
- GP selection criteria: investment strategy 51%, sector-specific knowledge 30%, risk appetite 27%, **co-investment opportunities 22%**, reputation 21%, track record 21%, **fee structure 14%**.
- **ESG: 77% call a GP's ESG credentials "very important" in due diligence**, despite the political climate.
- To improve the relationship: better digital interfaces (25%), better reporting analytics (23%), plus standardised reporting including ILPA templates and more contact with portfolio managers.
- 14% are frustrated by lack of co-investment access.
- 86% of LPs already use GenAI to monitor investments; 92% expect it to be highly impactful.

#### 1.15 Regulation shaping IR (exact instruments and dates)

**EU: AIFMD II, Directive (EU) 2024/927** [S163] [Confirmed, EUR-Lex]
- Adopted 13 March 2024; published OJ L 26 March 2024; enters into force "on the twentieth day following that of its publication" (so 15 April 2024).
- Article 3: Member States "shall adopt and publish, by 16 April 2026" transposing laws and apply them from 16 April 2026, with exceptions (some UCITS measures apply from 16 April 2027).
- Loan-origination AIFs: leverage capped at 175% (open-ended) and 300% (closed-ended) on the commitment method; 5% risk retention of each transferred loan; 20% of capital limit on loans to a single financial undertaking, AIF or UCITS; ban on originate-to-distribute strategies; no lending to the AIFM, its staff, depositary or delegates; loan-originating AIFs should in principle be closed-ended unless liquidity conditions are met (recital 21).
- Liquidity management: open-ended AIFs must choose at least two tools from a harmonised list (new Annex V).
- Reporting: Annex IV supervisory reporting to be revamped via ESMA draft RTS/ITS due 16 April 2027; delegation reporting expanded.
- Commission review from 16 April 2029 (Article 110a). No loan-origination grandfathering provision found in the text I read; commonly cited grandfathering for existing funds should be checked in Article 1 detail [Gap].
- Why IR cares [Inferred]: LP DDQs on EU funds now ask about AIFMD II compliance, leverage limits and liquidity tools; Annex IV changes alter what data the firm must produce.

**EU: ELTIF 2.0, Regulation (EU) 2023/606** [S164] [Confirmed, EUR-Lex]
- Adopted 15 March 2023; OJ L 80, 20 March 2023; Article 2: "It shall apply from 10 January 2024." Existing ELTIFs deemed compliant until 11 January 2029.
- Eligible-asset minimum cut from 70% to 55%; retail ELTIF borrowing up to 50% of NAV (100% for professional-only); EUR 10,000 retail minimum and 10% cap removed; fund-of-funds and master-feeder allowed; secondary matching mechanisms.
- RTS: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2759, published OJ 25 October 2024, covering redemption policy, liquidity tools, matching and cost disclosure [S165] [Reported, SNIPPET].

**EU: SFDR and "SFDR 2.0"** [S174] [Reported, law firm SNIPPET]
- SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088) is the current regime with Article 8 and 9 funds [Inferred, general knowledge].
- On 20 November 2025 the Commission proposed SFDR 2.0: three voluntary categories, Transition (Art 7), ESG Basics (Art 8), Sustainable (Art 9); a 70% alignment threshold; exclusions aligned with ESMA fund-name guidelines; only categorised products may use sustainability terms in marketing; entity-level disclosures deleted. Legislative status as of October 2026 not verified [Gap].

**UK: AIFM regime review** [S167][S168] [Confirmed for FCA page; thresholds Reported]
- HM Treasury consultation and FCA Call for Input in April 2025 (closed 9 June 2025), originally proposing a GBP 100m lower threshold.
- 14 July 2026: HMT draft SI plus FCA **CP26/28 "The UK AIFM regime"**, proposing a three-tier structure (small, medium, large) and a new Alternative Investment Funds sourcebook (ALTS) to "bring most AIFM rules together in one place". Tiers reported as small under GBP 750m, medium GBP 750m-5bn, large above GBP 5bn, measured by NAV [S168].
- Closing dates: most discussion chapters 18 September 2026; consultation chapters and prudential chapter 22 October 2026 (extended) [S167].
- Parallel FCA CP26/26 (FRAME: fund reporting) and CP26/27 (remuneration) [S167].
- Final rules expected 2027, implementation possibly 2028 [S168] [Reported].

**UK: National Private Placement Regime (NPPR).** The route by which non-UK AIFs (e.g. Luxembourg funds) are marketed to UK professional investors. Not specifically researched in 2026 sources [Gap]. [Inferred]

**UK: Financial promotions and Consumer Duty.** The Consumer Duty applies to retail-facing activity. A May 2026 FCA review of firms approving promotions found some let retail investors see promotions intended for professional clients [S203] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Relevance [Inferred]: as managers push into wealth channels (LTAF, ELTIF, feeder funds), IR and marketing materials need compliance sign-off and retail-appropriate disclosures.

**UK: LTAF (Long-Term Asset Fund).** FCA authorised open-ended fund for illiquid assets. First LTAF authorised March 2023 (Schroders). PS23/7 (June 2023) broadened retail distribution [S200] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Current count not found [Gap].

**UK: FCA private market valuations multi-firm review** (published 5 March 2025) [S166] [Confirmed]
- 36 firms, about GBP 3tn global private AUM (GBP 1tn UK).
- Weaknesses: valuation conflicts (including "investor marketing", fees, redemptions) only partly identified; some valuation committees dominated by investment staff; poor minutes; many firms lacked a process for ad hoc revaluations.
- Good practice: third-party valuation advisers, "value bridge" reporting, separating realised from unrealised performance in marketing, backtesting.
- Next steps: targeted follow-up with outliers, further work on continuation funds and co-investments.
- IR link [Inferred]: marketing materials and case studies that cite unrealised performance must show how values were set.

**UK Stewardship Code 2026** [S194] [Reported, multiple law firm SNIPPETs]
- Published by the FRC on 3 June 2025; effective 1 January 2026; autumn 2026 application window closes 31 October 2026. Adds guidance for non-equity asset classes, relevant to credit managers.

**PRI.** ILPA's DDQ 2.0 ESG section is "entirely based on the PRI's LP private equity responsible investment due diligence questionnaire" [S171 search summary] [Reported, SNIPPET]. PRI released a supplementary climate module with ILPA in 2025 [S171 summary] [Reported].

**ILPA standards** [S169][S170][S171]
- **ILPA Reporting Template v2.0** released January 2025 (QRSI initiative), replacing the 2016 template for funds still in their investment period during Q1 2026 and funds starting on or after 1 January 2026 [S169] [Confirmed]. Changes: single standard format, locked line items, new gross-to-net fee reconciliation (rebates, waivers, offsets), placement fees and internal chargebacks [S170] [Reported].
- **ILPA Performance Template** released January 2025 for new funds; data capture from Q1 2026; first delivery after quarter ending 31 March 2027 per law-firm commentary; "Granular" and "Gross Up" methods; v1.1 April 2025 aligned to SEC Marketing Rule FAQs [S170] [Reported; timing disputed between sources].
- **ILPA DDQ 2.0** finalised November 2021 (draft August 2021); covers succession, co-investments, GP-led secondaries, credit facilities, cyber; DEI and ESG sections [S171] [Reported, SNIPPET of ILPA PDF].
- **ILPA Principles 3.0** published 2019 [Inferred, not verified this session] [Gap].
- Intralinks expects the ILPA Performance Template "to further enhance reporting standards and consistency across the industry" [S189] [Confirmed].

**US** [S172][S173][S201]
- SEC Private Fund Adviser Rules (adopted August 2023) were vacated in full by the Fifth Circuit on 5 June 2024 in *National Association of Private Fund Managers v. SEC*; the court held the SEC "exceeded its statutory authority" [S201] [Reported, multiple law firms]. Vacated rules included quarterly statements, audits, adviser-led secondaries and preferential treatment [S172] [Reported].
- Practical effect [Inferred]: what the SEC could not mandate (quarterly fee and performance statements), ILPA's templates now push via LP pressure.
- SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1): compliance date 4 November 2022 [Inferred, not verified]. Staff FAQs of 19 March 2025 allow gross-only extracted performance (e.g. a single deal) if total portfolio gross and net are shown [S173] [Reported]. Relevant to case studies in a US-facing pitch book.

## 6. Interview angles for a PSG / IR intern candidate

1. **Know the three default numbers and why they differ.** Proskauer 2.51% (payment defaults, senior loans), Fitch about 6% (counts PIK and extensions), Lincoln 6.2% "shadow default". Saying "it depends on the definition, and LPs now ask for the PIK and amendment data, not just the headline" shows real understanding. [S155][S159][S162]
2. **Market size is a definitions debate.** IMF USD 2.1tn, FSB USD 1.5-2.0tn, ACC USD 3.5tn, Apollo USD 40tn. Hayfin's game is the classic sponsor-backed, mid-market and special situations slice, not investment-grade ABF. [S150][S178][S182][S179]
3. **Fundraising is "more money, fewer funds, longer time".** H1 2026: USD 132.5bn raised, 80 funds, top 10 took 64%, 39 months to close (Preqin). That raises the bar for every pitch book, DDQ and quarterly report a mid-to-large European manager produces, which is the PSG job. [S153][S151]
4. **Europe is in favour.** 35.9% of 2025 private debt capital went to Europe; LPs prefer UK and Europe over North America (54% vs 50%); European direct lending had a record H1 2026. Ask how Hayfin positions against US entrants like Ares and Apollo, which lead European league tables. [S152][S189][S186]
5. **The 2026 retail redemption wave is a closed-end fund argument.** BDCs gated at 5% after 10-40% requests. An IR answer: closed-end drawdown funds and SMAs (which Hayfin offers) match liquidity to assets. Good question to ask: how Hayfin thinks about the wealth channel and ELTIF/LTAF. [S184][S164]
6. **Regulation is IR content.** AIFMD II (transposed by 16 April 2026) sets loan-origination fund rules: 175%/300% leverage caps, 5% retention, no originate-to-distribute. DDQs will ask about it. UK CP26/28 consults until 22 October 2026, a day after the Hayfin application deadline. [S163][S167]
7. **Reporting standards are moving.** ILPA Reporting Template v2.0 applies from Q1 2026 for funds in investment period; Performance Template for new funds. Intern tasks ("updating performance data and proofreading reports") sit right on this. [S169][S170]
8. **Valuation and transparency are the trust currency.** FCA's March 2025 review praises "value bridge" reporting and separating realised from unrealised performance in marketing. Mention this when discussing case studies. [S166]
9. **What LPs value, with numbers.** Strategy 51%, sector expertise 30%, co-investment 22%, fees 14%; 77% say ESG credentials are very important; 25% want better digital interfaces and 23% better analytics. [S189]
10. **AI in IR, concretely.** A single DDQ can take 40-60 hours (vendor claim); ILPA DDQ grew from 13 to 20 modules. An intern can show value by building a tagged answer library, version control on DDQ responses, and AI first drafts with human review and source citation, while being careful about confidentiality and compliance sign-off. [S190][S191]
11. **Have a view on the 3-5 year debate.** A balanced line: "I side with the ECB and Cambridge Associates that it is not systemic, but Dimon's point stands on underwriting dispersion. That favours managers with workout capability and transparent reporting, which is Hayfin's stated edge." [S157][S158][S177]
12. **Placement agents are consolidating.** Lazard bought Campbell Lutyens (USD 575m, April 2026). Ask how PSG splits work between in-house coverage and agents. [S192]
