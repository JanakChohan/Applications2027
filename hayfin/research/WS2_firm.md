## 1. Findings

### A. Founding insight

**Who and when.** Hayfin Capital Management LLP was registered in England on 11 June 2009 (LLP number OC346320). [Confirmed] [S87][S86] Hayfin's history page says Tim Flynn and Mark Tognolini founded the firm in 2009. [Confirmed] [S79][S80] It began life as Haymarket Financial LLP. The name changed to Hayfin in 2013 by joining the first three letters of each word. [Confirmed] [S79][S82] The SEC adviser database still lists "HAYMARKET FINANCIAL LLP" as a former name. [Confirmed] [S82]

**What the founders did before.** In April 2024 Flynn told Proskauer's podcast about his career. He started out as a beekeeper on a CARE Canada project in Nicaragua. He then went to law school and worked as a lawyer at Sullivan & Cromwell. After that he spent just over a decade at Goldman Sachs, moving from M&A into leveraged finance, and became a partner. [Reported] [S105] Trade press calls both founders "former Goldman Sachs leveraged buyout specialists". [Reported] [S80][S100] Tognolini's exact Goldman title is not confirmed in the sources read. Third-party profiles also mention an earlier legal career for him. Treat that as unverified.

**The founding thesis.** Hayfin's history page says the founders saw a chance for new private lenders to fill a funding gap. The gap opened after the financial crisis, when traditional banks pulled back from mid-market European companies. [Confirmed] [S79] Flynn's own account is more specific. His thesis was that Basel II risk weighting would force banks to reprice middle-market and non-investment-grade lending. He said the combination "could create a window to build" a European market leader. [Reported] [S105] He also noted the timing was hard: "the crisis happened while I was on garden leave, which made capital formation certainly harder." [Reported] [S105]

**How the thesis shaped the design.**
- The firm started at the "pointy end of credit" with stressed and distressed investing. Its first direct lending loan came around 2011 or 2012. A high-yield and syndicated loan CLO platform followed, and later a private equity solutions business. [Reported] [S105]
- Flynn argues that Europe's market is shallow and inefficient. Hayfin therefore runs broader mandates than a typical US manager would. [Reported] [S105]
- The firm's own words: "Hayfin was established to build bespoke investment strategies tailored to the European market". [Confirmed] [S106]
- The stated founding goal was "delivering flexible, collaborative and disciplined solutions for their clients". [Confirmed] [S79]

**Mission in its own words.** The homepage describes Hayfin as "a leading European alternative asset management platform". It provides debt, equity and hybrid capital to corporates (sponsor and non-sponsor) and real asset owners. It has "transatlantic capabilities" and a growing presence in Asia. The homepage tagline is "Excellence. Collaboration. Responsibility." [Confirmed] [S106][S79] The JD's values list (Responsibility, Collaboration and transparency, Excellence, Entrepreneurial, Diversity) is consistent with this. [Inferred]

**Early timeline (Hayfin history page; the page pairs some years with events by position, so treat these pairings as approximate).** [Reported] [S79]
- 2009: founded, backed by TowerBrook, PSP, OMERS and Future Fund.
- 2012: Special Opportunities Fund I (c.EUR1bn).
- 2013: rename to Hayfin. Direct Lending Fund I (c.EUR2bn).
- 2015: liquid credit platform launched.
- 2017: BCI buys the majority stake held by TowerBrook and its co-investors. Management and staff stay substantial shareholders.
- 2017 (13 Dec): Hayfin agrees to buy US CLO manager Kingsland Capital Management (c.USD1bn of CLOs). Hayfin AUM was expected to reach c.EUR10bn after the deal. [Confirmed] [S112]
- 2018: Private Equity Solutions launched; PRI signatory.
- 2019: Breakwater Capital assets acquired. The LLP accounts confirm a 1 July 2019 purchase that produced GBP601,225 of goodwill. [Confirmed] [S87]
- 2020: Tactical Solutions launched.
- 2021: specialist Healthcare and Maritime funds.
- 2023: Direct Lending Fund IV.
- 2024: Arctos-backed MBO agreed.

- Recurring management-type fees grew. They rose about 13%, from GBP65.9m to GBP74.4m.
- Performance fees fell from GBP8.6m to GBP3.2m. This fits the accounting policy. Performance fees are recognised only when a significant reversal is "highly probable" not to occur, on a sliding scale tied to capital returned. [Confirmed for the policy] [S87]
- The LLP mostly advises related group entities. Its credit-risk note says its "core business is to advise related party entities on investment decisions". [Confirmed] [S87] Group revenue is therefore larger and sits partly elsewhere. Examples are the Cayman and Luxembourg managers and Hayfin Management Holdings Limited, the parent of the largest group that prepares consolidated accounts. [Confirmed] [S87]

**Fee norms in European direct lending (industry norms, not Hayfin's terms).**
- Macfarlanes, Hayfin's own fund counsel, wrote in November 2024 that the typical unlevered direct lending fund charges "a management fee on net invested capital, a performance fee subject to a preferred return with catch-up". The headline management fee in its base case is 1%. The effective average is slightly below 1% after discounts for early, large and loyal commitments. Over a fund's life, manager income splits roughly 50/50 between management and performance fees. [Reported] [S104]
- The same note says "A 2% management fee would be seen as commercially unviable within direct lending." Some managers drop performance fees for evergreen funds. [Reported] [S104]
- Hayfin's actual LPA fee terms, hurdle and carry percentages are not public. See Gaps.

### C. Contracts: what each party gives and gets

**LPs (investors).** From Hayfin's Stewardship report. [Confirmed] [S107]
- **Term.** The investment period for private credit funds is three to four years, followed by a three to four year harvest. Two one-year extensions are allowed, so that unrealised positions can exit in an orderly way.
- **Governance.** Every fund has an Advisory Board of selected investors. It meets twice a year to review performance, conflicts and fund expenses.
- **LPA amendments.** These need Ordinary Consent (more than 50% of commitments), Two-Thirds Consent (more than 66.66%) or Special Consent (more than 75%), depending on how material the change is.
- **Quarterly reporting, produced by Investor Relations and the investment teams.** This is directly the PSG intern's work. Each fund and SMA gets:
  - a Summary of Investments and Portfolio Update, with a line-by-line track record, amounts invested and called, carrying value, realised proceeds, expected gross IRR at deal and fund level, and the deployment and repayment profile;
  - a Portfolio Breakdown, including each company's LTM EBITDA, enterprise value and leverage;
  - a CEO Letter, posted through the administrator portal;
  - audited financials as at 31 Dec, released within 90 days.
- **Who the LPs are.** "Due to the nature of the private credit asset class, all of our investors are institutional."
  - By type at 31 Mar 2025: pension 51.1%, insurance 19.5%, sovereign wealth 12.1%, bank 6.9%, fund of funds 4.3%, family office/HNW/endowments 4.0%, consultant 2.1%. [Inferred mapping from chart, high confidence] [S107]
  - By geography: North America 26.2%, Europe 24.7%, Australia 22.8%, UK 19.5%, Asia 4.2%, Nordics 1.3%, Middle East 1.2%. [Inferred mapping from chart, high confidence] [S107]
  - Australia's large share explains the 2025 hire of a Melbourne-based MD to lead the Australian client franchise. [S77] [Inferred]
- **DLF V access routes.** These were unlevered commingled funds, modestly levered funds, SMAs, evergreen structures and a rated note feeder. The feeder was one of the first priced by a European direct lending manager and added about USD600m. It is aimed at insurers that need capital-efficient, rated exposure. [Confirmed] [S71][S94]
  - Assets in the levered commingled fund more than doubled versus DLF IV. [Confirmed] [S94]
  - More than half of DLF V capital came from EMEA, including 10% from the Middle East. European investors alone committed more than EUR6bn. [Confirmed] [S94]
- **Ownership-linked LPs.** BCI stayed a strategic LP in certain Hayfin funds after selling its stake. [Confirmed] [S72][S91] Samsung Life "will support Hayfin's investment strategies" as well as holding equity. [Confirmed] [S75]

**Borrowers and sponsors.**
- The core product is performing senior secured loans with a focus on downside protection, cash flow and asset coverage. [Confirmed] [S71][S88]
- 40% of the Direct Lending book comes from sole-lender deals. The rest are usually clubs of two to three lenders. [Reported] [S95]
- Hayfin says European credits carry a premium over comparable US credits, with lower leverage, tighter documentation and smaller lender groups. [Reported] [S95]
- About half of direct lending capital deployed over the past two years went to existing borrowers. Tognolini calls this incumbency "a competitive advantage". [Reported] [S117]
- Software exposure across Direct Lending is under 6%, and under 5% in the latest vintage. [Reported, firm claim] [S111]
- Recent sponsor deals:
  - Condis, EUR305m for Portobello (Sep 2026)
  - Serwent for Norvestor (Sep 2026)
  - Hyve for Hellman and Friedman, fully underwritten (Jun 2026)
  - CloserStill for Searchlight (May 2026)
  - Geomatikk for Axcel (Mar 2026)
  - Juvise, a EUR550m refinancing (Feb 2026)
  - These are listed in the press feed. [Confirmed] [S70]

**Employees.**
- Pay has fixed and variable parts. Variable pay covers an annual cash bonus plus long-term incentives: carried interest awards, and equity through a restricted share scheme and a management incentive plan (MIP). There is also an "annual liquidity window" in which employees can buy and sell interests in the Hayfin group. [Confirmed] [S108]
- The LLP accounts describe several equity schemes. [Confirmed] [S87]
  - MIP 2.0 vested on 31 Dec 2022.
  - MIP 3.0 was finalised Dec 2023. The fair value of a C share point was EUR2,055.33. Its vesting was accelerated because of the change of control, which the accounts date to 7 Feb 2025.
  - A new B Share Award scheme was approved Dec 2024. A B share point issued by Atlas Preferred SPV LP is valued at EUR4.75. 80% vests over four years from 1 Jan 2025 and 20% over 4.75 years on performance conditions. The total fair value for the LLP is EUR12.9m.
- Remuneration disclosed for FY2023 under MIFIDPRU 8, for the UK firm on a broad definition of staff. [Confirmed] [S108]
  - 16 material risk takers received GBP12.3m in total (GBP5.2m fixed, GBP7.1m variable).
  - Other staff received GBP43.3m (GBP24.9m fixed, GBP18.4m variable).
- Tognolini links broad employee ownership to independence and long-term investment in leaders. He rejects a multi-boutique or "pod" model: "We aren't one of them." [Reported] [S117]

### D. Risk architecture

- **Investment Committee (IC).** Final approver for investments. Private credit deals pass through an ESG Deal Committee before final IC. That committee's minutes go into the final IC memo, and it has a first-IC ESG page. [Confirmed] [S107]
- **Executive Committee.** Sits at Hayfin Capital Holdings Limited, the ultimate parent. It takes day-to-day decisions and has ultimate responsibility for risk management. [Confirmed] [S108]
- **Audit and Risk Committee.** Set up in 2019 for non-investment risk.
  - It is chaired by an independent non-executive board member.
  - Its members are the COO, General Counsel, CFO, COO Private Debt, COO Liquid Debt, one non-executive director and a shareholder representative.
  - It reports quarterly to the Board. [Confirmed] [S107]
  - Within the LLP, a Business Operations Committee handles day-to-day risk management. [Confirmed] [S87]
- **Investments Conflicts Committee.** Members are the CEO, CRO, COO, CCO and General Counsel. It approves cases where Hayfin funds sit in different parts of the same capital structure. [Confirmed] [S107]
- **Chief Risk Officer.** Andrew McCullagh, a member of the LLP since March 2010. [Confirmed] [S83]
- **Portfolio monitoring and workouts.** In Apr 2024 Michaela Campbell joined as MD to oversee private credit portfolio monitoring and risk. [Reported] [S70] Carlos Pla is Head of Portfolio Management. [Confirmed] [S76][S93] Flynn says LPs now have "a far more focused mindset on workout capability". At a Hayfin Investor Day, workout capability was the top-voted factor in a poll. He describes the firm as "hyper-prepared" for a default spike. [Reported] [S105] The JD's "Portfolio Management (PMG)" intern track matches this function. [Inferred]
- **Portfolio health claims (firm's own).** The Direct Lending watchlist is down by almost a third since year-end 2023. Weighted interest coverage is stable and most interest is paid in cash. Undrawn capital is about EUR7bn. [Reported, firm claim] [S95][S111]
- **Funding stability.** Private credit strategies "rely exclusively on fully locked-up institutional drawdown funds, with no retail capital." [Reported, firm claim] [S111]
- **Fund-level leverage.** Hayfin offers modestly levered fund versions and SMAs. Macfarlanes advised on the DLF V leverage facility. [Confirmed, terms not public] [S94]
- **Capital Markets.** In Sep 2025 Hayfin hired Daniel Stevenson from Deutsche Bank's leveraged debt capital markets team as MD and Head of Capital Markets. The aim was to widen origination and execution across deal sizes and types. [Confirmed] [S113]
- **Regulatory capital (UK LLP).** As a non-SNI MIFIDPRU firm, its own-funds requirement was GBP9.26m, driven by fixed overheads. Own funds were GBP14.25m at 31 Dec 2023. [Confirmed] [S108]
- **Regulatory record.** All three SEC-filing Hayfin advisers show disciplinary disclosure flag "N". [Confirmed] [S82] The FCA register page would not render. FRN 503730 was authorised on 23 Jun 2010. [Confirmed] [S87]

### I. Skeletons and controversies

- **No regulatory enforcement found.** The SEC ADVs show no disciplinary disclosures. [Confirmed] [S82] No FCA final notice turned up in the searches. The FCA register itself could not be rendered, so this is [Reported absence].
- **Kingsland litigation.** Kingsland's founder, Joyce DeLucca, sued Hayfin entities in New York (DeLucca v. Hayfin Capital Holdings Ltd, index 652210/2020).
  - Her claims were breach of contract, mistake and fraud over the December 2017 purchase agreement. The agreement required a USD120m BCI commitment to help meet US risk-retention rules. A court struck those rules down for open-market CLO managers about a month after closing.
  - Justice Borrok dismissed the case with prejudice on 7 May 2021. [Confirmed] [S110]
  - Context: the US CLO business that came from Kingsland is now under strategic review. [S96] [Inferred link]
- **A failed sale process in 2023.** Hayfin explored a sale of the business. Talks with DWS and Janus Henderson were reported, but no deal was struck. The MBO followed instead. [Reported] [S100][S80]
- **Portfolio stress.** Hayfin was named among the lenders (with Pemberton and Blue Owl) that took ownership of Cinven-backed Netceed through a debt-for-equity swap in Oct 2025. Netceed had more than USD1bn of term loans and received about EUR70m of new liquidity. The size of the write-down was not disclosed. [Reported, citing Bloomberg] [S109]
- **Financial softening in the UK LLP.** FY2025 LLP revenue fell 10% and profit before members' remuneration fell 25%. Performance fees fell from GBP8.6m to GBP3.2m. Trade receivables halved, and GBP17.4m of borrowings at end-2024 were repaid. These are entity-level figures and do not show group performance. [Confirmed figures] [S87]
- **Retrenchment signal.** The US CLO strategic review reverses the 2017 US CLO expansion, even as Hayfin expands US fundraising coverage. [Inferred from S96, S97, S99]
- **Leadership transition.** Gina Germano, a long-time HYSL head, is stepping back from day-to-day portfolio management. Hayfin framed it as a planned transition. [Confirmed] [S98] No other senior departures were found in the searches. See Gaps.
