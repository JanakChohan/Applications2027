**Evidence base is thin.** Glassdoor's Hayfin interview pages hold roughly 13 to 14 reviews across all roles [S261] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Then a case study and group exercise with 4 students, lasting about one hour in total. The case was to assess a dental business as a private credit opportunity [S260] [Reported, SNIPPET]. This was very likely an investing-team intern track (credit case), not Partner Solutions [Inferred].

**Summer internship, 2025 (Glassdoor review).** Two stages, both online via video call. A general chat, then a "virtual assessment centre" with a few people on each call asking questions. Whole process about 3 weeks [S260] [Reported, SNIPPET].

**Investor Relations role, London, August 2025 (Glassdoor review).** Two interviews, then one case at the office. The case was "simple investor relations tasks": Excel for fund performance, plus a presentation of a fund. First round covered commercial awareness, including recent deals and relevant news. Also usual motivation questions: why firm, why PE, tell me about yourself, walk me through an LBO. Advice given: be "very self aware about how LPs & GPs work as well as fund performance metrics" [S261] [Reported, SNIPPET]. The review does not say whether this was an intern or full-time IR hire. Flag: level uncertain. It is still the single most relevant data point for a PSG intern, because it is the only one from the IR side.

**Private credit associate (WSO, June 2023).** Four rounds. (1) 30 minutes with an MD: conversational, motivation, fit, easy technicals. (2) Two back-to-back interviews with Principals and VPs, credit-focused on past experience. (3) Take-home case study plus presentation. (4) Meet the rest of the team [S263] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Associate level, investing team. Useful only as a sign of house style: conversational first round, case plus presentation later.

**Glassdoor aggregates (all Hayfin roles).** Stage mix reported as: one-on-one interview 25%, skills test 25%, group panel interview 15%, presentation 10%, phone interview 10% [S261] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Experience 84.6% positive, difficulty 3.38 out of 5 [S261] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Summer Intern roles had the slowest hiring process at about 21 days on average. Investor Relations was reported as quickest, about 7 days, and IR plus Summer Legal Intern were rated hardest [S261] [Reported, SNIPPET]. Different Glassdoor snapshots show 14 or 26 days as the overall average [S261], so these numbers are unstable.

**HireVue or online tests?** No source mentions HireVue, numerical reasoning tests or psychometrics for Hayfin [Inferred from absence across S260, S261, S263]. ### 1.6 Current themes PSG is talking about (for market-awareness answers)

- **Direct Lending Fund V record close, over €15bn** (2 July 2026), against Fund IV's over €6bn (Aug 2023). Includes a rated note feeder of about $600m. Investors: public and private pensions, financial institutions, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, funds of funds, endowments, consultants, family offices. Over 50% deployed across 35+ companies at close; strategy has deployed over €38bn across 350+ investments since inception [S273] [Confirmed, FETCHED].
- **PSG's own read of that raise** (Lipsky and Bringardner, 22 Sept 2026): over half of capital from EMEA including 10% from the Middle East; Europeans still committed over €6bn; growth now from US and APAC investors seeking European diversification; European direct lending becoming "a core, strategic holding rather than a satellite one"; multiple wrappers (unlevered and levered commingled, SMAs, evergreen, rated note feeder); levered commingled assets more than doubled from DLF IV; LPs scrutinising PIK usage, technology exposure and loss rates; consultants more influential; "Scale should be an outcome of repeatability, rather than an objective in itself" [S298] [Confirmed, FETCHED].
- **PIK debate.** Hayfin's "A Tale of Three PIKs" (Brennan Schol, Director, 28 July 2026) splits PIK into good, bad and "fake good"; key tests are cap, time limit, premium pricing and a self-liquidating use; "Done well, PIK is not something to be feared" [S264].
- **Complexity premium.** Atkinson and Laws (15 Sept 2026): extra return where structure, asset type, process or jurisdiction keeps conventional lenders out; returns "driven by complexity and capital scarcity rather than leverage alone"; covenant protection "increasingly rare" across leveraged lending [S262].
- **Distribution footprint.** New Seoul office (Oct 2026) [S276]; Dubai DIFC office (Jan 2024) [S275]; Japan, Australia, Nordics, DACH, North America coverage [S289-S293]. Ownership: management buyout completed February 2025; Samsung Life, Mubadala and AXA IM Prime are strategic minority shareholders [S276, S272].

### 1.7 IR interview technical grounding (DDQ)

- The ILPA DDQ 2.0 (Nov 2021) is the industry-standard template. It has 20 topics: firm general information, fund general information, succession/key persons, investment strategy, co-investments, GP-led secondaries/continuation funds, credit facilities, investment process, team, alignment of interests, market environment, fund terms, governance/risk/compliance, track record, accounting/valuation, reporting, legal, data security/technology/third parties, ESG, and diversity, equity and inclusion [S299] [Confirmed, FETCHED].
- Appendices include requested documents and templates for team, references, fund, portfolio investments, GP-led secondaries and credit facilities [S299].
- ILPA's own rationale: customised DDQs created "an extraordinary administrative burden" on LPs, GPs and placement agents [S299]. This is exactly the work the PSG JD describes (DDQ and RFP responses).

### 1.8 Raw question bank (46 questions)

Format: Question. Tested: what they want. Traps: common mistakes. All [Inferred] unless sourced.

**A. Motivation (6)**
1. Why Investor Relations rather than an investing seat? Tested: genuine interest in the client side, not a back door into deals. Traps: saying IR is a stepping stone; not knowing what IR does day to day (DDQs, RFPs, reporting, pitch books, per JD).
2. Why private credit? Tested: understanding of the asset class's growth and role. Traps: generic "fast-growing" answer without the bank-retrenchment story Flynn tells [S281].
3. Why Hayfin over Ares, Arcmont, Pemberton, Blackstone Credit, ICG? Tested: firm-specific research. Traps: listing size only; not mentioning breadth of mandate, workout capability, specialist verticals, DLF V and the multi-wrapper approach [S273, S298].
4. Walk me through your CV. Tested: concise story, link of prior internship to IR skills. Traps: over 2 minutes; no link to client work or attention to detail.
5. What did you do in your previous internship that is relevant here? Tested: transferable output (decks, data, client materials). Traps: describing team activity rather than your own contribution.
6. Where do you see yourself in five years? Tested: retention risk. Traps: "running my own fund"; "moving to the deal team" as the main goal.

**B. Firm (6)**
7. What do you know about Hayfin? Tested: strategies, scale, history, ownership. Traps: out-of-date AUM; missing the 2025 management buyout and minority investors [S276].
8. What has Hayfin announced recently? Tested: news flow (Glassdoor IR review says round one covered recent deals and news [S261]). Traps: no specific item. Have DLF V, Seoul office, a recent deal ready.
9. What does the Partner Solutions team do? Tested: you read the JD. Traps: describing it as sales only; forgetting reporting and SMAs.
10. Which Hayfin strategy would you pitch to a Korean insurer, and in what wrapper? Tested: link between investor type and product. Traps: ignoring regulatory capital; not knowing the rated note feeder exists [S298].
11. What do you think of our recent insight pieces? Tested: depth of preparation. Traps: claiming to have read without being able to summarise. Know the €15bn piece [S298] and the PIK piece [S264].
12. What makes Hayfin's culture different? Tested: values fit. Traps: generic. Use "no superstar culture", "midfield general", persistence [S281] and the five values in the brief.

**C. Industry and technical credit (9)**
13. What is direct lending and how does it differ from syndicated loans and high yield bonds? Tested: basic product knowledge. Traps: confusing floating vs fixed rate; not mentioning bilateral negotiation, covenants, illiquidity.
14. Why would an investor choose private credit over high yield? Tested: illiquidity premium, floating rate, covenants, lower mark-to-market volatility. Traps: claiming it is lower risk outright; ignoring illiquidity and valuation lag.
15. What makes direct lending attractive now? Tested: current market view. Traps: one-sided bull case. Mention spread compression and competition, plus record fundraising [S273].
16. What are the risks in private credit today? Tested: balance. Traps: ignoring PIK creep, covenant erosion, concentration in software, valuation transparency, political scrutiny [S264, S262, S281].
17. Explain PIK. When is it good or bad? Tested: nuance. Traps: "PIK is always a red flag" [S264].
18. What is unitranche? Tested: structure knowledge. Traps: confusing with mezzanine.
19. What is an LBO and how does a lender get repaid? Tested: credit lens on an LBO (Glassdoor IR review mentions "walk me through an LBO" [S261]). Traps: answering only from the equity side; forgetting cash flow, leverage multiples and covenants.
20. How does rising or falling base rate affect a floating-rate lender? Tested: rate sensitivity. Traps: forgetting borrower interest coverage stress when rates rise.
21. What is a covenant and what is covenant-lite? Tested: lender protections. Traps: not linking to early warning and workout.

**D. IR-specific (10)**
22. What is a DDQ and what goes in it? Tested: core intern task. Traps: vague. Cite ILPA DDQ topics: strategy, team, track record, valuation, reporting, ESG, DEI, cyber [S299].
23. What is an RFP and how is it different from a DDQ? Tested: process understanding. Traps: treating them as the same.
24. Gross vs net IRR? Tested: fees, carry and fund expenses. Traps: forgetting fund-level leverage or subscription lines inflating IRR.
25. Explain IRR vs MOIC vs DPI vs TVPI. Tested: LP metrics (Glassdoor review stresses fund performance metrics [S261]). Traps: mixing realised and unrealised.
26. How do LPs choose managers? Tested: allocator thinking. Traps: "best returns" only. Cover team, track record, process, alignment, operations, consultants, fit in portfolio.
27. Who are Hayfin's investors and how do their needs differ? Tested: client segmentation. Traps: forgetting insurers, sovereign wealth funds and family offices [S273, S298].
28. What is an SMA and why would an investor want one? Tested: JD vocabulary. Traps: not mentioning customisation, fees, control, minimum size.
29. What role do investment consultants play? Tested: gatekeeper awareness. Traps: ignoring them. PSG says consultants matter more now [S298], and Bringardner led consultant relations [S274].
30. What goes into a quarterly LP report? Tested: reporting basics. Traps: forgetting portfolio company updates, NAV, capital account, cash flows, watchlist.
31. How would you use AI tools to make the team more efficient? Tested: JD skill. Traps: suggesting AI drafts client documents unchecked; ignoring confidentiality and accuracy.

**E. Market awareness (5)**
32. Tell me about a recent private credit headline and what it means for LPs. Traps: no view; or repeating a headline without implication.
33. Is private credit a bubble? Tested: balance and composure. Traps: either extreme. Use Flynn's "bullish long term, mindful of political risk" framing [S281].
34. Why are US and Asian investors buying European direct lending? Tested: link to PSG's own thesis [S298]. Traps: not mentioning diversification.
35. How are insurers changing private credit? Tested: rated feeders, capital treatment [S298]. Traps: no knowledge of Solvency II or capital efficiency concept.
36. What is happening with fundraising across private markets generally? Tested: concentration in large managers, slower PE fundraising, credit resilience. Traps: unsourced numbers. Quote figures only if sure.

**F. Behavioural and competency (5)**
37. Tell me about a time you produced work with a very high attention to detail. Traps: no concrete error caught.
38. Tell me about a time you handled multiple deadlines. Traps: no prioritisation logic.
39. Tell me about a time you persisted after rejection or setback. Tested: persistence, which Flynn names as the trait he looks for [S281]. Traps: choosing a trivial example.
40. Tell me about a time you made a team better rather than shining alone. Tested: "midfield general" [S281]. Traps: hero story.
41. Tell me about a time you explained something complex to a non-expert. Tested: client communication and "gravitas" (JD). Traps: jargon.

**G. Case and written exercises (5)**
42. Here is fund performance data in Excel. Calculate and comment on returns, and build a chart. Tested: Excel speed, accuracy, commentary [S261]. Traps: formatting errors; no "so what"; mixing gross and net.
43. Pitch this fund to a pension fund in 5 minutes. Tested: structure, investor-centred framing. Traps: listing features without linking to the pension's liabilities, yield needs and risk limits.
44. Draft a response to this DDQ question (for example, describe your valuation policy or your approach to ESG). Tested: precision and tone. Traps: overclaiming; inventing facts; not flagging what you need to confirm with compliance.
45. Write a one-page investment case study for marketing from this deal summary. Tested: JD task (case studies). Traps: disclosing confidential information; no outcome or lesson.
46. Group exercise: assess a borrower as a private credit opportunity (as in the 2024 dental case [S260]). Tested: teamwork, structure, credit basics. Traps: dominating; ignoring downside; not managing time.

### 1.9 How to adapt to interviewer types [Inferred guidance]

**IR / PSG practitioner** (most likely: London PSG associates to MDs such as Coriani or Atkinson). They care about: client empathy, accuracy, written and spoken clarity, curiosity about allocators. Do: speak about investors' objectives first, product second. Use LP vocabulary (pacing, commitment, wrapper, consultant, DDQ). Show attention to detail with a concrete example. Avoid: sounding like a deal-team applicant. Good closing question: "Which investor types are taking the most PSG time this year after DLF V?"

**Ex-consultant or ex-allocator in PSG** (Atkinson, Carew, Ballard backgrounds [S287, S290, S289]). They test whether you can think like the buyer. Do: frame answers as "an LP would ask...". Be ready for "how would you evaluate a manager?". Avoid: marketing superlatives.

**Investment professional** (from Direct Lending or Special Opportunities if the process is shared). They care about credit fundamentals and commercial sense. Do: be crisp on leverage, coverage, covenants, downside and recovery. Use one Hayfin deal from recent news. Avoid: overclaiming technical depth. Saying "I am not certain, but my reasoning is..." works better than bluffing.

**HR / early careers** (David Slack's team [S295] or SEO London staff). They test motivation, values and consistency. Do: map stories to Hayfin's values (responsibility, collaboration and transparency, excellence, entrepreneurial, diversity) and SEO background. Be warm and specific. Avoid: inconsistency between CV, cover letter and spoken answers.

**Senior leader** (unlikely, but possible in a final chat). Do: short, confident answers, a view on the market, and a smart question. Use the "midfield general" idea only if natural.

## 6. Interview angles for a PSG/IR intern candidate

1. **Read the two PSG-authored pieces before any round.** The €15bn fundraise piece is written by the two Global Co-Heads of Client Coverage [S298]. The Complexity Premium is by the London product-strategy MD [S262]. Being able to summarise each in 30 seconds is the strongest signal of genuine IR interest.
2. **Frame Hayfin in investor terms.** "One underlying senior-secured strategy, many wrappers for different investors" (commingled, levered, SMA, evergreen, rated note feeder) [S298]. This shows you understand what PSG actually sells.
3. **Know the distribution map.** London HQ plus coverage in DACH, Nordics, MENA, Southern Europe, Japan, Korea, Australia, North America [S288-S294]. Seoul opened 1 Oct 2026 [S276]. A question about where growth comes from is likely.
4. **Use Flynn's hiring criteria in behavioural answers.** Persistence, adaptability ("athletic"), low ego, team-first "midfield general" [S281]. Pick stories that show these.
5. **Prepare an Excel and pitch drill.** Practise computing gross and net returns, TVPI/DPI and a simple performance chart in under 30 minutes, then a 5-minute fund pitch to a named investor type (UK DB pension, Korean insurer, US endowment). This mirrors the reported IR case [S261] and JD tasks.
6. **Have a DDQ answer ready.** Name ILPA DDQ topics [S299] and explain why accuracy, consistency across documents and compliance sign-off matter.
7. **Show balanced market views.** Bull case (bank retreat, record DLF V, global LP demand [S273, S298]) and risks (PIK, covenant erosion, political scrutiny [S264, S262, S281]). Flynn's own stance is long-term bullish but cautious; mirror that tone.
8. **Do not name departed people as current.** Avoid citing Alex Wolfman as head of PSG. Refer to "the Partner Solutions leadership" or named current MDs.
9. **Smart questions to ask:** "After DLF V, which investor segments are PSG prioritising?"; "How has the rated note feeder changed conversations with insurers?"; "How does PSG use AI in DDQ and reporting workflows today?"; "What did last year's interns work on that had the most impact?"
10. **Use SEO London fully.** Request SEO mock interviews and the Hayfin format as soon as the shortlist lands (w/c 2 Nov 2026). Prior-cycle timing suggests interviews in mid to late November [S265] [Inferred].
