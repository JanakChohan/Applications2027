# Hayfin Partner Solutions Group: Question Bank

48 likely questions for the Summer 2027 PSG internship. Scaffolds, not scripts. [Sxx] markers refer to the source table in the main pack (Part 15). Built 8 October 2026.

## A. Motivation

### 1. Why investor relations rather than an investing seat?
- **Tested:** Genuine interest in the client side; retention risk.
- **Structure:** What IR is to you (explaining, evidencing, serving) → a moment you enjoyed that kind of work → why it matters at a credit manager specifically.
- **Facts to weave in:** PSG sits on the ESG Deal Committee [S305]; DLF V was raised through five wrappers [S222]; Hayfin hired ex-consultants and allocators into PSG [S1].
- **Trap:** Calling IR a stepping stone to deals; not knowing the daily work (DDQs, RFPs, reporting).

### 2. Why private credit?
- **Tested:** Understanding of why the asset class exists.
- **Structure:** Bank retreat → companies need lenders → investors want contractual floating-rate income → private credit fills the gap; one risk you take seriously.
- **Facts to weave in:** Flynn's founding thesis on bank capital rules [S105]; Europe 35.9% of 2025 private debt capital [S152].
- **Trap:** A generic 'fast-growing' answer; claiming it is low risk.

### 3. Why Hayfin?
- **Tested:** Firm-specific research expressed as mechanism.
- **Structure:** Use the 30-second answer in Part 12: one platform with several doors → why that matters to an LP → why PSG is where that is explained.
- **Facts to weave in:** 8%+ and 15%+ lender [S307]; DLF V >€15bn [S71]; conflict rules [S305].
- **Trap:** Adjectives ('leading', 'prestigious'); size alone; anything transplantable to Ares or ICG.

### 4. Walk me through your CV.
- **Tested:** Concise story; link from past work to IR skills.
- **Structure:** 90 seconds: who you are → two experiences, each with one output and one skill (accuracy, client materials, data) → why this role now.
- **Facts to weave in:** JD: 'proactive', 'research... summarise... present', Excel and PowerPoint.
- **Trap:** Over two minutes; describing what the team did, not what you did.

### 5. What did you do in your last internship that is relevant here?
- **Tested:** Transferable output.
- **Structure:** Situation → the thing you produced (deck, model, data pack) → how you checked it → what happened.
- **Facts to weave in:** Map to JD duties: pitch materials, data, proofreading.
- **Trap:** No concrete output; no error you caught.

### 6. Where do you see yourself in five years?
- **Tested:** Retention and realism.
- **Structure:** Building expertise in investor work → a product or coverage track → specific skill you want (e.g. structuring vehicles for insurers).
- **Facts to weave in:** Two PSG tracks: product strategy and coverage [S1]; Coriani's path from intern to MD [S288].
- **Trap:** 'Running my own fund'; 'moving to the deal team'.

## C. Industry and credit technicals

### 14. What is direct lending, and how is it different from syndicated loans and high-yield bonds?
- **Tested:** Product basics.
- **Structure:** Who lends → how terms are set → whether it trades → rate type → protections.
- **Facts to weave in:** Floating, senior secured, bilateral or small club; 40% of Hayfin DL sole-lender [S95].
- **Trap:** Mixing fixed and floating; forgetting illiquidity.

### 15. Why would an investor choose private credit over high yield?
- **Tested:** Trade-offs.
- **Structure:** Extra spread for illiquidity and complexity → floating rate → covenants and control → but: illiquid, model marks, lock-up.
- **Facts to weave in:** PitchBook: the key metric is 'excess spread' over public fixed income [S151].
- **Trap:** Calling it lower risk outright.

### 16. What makes direct lending attractive now, and what are the risks?
- **Tested:** Balance.
- **Structure:** Two tailwinds, two risks, one view.
- **Facts to weave in:** Bank retreat [S221]; Europe in favour [S152]; PIK creep [S245]; deal volumes down 29% [S240].
- **Trap:** One-sided bull case.

### 17. Explain PIK. When is it good or bad?
- **Tested:** Nuance on the 2026 hot topic.
- **Structure:** Definition → planned PIK at entry vs PIK forced by stress → how an LP should see it.
- **Facts to weave in:** 'Three PIKs': good, bad, fake good [S229]; Lincoln 'shadow default' 6.2% [S162].
- **Trap:** 'PIK is always a red flag.'

### 18. What is unitranche?
- **Tested:** Structure knowledge.
- **Structure:** One loan replacing senior plus junior → one price, one lender group → speed and certainty for the sponsor.
- **Facts to weave in:** Condis €305m sole-lender unitranche [S231].
- **Trap:** Confusing it with mezzanine.

### 19. Walk me through an LBO from the lender's side.
- **Tested:** Credit lens (asked in a 2025 IR process [S261]).
- **Structure:** Sponsor buys company with equity plus debt → lender sizes debt on EBITDA and cash flow → covenants → repaid from cash flow or refinancing.
- **Facts to weave in:** Leverage = debt / EBITDA; Portfolio Breakdown reports LTM EBITDA, EV, leverage [S305].
- **Trap:** Answering only from the equity side.

### 20. How do rate moves affect a floating-rate lender?
- **Tested:** Rate sensitivity both ways.
- **Structure:** Higher rates → more income → but weaker interest coverage for borrowers → more PIK and amendments; cuts do the reverse.
- **Facts to weave in:** PitchBook expects 'cooler returns' as rates fall [S152].
- **Trap:** Forgetting borrower stress.

### 21. What is a covenant, and what is covenant-lite?
- **Tested:** Lender protections.
- **Structure:** A promise (e.g. leverage below X) → early warning → seat at the table before default; cov-lite removes maintenance tests.
- **Facts to weave in:** Hayfin: Europe has 'tighter documentation' than the US [S95].
- **Trap:** Not linking covenants to workout capability.

### 22. Why do private credit default rates differ so much?
- **Tested:** Data literacy.
- **Structure:** Definitions: payment default vs PIK switches vs covenant breaches → numbers → what LPs now ask for.
- **Facts to weave in:** Proskauer 2.51% [S155]; Fitch 6.3% [S245]; Lincoln 6.2% shadow [S162].
- **Trap:** Quoting one number as 'the' default rate.

## D. Investor relations specifics

### 23. What is a DDQ and what goes in it?
- **Tested:** Core intern task.
- **Structure:** Purpose → standard templates → main sections → how a team answers efficiently.
- **Facts to weave in:** ILPA DDQ 2.0: 20 topics incl. succession, credit facilities, valuation, ESG, DEI, cyber [S299]; AIMA credit modules [S48].
- **Trap:** Vague answer; not mentioning consistency and sign-off.

### 24. How is an RFP different from a DDQ?
- **Tested:** Process understanding.
- **Structure:** RFP = competitive search for a mandate with fixed questions and deadline; DDQ = diligence on a chosen manager; both reuse the library.
- **Facts to weave in:** eVestment and consultant databases [S55].
- **Trap:** Treating them as the same.

### 25. Gross vs net IRR: what is the difference and why does it matter?
- **Tested:** Fees and honesty in marketing.
- **Structure:** Gross before fees and carry → net after → subscription lines lift IRR → rules require net alongside gross.
- **Facts to weave in:** SEC Marketing Rule FAQ, 19 Mar 2025 [S53]; SOCF I 13.1% gross vs 8.1% net [S307].
- **Trap:** Forgetting fund leverage and subscription lines.

### 26. Explain IRR, MOIC, DPI and TVPI.
- **Tested:** Fund metrics fluency.
- **Structure:** Define each in one line → which is realised vs unrealised → which LPs watch in a slow-exit market (DPI).
- **Facts to weave in:** ILPA Performance Template shows IRR and TVPI with and without subscription lines [S45].
- **Trap:** Mixing realised and unrealised.

### 27. How do LPs choose managers?
- **Tested:** Allocator thinking.
- **Structure:** Strategy fit → team and track record → process and controls → alignment and terms → operations → consultant view.
- **Facts to weave in:** Intralinks: strategy 51%, sector knowledge 30%, co-invest 22%, fees 14% [S189].
- **Trap:** 'Best returns' only.

### 28. Who are Hayfin's investors and how do their needs differ?
- **Tested:** Client segmentation.
- **Structure:** Pensions (liabilities, pacing) → insurers (capital, ratings) → SWFs (scale, co-invest) → consultants (process).
- **Facts to weave in:** Pension 51.1%, insurance 19.5%, SWF 12.1%; Australia 22.8% [S305].
- **Trap:** Forgetting insurers and SWFs.

### 29. What is an SMA and why would an investor want one?
- **Tested:** JD vocabulary.
- **Structure:** Single-investor vehicle → custom terms, control, fees, reporting → needs size.
- **Facts to weave in:** JD's 'Special Memorandum Accounts' means separately managed accounts [S25].
- **Trap:** Not knowing the term, or repeating the JD's error.

### 30. What role do investment consultants play?
- **Tested:** Gatekeeper awareness.
- **Structure:** Advise many pension funds → rate managers → RFPs and databases → one rating can unlock many commitments.
- **Facts to weave in:** DLF V consultant involvement highest ever [S222]; Bringardner ex-BlackRock consultant relations [S4].
- **Trap:** Ignoring them.

### 31. What goes into a quarterly LP report?
- **Tested:** Reporting basics.
- **Structure:** Portfolio summary and track record → company-level data → letter → capital account → audited accounts annually.
- **Facts to weave in:** Hayfin's list: Summary of Investments, Portfolio Breakdown, CEO Letter, audited financials within 90 days [S305].
- **Trap:** Forgetting fees and expenses, watchlist, ESG.

### 32. How would you use AI tools to make the team more efficient?
- **Tested:** JD skill, with judgement.
- **Structure:** One use case → guardrails (approved sources, no confidential data in public tools, human check) → how you would measure the gain.
- **Facts to weave in:** Chicago posting: AI 'used with careful attention to accuracy' [S19]; DDQ 40-60 hours [S190].
- **Trap:** AI drafting client documents unchecked.

## G. Case and written exercises

### 44. Here is fund performance data in Excel: calculate returns and comment.
- **Tested:** Excel speed, accuracy, commentary (reported 2025 IR case [S261]).
- **Structure:** Check data → compute IRR (XIRR), MOIC, DPI, TVPI → one chart → three bullet 'so whats' → caveats.
- **Facts to weave in:** Gross vs net; subscription line effect; currency.
- **Trap:** Formatting errors; no conclusion.

### 45. Pitch this fund to a UK pension fund in five minutes.
- **Tested:** Structure, investor-first framing.
- **Structure:** Their need → the strategy → why this manager (evidence) → risks and how managed → terms and next step.
- **Facts to weave in:** Pension 51% of Hayfin AUM [S305]; downside, PIK, loss-rate questions [S222].
- **Trap:** Listing features without linking to the pension's liabilities.

### 46. Draft a DDQ answer: describe your valuation policy.
- **Tested:** Precision and tone.
- **Structure:** Who values, how often, independent input, committee, escalation; mark what you would confirm with Finance and Compliance.
- **Facts to weave in:** Valuation Committee; Finance reviews NAV before investors [S333]; FCA review good practice [S166].
- **Trap:** Overclaiming; inventing facts.

### 47. Write a one-page case study from this deal summary.
- **Tested:** JD task with compliance instincts.
- **Structure:** Situation → why Hayfin won → structure and protections → outcome → selection basis and disclaimers.
- **Facts to weave in:** COBS 4.2 [S49]; SEC FAQ [S53]; anti-greenwashing [S52].
- **Trap:** Disclosing confidential terms; no selection basis.

### 48. Group exercise: assess this borrower as a credit opportunity.
- **Tested:** Teamwork and credit basics (2024 dental case [S260]).
- **Structure:** Agree a structure and timekeeper → business quality → cash flow and leverage → downside and recovery → decision.
- **Facts to weave in:** Leverage, interest cover, covenants, sponsor support.
- **Trap:** Dominating; ignoring downside.
