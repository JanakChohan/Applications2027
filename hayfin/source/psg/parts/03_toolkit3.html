Packaging: why the same loans need different wrappers

The rated note feeder, insurer capital rules in the EU, US and Korea, and the look-through data insurers need. The most Hayfin-specific technical topic in this guide

The PSG co-heads put the problem in one sentence: "A European pension fund, a US institution and an Asian insurer may all want exposure to the same underlying senior-secured loans, but they may not want to access that exposure in the same form" [S403]. This section explains why, with the actual rule numbers.

The five wrappers, technically

Unlevered commingled fundMany LPs in one closed-ended fund; no borrowing at fund level beyond short-term linesPensions. For EU insurers, units of an unleveraged closed-ended EU AIF can get a 39% equity charge instead of 49% even without look-through (Solvency II Art 168(6)(c)) [S407]

How a rated note feeder works

Why it saves capital: three insurer rulebooks

Key ideaInsurers must hold capital against each investment, in proportion to how risky the regulator thinks it is. An opaque fund stake is treated like equity (high charge). A rated bond is treated like a bond (low charge). Same loans underneath; very different capital cost.

The three rulebooks, simply

  • EU insurers (Solvency II). Without look-through, fund units are "type 2" equity at 49% plus a symmetric adjustment of -10% to +10% (widening to ±13% from 30 January 2027) [S408][S409][S414]. Unleveraged closed-ended EU funds can be "type 1" at 39% [S407]. With look-through, each loan is charged as spread risk; unrated loans cost 3% × duration [S410], which is why insurers demand line-by-line data (below). A rated note gets bond factors, unless it counts as a securitisation, in which case much higher factors apply [S412]. Macfarlanes says a feeder that works for Solvency II insurers, as opposed to US ones, "remains a work in progress" [S448]. The Solvency II review applies from 30 January 2027 [S414][S415].
  • US insurers (NAIC). A private fund LP interest carries a 30% risk-based capital charge; investment-grade debt is "generally under 2%" [S419]. Rated feeders started here ("first designed for US insurers") [S418]. Regulators push back: the NAIC worried feeders could "repackage equity-like exposure", one driver of the principles-based bond definition effective 1 January 2025 [S419][S423], and the NAIC's securities office gained power from 1 January 2026 to challenge private ratings [S422].
  • Korean insurers (K-ICS). Since 2023 Korea uses K-ICS, a market-consistent regime at a 99.5% confidence level [S429]. Regulators cut the recommended ratio from 150% to 130-140% in 2025 and add a core capital rule from 2027 [S426][S427]. Samsung Life, a Hayfin shareholder, reported 208.2% at end-June 2026 [S428]. Exact fund and credit factors were not found [Gap], but one industry comment says some Korean insurers restructure alternatives into "rated" notes to get better treatment [S430] (snippet). This is the context for Hayfin's new Seoul office [S404].
Why this is the most distinctive thing you could learn at HayfinMost students can define IRR. Very few can explain why an insurer would buy the same European loans through a rated note rather than a fund stake, what the capital difference is, and what can go wrong (the securitisation classification, the NAIC's scrutiny). Hayfin did this in its flagship raise in 2026 [S402]. That is a conversation you can have in an interview.

Look-through data: the TPT and EET

If an insurer wants the lower look-through charge, it needs data on every loan. The industry template is the Tripartite Template (TPT), maintained by FinDatEx: "a widely used European data exchange template designed to support the provision of asset data from asset managers to insurers for Solvency II reporting purposes" [S431]. Version 7 (December 2024) added "Improved handling of bonds or loans with payment-in-kind features" [S432]. Version 8 was consulted on from July to September 2026 and is expected to be mandatory in Q1 2027 [S431]. Whether V8 was finalised is not confirmed [Gap].

The European ESG Template (EET) does the same job for sustainability data under SFDR and the EU's suitability rules; version 1.1.3 has applied since 1 January 2025 [S434]. Hayfin runs six SFDR Article 8 funds [S25].
