The worryWhat it means in plain EnglishHayfin's stated answer
PIK"Payment in kind": a borrower adds interest to its debt instead of paying cash. Sometimes planned, sometimes a sign of trouble. Fitch counted PIK switches and deferrals as 47% of US private credit defaults in August 2026 [S245].Hayfin published "A Tale of Three PIKs" separating good, bad and "fake good" PIK: "Done well, PIK is not something to be feared" [S229]. Most interest across its book is paid in cash (firm claim) [S95].
Technology exposureLenders to software companies worry AI could undercut those businesses. Around 19% of direct loans went to software firms by end-2025 (BIS) [S242].Software under 6% of the Direct Lending book, under 5% in the latest fund (Hayfin's figure) [S227].
Loss ratesHow much lent money is actually lost.Not published recently. The last public statement (2015) was no realised losses from borrower default [S307]. In 2026 Hayfin said its watchlist had fallen by almost a third since end-2023 (firm claim) [S95].
Know what is not publicHayfin's current loss rate and recent fund returns are not public. If an interviewer asks, say what is public (the 2015 statement, the watchlist claim, the software figure) and that the full track record sits in the fund documents investors receive. That honesty is itself an investor relations skill.

The packaging: same product, different wrappers

Shop analogyThe same coffee beans can be sold as a retail bag, a bulk sack for a café, a monthly subscription, or a sealed and certified box for an airline. The beans do not change. The packaging changes so each customer can buy. In finance the packaging is called a wrapper.
Why this matters for your teamDesigning and explaining the packaging is Partner Solutions work. Hayfin's own product strategy team "owns" it: Maura English heads Product Strategy, and Tim Atkinson leads product strategy for the opportunistic strategies from London [S284][S287]. When you say "the strategy stays the same, the wrappers change", you are describing your future job.
