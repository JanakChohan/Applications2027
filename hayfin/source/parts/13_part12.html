Part 12

"Why Hayfin?"

Built from mechanism: thirty seconds, two minutes, a close, the comparison table, and the honest counter-case

Thirty seconds

Say this in the interview"Hayfin is one platform with several doors. The same local teams can offer a company a senior loan from Direct Lending, gap capital from Tactical Solutions or a rescue from Special Opportunities, and the workout team stands behind all of them. That is why LPs, half of them pension funds, gave DLF V over €15 billion, two and a half times the last fund, in a hard year for private credit. Partner Solutions is where that mechanism is explained, evidenced and reported every quarter. That is the work I want to learn."

Two minutes

Say this in the interview

"I looked at Hayfin from the investor's side, because that is the PSG seat.

First, the mechanism. Hayfin started in 2009 as a lender using its backers' balance sheet, then moved to managing funds. A 2016 Pennsylvania pension memo made a point I found striking: because the market sees Hayfin as both an 8% and a 15% lender, it sees more companies and gets better terms. One relationship, several products, and a legal and workout team that has worked in each European creditor regime. Tim Flynn's explanation is that Europe is too shallow for a narrow mandate.

Second, the cost, and how Hayfin handles it. If two Hayfin funds can sit in the same company, someone has to protect each fund's investors. The Stewardship report sets rules: one fund must be a non-blocking minority, cross trades need CCO and COO sign-off, and exceptions go to a Conflicts Committee. Those are exactly the questions in an LP's DDQ, so for PSG they are part of the product.

Third, the investors. About half of AUM is pension money, a fifth insurance, an eighth sovereign wealth. Each group needs a different wrapper, which is why DLF V was raised through unlevered and levered funds, SMAs, evergreen vehicles and a rated note feeder for insurers. And LPs pressed hardest on PIK, tech exposure and loss rates.

Fourth, the seat. The firm is concentrating its investing on Europe and globalising its investor base: Seoul last week, Melbourne and Dallas last year. A Finance job post already mentions DLF VI. So PSG is one of the firm's growth engines, and I would join it while it is still being built. That is why Hayfin, and why Partner Solutions."

The honest counter-case

Know these so that a hard question does not surprise you. Raise them only as questions, never as accusations.

  1. The only public fund-level net return is old: the first Special Opportunities fund showed 8.1% net in 2015 against an 11-15% target [S306][S307].
  2. Currency risk is real for non-euro investors: 8.0% in euros became -2.6% in dollars for one unhedged LP [S307].
  3. PSERS booked a -$3.9m carried interest accrual on its Hayfin funds in 2024 [S308].
  4. Direct Lending dominance means a European default cycle would hit the flagship hardest [S305][S71].
  5. Fast growth tests whether underwriting and workout capacity kept pace: 228 staff and 100 investment professionals in 2025 [S305].
  6. No current loss rate is published; broad direct lending index losses are estimated around 1.5% a year [S344].
  7. Three ownership changes since 2017, and some senior names have left the website [S315][S74][S296].
  8. Multi-strategy creates conflicts; Hayfin's detailed rules show they occur [S305].
  9. Much larger global platforms compete in European direct lending [S186][S135].
