The 10-minute emergency version

Ten facts to have cold

  1. What Hayfin is. A London-headquartered, European-focused private credit and alternative asset manager, founded in 2009 by Tim Flynn and Mark Tognolini, both ex-Goldman Sachs leveraged finance [S79][S282][S283]. About €40bn of assets under management (AUM) at Q2 2026 [S224].
  2. Owners. Management bought the firm back from the Canadian pension fund BCI in a buyout funded by Arctos, completed 7 February 2025. Mubadala, AXA IM Prime and Samsung Life later bought minority stakes [S73][S74][S75]. Hayfin is private and independent.
  3. The big news. Direct Lending Fund V (DLF V) closed on 2 July 2026 at over €15bn, more than double DLF IV (over €6bn, August 2023) [S71]. About $600m came through a rated note feeder built for insurers [S237].
  4. Strategies. Direct Lending, Special Opportunities, Tactical Solutions, High Yield and Syndicated Loans with CLOs, Private Equity Solutions, plus specialist Healthcare, Maritime and Real Estate teams [S327][S328].
  5. AUM mix (March 2025). Direct Lending €16.2bn, High Yield and Syndicated Loans €9.2bn, Special Opportunities €2.6bn, Tactical Solutions €2.3bn, others smaller [S305]. Read from a chart.
  6. Investors. All institutional. Pensions 51%, insurers 19.5%, sovereign wealth funds 12% (March 2025) [S305]. No retail money in private credit [S227].
  7. PSG. Partner Solutions Group: fundraising, investor coverage, product strategy, and reporting for funds and separately managed accounts (SMAs) [JD]. Global co-heads of client coverage: Risa Lipsky and Steve Bringardner [S285][S286].
  8. New offices. Seoul opened 1 October 2026 as a dedicated investor relations office [S223]. Dubai opened January 2024 [S275]. Chicago opened November 2024 [S97].
  9. What investors asked about in DLF V. PIK (interest paid in kind), technology exposure and loss rates. Consultants were more involved than in any earlier Hayfin fund [S222].
  10. The market. 2026 was the year US retail private credit funds were hit by withdrawal requests far above their 5% quarterly limits. Hayfin turned its locked-up, institutional-only structure into a selling point [S221][S243].

The 30-second "why Hayfin"

Say this in the interview"Hayfin is one origination platform with several doors. The same local teams can offer a company a senior loan, gap capital, or a rescue, and the workout team stands behind all of them. A 2016 Pennsylvania pension memo put it neatly: because the market sees Hayfin as both an 8% and a 15% lender, it sees more companies and gets better terms. Partner Solutions is where that mechanism gets explained, evidenced and reported to investors. DLF V raised over €15bn through five different wrappers for one strategy. I want to learn how you turn deal-level evidence into something an LP can underwrite."

Evidence: [S307] for the 8% / 15% line; [S222] for the wrappers.

Three questions to ask

  1. "DLF V had more consultant involvement than any earlier Hayfin fund. How has that changed what PSG produces day to day?" [S222]
  2. "Your 'Tale of Three PIKs' piece separates good, bad and fake-good PIK. Does that framework show up in quarterly LP reporting?" [S229]
  3. "How does PSG use AI tools in DDQ and reporting work today, and what has to stay human?"

Three things never to say

Don't say this"Hayfin has €30bn" (stale). "Hayfin sells to retail investors" (it says it takes no retail capital in private credit [S227]). "IR is my route into the deal team" (signals you will leave). Do not name Alex Wolfman as head of PSG: he appears to have left [S296].
