Part 14

Glossary

86 terms in plain English
TermPlain-English meaning
Advisory Board (LPAC)A group of selected investors in a fund that reviews conflicts, expenses and performance; at Hayfin it meets twice a year [S305].
AIF / AIFMAlternative investment fund / its manager. The EU and UK regulate managers of funds like Hayfin's.
AIFMD IIDirective (EU) 2024/927 amending the EU fund managers directive; transposition by 16 April 2026; adds loan-origination fund rules [S163].
AIMA DDQA due diligence questionnaire from the Alternative Investment Management Association, with modules for private credit [S48].
Allocation policyThe firm's rules for sharing deals fairly between its funds [S305].
Amend-and-extendChanging a loan's terms and pushing out its maturity, often to avoid a default.
Annex IVThe EU's regulatory reporting template for fund managers; enhanced from 16 April 2027 [S251].
Asset-based finance (ABF)Loans secured on pools of assets such as receivables or equipment rather than a company's cash flow.
AUMAssets under management: how much money a manager looks after.
BDC (non-traded)A US business development company, a lending fund often sold to wealthy individuals with limited quarterly redemptions.
Basis point (bp)One hundredth of one percent; 75bps = 0.75%.
Capital account statementA report of one investor's contributions, distributions, fees and value in a fund.
Capital call (drawdown)The fund asking investors to send part of their committed money when a loan is made.
Carried interest (carry)The manager's share of profits above a hurdle.
Catch-upA clause letting the manager receive a larger share of profits after the hurdle until it reaches its agreed split.
CLOCollateralised loan obligation: a vehicle that buys syndicated loans and funds itself by selling rated slices of debt and equity.
Club dealA loan provided by a small group of lenders rather than one.
COBS 4The FCA rules on communications and financial promotions, including "fair, clear and not misleading" [S49].
Co-investmentAn investor investing directly alongside a fund in a specific deal, usually at lower fees.
CommitmentThe amount an investor promises to a fund, drawn over time.
Consultant (investment)A firm advising pension funds on which managers to hire; often a gatekeeper.
Continuation fundA new vehicle that buys one or more assets from an older fund so they can be held longer.
CovenantA borrower's promise in a loan, such as keeping leverage below a level.
Covenant-liteA loan without regular financial tests, giving lenders less early warning.
DDQDue diligence questionnaire: a standard list of questions investors send before investing.
Direct lendingA fund lending directly to a company, usually PE-owned, rather than through a bank.
Distressed debtDebt of a company in or near default, often bought at a discount.
DPIDistributions to paid-in capital: cash returned divided by cash called.
Dry powderCommitted capital not yet invested.
EBITDA (LTM)Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, over the last twelve months.
ELTIF 2.0The revised EU long-term investment fund rules (Regulation (EU) 2023/606), applying from 10 January 2024 [S164].
Evergreen fundA fund with no fixed end date that takes new money and allows limited redemptions.
eVestmentA database where managers submit data that consultants and investors search [S55].
Feeder fundA vehicle through which investors invest into a main fund, often to suit tax or regulation.
Financial promotionAny communication inviting someone to invest; regulated in the UK.
First close / final closeThe first date a fund accepts commitments and starts investing / the date it stops taking new money.
Floating rateInterest that moves with a base rate such as Euribor or SONIA.
Fund of fundsA fund that invests in other funds.
GP / LPGeneral partner (the manager) / limited partner (the investor).
Gross vs net returnBefore vs after fees, carry and expenses.
Hard capThe maximum size a fund may raise.
High yieldTraded bonds rated below investment grade.
Hurdle (preferred return)The minimum return investors receive before the manager earns carry.
ILPAInstitutional Limited Partners Association, which sets investor-backed templates and principles.
ILPA Reporting / Performance TemplateStandard formats for fees and capital accounts / performance, released 22 January 2025 [S43].
Information barrierRules that stop confidential information passing between teams; at Hayfin, deal "trees" [S305].
Investment period / harvestThe years a fund makes new loans / the years it collects repayments; 3-4 years each at Hayfin [S305].
IRRInternal rate of return: the annual return that makes a fund's cash flows net to zero.
Key person clauseA fund term triggered if named senior people leave.
Leverage (company)Debt divided by EBITDA.
Leverage (fund)Borrowing by the fund itself to increase returns; DLF V had levered and unlevered versions [S222].
Loan-origination fundUnder AIFMD II, a fund whose main strategy is making loans; subject to leverage caps and retention [S163].
Loss rateMoney lost on loans as a share of capital lent, usually annualised.
LPALimited partnership agreement: the fund's legal contract with investors.
LTAFThe UK's Long-Term Asset Fund, an authorised open-ended fund for illiquid assets.
Management feeAn annual fee on committed or invested capital; around 1% is the European direct lending norm [S104].
Marketing Rule (SEC)US Rule 206(4)-1 governing adviser marketing, including gross and net performance [S53].
MezzanineJunior debt between senior loans and equity.
MFN clause"Most favoured nation": an investor's right to terms as good as those given to similar investors.
MNPIMaterial non-public information; trading on it is illegal.
MOICMultiple on invested capital: total value divided by money invested.
NAVNet asset value: what a fund's assets are worth minus its liabilities.
NAV lendingLoans to a PE fund secured on the value of its portfolio.
Operational due diligenceAn investor's review of a manager's operations, controls, IT and compliance.
PIK (payment in kind)Interest added to the loan balance instead of paid in cash [S229].
Placement agentA firm paid to introduce a manager to investors and help run a fundraise.
PRIPrinciples for Responsible Investment; Hayfin has been a signatory since 2018 [S25].
Private creditLoans made by non-bank lenders, negotiated directly and usually held to maturity.
Rated note feederA feeder that issues rated notes so insurers can hold the exposure more capital-efficiently [S237].
Re-upAn existing investor committing to the manager's next fund.
RFPRequest for proposal: a formal manager search with a fixed questionnaire.
Secondary marketWhere investors sell fund stakes or loans to other investors.
Senior securedDebt with the first claim on a company's assets.
SFDR (Article 8 / 9)EU sustainability disclosure rules; Article 8 funds promote ESG characteristics, Article 9 have a sustainable objective.
Side letterAn agreement giving one investor specific terms alongside the LPA.
SMASeparately managed account: a single-investor vehicle with custom terms (the JD's "Special Memorandum Accounts").
Solvency IIEU insurance capital rules; they shape how insurers prefer to hold private credit.
Special opportunities / situationsFlexible capital for complex or stressed situations.
SponsorThe private equity firm that owns the borrower.
Subscription lineA short-term loan to a fund that delays capital calls; it lifts IRR without changing the multiple.
Syndicated loan (BSL)A loan arranged by banks and sold to many investors, then traded.
TVPI / RVPITotal value to paid-in (DPI + RVPI) / remaining value to paid-in.
UnitrancheOne loan blending senior and junior debt, at one price, from one lender or a small club.
Value bridgeA report showing what drove a change in valuation; praised by the FCA [S166].
VintageThe year or period a fund was raised.
WorkoutThe process of fixing or recovering a problem loan, including restructuring.
