Part 3

Why Hayfin is built this way

Structure, how the teams work together, what investors actually buy, and the non-investment side

This part answers four questions with evidence rather than adjectives. Why is Hayfin a multi-strategy credit platform with local offices, and not a single-strategy lender? How do its teams actually work together? What exactly do its investors buy? And how big is the non-investment side that PSG belongs to?

1. Why this structure: the theory, and what it costs

The theory in one sentence. A multi-strategy credit manager shares one origination network and one underwriting culture across several funds, so it can lend across the capital structure and across the cycle, with a workout team behind every loan. Each link below has evidence.

The evidence, link by link

  1. One origination engine, several doors. The 2016 PSERS memo: "the continued evolution of Hayfin's Direct Lending strategy has served to benefit the Firm's Special Opportunities strategy. This has allowed Hayfin to cast a wider net and engage a broader range of companies with the market now not only viewing Hayfin as a 15%+ lender but an 8%+ lender. Once engaged, Hayfin has a greater ability to obtain favorable pricing" [S307]. [Confirmed, primary LP document] In plain words: a company that meets Hayfin for a cheap senior loan is a future customer for a dearer, more complex one, and the other way round.
  2. Deploy at every point of the cycle. Special Opportunities uses "both direct origination and secondary purchases of illiquid assets" to "find attractive deployment at all points of the cycle" [S328]. The 2016 memo: "Secondary sourcing provides flexibility of deployment as cycles turn" [S307]. In 2020, when competitors were nursing portfolios, Hayfin invested a then-record €3.7bn in direct lending [S314]. [Confirmed]
  3. Europe forces breadth. Flynn: "We have to run extremely broad mandates compared to what we would do in the U.S." [S324]. Europe has many countries, legal regimes and languages, and banks still dominate lending; no single niche is deep enough to deploy €15bn alone [Inferred].
  4. Workouts are a product feature. "Most of our team are industry generalists, with our edge lying in our origination networks and proven expertise in managing complex restructurings, workouts and liquidations" [S331]. Hayfin "utilizes internal legal resources, who are experienced in structuring and documenting debt investments at all levels of an investee company's capital structure and in each of the relevant creditor regimes in Europe" [S307]. [Confirmed]
  5. Local presence. "We believe in being local in each of our markets, and that our diverse mix of nationalities, cultures and languages makes us more connected to these markets" [S305]. Tognolini: "We consider ourselves the European home team" [S227]. [Confirmed]
  6. Specialists where generalists lose. Healthcare (€0.6bn) and Maritime (€0.5bn) are small in AUM [S305] but add sector knowledge and low-correlation assets; healthcare is pitched on "value protection through intellectual property, diversification and low correlation" [S328]. [Confirmed]
  7. The liquid side is a separate muscle. In private credit Hayfin "does not rely on relationships with investment banks for allocations of broadly syndicated leveraged loan and high yield investment opportunities" [S305], but it also runs a €9.2bn High Yield and Syndicated Loans business [S305]. [Confirmed]

What the structure costs, and how Hayfin manages it

If two Hayfin funds can sit in the same company, someone must protect each fund's investors. Hayfin's Stewardship report sets out unusually specific rules [S305]:

Say this in the interview"Those conflict rules are not compliance detail for PSG. They are part of the product. An investor in both DLF and Special Opportunities will ask in the DDQ how you stop one fund benefiting at the other's expense. Hayfin has a written answer: non-blocking minority stakes, CCO and COO sign-off on cross trades, and a Conflicts Committee."

2. How the teams work together, and where collaboration actually lives

Read Hayfin's own wording precisely. Four places stand out [Confirmed, from S305 and S333]:

  • The ESG Deal Committee, which every private credit deal passes before final Investment Committee, "includes members of our investment, legal, ESG, and partner solutions teams" [S305]. PSG is inside the approval workflow, not only downstream of it.
  • Quarterly reports: "Hayfin's Investor Relations and Investment teams produce the following quarterly reports for each fund and Separately Managed Account" [S305]. Joint production.
  • Finance: the Fund Controller reviews NAVs "before it goes to investors" and supports "Investor Reporting with timely query resolution". The posting says the role "reaches across all of Hayfin's Private Credit strategies, so you'll see how a multi-strategy manager works" [S333].
  • Pay: "Our investment professionals are rewarded to collaborate, rather than outperform peers" [S305]. Flynn: "Ours is a no superstar culture where we value the midfield general, who makes everyone else 10 percent better, over the superstar striker" [S281]. Tognolini rejects a multi-boutique or "pod" model: "We aren't one of them" [S117].

Where collaboration actually lives [Inferred from the Confirmed pieces]: (a) the shared origination network; (b) the Investment and ESG Deal Committees; (c) the legal and workout team; (d) the reporting chain from deal teams and Finance into PSG. Where it does not live: between funds holding different layers of one company (walled by rule) and between competing bid teams (deal trees).

3. What investors actually buy

Who they are

Australia at 22.8% is striking for a European manager [Inferred link]: Australia's Future Fund was a founding backer [S307], and Hayfin hired a Melbourne-based ex-CIO in 2025 to lead its Australian clients [S272]. Named public investors include Pennsylvania PSERS (€200m to the first Special Opportunities fund in 2012; up to $200m to SOF II in 2016) [S306][S307], Nashville's employee pension ($60m to the Direct Lending Fund, with a euro hedge, November 2013) [S309], and Vermont ($50m to the Tactical Solutions Evergreen Fund, reported August 2026) [S322]. BCI stayed "a significant and strategic limited partner in certain key Hayfin fund strategies" after selling its stake [S315].

Why they accept the terms, and the counter-case with numbers

They accept a seven to ten year lock-up, no redemption rights ("Investors in the Funds are not entitled to have their interests in the respective Fund redeemed" [S301]), fees and carry, currency risk and reliance on the manager's own valuations. In return they get a floating-rate, senior, contractual income stream with covenants and a manager that can work out problems. Both sides of the evidence:

4. The non-investment side: size, organisation and wiring

How it is organised. The SEC Form ADV lists the officers: Co-CEOs Flynn and Tognolini, CFO Kevin Sarafilovic, COO Raj Paranandi, CRO Andrew McCullagh, General Counsel David Rushford, CCO Lara Dressler [S303]. The people page filter lists titles including Chief Technology Officer and Head of ESG [S329]. Named committees: Executive, Investment, ESG Deal, ESG Steering, ESG Governance, Investments Conflicts, Risk and Compliance, Audit and Risk, Valuation, Culture [S305][S333]. Fund administration is outsourced in the UK, Luxembourg and the US, with Vistra as third-party AIFM for some Luxembourg funds [S303][S333]. [Confirmed]
