Investor relations in private markets

How investors pick managers, the fundraising market, the supplier map, what LPs value, and the regulation that shapes the job

A short history of the IR function

No dedicated source on the history of private markets IR was found, so this paragraph is the author's synthesis [Inferred]. In the 1990s and 2000s, founders and partners raised money themselves, with placement agents doing much of the legwork. As funds multiplied and investors professionalised (consultants, operational due diligence teams, ILPA standards from 2009), managers built dedicated IR teams covering fundraising, reporting and servicing. SMAs, co-investment and wealth channels widened the job into product design and client servicing. Hayfin's rename from "Business Development" to "Partner Solutions" between 2021 and 2023 [S12][S11] fits that arc.

Placement agents and the supplier map

Placement agents introduce managers to investors, shape the pitch and run the process, often for 1% to 3% of capital raised (vendor estimate, low weight) [S193]. Big names include Park Hill (PJT), Evercore Private Capital Advisory, Campbell Lutyens, Lazard Private Capital Advisory, Eaton Partners and Monument Group [S193]. Consolidation: on 30 April 2026 Lazard agreed to buy Campbell Lutyens for $575m plus up to $85m contingent [S192]. [Confirmed for terms] Hayfin's UK LLP booked £1.35m of placement fees in 2025 [S23] (read from a scanned image), so it uses agents in some channels. Credit Suisse was exclusive placement agent for SOF II in 2016, paid by Hayfin [S307].

The regulation that shapes the job

This is the highest-leverage technical area for a PSG candidate. Get the instrument names and dates exactly right.

Why each rule matters to the PSG seat

RuleWhat it changes in your work
AIFMD II (EU)DDQs on EU funds now ask about leverage limits, liquidity tools, risk retention and delegation. Closed-ended drawdown funds such as DLF V fit the default model; evergreen structures need liquidity tool disclosures [Inferred from S163, S251]. Hayfin has a Luxembourg office and uses a Luxembourg AIFM for some funds [S303].
UK AIFM reform (CP26/28)At about €40bn AUM Hayfin would sit in the proposed large tier [Inferred]. The UK is lightening and simplifying while the EU tightens loan-origination rules; a UK manager with EU funds runs both regimes.
FCA COBS 4 and anti-greenwashingEvery pitch page and case study is a communication that must be fair, clear and not misleading [S49]; sustainability claims must match the product [S52]. Hayfin trained PSG on this in 2024 [S25].
SEC Marketing RuleUS-facing material: net next to gross; single-deal case studies need the whole-portfolio context [S53]. Hayfin's PES update uses this language [S330].
ILPA templatesStandard capital account, fee reconciliation and performance layouts; new items such as subscription line costs shown separately [S258]. Likely live work in summer 2027.
SFDR and SFDR 2.0Even with a professional-AIF opt-out, LPs are likely to keep asking for SFDR-style data through "bespoke diligence, side letters, and parallel disclosure packs" (Proskauer, paraphrase) [S254].
ELTIF 2.0, UK LTAFThe wrappers for selling private credit to wealthier individuals in Europe and the UK. Hayfin has none today [S227]; know them for the "would you launch one?" question.
Say this in the interview"AIFMD II had to be transposed by 16 April 2026, and it sets rules for loan-originating funds: 300% leverage cap for closed-ended funds, 5% risk retention, no originate-to-distribute. Closed-ended drawdown funds like DLF V fit that model well. In the UK the FCA is going the other way on burden, with CP26/28 proposing a tiered regime; the consultation closes on 22 October."
Don't say this"The UK has to implement AIFMD II." It does not: the UK left the EU, so AIFMD II binds EU managers and EU funds, while the UK runs its own reform. Hayfin is affected by both because it has a UK manager and EU fund vehicles [Inferred].
