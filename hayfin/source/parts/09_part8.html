Part 8

Forming your own view

Eight debates an interviewer could open, each with both sides, the evidence, and a position you can defend

Interviewers do not want your opinion to match theirs. They want to see you hold a view, show the evidence on both sides, and change your mind for a good reason. Use this method for any debate:

Eight debates, both sides

DebateForAgainstA position you can defend
1. Is private credit a bubble?Fitch default rate 6.3% (Aug 2026) [S245]; 2026 BDC gating [S184]; Dimon's "cockroach" [S157]; marks are model-based [S150]Cash defaults in senior loans ~2.5% [S155]; ECB: "unlikely... a source of systemic financial instability at present" [S177]; 47% of Fitch defaults are PIK or deferrals, only 4% bankruptcies [S245]"Not a bubble in the system; a dispersion story. Losses will be manager-specific, so workout skill and honest reporting decide winners. I would change my mind if bank exposures grew fast or cash defaults rose above about 5%."
2. Should Hayfin launch a wealth product (ELTIF or LTAF)?Fastest-growing pool: evergreen private credit ~$400bn by end-2025 [S152]; Ares raised $1.0bn into its EU evergreen fund in one quarter [S136]; ELTIF 2.0 made access easier [S164]Redemption risk on illiquid loans [S184]; brand built on "no retail capital" [S227]; distributors, daily data, Consumer Duty costs [S203]; ICG says LPs "do not want their deployment cycle to be governed by the ebbs and flows of wealth capital" [S134]"Not now. The 2026 gating made 'locked-up and institutional' a selling point. If Hayfin ever did it, I would start with a closed-ended or semi-liquid feeder with strict gates, sold through private banks, not mass retail."
3. Is a €15bn fund too big?Scale lets Hayfin underwrite whole deals (Hyve [S233]); €7.1bn deployed in 12 months [S71]; over 50% deployed at close [S71]Deal volumes down 29% [S240]; "so much money chasing for so few deals" [S240]; 100 investment professionals in 2025 [S305]"It depends on pacing and discipline, which LPs can track: deployment speed, leverage, spreads and loss rates. Hayfin's own test is that scale should follow repeatability [S222]. I would watch whether leverage creeps up in DLF V versus DLF IV."
4. Multi-strategy or specialist?"8%+ and 15%+ lender" deal flow [S307]; shared workout team; Europe's shallow markets force breadth [S324]Conflicts between funds [S305]; complexity; harder to explain; ICG says most clients use one strategy (reported [S134])"In Europe, multi-strategy is right because no single niche is deep enough. The price is conflicts, which Hayfin manages with written rules. For PSG that is a cross-sell opportunity and a disclosure duty."
5. Will AI replace IR analysts?A DDQ can take 40-60 hours [S190]; new AI DDQ tools launched in 2026 [S190][S191]; 86% of LPs use GenAI [S189]Errors are costly and regulated (COBS 4 [S49]); confidentiality; relationships and judgement are human; consultants check consistency"AI will take the first draft, not the sign-off. The analyst's job moves from writing answers to curating the approved library and checking every fact. That makes accuracy more valuable, not less."
6. Are consultants good for managers?One consultant rating can open many pensions; DLF V had the most consultant involvement ever [S222]Fee pressure, standardised questions, slower processes; consultant "prerequisite" makes them gatekeepers [S202]"Good for a scaled manager with a clean process. Hayfin has hired ex-consultants into PSG [S1], which tells me it has chosen to work with them rather than around them."
7. Is there really a European premium?Hayfin: lower leverage, tighter documents, smaller lender groups in Europe [S95]; banks still dominate European lending [S188]US managers entering Europe [S186]; bank partnerships (Citi-HPS) [S255]; spreads compress as capital floods in [S240]"There is a premium today, mainly from fragmentation, but it will narrow as US capital arrives. The durable advantage is local origination and workout skill, not the premium itself."
8. Independent or owned by a big group?Management owns the majority [S301]; alignment; no parent's agenda; Tognolini links ownership to long-term leaders [S117]Owned peers get distribution and balance sheet (Arcmont via Nuveen/TIAA [S131]); three ownership changes in eight years [S315][S74]"Hayfin has a hybrid: management control plus strategic minority holders who are also allocators. I would want to know how that affects distribution in Korea and the Gulf."
