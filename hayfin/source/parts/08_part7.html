What is happening right now

Twelve stories from April to October 2026, each with why it matters to Hayfin, to PSG, and what you could say

Date discipline: every story carries its date. Anything older than six months (before 8 April 2026) is marked CONTEXT. Paywalled articles were not read beyond their headline and are marked so in the source table.

1 · Hayfin closes Direct Lending Fund V above €15bn2 Jul 2026

Headline and outlet: "Hayfin announces record close of Direct Lending Fund V in excess of €15bn", Hayfin press release [S221]; Alternative Credit Investor same day [S236]; follow-up by the PSG co-heads, 22 Sep 2026 [S222].

In plain English: Hayfin raised over €15bn from large investors to lend directly to mid-sized companies. That is more than double the previous fund (over €6bn, August 2023). Over 50% was already invested across 35+ companies at close [S221]. About $600m came through a rated note feeder for insurers [S237]. A 9fin headline put the target at €10bn [S238] (body not read).

Why it matters to Hayfin: it doubles the flagship and shows investors trusted the firm in a rough year. Why it matters to PSG: it is PSG's output. The September piece says over half the capital came from EMEA including 10% from the Middle East; consultant involvement was "greater than in any prior Hayfin fundraise"; and scrutiny focused on "PIK, technology exposure and loss rates" [S222].

Say or ask: "DLF V drew more capital from outside Europe and more through consultants than any earlier fund. How has that changed what PSG produces, for example consultant data templates or ILPA-format reporting?"

2 · Hayfin opens a Seoul investor relations office1 Oct 2026

Outlet: Hayfin release [S223]; Korea Herald [S239]. In plain English: not a lending team. A sales and client-service office for Korean pension funds and insurers, at Three IFC, Yeouido, led by Sijin (Chris) Choi, ex-UBS Asset Management. Hayfin manages nearly €1bn for Korean investors and has since 2016 [S239].

Why it matters: Samsung Life became a shareholder in 2025 [S75]; Korea also matters to the Maritime strategy (over $1bn invested in Korean-built vessels or shipyards) [S239]. For PSG: a PSG office in all but name. Risa Lipsky told the Korea Herald: "The tone, the willingness or wanting to meet and discuss Hayfin's platform has definitely picked up" [S277].

Ask: "With coverage in Seoul, Tokyo, Melbourne and the US, how does London keep DDQ answers and reporting consistent across time zones and languages?"

3 · European CLO expansion; US CLO business under review18 May 2026

Outlet: Hayfin release [S225]; no independent coverage found. In plain English: a CLO buys a pool of loans and funds itself by selling bonds in slices of different risk. Hayfin raised equity for future European CLOs (€5.8bn across 14 deals) and hired Greensledge to "explore options" for its €1.4bn US CLO business. No outcome reported by 8 Oct 2026 [Gap].

Why it matters: a clear signal of "renewed strategic focus on its core European investing businesses" [S225]. For PSG: CLO investors are bond buyers with trustees and rating agencies; a review needs careful, consistent communication. Opinion you could offer: "Hayfin looks to be raising global capital for European assets rather than building US lending."

4 · Michael Marsh joins as Head of Investments7 May 2026

Outlet: Hayfin release [S226]; Alternative Credit Investor [S280]. A new role with "day-to-day leadership of Hayfin's investing businesses". Marsh spent almost two decades at Goldman Sachs, latterly Head of Capital Solutions EMEA, partner from 2014 [S226].

For PSG: investors ask about key people and succession in every DDQ (ILPA DDQ 2.0 has a succession section [S299]). A new investment leader is a standard DDQ and LP-letter update. Ask: "When a senior investment hire arrives mid-cycle, how does PSG communicate it to LPs and update key-person answers?"

5 · Deal flow and an awardMay to Sep 2026

CloserStill Media (8 May) and Hyve (3 Jun, fully underwritten for Hellman & Friedman), both B2B events and repeat borrowers [S220][S233]; Condis, €305m sole-lender unitranche in Spain, where Hayfin has invested over €2.5bn [S231]; Serwent, Nordic sole lender [S232]; Mergermarket "Private Credit Lender of the Year" at the European PE Awards, 30 Sep, with a stated target deal size of €100m to €400m [S224]. Hayfin also claims a Debtwire fund award that could not be independently confirmed [S224].

For PSG: case-study material. Note the award release carries a disclaimer that awards are not indicative of performance [S224]: typical compliance language an intern must keep in. Ask: "Hyve and CloserStill are both repeat borrowers in one sector. How does PSG present sector concentration when the firm has real conviction in a niche?"

6 · The US semi-liquid fund redemption waveQ1 to Q3 2026

Outlets: Advisor.ca compilation, 14 Apr 2026 [S184]; Bloomberg via Bloomberg Law, 4 Jun 2026 (headline only) [S243]; PitchBook News, 30 Sep 2026 [S244]. In plain English: non-traded BDCs are US lending funds sold to wealthy individuals that normally let up to 5% of the fund leave each quarter. In 2026 far more asked to leave, so funds paid only part ("gated"). Goldman Sachs Private Credit Corp's Q3 requests fell to 2%; PitchBook says requests "have begun to fall" but remain above 5% at many funds [S244].

Why it matters to Hayfin: it turned the stress into a selling point: strains in US semi-liquid funds "accelerated LP demand for conservative fund structures" [S221]; its private credit uses "fully locked-up institutional drawdown funds, with no retail capital" [S227]. Note Hayfin also offers "institutional evergreen vehicles" [S221]: know the difference between institutional evergreen and retail semi-liquid.

Opinion you could offer: "The gating showed the product worked as designed but the explanation did not. Liquidity terms should be explained so clearly that no investor is surprised. That is an IR job, not just a legal one."

7 · AI and software loans (theme of the year)CONTEXT: BIS, 16 Mar 2026

Outlet: BIS Quarterly Review, Box B [S242]. Loans to software firms grew from about $8bn in 2015 to over $500bn by end-2025, about 19% of direct loans; software stocks fell almost 30% from October 2025 to February 2026 [S242]. Hayfin: software under 6% of DL, under 5% in the latest vintage; software is 20-25% of US private credit activity [S227]. Investor Day themes included "views on AI disruption across our own portfolios" [S228].

Ask: "Fitch's August data shows software defaults at only 0.6% [S245]. Is AI a credit risk today, or a valuation and refinancing risk for later? How does PSG explain that nuance?"

8 · Fitch's US private credit default rate hits 6.3%14 Sep 2026

Outlet: Benzinga relay of Fitch [S245]; April record of 6% in a Bloomberg headline [S234]. 47% of default events were interest deferrals or PIK substitutions, 41% distressed maturity extensions, only 4% bankruptcies. Borrowers under $25m EBITDA defaulted at 12.0% [S245]. [Reported, single relay]

Why it matters: Hayfin's "A Tale of Three PIKs" (28 Jul 2026) separates good, bad and "fake good" PIK: "A PIK feature's origin tells you how it started; it says nothing about whether the position stays covered" [S229]. Ask: "Does Hayfin distinguish structured PIK from amendment PIK in quarterly reports, as the Three PIKs piece suggests investors should?"

9 · Valuations under the microscope24 Jun and 29 Sep 2026

Outlets: Alternative Credit Investor [S249][S250]. The SEC chief accountant and the Director of Investment Management issued a joint staff statement on 28 Sep 2026 calling private credit an area requiring "particular care"; private credit in US registered funds rose from $170bn (Dec 2020) to $270bn (Dec 2025) [S250]. MSCI data (May 2026) showed over 10% of private credit fund loans written down by half or more [S249]. The Bank of England's private markets stress scenario reports early 2027 [S175]. Note: ACI dated the FCA valuation review to March 2026; the FCA page shows 5 March 2025 [S166].

For PSG: valuation policy is a core DDQ section and quarterly reports carry the marks. Ask: "Are LPs asking for more third-party valuation support, and has it changed Hayfin's quarterly reporting?"

10 · Bank of England and IMF warnings14 Apr and 7 Jul 2026

IMF April 2026 GFSR (via Reuters): private credit about $3.5tn; "signs of more borrower defaults could cascade into broader concerns about corporate credit overall", but likely "contained systemic impact" [S247]. BoE July 2026 FSR: vulnerabilities "including private credit, remain" and some "have become more pronounced" [S248]. Backdrop: a 2026 Middle East conflict involving Iran and a "fragile ceasefire reached in April" (Hayfin's description) [S227].

For PSG: pension funds and insurers read these reports and bring the questions to managers; good IR pre-empts them in quarterly letters [Inferred].

11 · Citi and HPS launch a €15bn European programme18 May 2026

Outlet: Alternative Credit Investor [S255]. Citi sources deals, HPS (BlackRock) provides capital, over an initial five years, starting in Europe and the UK. CONTEXT: Apollo and BNP Paribas were reported "nearing" a European tie-up in February 2026 [S256].

Opinion: "Bank partnerships are a threat at the large end, but they prove banks are stepping back from holding loans, which grows the market Hayfin's local teams compete in."

12 · Fundraising concentrates in fewer, bigger managers12 Aug and 15 Sep 2026

With Intelligence: H1 2026 private credit final closes $190bn, up 53%; funds over $5bn took 59%; Europe $44bn, 23% share [S241]. ACI citing PitchBook: deal volumes 29% below 2025 [S240]. Hayfin's Marc Chowrimootoo cited "the stability of our platform and the resulting ability to be patient investors"; Federated Hermes: "There's so much money chasing for so few deals" [S240].

Ask: "With deal volumes down and fund sizes up, which metrics best show LPs that DLF V is not deploying too fast: pacing, leverage, spreads or loss rates?" Hayfin's own line: "Scale should be an outcome of repeatability, rather than an objective in itself" [S222].

The 60-second "what have you been reading?" answer

Say this in the interview"The story I find most interesting is how the US retail redemption wave helped Hayfin's fundraising rather than hurt it. Non-traded BDCs like BCRED had to cap withdrawals at 5% when around 10% of investors wanted out. Hayfin closed DLF V in July at over €15bn, more than double the last fund, with only locked-up institutional money. What struck me in your September piece was that the strategy stayed the same but the wrappers multiplied: levered and unlevered funds, SMAs, evergreen vehicles and a rated feeder for insurers. That is a Partner Solutions story as much as an investment one. And the Seoul office last week says the firm is still investing in investor coverage."
