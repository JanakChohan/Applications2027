The question bank: 48 questions with scaffolds

Scaffolds, not scripts. Each card gives what is really being tested, a structure, facts to weave in and the trap. Practise aloud; never memorise word for word.

A. Motivation

1. Why investor relations rather than an investing seat?
Tested: Genuine interest in the client side; retention risk. Structure: What IR is to you (explaining, evidencing, serving) → a moment you enjoyed that kind of work → why it matters at a credit manager specifically.
Facts to weave in: PSG sits on the ESG Deal Committee [S305]; DLF V was raised through five wrappers [S222]; Hayfin hired ex-consultants and allocators into PSG [S1]. Trap: Calling IR a stepping stone to deals; not knowing the daily work (DDQs, RFPs, reporting).
2. Why private credit?
Tested: Understanding of why the asset class exists. Structure: Bank retreat → companies need lenders → investors want contractual floating-rate income → private credit fills the gap; one risk you take seriously.
Facts to weave in: Flynn's founding thesis on bank capital rules [S105]; Europe 35.9% of 2025 private debt capital [S152]. Trap: A generic 'fast-growing' answer; claiming it is low risk.
3. Why Hayfin?
Tested: Firm-specific research expressed as mechanism. Structure: Use the 30-second answer in Part 12: one platform with several doors → why that matters to an LP → why PSG is where that is explained.
Facts to weave in: 8%+ and 15%+ lender [S307]; DLF V >€15bn [S71]; conflict rules [S305]. Trap: Adjectives ('leading', 'prestigious'); size alone; anything transplantable to Ares or ICG.
4. Walk me through your CV.
Tested: Concise story; link from past work to IR skills. Structure: 90 seconds: who you are → two experiences, each with one output and one skill (accuracy, client materials, data) → why this role now.
Facts to weave in: JD: 'proactive', 'research... summarise... present', Excel and PowerPoint. Trap: Over two minutes; describing what the team did, not what you did.
5. What did you do in your last internship that is relevant here?
Tested: Transferable output. Structure: Situation → the thing you produced (deck, model, data pack) → how you checked it → what happened.
Facts to weave in: Map to JD duties: pitch materials, data, proofreading. Trap: No concrete output; no error you caught.
6. Where do you see yourself in five years?
Tested: Retention and realism. Structure: Building expertise in investor work → a product or coverage track → specific skill you want (e.g. structuring vehicles for insurers).
Facts to weave in: Two PSG tracks: product strategy and coverage [S1]; Coriani's path from intern to MD [S288]. Trap: 'Running my own fund'; 'moving to the deal team'.

B. Firm

7. What do you know about Hayfin?
Tested: Accuracy and currency of facts. Structure: Founded 2009 → strategies → size and DLF V → ownership → one thing that interests you.
Facts to weave in: ~€40bn AUM Q2 2026 [S224]; MBO completed Feb 2025 [S73]; Mubadala, AXA IM Prime, Samsung Life [S74][S75]. Trap: Saying €30bn or ten offices (stale).
8. What has Hayfin announced recently?
Tested: News flow; round one in a 2025 IR process covered recent deals and news [S261]. Structure: Pick three: one fund, one office or hire, one deal; give a 'so what' for each.
Facts to weave in: DLF V [S71]; Seoul office [S223]; Condis €305m [S231]; Marsh hire [S226]. Trap: Listing headlines without implication.
9. What does the Partner Solutions team do?
Tested: Whether you read the JD and beyond. Structure: Raise → diligence → service → content; who it serves; where you would sit.
Facts to weave in: Coverage co-heads, product strategy, regions [S1]; quarterly report contents [S305]. Trap: Describing it as sales only; forgetting reporting and SMAs.
10. Which strategy would you pitch to a Korean insurer, and in what wrapper?
Tested: Link between investor type and product. Structure: Investor's need (long-dated, capital-efficient, rated) → strategy (senior DL) → wrapper (rated note feeder or SMA) → one risk to disclose (currency).
Facts to weave in: $600m rated feeder [S237]; ~€1bn for Korean clients [S239]; Samsung Life shareholder [S75]. Trap: Ignoring regulatory capital and currency.
11. What did you think of our recent insight pieces?
Tested: Depth of preparation. Structure: Name one, summarise in two sentences, add one question it raised for you.
Facts to weave in: 'What a €15bn Fundraise Tells Us' [S222]; 'A Tale of Three PIKs' [S229]; 'The Complexity Premium' [S262]. Trap: Claiming to have read without being able to summarise.
12. What makes Hayfin's culture different?
Tested: Values fit, using Hayfin's own words. Structure: One quote → one example from your life that matches it.
Facts to weave in: 'rewarded to collaborate, rather than outperform peers' [S305]; 'no superstar culture... midfield general' [S281]; five values in the JD pack. Trap: Generic 'collaborative and entrepreneurial'.
13. Who are our competitors and how are we different?
Tested: Competitive awareness (a named 2026 intern task [S265]). Structure: Name three peers → one axis where Hayfin differs from each → where Hayfin does not lead.
Facts to weave in: Ares ACE VI €17.1bn [S135]; ICG SDP 5 [S132]; Arcmont (Nuveen) [S131]; independence and breadth. Trap: Claiming Hayfin is the biggest or has the best returns.

C. Industry and credit technicals

14. What is direct lending, and how is it different from syndicated loans and high-yield bonds?
Tested: Product basics. Structure: Who lends → how terms are set → whether it trades → rate type → protections.
Facts to weave in: Floating, senior secured, bilateral or small club; 40% of Hayfin DL sole-lender [S95]. Trap: Mixing fixed and floating; forgetting illiquidity.
15. Why would an investor choose private credit over high yield?
Tested: Trade-offs. Structure: Extra spread for illiquidity and complexity → floating rate → covenants and control → but: illiquid, model marks, lock-up.
Facts to weave in: PitchBook: the key metric is 'excess spread' over public fixed income [S151]. Trap: Calling it lower risk outright.
16. What makes direct lending attractive now, and what are the risks?
Tested: Balance. Structure: Two tailwinds, two risks, one view.
Facts to weave in: Bank retreat [S221]; Europe in favour [S152]; PIK creep [S245]; deal volumes down 29% [S240]. Trap: One-sided bull case.
17. Explain PIK. When is it good or bad?
Tested: Nuance on the 2026 hot topic. Structure: Definition → planned PIK at entry vs PIK forced by stress → how an LP should see it.
Facts to weave in: 'Three PIKs': good, bad, fake good [S229]; Lincoln 'shadow default' 6.2% [S162]. Trap: 'PIK is always a red flag.'
18. What is unitranche?
Tested: Structure knowledge. Structure: One loan replacing senior plus junior → one price, one lender group → speed and certainty for the sponsor.
Facts to weave in: Condis €305m sole-lender unitranche [S231]. Trap: Confusing it with mezzanine.
19. Walk me through an LBO from the lender's side.
Tested: Credit lens (asked in a 2025 IR process [S261]). Structure: Sponsor buys company with equity plus debt → lender sizes debt on EBITDA and cash flow → covenants → repaid from cash flow or refinancing.
Facts to weave in: Leverage = debt / EBITDA; Portfolio Breakdown reports LTM EBITDA, EV, leverage [S305]. Trap: Answering only from the equity side.
20. How do rate moves affect a floating-rate lender?
Tested: Rate sensitivity both ways. Structure: Higher rates → more income → but weaker interest coverage for borrowers → more PIK and amendments; cuts do the reverse.
Facts to weave in: PitchBook expects 'cooler returns' as rates fall [S152]. Trap: Forgetting borrower stress.
21. What is a covenant, and what is covenant-lite?
Tested: Lender protections. Structure: A promise (e.g. leverage below X) → early warning → seat at the table before default; cov-lite removes maintenance tests.
Facts to weave in: Hayfin: Europe has 'tighter documentation' than the US [S95]. Trap: Not linking covenants to workout capability.
22. Why do private credit default rates differ so much?
Tested: Data literacy. Structure: Definitions: payment default vs PIK switches vs covenant breaches → numbers → what LPs now ask for.
Facts to weave in: Proskauer 2.51% [S155]; Fitch 6.3% [S245]; Lincoln 6.2% shadow [S162]. Trap: Quoting one number as 'the' default rate.

D. Investor relations specifics

23. What is a DDQ and what goes in it?
Tested: Core intern task. Structure: Purpose → standard templates → main sections → how a team answers efficiently.
Facts to weave in: ILPA DDQ 2.0: 20 topics incl. succession, credit facilities, valuation, ESG, DEI, cyber [S299]; AIMA credit modules [S48]. Trap: Vague answer; not mentioning consistency and sign-off.
24. How is an RFP different from a DDQ?
Tested: Process understanding. Structure: RFP = competitive search for a mandate with fixed questions and deadline; DDQ = diligence on a chosen manager; both reuse the library.
Facts to weave in: eVestment and consultant databases [S55]. Trap: Treating them as the same.
25. Gross vs net IRR: what is the difference and why does it matter?
Tested: Fees and honesty in marketing. Structure: Gross before fees and carry → net after → subscription lines lift IRR → rules require net alongside gross.
Facts to weave in: SEC Marketing Rule FAQ, 19 Mar 2025 [S53]; SOCF I 13.1% gross vs 8.1% net [S307]. Trap: Forgetting fund leverage and subscription lines.
26. Explain IRR, MOIC, DPI and TVPI.
Tested: Fund metrics fluency. Structure: Define each in one line → which is realised vs unrealised → which LPs watch in a slow-exit market (DPI).
Facts to weave in: ILPA Performance Template shows IRR and TVPI with and without subscription lines [S45]. Trap: Mixing realised and unrealised.
27. How do LPs choose managers?
Tested: Allocator thinking. Structure: Strategy fit → team and track record → process and controls → alignment and terms → operations → consultant view.
Facts to weave in: Intralinks: strategy 51%, sector knowledge 30%, co-invest 22%, fees 14% [S189]. Trap: 'Best returns' only.
28. Who are Hayfin's investors and how do their needs differ?
Tested: Client segmentation. Structure: Pensions (liabilities, pacing) → insurers (capital, ratings) → SWFs (scale, co-invest) → consultants (process).
Facts to weave in: Pension 51.1%, insurance 19.5%, SWF 12.1%; Australia 22.8% [S305]. Trap: Forgetting insurers and SWFs.
29. What is an SMA and why would an investor want one?
Tested: JD vocabulary. Structure: Single-investor vehicle → custom terms, control, fees, reporting → needs size.
Facts to weave in: JD's 'Special Memorandum Accounts' means separately managed accounts [S25]. Trap: Not knowing the term, or repeating the JD's error.
30. What role do investment consultants play?
Tested: Gatekeeper awareness. Structure: Advise many pension funds → rate managers → RFPs and databases → one rating can unlock many commitments.
Facts to weave in: DLF V consultant involvement highest ever [S222]; Bringardner ex-BlackRock consultant relations [S4]. Trap: Ignoring them.
31. What goes into a quarterly LP report?
Tested: Reporting basics. Structure: Portfolio summary and track record → company-level data → letter → capital account → audited accounts annually.
Facts to weave in: Hayfin's list: Summary of Investments, Portfolio Breakdown, CEO Letter, audited financials within 90 days [S305]. Trap: Forgetting fees and expenses, watchlist, ESG.
32. How would you use AI tools to make the team more efficient?
Tested: JD skill, with judgement. Structure: One use case → guardrails (approved sources, no confidential data in public tools, human check) → how you would measure the gain.
Facts to weave in: Chicago posting: AI 'used with careful attention to accuracy' [S19]; DDQ 40-60 hours [S190]. Trap: AI drafting client documents unchecked.

E. Market awareness

33. Tell me about a recent private credit headline and what it means for LPs.
Tested: Commercial awareness with implication. Structure: Fact → why it happened → what it means for an investor → link to Hayfin.
Facts to weave in: Redemption wave [S184][S243]; Fitch 6.3% [S245]; SEC valuation statement [S250]. Trap: No 'so what'.
34. Is private credit a bubble?
Tested: Balance and composure. Structure: Use the Part 8 debate: claim, evidence, objection, what would change your mind.
Facts to weave in: ECB [S177] vs Dimon [S157]; Coller barometer [S183]. Trap: Either extreme.
35. Why are US and Asian investors buying European direct lending?
Tested: Link to PSG's own thesis. Structure: Diversification → fragmentation premium → bank retreat → closed-end structures after 2026 gating.
Facts to weave in: Over half of DLF V from EMEA; growth from US and APAC [S222]; Pla on diversification [S277]. Trap: Not mentioning diversification or currency.
36. How are insurers changing private credit?
Tested: Wrapper and capital awareness. Structure: Long liabilities → need for yield with capital efficiency → rated feeders, SMAs, ABF.
Facts to weave in: $600m rated note feeder [S237]; Solvency II reporting in Finance [S333]. Trap: No idea of capital treatment.
37. What is happening in fundraising across private markets?
Tested: Market structure. Structure: More money, fewer funds, longer closes, concentration in experienced managers.
Facts to weave in: Preqin: 80 funds, top 10 took 64%, 39 months [S153]; PitchBook 93% to fund 4+ [S152]. Trap: Unsourced numbers.

F. Behavioural and competency

38. Tell me about a time you produced work that needed very high attention to detail.
Tested: JD: 'see things others don't see'. Structure: STAR, with the specific error you caught and the check that caught it.
Facts to weave in: Proofreading quarterly reports [S305]. Trap: No concrete error.
39. Tell me about juggling several deadlines.
Tested: Prioritisation. Structure: The deadlines → how you ranked them (client impact, hard dates, dependencies) → what you told people → result.
Facts to weave in: Quarter-end reporting plus live DDQs. Trap: No logic for the order.
40. Tell me about a setback and how you persisted.
Tested: Persistence, which Flynn names [S281]. Structure: Setback → what you changed → second attempt → what you still do differently.
Facts to weave in: 'persistence is an undervalued but incredibly important trait' [S281]. Trap: A trivial example.
41. Tell me about making a team better rather than shining alone.
Tested: 'Midfield general' [S281]. Structure: A team result → your enabling role (organising, checking, unblocking) → credit to others.
Facts to weave in: 'rewarded to collaborate, rather than outperform peers' [S305]. Trap: A hero story.
42. Tell me about explaining something complex to a non-expert.
Tested: Client communication; 'gravitas'. Structure: The concept → your analogy or picture → how you checked understanding.
Facts to weave in: Explaining PIK or gross vs net to a trustee board. Trap: Jargon.
43. Tell me about a mistake you made.
Tested: Honesty; Hayfin values say 'We do not hide our mistakes'. Structure: Own it in one sentence → fix → what you changed so it cannot recur.
Facts to weave in: Value 'Excellence' in the JD pack. Trap: A fake weakness.

G. Case and written exercises

44. Here is fund performance data in Excel: calculate returns and comment.
Tested: Excel speed, accuracy, commentary (reported 2025 IR case [S261]). Structure: Check data → compute IRR (XIRR), MOIC, DPI, TVPI → one chart → three bullet 'so whats' → caveats.
Facts to weave in: Gross vs net; subscription line effect; currency. Trap: Formatting errors; no conclusion.
45. Pitch this fund to a UK pension fund in five minutes.
Tested: Structure, investor-first framing. Structure: Their need → the strategy → why this manager (evidence) → risks and how managed → terms and next step.
Facts to weave in: Pension 51% of Hayfin AUM [S305]; downside, PIK, loss-rate questions [S222]. Trap: Listing features without linking to the pension's liabilities.
46. Draft a DDQ answer: describe your valuation policy.
Tested: Precision and tone. Structure: Who values, how often, independent input, committee, escalation; mark what you would confirm with Finance and Compliance.
Facts to weave in: Valuation Committee; Finance reviews NAV before investors [S333]; FCA review good practice [S166]. Trap: Overclaiming; inventing facts.
47. Write a one-page case study from this deal summary.
Tested: JD task with compliance instincts. Structure: Situation → why Hayfin won → structure and protections → outcome → selection basis and disclaimers.
Facts to weave in: COBS 4.2 [S49]; SEC FAQ [S53]; anti-greenwashing [S52]. Trap: Disclosing confidential terms; no selection basis.
48. Group exercise: assess this borrower as a credit opportunity.
Tested: Teamwork and credit basics (2024 dental case [S260]). Structure: Agree a structure and timekeeper → business quality → cash flow and leverage → downside and recovery → decision.
Facts to weave in: Leverage, interest cover, covenants, sponsor support. Trap: Dominating; ignoring downside.
