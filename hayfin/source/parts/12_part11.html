The scenario playbook

A frame-holding method, the priority order, eight ways interviewers break a frame, 42 scenario cards, the composure set, and the CV grill

No insider intelligence was supplied for this pack, so it does not assume Hayfin's interviewers try to break your frame. The 2025 IR case reported an Excel task and a fund presentation [S261], and assessment centres often add pressure questions. The method below works for a friendly interviewer and a hostile one.

The one sentence that holds every frame"I'll always be fast, but never at the cost of accuracy, fairness between investors, or a compliance sign-off."

Eight ways interviewers break a frame, and the counter to each

Staying calm physically

  • Breathe out longer than in (count four in, six out) before you answer a hard question. It slows the heart rate.
  • Feet flat, hands still, camera at eye level on video. Look at the lens when you make your main point.
  • Water within reach. Sipping buys three seconds without looking evasive.
  • Write the four words STEADY · SORT · ACT · CLOSE on a sticky note by the screen.
  • Say "Good question" only if it is; otherwise "Let me think about that for a second" is stronger.

The 42 scenario cards

R1 · Pitch materials, DDQ responses, investor presentations

Stale numberEasy

Setup: You notice the pitch book shows last quarter's AUM, and the coverage MD is sending it this afternoon.

Trap: Fixing it silently, or saying nothing to avoid looking pushy.

Say: "I've spotted the AUM on page 3 is Q1's; the Q2 figure is X from the investor report. Shall I update it and flag the change in the version log before it goes?"

Why it works: Accuracy and consistency across documents is the product; one wrong number in a deck an LP compares with a DDQ costs trust [S20][S59].

Twist: "There's no time, just send it." Hold: "Understood. It's a two-minute fix; can I make it and send you the updated PDF in five minutes?"

DDQ answer you cannot sourceMedium

Setup: A DDQ asks for DLF V's loss rate since inception. The library answer is from 2023 and you cannot find a newer approved figure.

Trap: Inventing or extrapolating a number to finish on time.

Say: "I've drafted everything except Q42 on loss rates. The approved figure is from 2023. Who should confirm the current number: Portfolio Management or Finance?"

Why it works: Hayfin says its "track record and our loss rate are used by our investors to assess our success" [S305]; an unapproved figure is a regulatory and reputational risk [S49].

Twist: "Just use the old one with today's date." Hold: "I'd rather mark it 'as of 2023, update to follow' than put a wrong date on it. That keeps us accurate and still meets the deadline."

Two decks, two storiesHard

Setup: Preparing an investor presentation, you find the software exposure figure differs between the pitch book (under 6%) and a draft case study (8%).

Trap: Picking the better number.

Say: "These two figures disagree. They may use different bases, for example the whole DL book versus one fund. Before either goes out, can we confirm the definition and source with the deal team?"

Why it works: Hayfin publishes "less than 6%" for the DL book and under 5% for the latest vintage [S227]; definitions matter as much as numbers.

Twist: "The investor won't notice." Hold: "They might compare it with the DDQ, and the consultant certainly will. It's quicker to fix now than to explain later."

R2 · Ad hoc client requests

Urgent request on a FridayEasy

Setup: An Australian SMA client asks at 4pm Friday for UK exposure by sector, needed Monday.

Trap: Promising a time you cannot meet, or waiting until Monday to acknowledge.

Say: "I'll acknowledge today, confirm exactly what they need (which date, which measure), build it from the portfolio file, and get a senior check on Monday morning."

Why it works: Fast acknowledgement plus checked output is how IR is judged [S20]; Australia is 22.8% of AUM [S305].

Twist: "Can you just send it tonight unchecked?" Hold: "I can have it ready tonight and reviewed first thing Monday, before their deadline."

Request that needs another investor's dataMedium

Setup: A pension asks how its terms compare with other LPs in the fund.

Trap: Sharing or hinting at another investor's terms.

Say: "I can't share other investors' terms. I'll ask the coverage MD how we usually answer this, for example by pointing to the MFN process in their side letter."

Why it works: Investor confidentiality is a wall around PSG (Figure 1.5) [Inferred]; fair treatment of every LP comes before speed.

Twist: "They're our biggest client." Hold: "That's exactly why we should answer it the same way we would for anyone."

A request that looks like it wants inside informationHard

Setup: A hedge fund that is also an LP asks for the latest numbers on a named borrower whose bonds trade publicly.

Trap: Sending borrower data because they are an LP.

Say: "Borrower information may be confidential or non-public, and this investor may trade the bonds. I'd pass this straight to Compliance before replying."

Why it works: MNPI rules apply [S301]; Hayfin runs public high-yield as well as private credit [S305].

Twist: "It's in the quarterly report anyway." Hold: "Then Compliance will clear it quickly. I'd still rather they decide."

R3 · Case studies

Only winnersEasy

Setup: You are asked to pick three case studies for a pitch. The best returns are all realised deals in one sector.

Trap: Choosing the three best performers.

Say: "I'd propose a selection basis first, for example size, recency and a mix of sectors and outcomes, and note it in the footnote."

Why it works: Cherry-picking misleads; COBS 4.2 fair, clear and not misleading [S49]; SEC rules require context for extracted performance [S53].

Twist: "Marketing wants the best ones." Hold: "We can still lead with strong deals; the basis just protects us if a consultant asks."

Gross onlyMedium

Setup: A draft case study shows only gross IRR because net is not available at deal level.

Trap: Leaving it as is because everyone does it.

Say: "For US-facing material, gross for one deal needs the whole portfolio's gross and net shown with at least equal prominence. Should I add the fund-level figures and the standard disclaimer?"

Why it works: SEC Marketing Rule FAQ, 19 March 2025 [S53]; Hayfin's PES update already uses Rule 206(4)-1 language [S330].

Twist: "This one is only for Europe." Hold: "Then COBS 4.6 best practice still asks for the effect of fees. Compliance can tell us which version applies."

An ESG claim you cannot evidenceHard

Setup: A case study calls a borrower 'net zero aligned'; the ESG team has no data to support it.

Trap: Keeping the phrase because it reads well.

Say: "I'd change it to what we can evidence, or remove it, and check with the ESG team. The anti-greenwashing rule needs the claim to match the facts."

Why it works: FCA ESG 4.3.1R [S52]; Hayfin trained PSG on it in 2024 [S25].

Twist: "The sponsor said so." Hold: "Then we can quote the sponsor's statement with attribution, if Compliance is happy."

R4 · Quarterly LP reporting and proofreading

A total that does not addEasy

Setup: Proofreading the Portfolio Breakdown, the sector percentages sum to 101.3%.

Trap: Assuming it is rounding and moving on.

Say: "Probably rounding, but 1.3 points is more than rounding usually explains. I'll check the source data and either fix it or add a rounding note."

Why it works: Reports carry a "line-by-line track record" [S305]; consultants check totals.

Twist: "It's always been like that." Hold: "Then it's worth one check; if it's rounding, a footnote fixes it for good."

Late data from a deal teamMedium

Setup: Quarter-end. One deal team has not sent its company commentary and the report is due tomorrow.

Trap: Writing commentary yourself from old notes.

Say: "I'll chase with a specific ask and deadline, offer a draft from last quarter's text for them to edit, and tell my manager the risk now, not tomorrow."

Why it works: IR and investment teams produce the reports jointly [S305]; escalate early, not late.

Twist: "Just write something." Hold: "I can draft it, but it has to be approved by the deal team before it goes out."

An error found after sendingHard

Setup: After the quarterly report goes out you find a borrower's leverage was misstated.

Trap: Hoping nobody notices.

Say: "I'd tell my manager straight away with the correct figure and the source, so we can decide how to correct it with investors."

Why it works: Hayfin's values: "We do not hide our mistakes" (JD pack); correction is part of trust.

Twist: "It's immaterial." Hold: "That may be right; I'd still like the person who decides materiality to see it."

S1 · Research across topics, summarise, present

Open research briefEasy

Setup: A coverage MD asks: 'What are Korean insurers allocating to private credit?'

Trap: Delivering a 20-page dump.

Say: "I'll come back with one page: the headline answer, three facts with sources, what it means for us, and what I could not find."

Why it works: PSG values short, sourced notes [S19]; Seoul office context [S223].

Twist: "Can you also add Japan?" Hold: "Yes; I'll do Korea first by tomorrow and Japan by Thursday unless you'd rather swap."

Conflicting sourcesMedium

Setup: Two reputable sources give different market sizes for European private credit.

Trap: Picking one silently.

Say: "I'd show both, explain the definitions behind each, and say which I'd use and why."

Why it works: Definitions drive size (Figure 5.2) [S150][S182].

Twist: "Just give me one number." Hold: "Then I'd use X because it matches how we define our market, with a footnote."

Presenting to someone senior who disagreesHard

Setup: You present your findings and a senior person says your conclusion is wrong.

Trap: Collapsing, or arguing.

Say: "That's useful. Which part do you see differently? If it's the data, I'll recheck it; if it's the interpretation, here's my reasoning, and I'd like to understand yours."

Why it works: Frame-holding: separate facts from judgement (Part 11 method).

Twist: "You clearly haven't understood the market." Hold: steady voice, "That may be right. What would you look at first?"

S2 · Career in IR; discuss fundraising knowledgeably

Why not investing?Easy

Setup: "Wouldn't you rather be on the deal team?"

Trap: Admitting IR is plan B.

Say: "I like the investing side, which is why I want to explain it well. What draws me to PSG is turning deal evidence into something an investor can underwrite."

Why it works: PSG sits inside the deal approval workflow [S305].

Twist: "Most interns say that." Hold: give your one concrete example of enjoying explanation work.

Explain fundraisingMedium

Setup: "How does a fundraise actually work?"

Trap: Vague talk about 'networking'.

Say: "Pre-marketing, then a first close, then subsequent closes to a final close; LPs go through consultant screens, RFPs, DDQs, IC and legal; the manager offers different wrappers for different investors."

Why it works: Allocation process (Figure 6.1); DLF V wrappers [S222].

Twist: "What's a first close for?" Hold: "It lets the fund start investing and creates momentum; later investors often pay the same fees."

Knowledge trapHard

Setup: "What's the hard cap on DLF V?"

Trap: Guessing.

Say: "I haven't seen a hard cap published. The release says in excess of €15bn including related vehicles, and 9fin reported a €10bn target."

Why it works: [S71][S238]; honesty beats a guess.

Twist: "Surely you know that." Hold: "I'd rather not guess a number you'd then repeat to an investor."

S3 · AI tools; Excel and PowerPoint

AI ideaEasy

Setup: "How would you use AI here?"

Trap: Grand claims about automation.

Say: "A first-draft DDQ answer drawn only from the approved library, with every fact checked by a person and no confidential data in public tools."

Why it works: Chicago posting: AI used "with careful attention to accuracy" [S19].

Twist: "Isn't that slower?" Hold: "The draft saves time; the check protects us. The net is still faster."

AI got it wrongMedium

Setup: An AI tool drafts an answer that cites a fund size that does not exist.

Trap: Trusting the tool because it sounds right.

Say: "I'd discard the number, find the source figure, and note the failure so the team knows where the tool is weak."

Why it works: Accuracy rules (COBS 4.2) [S49].

Twist: "Everyone uses it." Hold: "I'll keep using it; I just won't ship anything I haven't checked."

Excel under pressureHard

Setup: Live case: compute IRR and TVPI from a cash flow table in 20 minutes.

Trap: Formatting before checking signs and dates.

Say: "I'd set up dates and signed cash flows first, use XIRR, then MOIC, DPI and TVPI, sanity-check against a rough estimate, and only then chart."

Why it works: Reported 2025 IR case [S261].

Twist: "Your IRR looks high." Hold: "Let me check whether a subscription line or a sign error is driving it."

S4 · Fluent English, written and spoken

Rewrite for an LPEasy

Setup: Rewrite a jargon-heavy paragraph for a pension trustee.

Trap: Adding more jargon to sound expert.

Say: "Short sentences, one idea each, define PIK in plain words, and end with what it means for them."

Why it works: Writing standard of this pack; LP audience [S305].

Twist: "Too simple?" Hold: "Trustees decide; clarity helps them decide."

Email toneMedium

Setup: A consultant emails angrily about a late data template.

Trap: Being defensive.

Say: "Acknowledge, apologise for the delay without excuses, give a firm time, and deliver early."

Why it works: Consultants matter more than ever [S222].

Twist: "It wasn't our fault." Hold: "Possibly; the fix is the same either way."

Explain a lossHard

Setup: Explain to an LP in two minutes why a loan was restructured.

Trap: Hiding behind jargon.

Say: "What happened, what we did, where the investor stands now, and what we learned."

Why it works: Workout capability is what LPs buy [S281].

Twist: "So you lost money?" Hold: "On this loan, the recovery is X so far; across the portfolio, here's the context."

A1 · Passion for markets and private capital

Why does private capital matter?Easy

Setup: "What role does private credit play in the economy?"

Trap: A purely financial answer.

Say: "It lends to mid-sized companies banks now find costly to hold, which funds growth and jobs; Hayfin says over €59bn invested across 600+ companies since 2009."

Why it works: [S79]; bank retreat [S105].

Twist: "Isn't it just shadow banking?" Hold: "It is non-bank lending, with locked-up money rather than deposits; the risk is opacity, which is why reporting matters."

How do investors allocate?Medium

Setup: "Why would a pension commit to a seven-year illiquid fund?"

Trap: Saying 'for returns'.

Say: "Its liabilities are long, it can accept illiquidity, and it gets floating-rate contractual income with covenants, above public credit."

Why it works: Pension 51% of Hayfin AUM [S305]; excess spread [S151].

Twist: "And if rates fall?" Hold: "Income falls too, but so does borrower stress."

Contrarian pushHard

Setup: "I think private credit will blow up within two years. Convince me otherwise."

Trap: Either caving or fighting.

Say: "I wouldn't fully disagree: defaults are rising on broad measures. But cash defaults in senior loans are about 2.5%, and the ECB sees it as not systemic. The risk is dispersion, so manager choice matters."

Why it works: [S245][S155][S177].

Twist: "So you admit it's risky." Hold: "Yes. Credit is always about pricing risk, not avoiding it."

A2 · Organisation, priorities, deadlines

Three asks, one afternoonEasy

Setup: Three people ask you for work due by 5pm.

Trap: Doing them in order received.

Say: "I'd list them with deadlines and client impact, tell each person when they'll get it, and ask my manager to rank if two truly clash."

Why it works: Priority order (Figure 11.2).

Twist: "Mine is most urgent." Hold: "Then let's confirm with [manager] so the others know."

Quarter-end crunchMedium

Setup: Reporting deadline and a live DDQ in the same week.

Trap: Quietly working all night and missing one.

Say: "Flag the clash early, propose a plan, and protect the external deadline that cannot move."

Why it works: Audited accounts within 90 days; DDQ deadlines are external [S305].

Twist: "Can't you do both?" Hold: "Yes, if X can review the DDQ on Thursday rather than Wednesday."

Two senior people, one internHard

Setup: Two MDs both say their request is top priority.

Trap: Choosing the more senior person.

Say: Use the composure decision tree (Figure 11.5).

Why it works: Fair service to every internal client.

Twist: "Just do mine." Hold: "Happy to; can I let [the other MD] know so they aren't left waiting?"

A3 · Attention to detail; complete all tasks

Typo in a borrower nameEasy

Setup: A borrower name is misspelt in a report going out tomorrow.

Trap: Thinking it's too small to mention.

Say: "Fix, log, and check the same name elsewhere in the pack."

Why it works: "see things others don't see" (JD).

Twist: "Nobody reads that page." Hold: "The borrower's sponsor might."

Loose ends at internship endMedium

Setup: Your last week; two tasks are 80% done.

Trap: Leaving them undocumented.

Say: "A handover note: status, files, sources, next steps, and who now owns each."

Why it works: "able to complete all tasks" (JD).

Twist: "Don't worry about it." Hold: "It'll take me 20 minutes and saves someone two hours."

Detail versus deadlineHard

Setup: Perfecting a deck will miss the deadline by an hour.

Trap: Missing the deadline for cosmetic polish.

Say: "Send an accurate version on time; list the cosmetic fixes for the next version."

Why it works: Priority: accuracy over polish, deadline over cosmetics.

Twist: "It has to be perfect." Hold: "Numbers will be perfect; fonts can follow."

Say: "I'd first learn how the library works, then propose tagging answers by date and owner so stale ones flag themselves."

Why it works: DDQ burden [S190].

Twist: "We tried that." Hold: "What stopped it? That's what I'd want to fix."

Case-study libraryMedium

Setup: Pitch an idea in two minutes.

Trap: Vague 'use AI more'.

Say: "A case-study library linked to source data and pre-approved disclaimers, so every case study and DDQ answer pulls from one checked record."

Why it works: Part 12 close; Marketing Rule disclaimers [S330].

Idea rejectedHard

Setup: Your idea is rejected in a team meeting.

Trap: Sulking or pushing harder.

Say: "Thank them, ask what would make it worth trying, and offer a small test."

Why it works: Hayfin values: healthy debate, humbleness (JD pack).

Twist: "It's a waste of time." Hold: "Understood. Could I try it on one DDQ to see?"

A5 · Articulate; presenting with gravitas

Five-minute fund pitchEasy

Setup: Pitch DLF V to a UK pension.

Trap: Starting with Hayfin's history.

Say: "Start with their need, then the strategy, the evidence, the risks, and the next step."

Why it works: Part 9 case scaffold.

Twist: "Why not just tell me returns?" Hold: "Net returns depend on the vintage; here's what drives them."

Interrupted mid-pitchMedium

Setup: An interviewer cuts in: 'Skip that, what's the catch?'

Trap: Losing the thread.

Say: "The main risks are illiquidity, PIK creep in stressed borrowers, and currency for non-euro investors. Here's how each is managed."

Why it works: Frame method: answer, then return.

Twist: "That's it?" Hold: "Those are the three I'd put first; happy to go deeper on any."

Silence after your answerHard

Setup: You finish; the interviewer says nothing for five seconds.

Trap: Filling the silence by undermining your answer.

Say: Wait two seconds, then: "Would it help if I went deeper on any part of that?"

Why it works: Counter to the 'silence' tactic (Figure 11.3).

Twist: Still silence. Hold: smile, wait; they will move on.

The composure set: five hard scenarios with decision trees

The other three composure scenarios (draw these from memory)

C3 · A senior person asks you to skip a controlHard

Setup: "Send the case study now; Compliance can see it afterwards." Tree: Is it external? → yes → it needs sign-off first (COBS 4.2 [S49]). Can sign-off be fast? → ask Compliance for a 15-minute review, tell the senior person the time. → If they insist, escalate politely to your manager. Never send. Say: "I want to get this out today; I've asked Compliance for a quick look and will send it the moment it's cleared." Twist: "I'll take responsibility." Hold: "Thank you; I still need their sign-off before it leaves, and I'll chase it now."

C4 · A mistake you madeHard

Setup: You sent an LP a table with last quarter's figures. Tree: Has it left the building? → yes → tell your manager within the hour with the right figures and the source → they decide how to correct with the client → you log what went wrong and add a check. Say: "I made an error, here is the correct data, and here is the check I've added so it doesn't recur." Why: Hayfin's value: "We do not hide our mistakes and recognise them as a natural part of our personal and collective growth" (JD pack). Twist: "Why should we trust you now?" Hold: "Because you heard it from me first, with the fix."

C5 · Acting on incomplete information against a deadlineHard

Setup: A consultant's RFP is due at 5pm; two answers need data you cannot get in time. Tree: Can the data be obtained before 5pm? → no → can the deadline move? → ask the consultant early (by noon) → if not, submit on time with those answers marked "as of [date], update to follow" and a named follow-up date. Never estimate silently. Say: "We'd rather give you an accurate answer on Tuesday than a guess today; everything else is complete." Twist: "Other managers answered in full." Hold: "Then you'll be able to compare like with like on Tuesday."

The CV grill: three levels deep

A CV grill takes one line of your CV and goes three levels down: what you did, how exactly, and why it mattered or what you would do differently. Most candidates hold level one and lose level three. Your CV was not supplied, so the attacks below are generic; run the drill on every bullet.

#AttackFrame-holding answer pattern
1"What exactly did you do, not the team?""I owned X: I built the table, checked it against Y, and presented it to Z." Use "I" for your work and "we" for the team's.
2"That number on your CV: how did you calculate it?"Give the formula and the source in one breath. If it was a team estimate, say so.
3"Sounds like admin. What was the analysis?"Name the decision your work fed and one judgement you made.
4"What went wrong?"One real problem, your fix, what you would do differently (Hayfin values mistakes as learning, JD pack).
5"Why did you leave / not get a return offer?"Neutral facts, no blame, forward-looking: what you now want and why PSG fits.
6"How is that relevant to investor relations?"Map it to a JD duty: pitch materials, data accuracy, client requests, presenting.
7"You list Excel. Build me X now."Talk while you build: set-up, check, result. Say where you would double-check.
8"Your interest in private credit looks recent.""It is recent and deliberate; here is what I read and what I concluded" (cite Part 5 and Part 7).
The four-line drill for every CV bullet1. Context in one line (where, when, for whom). 2. My action in one line, with a verb you can defend. 3. Result with one number and its source. 4. Lesson that links to PSG (accuracy, client, data, presenting). Write all four for each bullet, then answer three "why?" questions on each aloud.
