# CROX - Q&A prep: the 20 toughest questions

Each answer is about 30 seconds spoken (60-80 words) and contains at least one number. Figures as of 2026-11-25; (E) estimates from `model.xlsx`.

### 1. Growth runway: North America is saturated and shrinking. Where does growth come from?

From outside the US. International is 51% of Crocs Brand revenue and grew 11.8% in Q3. Management says share in China, India, Japan, Germany and France is one-third of established-market levels. My base case needs only 4.5% group growth, with North America flat.

### 2. What happens when the cyclical driver, the clog fashion cycle, slows?

It already has in North America, down 6.8% in FY25. If it gets worse my bear case has North America falling 8% a year for two years, EPS of $13.17 in FY28 and a $92 stock. The full fad unwind in my stress test is $7.39 of EPS.

### 3. Why a 12-18 month holding period?

Because it spans the two events that settle the debate: the FY27 guide in February and Q3 2027, when the 700-800 basis point North America accounting distortion is lapped. It is also long enough for two more years of buybacks to show: my share count falls 16% by FY28.

### 4. The stock has doubled. Is it not already priced in?

The re-rating is, the compounding is not. On a reverse DCF the price implies about 1% revenue growth for a decade. On my numbers it is 9.3x FY27 earnings. My base target is $207. But I agree the easy money was made between $76 and $160.

### 5. What if results beat but the stock falls?

It has happened twice in eight quarters: October 2025 (-2.5%) and July 2026 (-7.4%), both on a soft next-quarter guide. The average absolute move on results is 15%. That is why I would start at half size and treat a guide-driven dip below $140 as the entry, provided the kill criteria are intact.

### 6. The discount to Deckers has closed. Could the gap reopen the wrong way?

Yes. The five-year median discount was 52% and today it is about 7%. Deckers has net cash and grows faster. I do not rely on relative value; my base target uses 10.5x, roughly today's multiple, not a peer multiple.

### 7. How exposed is this to interest rates?

Modestly. About 47% of the $1.3bn of debt is floating; 100 basis points costs $5.6m, or 0.6% of EPS. The bigger rate effect is on the multiple: every 50 basis points on WACC moves my DCF by about $14 a share.

### 8. Regulation: what is the tariff exposure now?

US imports from Vietnam, China, Indonesia, India and Cambodia carry a 10-12.5% Section 301 duty, down from the 18-20% IEEPA layer that the Supreme Court struck down in February 2026. Each point is about $4m, or $0.08 of EPS. The $64m of refunds is excluded from adjusted earnings.

### 9. Elections and politics: does the November 2026 midterm result matter?

Indirectly. Trade policy is the channel, and tariffs now rest on Section 301 with no sunset, so Congress matters less than the courts and USTR. A 5-point tariff swing either way is about $0.38 of EPS, roughly 2%. The consumer effect of any fiscal change is larger and I cannot quantify it.

### 10. War and geopolitics?

Two exposures. Sourcing: 45% of Crocs production is in Vietnam and two manufacturers make 73% of it. Demand and cost: the Middle East conflict cut distributor revenue and raised oil-linked resin and freight costs in 2026; that is why the international guide was trimmed from about 10% to high single digits in April.

### 11. Currency?

44% of revenue is outside the US. A 1% stronger dollar costs about 0.7% of EPS on my 35% flow-through assumption. FX added only 0.2% to FY25 revenue, and Crocs hedges $223m notional.

### 12. What is the downside in a market sell-off?

Plan for 25-30% down in a 20% market fall, to about $112-120. Beta is 1.2 on five years of weekly data and 1.5 on Yahoo's monthly measure. In 2022 the stock fell 42% against 25% for the index, and 74% peak to trough.

### 13. How subjective are your assumptions in the revenue-at-risk work?

The nine scores and the haircut map are mine. Moving every score one notch changes revenue at risk from 16% to 9% or 24%. A Monte Carlo of independent one-notch moves gives a 5th-95th percentile EPS range of $6.11 to $8.70. The conclusion that a full unwind halves EPS survives.

### 14. What are the numbers behind the main catalyst?

The FY27 guide, expected around 11 February. Consensus EPS is $16.01. My base is $17.27; excluding all 2027 buybacks, as guidance does, it is $16.61. I put 60% on a guide midpoint at or above $16.

### 15. And the numbers behind the HEYDUDE acquisition?

$2.5bn headline in February 2022: $2.05bn cash and $450m in stock, funded by a $2.0bn term loan. Revenue peaked at $949m in 2023 and was $715m in 2025. Crocs wrote off $737m, about 30% of the price. The term loan is down to $500m. It was a poor deal; it is also now only 17% of revenue.

### 16. Operating income is below 2023. Is this not just financial engineering?

Largely, yes, and I say so. Adjusted operating income is $943m against $1,099m in FY23. But the buybacks are funded by $754m of real free cash flow, not debt: leverage is 1.2x. Retiring 8% of the company a year at 10x earnings is a legitimate source of per-share value.

### 17. The CEO has been selling. Why should we buy?

He sold 102,688 shares for $14.1m since June with no 10b5-1 plan disclosed on the forms, which I flag as a red flag. He retains about 673,293 shares, so he sold roughly 13%. A further 10% sale is one of my kill criteria.

### 18. Why is your EPS above consensus? What do you know that the Street does not?

Nothing secret: it is arithmetic. I am 8% above consensus for FY27 because I model $605m of buybacks and the Street models few or none. Consensus FY27 EPS has already risen 7% in 30 days, from $14.95 to $16.01.

### 19. What would make you wrong fastest?

A second leg down in North America. If DTC excluding the accounting shift turns negative for two quarters, the 'self-inflicted' explanation fails and my bear case, 25% probability and $92, becomes the base case. That number is disclosed quarterly, so I will know by April.

### 20. Why not wait for a better entry, or just own Deckers?

Waiting is reasonable: I would buy half now and half below $140, where expected return rises from 24% to about 42%. Deckers is the higher-quality company at a similar 10.9x, but it returns 7% of its market cap a year in buybacks against 11% for Crocs.
