# Insight Days &amp; Thin Experience, Handled Honestly {#sec5}

<p class="sectionmeta">How to make short, observational programmes count without implying you did a job you didn't — and how to make ordinary work genuinely relevant to client-facing finance.</p>

## 5.1 Where do insight days and spring weeks go?

Under **Work Experience**, in reverse-chronological order, with the firm name — but described honestly and briefly. The UK-specific consensus from r/FinancialCareers is: "*Just include the experience and 1 bullet point to indicate something you accomplished… Just don't include several bullet points and try to pass it off as an actual internship*" *(r/FinancialCareers "[UK] how to write about spring weeks" — Appendix §5)*.

<div class="box disagree" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">The genuine disagreement</span>

**Include them (majority).** They are a recognised UK signalling credential — evidence of early sector commitment and of clearing a competitive selection. Bright Network explicitly advises listing insight weeks "*to show your commitment to the sector.*"

**Omit the thin ones (minority).** Some argue a single observational *day* isn't worth a line and can dilute a page that has real experience on it. In the UK spring-week thread, a commenter argued for omitting them and was challenged — the prevailing view kept them, but *did not inflate them.*
</div>

**Applied to you:** your **Goldman spring** earns its place. But note *what* it currently says — a hedge-fund trading simulation (Section 9) — which points away from your target roles. For **UBS / Barclays / JP Morgan**: if these were multi-day spring/insight programmes you were selected onto, add them as one honest line each (they reinforce "commitment + selection"). If any was a single open day or a virtual Forage module, it is a judgement call — one combined line ("*Insight programmes: UBS, Barclays, J.P. Morgan, 2024–25*") can capture them without pretending each was a role. **Do not give a one-day event three achievement bullets.**

## 5.2 Honest language vs inflated language

The failure mode is vague "exposure" bullets. A recruiter publicly panned a real one — "*Developed a better understanding of wealth management*" — as providing "*absolutely no insight into what you actually did, what you contributed*" *(r/FinancialCareers — Appendix §5)*. The fix is to describe the **concrete thing you did** during the programme (a group case, a stock pitch, a specific session, a networking outcome), not the feeling you came away with.

| <span class="cross">Reads as inflated / empty</span> | <span class="tick">Reads as honest &amp; specific</span> |
|---|---|
| "Worked as an analyst at Goldman Sachs" (it was a spring week) | "Selected for Goldman Sachs spring programme (Sales & Trading); completed a live trading simulation and desk sessions" |
| "Gained exposure to investment banking" | "Shadowed a coverage team over 3 days; completed a group case on [X] presented to associates" |
| "Developed a deeper understanding of markets" | "Attended sessions across FICC and equities; built a mock pitch on [name the stock/idea]" |
| "Responsible for client relationship management" | "Observed client-review meetings; summarised follow-up actions for the team" |

<div class="box honest" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">The honest line — and where it's crossed</span>

The line is **verb honesty about your role.** "*Attended, completed, shadowed, observed, presented, built [a mock/simulated X]*" are honest. "*Managed, led, ran, owned, advised clients*" applied to a spring week are **crossings** — they claim responsibility you didn't hold. WSO's non-target guide is blunt on why this matters: "*Faking an interest or skill is as close to an auto-ding as there is.*" The interview is where inflation dies: a coverage interviewer asks "*walk me through what you actually did day to day*," and an inflated bullet collapses. **Every line must survive that question.**
</div>

## 5.3 Making Pictet carry maximum legitimate weight

Your **Global Client Group Summer Analyst** role at Pictet is your single most on-target credential and deserves the most page space. To make it defensible *and* impressive, gather the specifics now, while the experience is fresh (it ended weeks ago):

<div class="box note" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">What to dig up / ask your former manager for</span>

- **Numbers you can stand behind.** How many market openings, accounts, queries, teams, reports? Even ranges ("~40 queries/day") beat none. You already have several — verify them.
- **The systems and terms you actually touched.** CRM used (Salesforce? in-house?), reporting tools, the fund ranges (SICAV? UK OEIC?), which client segment (US institutional — you have this).
- **Before/after of anything you built.** The DDQ portal and the "400-line Excel tracker" it replaced is gold — confirm the scale and whether it's still in use.
- **A quantified outcome for at least one bullet.** Ask your manager: "*did the portal save the team time / reduce errors — roughly how much?*" A sentence of their memory becomes a defensible metric.
- **A reference line for the future** — a manager willing to speak to your client-servicing work is worth more than any bullet in a referral-driven process (Section 8).
- **Permission and accuracy check.** Ask your manager to sanity-check your three Pictet bullets for accuracy and anything confidential. This protects you in interview *and* builds the relationship.
</div>

Then position it: Pictet sits **top of Work Experience**, gets **three tight bullets** (servicing, onboarding/market-openings, reporting), and its language is tuned buy-side (retention, flows, SLA, reporting — Section 4.5).

## 5.4 Making coffee shop, accounting and real estate genuinely relevant

For *client-facing* roles, these are not filler — handled right, they can out-signal a trading simulation, because they are literally about **handling people, volume, accuracy and trust.** The technique (Section 3.3): pull out the client-facing, high-volume, accuracy and commercial slivers and quantify them.

| Job | The transferable spine to sell | Example reframed bullet |
|---|---|---|
| **Coffee shop / retail** | Client interaction under pressure; volume; complaint handling; upselling to targets; cash accuracy | "Served ~[N] customers per shift at peak, resolving complaints and hitting upsell targets while balancing the till to zero variance" |
| **Accounting firm (S&B)** | Attention to detail; client-data handling; compliance-adjacent judgement | Your real bullets already work: "Entered expenses against the chart of accounts… separated allowable from disallowable items before review" |
| **Real estate** | Relationship management; pipeline; needs discovery; closing; following up | "Managed a viewing pipeline of [N] prospects, matching requirements to listings and following up to offer stage" |

<div class="box good" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Why this is on-target, not a stretch</span>

A retail-bank poster on r/FinancialCareers described their own client work as "*account opening, servicing, and pitching possible investment account opening*" — the exact verbs (open, service, pitch, retain) your target roles use. Client-facing finance screens for people it "*can put in front of a client*": someone who has handled a queue of irritated customers at 8am and kept them happy has *proven* that, in a way a stock pitch never can. The reframing is honest — you're not inventing duties, you're naming the competencies the duties built.
</div>

**Decision for your CV:** if space forces a cut, keep the roles that are either *most recent + on-target* (Pictet, Box) or *most client-transferable* (coffee shop/real estate for a pure client-servicing application). A 7-year-old two-month accounting placement (S&B, 2019) is the most cuttable — keep it only if it adds a detail nothing else does, and compress it to one line. Kyle Blockley's recruiter advice: "*Space is precious… limit yourself to what sells 'you' best*" — but note the client-facing counterpoint above before cutting a customer-facing job.

## 5.5 The general principle for thin experience

You have less "thin experience" than you think — but where a line is genuinely light, the move is never to inflate it. It is to (a) extract the one concrete, defensible thing you did, (b) attach the most honest number you have, and (c) let its *placement* (under Experience, with a real firm name) do the signalling. A short honest line under a strong firm name beats a padded paragraph every time — and it's the only version that survives the interview.
