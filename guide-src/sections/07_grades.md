<p class="sectionmeta">Which filters are real, which have quietly disappeared, and what actually moves the needle when the university name doesn't.</p>

Section 0 established the headline: **your First clears every grade gate in your target set.** This section gives you the full evidence, tells you what genuinely needs mitigating (non-target status, not grades), and settles the IMC-vs-CFA question for *your* roles.

## 6.2 Contextualising A-levels on the CV

Keep A-levels — one line, in Education, under your degree: *"A-Levels: Economics (A), Mathematics (B), Physics (C)."* Do not editorialise or apologise. They total **120 UCAS**, they're above the surviving floors, and the A in Economics + B in Maths signals quant capability relevant to finance. No firm in your set publishes an A-level bar for graduate roles. If a specific application form asks for GCSE/A-level detail, complete it factually; on the CV, one line is enough — the First is the headline, not the A-levels.

## 6.3 What goes where "grades" would otherwise sit

For a candidate with a weak degree, advice is to fill the Education section's "sore spot" with modules, prizes and activities. **You don't have that problem — lead with the First.** But strengthen Education anyway:

- **Relevant modules** — list 2–3 that signal markets/quant/commercial awareness (e.g. Investments, Financial Regulation, Econometrics). This lets terms like "portfolio construction" or "MiFID II" appear naturally (Section 4.4).
- **The First, stated once, prominently** — "First-Class average (81%)". If you've ever ranked in your cohort, add it.
- **Head Boy** — you have this; it's a genuine selection-from-250 leadership signal. Keep it, in Education or Leadership.

## 6.4 Substitutes that carry real weight

For a non-target, these are what convert "no brand" into "obviously serious":

- **A professional qualification in progress** — the single most efficient signal for your roles (see 6.5).
- **A society leadership position with output** — you have Oakwood (Equity Research President). Keep it as *leadership*, trim the trading-flavoured content (Section 9).
- **Self-directed work you can defend** — your URBN pitch is exactly this; frame it as intellectual curiosity, not as an audition for a PM seat.
- **Languages, with honest levels** — if you have any beyond English, list with proficiency (e.g. "French — professional working"). Genuinely valuable in cross-border AM distribution.
- **Networking-driven referrals** — not a CV item, but the highest-weight substitute of all (6.6, Section 8).

## 6.5 IMC or CFA Level 1 — the decision, for your roles

<div class="box good" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Verdict: do the IMC first, not CFA Level 1</span>

For **client services / sales / distribution / onboarding / RM-support** in UK asset management, the **IMC (CFA UK Level 4 Investment Management Certificate)** is the right first qualification. It is cheaper, roughly a third of the study time, UK-specific and FCA-relevant, and is exactly what several target firms fund for grads — **M&G names it explicitly.** CFA Level 1 is a stronger, longer, costlier signal aimed more at *investment* roles; add it later only if you drift toward portfolio-facing work.
</div>

| | **IMC** | **CFA Level 1** |
|---|---|---|
| Study time | ~**240 hours** (2 units: ~100 + ~140) | ~**300 hours** |
| Cost | ~**£725** exam fees (£350 + £375) | ~**$1,140–$1,490** per attempt |
| Exam windows | **On-demand** (Pearson VUE, book a slot) | Fixed: **Feb / May / Aug / Nov** |
| Relevance to your roles | **High** — FCA-appropriate for "managing investments"; the recognised sales/distribution benchmark | Moderate — prestige credential, more investment-facing |
| UK-specific | Yes | No (global) |

*(Sources: cfauk.org IMC page; 300hours.com CFA dates/fees — Appendix §6.)*

<div class="box note" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Timeline — can you do it before applications close?</span>

Graduate and off-cycle applications for 2027 start opening in **autumn 2026** and run on rolling deadlines (apply early). The IMC is **on-demand**, so a focused candidate can register and sit **Unit 1 within ~6–8 weeks** and have "*IMC (Unit 1 passed; Unit 2 in progress)*" on the CV before the first deadlines. That single line — a self-funded, in-progress professional qualification from a non-target student — is a disproportionate commitment signal for client-facing AM roles. CFA L1's next fixed windows and 3-month registration lead times make it far less time-flexible; don't let it block applications.
</div>

Put it on the CV honestly the moment you register: *"IMC (CFA UK) — Unit 1 passed [month/year]; Unit 2 scheduled"* or *"IMC — in progress."* Never claim a pass you don't have.

## 6.6 How much is the CV vs networking? Be blunt.

<div class="box honest" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">The blunt split</span>

Practitioners describe a consistent division of labour: **networking and referrals mostly determine whether you get the interview; the CV mostly determines whether you convert once screened.** For a non-target this is sharpest — "*The hardest part for a non-target student is getting an interview*", yet "*My resume was the only thing that got me my job*" once in the room *(r/FinancialCareers — Appendix §5, §8)*. A referral roughly multiplies your odds of passing the initial review.

**Two caveats specific to you:** (1) UK graduate schemes lean on structured online forms + aptitude tests, so the *test* gate is heavier and cold-networking lighter than in US IB — you can't network past a failed SHL test. (2) Buy-side/asset-manager hiring often runs through structured grad processes and headhunters, which dilutes pure cold-emailing but rewards *warm* connections and demonstrated sector commitment (the IMC, the Pictet role). Net: **budget your effort roughly half to networking/warm outreach, and treat the CV as the thing that must not let you down once attention arrives** — not as the lever that creates the attention.
</div>

The honest conclusion of this section: the CV cannot fix your non-target status, and it doesn't need to fix your grades. What it must do is be flawless enough to convert the interviews that *networking and the IMC* earn you. That is a solvable problem — Section 9 solves it.
