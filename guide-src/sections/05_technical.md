# Speaking the Language: Making It Technical {#sec4}

<p class="sectionmeta">This is where a non-target beats a target. Almost no undergraduate can speak fluent onboarding/servicing/distribution. If you can — accurately — you signal that you understand the job, not just that you want it.</p>

The rule that governs this entire section: **use a term only if you can define it under a follow-up question.** An interviewer who covers institutional clients will spot a bluffed "MiFID II" in one question. Everything below is defined so you can use it *honestly*. Where a definition rests on a regulator or primary body it is marked **[A]** (authoritative); where it is standard industry usage without a codified definition, **[I]** — still safe, but describe it by what you did, don't cite it as law.

## 4.1 Client onboarding &amp; compliance

| Term | What it means (plain) | Why it matters to a client-facing role |
|---|---|---|
| **KYC** — Know Your Customer **[A]** | Identifying and verifying who a client is, before and during the relationship. The operational expression of the legal CDD duties in the UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017. | Onboarding lives or dies on complete, current KYC files — the gate every client passes before money moves. |
| **AML** — Anti-Money Laundering **[A]** | The framework of laws and controls to stop criminal proceeds entering the system; UK-anchored in MLR 2017, FCA-supervised. | Client-facing staff are the first line of defence — spotting and flagging the unusual is part of servicing. |
| **CDD** — Customer Due Diligence **[A]** | The regulated standard KYC must meet: identify, verify, understand purpose, monitor. Three tiers — Simplified, Standard, Enhanced (Reg 28, MLR 2017). | Knowing CDD is the legal standard and KYC the practice signals you understand the backbone, not just the paperwork. |
| **EDD** — Enhanced Due Diligence **[A]** | Deeper diligence for higher-risk clients — PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures. Extra evidence + senior sign-off. | Handling high-risk clients without stalling the relationship shows commercial + compliance balance. |
| **Suitability vs Appropriateness** **[A]** | Under MiFID II / FCA COBS 9: *suitability* (advice/discretionary) assesses knowledge, finances and objectives; *appropriateness* (execution-only) checks only understanding of risk. | Any advised or discretionary relationship must evidence suitability — basic MiFID literacy. |
| **MiFID II** **[A]** | EU directive (in force Jan 2018) whose client-facing core is **client categorisation** — retail / professional / eligible counterparty — plus disclosure and call taping. | Which category a client sits in determines what you can say and must disclose. |
| **Sanctions screening** **[A]** | Checking clients and transactions against lists (UK/OFSI, OFAC, UN) at onboarding and ongoing. | A missed hit is a criminal-liability event; screening must clear before transacting. |
| **Source of Funds / Source of Wealth** **[A]** | SOF = origin of the specific money in a transaction; SOW = origin of a person's entire wealth. Different things; SOW required for higher-risk clients. | Getting SOF/SOW right — and knowing they differ — is central to onboarding HNW/high-risk clients. |
| **Account opening / static data** **[I]** | Setting up the account once diligence clears (entity details, SSIs, dealing permissions); *static data* is the slow-changing reference data everything keys off. | Often the literal day-to-day of onboarding; one wrong settlement instruction and a redemption fails. |
| **Onboarding SLA** / **Client lifecycle management** **[I]** | Agreed turnaround targets for onboarding; CLM frames onboarding as a continuous cycle (reviews, refreshes, offboarding), not a one-off. | "Onboarded N accounts within SLA" is exactly the metric a hiring manager wants. |

## 4.2 Fund structures &amp; operations

| Term | What it means (plain) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **NAV** — Net Asset Value **[A]** | Fund assets minus liabilities; per share it's the price investors buy/sell at. Struck at least daily for open-ended funds. | The number clients ask about daily — know how and when it's struck. |
| **UCITS** **[A]** | The EU framework for retail-sellable funds, with a cross-border marketing "passport" and diversification/liquidity rules. | Most retail/cross-border funds you'll service are UCITS. |
| **AIF / AIFMD** **[A]** | An Alternative Investment Fund is any non-UCITS fund (hedge, PE, real estate); AIFMD regulates the manager. | Tells you which rulebook and which investor base applies. |
| **SICAV / OEIC (ICVC)** **[A]** | Open-ended fund *company* structures — SICAV (Luxembourg/France), OEIC/ICVC (UK equivalent). | Cross-border ranges are often Lux SICAVs; UK funds are OEICs/unit trusts. |
| **Share classes** — acc/inc, clean/bundled, hedged **[A/I]** | *Accumulation* reinvests income, *income* pays it out; *clean* (post-RDR) charges only the fund fee, *bundled* rolled in commission; *hedged* strips FX risk. | Clients constantly ask which class they hold and why returns differ — a servicing staple. |
| **Subscriptions & redemptions** **[I]** | Money in (units issued) and money out (units cancelled), processed by the transfer agent at the applicable NAV. | Processing subs/reds accurately and on time is the operational heartbeat of TA/servicing. |
| **Dealing cut-off / forward pricing** **[I]** | The daily deadline to be dealt at the next valuation point; orders price at the *next* (unknown) NAV. | Miss a cut-off and a client's money is out of the market a day — front-line servicing. |
| **Transfer agency (TA)** **[I]** | Maintains the shareholder register and the investor lifecycle — subs/reds, records, income, investor-level AML. | Much fund client-servicing sits here; name it correctly. |
| **Custody** **[A]** | A custodian safekeeps assets, settles trades, collects income — held in nominee name, segregated from the bank's balance sheet. | Institutional clients care intensely about how their assets are held. |
| **Settlement / T+1 / T+2** **[A]** | Final exchange of securities for cash; T+2 = two business days, T+1 = one. US moved to T+1 in **May 2024**; UK & EU due **11 Oct 2027**. | Shorter cycles compress the window to fix breaks — a live topic clients are preparing for. |
| **Reconciliations** **[I]** | Comparing internal records against custodians/administrators to catch and resolve "breaks" before they hit NAV or clients. | Clean recs are how servicing guarantees holdings and cash are right. |
| **Corporate actions** **[I]** | Company events affecting securities — *mandatory* (dividends, splits) apply automatically; *voluntary* (tenders, rights) need a client election by a deadline. | Communicating voluntary-action deadlines and elections is core asset-servicing. |

## 4.3 Distribution &amp; sales

| Term | What it means (plain) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **AUM** **[A]** | Total market value of assets managed; moves via market performance + net flows. Fees are a % of it, so it drives revenue. | "Grew coverage AUM from £X to £Y" is a metric that lands. |
| **Net flows / net new money** **[A]** | New money in minus money out, excluding market movement. | The purest measure of asset-gathering — cleaner than AUM (strips out market luck). |
| **Mandate / segregated mandate** **[A]** | The objectives + constraints a manager is hired to run; a *segregated* mandate is a dedicated portfolio for one client (not pooled). | Winning/retaining mandates is the institutional-sales endgame. |
| **RFP / DDQ** **[I]** | RFP = the questionnaire to select a manager (start of a relationship); DDQ = recurring due-diligence questionnaire (ongoing). | "Supported RFP/DDQ responses" is a standard sales-support line. |
| **Consultant relations / gatekeepers** **[I]** | Investment consultants (Mercer, Aon, WTW) rate managers buy/hold/sell and gate institutional money; *gatekeepers* also include platform buy-lists and fund selectors. | Consultants control access to large capital pools — a prized relationship. |
| **Channels — institutional / wholesale / intermediary** **[A]** | Institutional = pension funds, insurers (often direct); intermediary/wholesale = advisers, private banks, platforms who aggregate retail money; retail = end individuals reached only via intermediaries. | Each channel has a different buyer and sales cycle — essential positioning. |
| **Model portfolios / platforms** **[A]** | Ready-made portfolios advisers run at scale; platforms are the tech intermediaries holding fund holdings. | Getting funds into models/platform buy-lists is how retail distribution scales. |
| **CRM** — Salesforce, MS Dynamics **[I]** | The systems distribution teams use to log interactions, track pipeline and manage RFP/mandate opportunities. | Distribution/RM-support runs on CRM — an expected skill. Name the one you used. |
| **Aladdin — NOT a CRM** **[A]** | BlackRock's portfolio- and risk-management platform (trading, risk, ops, accounting) — an *investment/risk* system, not a sales CRM. | **Interview tell:** calling Aladdin a CRM marks you as bluffing. Describe it correctly. |

## 4.4 How to weave this in — without keyword stuffing

<div class="box warning" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">The line between fluent and stuffed</span>

**Keyword stuffing** is listing terms you can't defend, or cramming a "Skills" line with 20 acronyms. Recruiters spot it, and the Ladders study flags "*excessive keyword stuffing*" as a low-performer trait. **Fluency** is using the *right* term *in the flow of a real achievement.*

- <span class="cross">Stuffed:</span> *"Skills: KYC, AML, CDD, EDD, MiFID II, UCITS, NAV, TA, RFP, DDQ, AUM, T+2, Salesforce, Aladdin, SICAV, OEIC…"* — reads as a glossary you memorised.
- <span class="tick">Fluent:</span> *"Progressed onboarding for institutional accounts by sequencing **KYC** and **account-opening** steps, clearing **custodian** documentation before **trade-ready** status."* — three terms, each doing work inside a real action.
</div>

Distribute the vocabulary across three places, sparingly: **(1) bullets** — one or two terms each, inside the achievement; **(2) a tight skills line** — the genuinely tool-level items only (Salesforce, Excel, IMC-in-progress, languages), not the concepts; **(3) module names** in Education (a "Financial Regulation" or "Investments" module lets "MiFID II" or "portfolio construction" appear naturally). Never repeat a term more than twice on the page.

## 4.5 Sell-side coverage vs buy-side servicing — different emphasis

The two look similar but reward different language. Tune the CV (and cover letter) to whichever you're applying to.

| | **Sell-side coverage (bank)** | **Buy-side client-servicing (asset manager)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cares about** | Winning the next transaction / flow | Retaining & growing the relationship and its AUM |
| **Horizon** | Short; deal-by-deal | Long; relationship over transaction |
| **Success measured in** | Revenue booked, trading volume, cross-sell, wallet share | AUM retained/gathered, net flows, client retention, SLA performance |
| **The role's verb** | Originate & distribute | Steward & service |
| **CV language that lands** | "covered N accounts", "generated £Ym revenue", "cross-sold across products", "supported execution of N deals" | "retained/grew £X AUM", "net inflows of £Ym", "onboarded N clients within SLA", "led DDQ/RFP responses" |

*(Source: Corporate Finance Institute, buy-side vs sell-side — Appendix §4.)* **One-line summary:** the sell-side CV shouts *revenue and deals won*; the buy-side servicing CV shouts *assets retained, flows gathered, clients kept happy through reliable service.* Your Pictet Global Client Group experience is squarely **buy-side servicing** — so for asset-manager applications, lead with retention, flows, onboarding and reporting; for a bank coverage seat, reframe the same work toward revenue enablement and cross-sell.
