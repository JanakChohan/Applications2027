# Cover Letters, LinkedIn &amp; Networking {#sec8}

<p class="sectionmeta">Brief but concrete. These are the levers that compensate for a weak CV — and for a non-target, networking is the one that actually creates interviews.</p>

## 8.1 The cover letter

Where required, keep it to **four short paragraphs** and make it about the *firm*, not you *(Preplounge; M&I; FE Training — Appendix §8)*:

1. **Who you are + the specific role** — one line, no "I am writing to apply for," which wastes the opening.
2. **How you found them / why them** — name a person you spoke to, an event you attended, a recent fund launch or a team structure. This is the paragraph that proves you did the legwork.
3. **Your most relevant experience** — one concrete proof point tuned to the role (for you: the Pictet Global Client Group work, in client-servicing language).
4. **Close** — brief, humble, forward-looking.

<div class="box note" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Openers</span>

- <span class="cross">Weak:</span> "I am writing to apply for the Client Services Graduate role at [Firm]."
- <span class="tick">Strong:</span> "After speaking with [Name] on your UK client group at the [event], I wanted to put myself forward for the Client Services graduate programme — the buy-side servicing work I did this summer at Pictet is exactly the seat I want to build a career in."
</div>

Tailor every letter; proofread obsessively (a typo here contradicts the "attention to detail" a servicing role needs). Don't restate the CV — *interpret* it toward this firm.

## 8.2 LinkedIn — fix the three things that matter

- **Headline** — replace the default "Student at University of East Anglia." Use: *"Final-year Economics @ UEA · Client-facing finance (asset management) · IMC candidate · Salesforce, Excel."* The headline is prime real estate; make it point at your target so recruiters searching find you *(Intervyo; M&I — Appendix §8)*.
- **About** — 3–4 lines for a student: who you are, the target, one proof point, what you're looking for. Keep it short.
- **Consistency** — dates, titles and firm names must **match your CV exactly.** Interviewers open LinkedIn live; discrepancies raise questions. Add 5–10 relevant skills, most valuable first, and quantify past roles as you do on the CV.

## 8.3 Networking — the highest-leverage activity for you

For a non-target, this is not optional garnish. M&I is blunt: "*if you do not attend a top university… networking is your only chance to break into the finance industry.*" And a non-target's thoughtful outreach "*often stands out more than a generic email from a target-school student coasting on their brand name*" *(Appendix §8)*.

<div class="box good" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">What actually works (ranked)</span>

1. **Warm connections first.** "*Networking with people you know is much more rewarding than sending emails.*" Alumni, former Pictet/Box colleagues, anyone one degree away. Your Pictet summer is a warm-network goldmine — stay in touch with that team.
2. **Informational interviews** — a 15-minute "I'd value your advice" call, built over weeks, not a cold ask for a job. The relationship is the asset.
3. **Targeted cold email** — "*bankers live in their email inboxes.*" Personalise every one (a recent fund, a mutual contact, a specific team). Even strong cold campaigns reported **~40% response rates**; generic blasts get near zero. Attach your one-page CV.
4. **Target the deciders, not just the seniors** — "*get in front of the people who make recruiting decisions… often more junior folks, since MDs are not reviewing resumes.*"
</div>

**The anti-patterns:** mass-identical messages to five people at the same firm at once; asking for a job in the first message; no personalisation; not sending a thank-you. And the reality check — networking isn't magic (one poster networked heavily across 550 applications and got zero interviews, then found the *CV/test* was the failure point). It works *with* a clean CV and passed assessments, not instead of them.

## 8.4 How these compensate for a weak CV

A non-target CV in a 188:1 pile gets ~7 seconds. A referral converts you from "resume in a pile" to "the person [Name] spoke to" — it buys you the **minutes that matter.** For your specific targets: an **advocate inside an asset manager's client group**, plus an **in-progress IMC**, plus a **CV tuned to client-servicing**, is a combination very few non-target applicants assemble. The CV's job in that system is narrow and achievable: **don't be the reason a warm introduction cools.** Section 9 makes sure it isn't.
