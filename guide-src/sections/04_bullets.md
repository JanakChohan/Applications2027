# Bullet-Point Architecture {#sec3}

<p class="sectionmeta">The deepest section. Bullets are where a CV is won or lost — a recruiter reviews yours in roughly 10 seconds, so every line must earn its place.</p>

## 3.1 The anatomy of a strong bullet

Every credible finance-CV source teaches the same skeleton. A strong bullet has four parts:

<div class="box note" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">The four-part bullet</span>

**[Action verb] + [what you did, specifically] + [the mechanism / how] + [quantified outcome]**

- **Verb** — a precise, plain action verb, front-loaded (the eye reads the first two words).
- **Task** — the specific thing, with enough detail to be credible, not so much it drowns.
- **Mechanism** — *how* you did it. This is what separates a doer from a describer, and it is the part most students omit.
- **Outcome** — the quantified result. Numbers get **bolded**; "*Don't be afraid to bold the metrics throughout the resume*" *(r/FinancialCareers hiring-manager AMA — Appendix §3)*.
</div>

Worked example (generic): *"**Rebuilt** the quarterly client-reporting checklist **by** mapping each data field to its source system, **cutting** report errors caught at sign-off from ~8 to **0** over three cycles."* Verb → task → mechanism → outcome. Compare that to "*Responsible for client reporting*" — same job, zero signal.

## 3.2 Framework comparison — and which fits finance

| Framework | Shape | Best for | Fit for a finance CV |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) | 4-part narrative | *Interview answers*; behavioural questions | **A thinking tool, not a sentence.** Too long for a bullet — compress it. |
| **XYZ / "Google formula"** | "*Accomplished [X], measured by [Y], by doing [Z]*" | Resume bullets | **Best fit.** Forces a quantified result + a mechanism into one line. |
| **CAR** (Challenge, Action, Result) | 3-part | Resume bullets; problem-solving roles | Strong, close to XYZ; use when there was a clear problem you fixed. |

The practitioner consensus is explicit: "*the STAR method on a resume is a thinking framework, not a sentence template*" — on paper you **compress STAR into one line by leading with the action and ending with the result**, which is exactly the XYZ formula *(KudosWall; Wonsulting — Appendix §3)*. **Use XYZ (or CAR) for the CV; save the full STAR for the interview**, where the fuller narrative belongs.

## 3.3 How to quantify when nothing you did was numerical

Client-servicing and onboarding work rarely comes with a P&L number attached. That does not exempt you from quantifying — it means you quantify a **different axis**. The rule, stated by every source: "*Quantify doesn't mean invent a percentage*" *(Jobscan/Resumly — Appendix §3)*. Legitimate axes:

<div class="box good" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Ways to put a number on non-numerical work</span>

- **Scope / volume** — accounts, queries, markets, clients, teams touched ("*across 20 subject-matter teams*", "*50 market openings*").
- **Frequency / cadence** — daily, monthly, per quarter ("*confirmed receipt same day*").
- **Time** — turnaround, time saved, SLA ("*within a day-60 plan*", "*cut a quarterly email cycle to a live portal*").
- **"Firsts" and state-changes** — you built/started/cleared something ("*replaced the 400-line Excel tracker*").
- **Clean-record / accuracy** — "*with no repeat breaks*", "*validated before dispatch*".
- **People served / ranking** — "*sole point of contact for X*", "*top of a Y-person cohort*".
- **Ranges** are acceptable when you're unsure ("*~40–50 queries a day*"). Honesty beats false precision.
</div>

You already do this instinctively — your Pictet bullets carry "50 market openings", "20 subject-matter teams", "400-line Excel tracker". That is exactly the technique. Section 9 sharpens where you under- or over-reach.

## 3.6 Bullets that sound impressive but signal nothing

<div class="box warning" markdown="1">
<span class="lbl">Empty "power" bullets — and why recruiters spot them instantly</span>

Recruiters are trained on these; they read as noise:

- **"*Leveraged synergies to drive value-added outcomes.*"** — abstract nouns, no object, no number. A recruiter can't picture a single action.
- **"*Championed a culture of excellence in client service.*"** — "*'Championed staff blogging' obscures meaning*"; use plain verbs *(r/FinancialCareers AMA — Appendix §3)*.
- **"*Utilised strong communication skills to interface with stakeholders.*"** — "communication skills" is a claim, not evidence. Show the meeting, the deadline, the number.
- **"*Gained exposure to financial markets.*"** — the classic insight-day filler (Section 5). "Exposure" is the tell that nothing was done.
- **"*Developed a deeper understanding of wealth management.*"** — a real bullet a recruiter publicly panned: it "*provides absolutely no insight into what you actually did, what you contributed*" *(r/FinancialCareers — Appendix §5)*.

The test: **could someone who did nothing write the same sentence?** If yes, it signals nothing. Every bullet must contain something only *you*, having actually done the work, could write — a specific system, team, number, or decision.
</div>
