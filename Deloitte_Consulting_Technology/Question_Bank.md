# Deloitte UK Consulting & Technology: interview question bank

Companion to Part 9 of the interview research pack. Prepared 4 October 2026 from public sources only.

**How to read it.** Each question is labelled *(Official)* where Deloitte UK describes the question type itself, *(Reported [Sxx])* where a candidate or careers publisher reports it, or *(Practice)* where it was built from official categories. These are scaffolds, not scripts: put your own stories in them. Source IDs are listed with URLs at the end.

Deloitte's current five Shared Values: Lead the way; Serve with integrity; Take care of each other; Foster inclusion; Collaborate for measurable impact [S266].

## A. Job Simulation: recorded video and written tasks

Stage 3. You answer on screen to a recorded prompt or type a written reply. Nobody is live. Answer in the first 15 seconds, then give evidence.

**Q1. “Introduce yourself to the members of your team and the senior colleagues who haven't met you yet.”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** A short, confident self-introduction pitched to a mixed audience.
- **Structure:** One-line headline (who you are, what you study, what you bring), one proof point, then what you look forward to working on with them. Aim for about 60 seconds.
- **Weave in:** Name the business in Deloitte's own words: Technology & Transformation, one of the four businesses since the 2024 restructure [S307] [S407].
- **Trap:** Reading your CV aloud. The reviewer is picturing a colleague, not a list.

**Q2. “What does successful collaboration mean to you?”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** The Shared Value “Collaborate for measurable impact”.
- **Structure:** Define it in one line (shared goal, clear roles, a result you can measure), give a short example with a number, then say what you would do differently next time.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte's wording: teams deliver “tangible, measurable, attributable impact” [S266].
- **Trap:** Describing harmony. Deloitte's value is about outcomes you can measure.

**Q3. “Give me an example of a time you've been creative.”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Lead the way; solving problems inside constraints.
- **Structure:** STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Make the creative step explicit: what you tried that others had not.
- **Weave in:** The Technology careers page asks for people who are “resilient and innovative” and enjoy “solving problems” [S270].
- **Trap:** An art project with no outcome. Choose creativity that solved a problem for someone.

**Q4. “What technology do you think could have a big impact on the industrial workplace?”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Curiosity about technology plus commercial sense.
- **Structure:** Name one technology. Explain the mechanism (what changes on the shop floor). Say who pays and who gains. Name one risk.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte's Tech Trends 2026 lists “AI goes physical” (AI in robots and devices) and says only 11% of organisations have agentic AI in production [S244].
- **Trap:** A list of buzzwords. Pick one technology and explain how it creates value.

**Q5. How would you approach being successful in a new team?** *(Reported [S288], paraphrased)*

- **Tests:** Adaptability and a learning mindset.
- **Structure:** Weeks one and two: listen and map the people, goals and ways of working. Then deliver one small thing well. Then ask for feedback.
- **Weave in:** The Consulting page asks for a “strong learning orientation” [S269]; new joiners get a Deloitte buddy [S261].
- **Trap:** Promising to change things on day one.

**Q6. A senior colleague asks for help late in the day when you already have a deadline. What do you do?** *(Reported [S288], paraphrased)*

- **Tests:** Prioritisation, honesty and the value “Take care of each other”.
- **Structure:** Clarify how urgent each task is. Tell both people about the trade-off. Agree a plan, and involve your manager if the two clash.
- **Weave in:** Shared Value “Take care of each other” [S266].
- **Trap:** Quietly doing both badly, or refusing without a conversation.

**Q7. A colleague emails to say they have found a discrepancy in your work. Write your reply.** *(Reported [S288], paraphrased)*

- **Tests:** Ownership, integrity and written tone.
- **Structure:** Thank them. Acknowledge the issue. Say what you will check and by when. Confirm the fix. Say how you will stop it happening again.
- **Weave in:** Shared Value “Serve with integrity” [S266].
- **Trap:** A defensive tone, or blaming the data.

**Q8. Written task: summarise this data about AI adoption and make a recommendation.** *(Reported [S288])*

- **Tests:** Synthesis, structured writing and the “so what”.
- **Structure:** Recommendation first, in one line. Three supporting points from the data. One risk and how to manage it.
- **Weave in:** Use only the data given. Deloitte says the information you need about AI is inside the question, and no prior AI experience is needed [S260].
- **Trap:** Padding with outside facts, or hedging with no recommendation.

**Q9. Email task: reply to a co-worker who says their workload is unmanageable.** *(Reported [S288])*

- **Tests:** Empathy and judgement about when to escalate.
- **Structure:** Acknowledge how they feel. Ask one or two specific questions. Offer a concrete step (reprioritise, take one task). Suggest raising it with the manager together.
- **Weave in:** “Take care of each other” [S266].
- **Trap:** Trying to fix a resourcing problem alone without the manager.

**Q10. AI scenario (practice version): your team wants to use a generative AI tool to draft a client summary. What do you check?** *(Official category [S260]; this wording is Practice)*

- **Tests:** Judgement about responsible AI use, not technical AI knowledge.
- **Structure:** Four checks: data (is client data allowed in this tool?), accuracy (check every fact and citation), disclosure (does the client know?), and a named human who signs it off.
- **Weave in:** In 2025 Deloitte Australia repaid part of a government contract after a report contained fabricated citations; the revised report disclosed it used Azure OpenAI GPT-4o [S316]. Candidates must also follow Deloitte's Integrity & AI guidance in the assessments themselves [S260].
- **Trap:** Saying AI should be banned, or assuming its output is right.

## B. Motivation

Final Stage, Skills and Motivation interview. These are about fit and research. Part 12 gives you full answers for “Why Deloitte?”.

**Q11. “Why Deloitte?”** *(Reported [S264] [S287] [S289])*

- **Tests:** Depth of research, and whether your reason is specific to this firm.
- **Structure:** Use the Part 12 structure: how the firm is built, the evidence, what it means for you.
- **Weave in:** In FY25 restated T&T revenue fell 10% but total revenue fell only 1% [S89] [S305]; UK technology investment rose to £180m in FY26 [S407].
- **Trap:** “Biggest” or “great culture”. Every candidate says it.

**Q12. “Why this service line?” (Technology & Transformation, Strategy, Risk & Transactions, or a named team)** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Whether you know what the team does and who buys it.
- **Structure:** Name the team. Say what its clients buy. Name the skill you would build. Give evidence of your interest.
- **Weave in:** UK T&T offerings: Enterprise, Technology & Performance; Engineering, AI & Data; Cyber; Customer; Human Capital; Defence & Security [S320]. UK T&T revenue £1.75bn, up 4% [S407].
- **Trap:** Choosing a team you cannot describe in one sentence.

**Q13. “Why consulting?”** *(Reported [S289])*

- **Tests:** A realistic picture of the job.
- **Structure:** What consultants do (help a client solve a problem it cannot solve alone, for a fee). What you like about that. Your evidence. One honest trade-off you accept (pressure, travel, changing teams).
- **Weave in:** In an MCA client survey the top barriers to AI were data security and privacy (40%), lack of skilled internal teams (34%) and governance (28%) [S240]. Those are gaps clients pay consultants to fill.
- **Trap:** “It keeps my options open.” That signals you will leave.

**Q14. “Why this role, and what can you bring to it?”** *(Reported [S287])*

- **Tests:** Fit between your strengths and the job.
- **Structure:** Two strengths, each matched to something the role needs, each with a short proof.
- **Weave in:** The Consulting page asks for curious, open-minded people who enjoy collaboration and relationship-building, with a strong learning orientation [S269].
- **Trap:** Strengths with no evidence.

**Q15. “What sets you apart from other candidates?”** *(Reported [S287])*

- **Tests:** Self-awareness without arrogance.
- **Structure:** One distinctive combination (for example your subject plus a job or project), the proof, and why it would help a client.
- **Weave in:** Tie it to a named team from [S320].
- **Trap:** Running down other candidates.

**Q16. “Why Deloitte rather than another Big Four firm?”** *(Practice; generic example in [S265])*

- **Tests:** Knowledge of competitors.
- **Structure:** One fact about the field. One Deloitte difference. The link to you.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte global revenue US$74.5bn in FY26 against PwC's US$56.9bn in FY25 [S120] [S126]; Deloitte says Gartner ranked it No. 1 consulting provider by revenue for the ninth year running [S125].
- **Trap:** Criticising rivals. Compare, do not attack.

**Q17. “Where do you see yourself in five years?”** *(Practice; generic example in [S265])*

- **Tests:** Ambition and whether you will stay.
- **Structure:** Talk about skills, not titles: a platform or qualification, client responsibility, leading a small workstream.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte UK promoted over 6,000 people in June 2026 [S280].
- **Trap:** “Doing an MBA” or “starting my own company”.

## C. Strengths and competency

Final Stage interview. Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Spend most of the time on Action and say “I”, not “we”.

**Q18. “Tell me about yourself” / “Walk me through your CV.”** *(Reported [S289])*

- **Tests:** Clarity, and whether your story leads to this role.
- **Structure:** Present (what you study now), past (two experiences that led here), future (why this role). About 90 seconds.
- **Weave in:** End on the team you want to join [S320].
- **Trap:** A chronological life story.

**Q19. “Tell me about a time you were challenged and how you overcame it.”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Resilience.
- **Structure:** STAR. The challenge in two sentences; your actions in detail; a result with a number.
- **Weave in:** The Technology page asks for people who are “resilient” [S270].
- **Trap:** A challenge where you did nothing personally.

**Q20. “What do you see as your best role in a team?” / “How do you work best in a team?”** *(Reported [S264] [S289])*

- **Tests:** Self-knowledge and flexibility.
- **Structure:** Name your natural role (for example, the person who structures the problem). Give an example. Say when you take a different role.
- **Weave in:** “Collaborate for measurable impact” [S266].
- **Trap:** Saying “leader” by default.

**Q21. “What is your proudest achievement?”** *(Reported [S264] [S287])*

- **Tests:** What you value and how hard you will work.
- **Structure:** One achievement with effort and a measurable result. Say why it matters to you.
- **Weave in:** Link the skill to client work in your chosen team [S269].
- **Trap:** Grades on their own.

**Q22. “What are two of your strengths?”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Self-awareness.
- **Structure:** Two strengths, each with a short example and what it would give a team.
- **Weave in:** Your Immersive Online Assessment produces a strengths report [S260]. Use its language if it fits you.
- **Trap:** Strengths you cannot prove.

**Q23. “What's your greatest weakness?”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Honesty and ability to improve.
- **Structure:** A real weakness, one example of its effect, what you now do about it, and evidence of progress.
- **Weave in:** “Serve with integrity” [S266].
- **Trap:** A disguised boast (“I work too hard”).

**Q24. “Tell me about a time you ran into conflict working with someone.”** *(Reported [S287])*

- **Tests:** Maturity; the values “Take care of each other” and “Foster inclusion”.
- **Structure:** STAR. Spend time on how you understood their view and how you reached an agreed outcome.
- **Weave in:** [S266].
- **Trap:** A story with a villain.

**Q25. “Tell me about a time you made a mistake.”** *(Reported [S287])*

- **Tests:** Integrity.
- **Structure:** Own it quickly. Fix it. Tell the people affected. Explain what you changed to prevent it.
- **Weave in:** “Serve with integrity” [S266]; Deloitte treats breaches of its assessment rules seriously [S260].
- **Trap:** A mistake that turns out to be someone else's fault.

**Q26. “Give me an example of when you've been responsible for a project.”** *(Practice; generic example in [S265])*

- **Tests:** Planning and ownership, the daily work of a junior consultant.
- **Structure:** Scope, plan, track, report, deliver. Name the tool you used to track it.
- **Weave in:** Analysts “collaborate across disciplines” with strategy, design, engineering and data colleagues [S321].
- **Trap:** No dates, no numbers, no sense of what you did.

**Q27. “Tell me about a time you demonstrated leadership.”** *(Practice; generic example in [S265])*

- **Tests:** “Lead the way”.
- **Structure:** Leadership without a title counts: you set direction, got others moving and got a result.
- **Weave in:** [S266].
- **Trap:** Equating leadership with being in charge.

## D. Values: Deloitte's five Shared Values

Deloitte's current Shared Values are: **Lead the way; Serve with integrity; Take care of each other; Foster inclusion; Collaborate for measurable impact** [S266]. Older guides list “Integrity, Outstanding value, Commitment, Diversity” [S264]. Do not use the old list. Each question below is a practice prompt built on one value.

**Q28. Lead the way: “Tell me about a time you changed how something was done.”** *(Practice [S266])*

- **Tests:** Initiative.
- **Structure:** What was wrong, what you proposed, how you persuaded people, what improved.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte says it aims to be “not only leading the profession, but also reinventing it” [S266].
- **Trap:** A change nobody else noticed.

**Q29. Serve with integrity: “A client calls when nobody else is in the office and asks you to shred one of their documents without telling anyone. What do you do?”** *(Practice; generic example in [S265])*

- **Tests:** Integrity and escalation.
- **Structure:** Do not act. Say politely you need to check. Escalate to your manager the same day. Keep a note.
- **Weave in:** Joiners must clear personal independence checks [S261]; conflict checks run before engagements are accepted [S94].
- **Trap:** Agreeing to keep the client happy.

**Q30. Take care of each other: “Tell me about a time you noticed someone struggling. What did you do?”** *(Practice [S266])*

- **Tests:** Care with good boundaries.
- **Structure:** What you noticed, how you raised it, what you did, and when you involved someone else.
- **Weave in:** [S266].
- **Trap:** Becoming the hero who fixed everything alone.

**Q31. Foster inclusion: “Tell me about a time you made sure a quieter voice was heard.”** *(Practice [S266])*

- **Tests:** Inclusive behaviour.
- **Structure:** The situation, what you changed (process or habit), and the effect on the outcome.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte UK introduced school- and university-blind recruitment in 2015 [S267]; 44% of September 2026 starters are based outside London [S271].
- **Trap:** Talking about inclusion in general with no action.

**Q32. Collaborate for measurable impact: “Tell me about a team result you can measure.”** *(Practice [S266])*

- **Tests:** Teamwork that produces a number.
- **Structure:** Team goal, your part, the measure, the result.
- **Weave in:** “tangible, measurable, attributable impact” [S266].
- **Trap:** No number at all.

## E. Commercial awareness

Topical questions are a listed category in the Final Stage interview [S263]. Use the pattern: the story in one line, the mechanism, the effect on Deloitte, your view.

**Q33. “Tell me about something in the news and how it will affect Deloitte.”** *(Reported [S264])*

- **Tests:** Whether you follow business news and can link it to the firm.
- **Structure:** The story in one line; the mechanism; the effect on Deloitte's revenue, people or risk; your view.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte won a Home Office data contract worth up to £200m despite the government's pledge to cut consultancy spend [S233].
- **Trap:** A story you cannot connect to Deloitte.

**Q34. “Deloitte grew revenue 2% but distributable profit 14% in FY26. What does that tell you?”** *(Practice [S407])*

- **Tests:** Basic financial reasoning.
- **Structure:** Margin rose. Possible drivers: pricing, mix, cost control (voluntary redundancy in audit [S227]), efficiency from technology. Say what you would want to know next.
- **Weave in:** £5.81bn is UK and Switzerland consolidated; UK T&T was £1.75bn [S407].
- **Trap:** Calling £5.81bn the UK-only figure.

**Q35. “Globally Technology & Transformation grew slowest (2.5%), but in the UK it grew fastest (4%). Why?”** *(Practice [S300] [S407])*

- **Tests:** Hypothesis thinking.
- **Structure:** Offer two or three hypotheses and how you would test each. Do not claim to know.
- **Weave in:** ERP and cyber demand [S400]; public sector data work [S233]; regional technology centres [S323].
- **Trap:** Stating one cause as fact.

**Q36. “What does the creation of Deloitte EMEA mean for UK clients and for a graduate?”** *(Practice [S124])*

- **Tests:** Understanding of the firm's structure.
- **Structure:** What changed, why (pooled investment), what it could mean for clients, and what it could mean for you.
- **Weave in:** From 1 June 2026: 16 firms, 132,000 people, about €20bn revenue, over €1.5bn of investment over four years in GenAI, sovereign cloud and sector solutions [S124].
- **Trap:** Implying the UK firm has disappeared. The UK firm still exists and has its own CEO [S407].

**Q37. “The government says it wants to cut consultancy spend. Is that bad news for Deloitte?”** *(Practice [S233] [S334] [S335])*

- **Tests:** Balanced judgement.
- **Structure:** Both sides, then a view: less advice, more delivery and capability-building.
- **Weave in:** Public sector consulting hit a record £3.7bn in 2024/25 with Deloitte the top supplier [S335]; the NAO says government lacks consistent spend data [S334].
- **Trap:** Cheerleading for consultants.

**Q38. “Accenture and Deloitte are now about the same size. How do they differ?”** *(Practice [S129] [S120])*

- **Tests:** Competitor knowledge.
- **Structure:** Size, mix, ownership, constraints.
- **Weave in:** Accenture FY26 US$74.18bn, half managed services, about 814,000 people, listed [S129]; Deloitte US$74.5bn, partnership with audit and tax [S120].
- **Trap:** Claiming Deloitte is the biggest on every measure.

## F. Technology and AI

Expect at least one of these for a Technology & Transformation role. Plain-English explanations beat jargon.

**Q39. “Explain an ERP implementation (or a cloud migration) to a non-technical client.”** *(Practice [S269] [S270])*

- **Tests:** Communication.
- **Structure:** Analogy, then what changes for staff, then why these programmes go wrong (data quality, change management, scope).
- **Weave in:** UK T&T growth came partly from “ERP implementation” demand [S407]. ERP (enterprise resource planning) is the core software that runs finance, HR and supply chain.
- **Trap:** Jargon and acronyms.

**Q40. “What cyber risk should every UK board worry about?”** *(Practice [S269])*

- **Tests:** Risk thinking.
- **Structure:** One risk, how it happens, the business impact, two actions.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte's Cyber team works on ransomware recovery, supply-chain security and resilience [S269]; 60% of CFOs named cyber-attacks a key risk in Q1 2026 [S225].
- **Trap:** Describing technology with no business consequence.

**Q41. “Your teammate used a generative AI tool to draft client content and did not check it. What do you do?”** *(Practice [S260] [S261])*

- **Tests:** Integrity and courage.
- **Structure:** Raise it privately first, check the content together, involve the manager if it has gone to the client.
- **Weave in:** The Australian refund [S316]; Hayley McKelvey's line on using AI “responsibly and strategically” [S226].
- **Trap:** Ignoring it, or reporting before talking to your teammate.

**Q42. “Will AI replace graduate consultants?”** *(Practice [S229] [S407])*

- **Tests:** A balanced view and your own place in it.
- **Structure:** What AI takes (first drafts, data work), what stays (judgement, client trust, change), how training is shifting.
- **Weave in:** Darren Graves: “the role our people play will matter more, not less” [S407]; Big Four firms moving trainees into judgement work earlier [S229]; CFOs name AI the second biggest dampener on graduate hiring after cost control [S224].
- **Trap:** Either extreme: “nothing will change” or “we are all doomed”.

**Q43. “Only 11% of organisations have agentic AI in production. Why so few?”** *(Practice [S244])*

- **Tests:** Understanding of AI adoption.
- **Structure:** Define agentic AI (AI that takes actions, not only answers). Give the barriers: process redesign, data, risk and governance.
- **Weave in:** Tech Trends 2026 says the hard part is redesigning processes “agent-first” [S244].
- **Trap:** Blaming the technology alone.

**Q44. “Deloitte research found nearly a third of GenAI users use it without their employer knowing. What would you advise a client?”** *(Practice [S243])*

- **Tests:** Turning a finding into advice.
- **Structure:** The risk (data leakage), the cause (staff want tools), the fix (approved tools, policy, training).
- **Weave in:** Workers spend about £958m a year of their own money on GenAI for work; only 17% have in-house tools [S243].
- **Trap:** Recommending a ban.

## G. Deloitte-specific

Questions that only make sense at Deloitte. Getting these right separates you from candidates who prepared generically.

**Q45. “What do you know about how Deloitte is structured?”** *(Practice [S407] [S302])*

- **Tests:** Basic firm knowledge.
- **Structure:** Network, then the UK firm, then the four businesses, then where your team sits.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte LLP is a member of Deloitte EMEA, which is a member of DTTL; “DTTL and Deloitte EMEA do not provide services to clients” [S407]. Advisory is T&T plus SR&T Advisory [S407].
- **Trap:** Calling Deloitte one global company.

**Q46. “Why hasn't Deloitte split audit from consulting, as EY tried to?”** *(Practice [S104] [S308] [S310])*

- **Tests:** Understanding of the business model.
- **Structure:** History, the argument for staying together, the costs, your view.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte dropped its own consulting spin-off in 2003 [S104]; Global CEO Joe Ucuzoglu said keeping the model was “not even a close call” [S308]; EY abandoned its split in April 2023 [S310].
- **Trap:** Taking a side without the costs.

**Q47. “What are personal independence rules and why do they matter to you?”** *(Practice [S261] [S314])*

- **Tests:** Whether you understand the audit link.
- **Structure:** Deloitte audits companies, so staff must not hold certain financial interests in audit clients. It also limits what consulting can sell to those clients.
- **Weave in:** “You will be required to clear Personal Independence checks before you join us” [S261]; non-audit fees for listed audit clients are capped at 70% of the average audit fee [S314].
- **Trap:** Not knowing that Deloitte is also an auditor.

**Q48. “What did you learn from the Deloitte Australia AI report case?”** *(Practice [S316])*

- **Tests:** Maturity on a sensitive topic.
- **Structure:** Facts in two sentences, the lesson (human checks, disclosure), what you would do as an analyst.
- **Weave in:** The refund was on an AU$440,000 contract; the report contained fabricated citations [S316]. It was a separate member firm, but the story ran under the Deloitte name [S302].
- **Trap:** Gloating, or pretending it did not happen.

## H. Final-stage topic discussion follow-ups

The topic discussion is “two-way”: the assessor asks questions throughout [S260]. These practice follow-ups test whether you can think on your feet. Prepare a short answer to each for your topic.

**Q49. “What is the strongest argument against your view?”** *(Practice, built on the official format [S260])*

- **Tests:** Intellectual honesty.
- **Structure:** State the counter-argument fairly, say why you still hold your view, or say what would change it.
- **Weave in:** Use the counter-case evidence from your topic notes.
- **Trap:** Saying there isn't one.

**Q50. “If you were advising the CFO, what would you tell them to do on Monday?”** *(Practice [S260])*

- **Tests:** Turning analysis into action.
- **Structure:** One action, one owner, one measure of success.
- **Weave in:** CFO priorities from the Deloitte CFO Survey [S224].
- **Trap:** Another round of analysis.

**Q51. “What evidence would change your mind?”** *(Practice [S260])*

- **Tests:** Scientific thinking.
- **Structure:** Name one data point and the threshold that would flip your view.
- **Weave in:** Point to a specific published number you used.
- **Trap:** “Nothing would.”

**Q52. “How would a firm like Deloitte help here, and how would it get paid?”** *(Practice [S260])*

- **Tests:** Commercial sense.
- **Structure:** The service, the team that sells it, the pricing model (time and materials, fixed fee or managed service).
- **Weave in:** Deloitte sells “Operate” managed services as well as projects [S120].
- **Trap:** Forgetting that consulting is a business.

## I. Curveballs

Unexpected questions test composure. Pause, structure, answer.

**Q53. “Teach me something in two minutes.”** *(Practice)*

- **Tests:** Clarity and structure.
- **Structure:** One idea, one analogy, one example, check understanding.
- **Weave in:** Choose something from your degree.
- **Trap:** Something too big to fit in two minutes.

**Q54. “What is one weakness of Deloitte?”** *(Practice [S316] [S318] [S227])*

- **Tests:** Honesty and balance.
- **Structure:** One real weakness, why it exists, what the firm is doing about it.
- **Weave in:** Reputational spillover between member firms [S316]; FRC sanctions [S318]; low attrition led to audit voluntary redundancies [S227].
- **Trap:** Refusing to name one, or naming one with venom.

**Q55. “Should governments use consultants at all?”** *(Practice [S337] [S332] [S334])*

- **Tests:** Balanced judgement on a contested issue.
- **Structure:** The case for (skills, speed), the case against (cost, dependence), your conditions.
- **Weave in:** Test and Trace: about £1,000 a day per Deloitte consultant [S332]; The Big Con argues reliance weakens state capability [S337]; the NAO found 86% of survey respondents said consultants added value [S334].
- **Trap:** One-sided either way.

**Q56. “If you don't get this offer, what will you do?”** *(Practice [S260])*

- **Tests:** Resilience and real motivation.
- **Structure:** Learn from the feedback, build the skill, reapply.
- **Weave in:** Deloitte gives feedback after the final stage and lets you reapply next academic year [S260].
- **Trap:** Naming a competitor as your plan B.
