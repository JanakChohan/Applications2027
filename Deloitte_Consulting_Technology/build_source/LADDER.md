# LADDER.md: the scenario ladder (canonical titles for Part 11)
Defined by the p11a writer (Figure 29). The p11b writer MUST use these exact titles, one card per row, in this order (D1 easy, D1 medium, D1 hard, D2 easy ...). Rung = easy / medium / hard (card class easy / medium / hard). The five composure-set scenarios in p11a are separate and are NOT repeated here.

| D# | Rung | Title | One-line setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | easy | First call with the CIO | You are asked to open a client call with a CIO you have never met while your manager is five minutes late. |
| D1 | medium | Director doubts your experience | A client director asks in a workshop, "How many of these have you actually done?" and the room goes quiet. |
| D1 | hard | Client wants the bad news buried | A client sponsor asks you to leave a red risk off the steering-committee pack because "the board will panic". |
| D2 | easy | Explain IT as business partner | A client finance lead asks what "moving IT from cost centre to business partner" actually means for her team. |
| D2 | medium | IT head resists the new model | The client's head of infrastructure says the proposed operating model will cut his team and he will not support it. |
| D2 | hard | Two sponsors, two operating models | The CIO and the COO each tell you privately they want a different target operating model in the final report. |
| D3 | easy | Map the application landscape | You are given 400 rows of an application inventory and two days to produce a first landscape view. |
| D3 | medium | Vendor already chosen before analysis | Early in a platform selection, a client executive says the ERP vendor is already decided and asks you to "make the analysis fit". |
| D3 | hard | Legacy system nobody understands | A core legacy system holds critical data, its only expert retires in a month, and the cutover plan assumes it is documented. |
| D4 | easy | Sense-check a benefits number | Your manager asks you to sanity-check a £12m savings figure in a business case before it goes to the client. |
| D4 | medium | Benefits that do not add up | You find the business case double-counts headcount savings already claimed by another programme. |
| D4 | hard | Partner wants a bigger number | A partner asks you to raise the benefits estimate so the investment case clears the client's hurdle rate. |
| D5 | easy | Add carbon to the case | The client asks whether the cloud migration business case should include a carbon estimate, and you are asked for a view. |
| D5 | medium | Workshop excludes remote colleagues | Your design workshop is run fully in person, and three key client staff who work part-time or remotely cannot attend. |
| D5 | hard | Green claim without evidence | A client wants the final report to say the programme "delivers net zero IT" when the data supports only a modest reduction. |
| D6 | easy | Update the RAID log | You are handed the programme RAID log the night before a status meeting and half the actions have no owner. |
| D6 | medium | Status report turning amber | Your workstream will miss a milestone by two weeks and the status report is due to the client tomorrow. |
| D6 | hard | Go-live with a known defect | The client wants to go live on Monday despite an open severity-one defect your testing team has raised. |
| D7 | easy | Run a requirements interview | You are asked to interview a client operations manager to capture requirements for a new case-management system. |
| D7 | medium | Business and IT disagree | Business users want a feature IT says is technically impossible within budget, and both ask you to take their side. |
| D7 | hard | Scope creep from a senior user | A senior client stakeholder keeps adding requirements by email outside change control and expects them in the next release. |
| D8 | easy | Tighten a slide for tomorrow | Your manager says your three-slide summary is "too busy" and needs it rewritten for a 9am client meeting. |
| D8 | medium | Typo found after sending | You spot a wrong figure in a client pack an hour after it was emailed to twelve client stakeholders. |
| D8 | hard | Pack contradicts the client's data | The night before a board pack goes out, you find the client's own data contradicts the headline recommendation. |
| D9 | easy | Which AI tool to use | You want to use an AI assistant to summarise interview notes and are unsure which tools the firm approves. |
| D9 | medium | Colleague pastes data into ChatGPT | You see a fellow analyst paste client interview transcripts into a public AI chatbot to save time. |
| D9 | hard | AI draft cites a fake source | An AI-drafted section of a client report cites a regulation paragraph and a study you cannot find anywhere. |
| D10 | easy | Hand-off to the delivery centre | You must hand a testing workstream to an offshore delivery-centre team with a six-hour time difference. |
| D10 | medium | Alliance partner oversells the client | A software alliance partner's salesperson promises the client features that are not on the product roadmap. |
| D10 | hard | Two practices, one client, conflict | Another Deloitte practice is bidding for work at your client that could compete with your engagement's recommendation. |
