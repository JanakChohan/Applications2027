Part 4

How Deloitte stands out, and what only it can claim

Deloitte against nine rivals on size, shape and design choices; the handful of things only Deloitte can honestly claim; and, just as useful in an interview, where it does not lead.

The field at a glance

Deloitte competes with three kinds of firm. The other Big Four (PwC, EY, KPMG) have the same shape: audit plus tax plus consulting, owned by partners. Listed technology services firms (Accenture, IBM Consulting, Capgemini) have no audit and are owned by shareholders. Strategy houses (McKinsey, BCG, Bain) are partner-owned, much smaller and focused on advice. Each latest year is different, because the firms' financial years end in different months. KEY TERM

Managed services (Deloitte calls its version "Operate"): running part of a client's operations or technology for years at a time, for a recurring fee, rather than doing a project and leaving. It brings steadier revenue. Half of Accenture's revenue is managed services [S129]; Deloitte's FY26 release named Operate as an area of "significant growth" without giving a number [S120] [Confirmed].

Six design choices, and where each firm sits

Size is the least interesting difference. The more useful comparison is design: six choices each firm has made, and what each choice costs. Figure 8 places each firm on each choice. Positions on five axes are approximate judgements from the evidence in this Part; axis 4 is calculated.

1. Audit inside the firm, or independent of audit?

The Big Four keep audit. That gives them a trust brand and board-level access, and costs them most consulting at their own audit clients (Part 3, Figure 7). Everyone else is free of that limit. EY's failed split [S141] and KPMG's new US law firm, which may not serve KPMG audit clients [S142], show how much the constraint bites. A newer UK challenger, Unity Advisory, launched in April 2025 with up to US$300m from Warburg Pincus and no audit at all; its co-founder Steve Varley pitches a firm that "crucially, has no conflicts" [S167] [Confirmed].

2. Strategy to operations, or specialism?

Deloitte spans strategy (Monitor Deloitte, bought in 2013 [S134]), technology delivery (T&T), risk, deals, tax, audit and Operate [S120]. Accenture spans strategy to managed services, without audit or tax [S129]. Strategy houses are narrower, though BCG now earns over 40% of revenue from AI and tech-focused work [S133].

3. Partnership or listed company?

Partnerships fund investment from partners' profits and can take a long view. Listed firms raise capital easily but answer to shareholders every quarter: Accenture returned a record US$11.5bn to shareholders in FY26 [S129] [Confirmed]. Private equity is a middle path in the mid-tier: New Mountain Capital took about 60% of Grant Thornton US's non-audit business in 2024 [S144] and Cinven bought into Grant Thornton UK in 2025 [S145] [Reported]. None of the Big Four has done this.

4. Pyramid shape

Revenue per head is a rough measure of the pyramid. Low revenue per head means big delivery teams and heavy offshore use; high means small teams of expensive experts. By the pack's arithmetic: BCG about US$430k, McKinsey about US$375-400k, PwC about US$156k, Deloitte about US$149k, KPMG about US$144k, EY about US$131k, Accenture about US$91k, Capgemini about €53k [Inferred] (from [S120] [S126] [S127] [S128] [S129] [S132] [S133] [S135]).

5. Alliance-led or vendor-neutral?

Deloitte and Accenture are built around platform alliances; Deloitte US cites "over 36,000 SAP-focused professionals" [S146] [Confirmed]. Strategy houses were traditionally neutral, though Bain now describes "a vibrant ecosystem of technology partners" [S406].

6. Time-and-materials or managed services?

Accenture is half managed services and reports bookings (signed future work) [S129]. Deloitte is mostly project work with Operate growing [S120]. Strategy houses are mostly project fees.

What only Deloitte can claim

These are the claims that survive checking. Each comes with the evidence, the safe wording, and why it matters to you.

1. The largest of the Big Four by a wide margin, level with Accenture

Evidence: US$74.5bn in FY26, against PwC US$56.9bn, EY US$53.2bn and KPMG US$39.8bn (their latest years) [S120] [S126] [S127] [S128] [Confirmed]. Accenture reported US$74.18bn for a year ending three months later [S129]. Safe wording: "the largest of the Big Four by a wide margin, and roughly level with Accenture as the largest professional services firm." Why it matters: scale funds investment such as the US$3bn AI programme [S120] and gives graduates exposure to more sectors.

2. No. 1 in Gartner's consulting services market share, nine years running

Evidence: Deloitte's release says it is the "No. 1 consulting services provider by revenue" for "the ninth consecutive year and 14th time in total" [S125] [Reported] (Deloitte's own release; the Gartner report is paywalled). Why it matters: an outside analyst measures it, and it covers consulting only, not audit.

3. It kept consulting when the others sold theirs

Evidence: after Enron, EY sold its consulting arm to Cap Gemini (2000), KPMG Consulting floated as BearingPoint and PwC Consulting went to IBM (2002). Deloitte planned to spin off "Braxton" and abandoned the plan on 31 March 2003 [S104] [Reported]. Safe wording: "Deloitte did not complete its planned consulting spin-off, while EY, KPMG and PwC sold theirs." Why it matters: a twenty-year head start in running audit and consulting as one firm; consulting is now about 62% of global revenue [S81] [Confirmed]. The peer details rest on one contemporary report, and all three rivals have since rebuilt consulting arms.

4. Strategy through Operate, plus audit, under one brand

Evidence: Monitor Deloitte (strategy, acquired 2013) [S134], T&T and SR&T [S120], Operate [S120] and Audit & Assurance. Accenture has no audit; strategy houses have no audit or large-scale implementation; PwC, EY and KPMG have the same shape but smaller consulting businesses [S126] [S127] [S128]. [Confirmed] for each part; the "only one" claim is [Inferred]. Why it matters: a graduate can see a problem from diagnosis to running the fix.

5. The largest single Big Four regional firm in Europe

Evidence: Deloitte EMEA: 16 firms, 132,000 people, 6,000 partners, €20bn, from 1 June 2026, with more than €1.5bn of planned extra investment [S124] [Confirmed]. KPMG's UK-Swiss combination is much smaller [S140]. Houston: "Deloitte EMEA uniquely strengthens our ability to invest at scale across borders" [S124]. ["Largest" and "first" are Inferred; verify before saying either.] Why it matters: a London graduate joins a firm that can staff and invest across 80+ countries.

6. The UK government's top consulting supplier five years running

Evidence: Tussell data: Deloitte top supplier of key consulting contracts for five years, £400m+ in fees, about four times the next firm [S335] [Reported]. A separate City AM analysis of Tussell data found £431m of UK government consultancy contracts to Deloitte in Labour's first year, ahead of PA Consulting (£310m) and KPMG (£301m) [S174] [Reported]. Why it matters: many T&T analysts start on public sector work, and some of it needs security clearance. It also means Deloitte is the first name critics reach for (Part 3).

7. The biggest enterprise AI rollout of its kind, at announcement

Evidence: Claude made available to more than 470,000 Deloitte people in October 2025, with 15,000 certifications [S325]; CNBC called it Anthropic's "biggest enterprise deployment ever" [S326] [Confirmed]. Deloitte was also NVIDIA's Global Consulting Partner of the Year for the fifth year running [S300] [Confirmed]. Why it matters: the tools you would use day to day, and the skills you could be certified in.

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

"Deloitte and Accenture are now almost exactly the same size, around US$74bn. The difference is that half of Accenture is managed services, while Deloitte also has audit and tax. What draws me is the breadth: strategy, technology and the Operate side in one firm, with the portfolio holding steady when consulting dips."

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte is the biggest consulting firm in the world on every measure." Accenture matches it on revenue, has about 300,000 more people and is far bigger in managed services [S129]. Gartner's ranking is by consulting revenue only [S125].

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte is the only Big Four firm with a consulting business." PwC Advisory is US$24.3bn and EY Consulting US$16.4bn [S126] [S127]. The defensible point is historical: Deloitte did not complete its 2002-03 spin-off [S104].

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte EMEA is the first firm of its kind." That is an inference, not something Deloitte or our sources state. Say "16 firms, 132,000 people, €20bn" and let the numbers do the work [S124].

Where Deloitte does not lead

Saying this plainly is a sign you have done the work. Interviewers trust candidates who know the weak spots.

  • Growth. Deloitte grew 3.8% in local currency in FY26 [S120]. Accenture grew 5% [S129], BCG 7% [S133] and KPMG 5.1% in its FY25 [S128]. Deloitte's T&T grew only 2.5%, the slowest of its four businesses [S120] [Confirmed].
  • UK consulting in FY25. Deloitte UK T&T fell 10%, the worst of the Big Four UK consulting falls found: EY UK consulting -6% [S139], KPMG UK Advisory -3% [S140], PwC UK consulting -3% [S401] [Reported].
  • Strategy prestige. McKinsey, BCG and Bain own the top-strategy brand. BCG's revenue per head is nearly three times Deloitte's [Inferred] (from [S120] [S133]).
  • Managed services scale and visibility. Accenture's managed services alone are US$37.3bn, and it reports bookings that show future work [S129]. Deloitte does not report bookings.
  • UK audit quality, 2026. In the FRC's July 2026 inspections, KPMG scored 95% of audits good or needing only limited improvement, Deloitte 90%, EY 90% and PwC 89% [S137] [Reported] (one secondary outlet). Deloitte ties for second.
  • UK headline size. Deloitte's £5.81bn includes Switzerland [S407]; PwC's UK group (with the Middle East) reported £6.155bn [S138]. On UK-only figures Deloitte (£5,014m) is bigger than PwC UK (£4.365bn), but the scopes differ [S88] [S138].
  • Reputation. No rival had the Australian AI report [S316], though every Big Four firm has FRC fines; KPMG's five-year total was the largest [S110] [Reported].
YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Prompt: "Why Deloitte and not Accenture?" (expect this if you mention technology).

Accenture's case: bigger technology and managed services business [S129], no audit independence limits, faster growth, and a published graduate salary of £35,190 plus a signing bonus on one listing [S402] [Reported].

Deloitte's case: breadth beyond technology (tax, deals, risk, strategy) [S120]; a partnership that kept investing through a 10% consulting fall [S88]; UK onshore technology centres [S323]; the UK's top government consulting supplier [S335].

Suggested stance: "If I only wanted to build systems at scale, Accenture would be a fair choice. I want to see the business problem as well as the system, and Deloitte's mix of strategy, risk and technology in one partnership gives a graduate that view."

Prompt: "Is the slow T&T growth a warning sign?"

Yes: global T&T grew 2.5%, slowest of the four [S120]; City AM framed it as "industry-wide pressure from artificial intelligence" [S222]; small UK firms are cutting consulting spend while paying AI companies [S239].

No: UK T&T grew 4%, the fastest UK business [S407]; MCA members forecast UK consulting growth of 5.7% for 2026 [S241]; clients are turning to consultants for AI transformation help [S240].

Suggested stance: "It is a signal that the work is changing, from big programmes measured in hours to AI-enabled delivery measured in outcomes. The firms that win will be the ones that change how they price."
