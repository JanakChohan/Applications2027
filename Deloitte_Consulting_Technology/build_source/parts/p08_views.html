Part 8

Forming your own view: both sides of every argument

Nine live debates an interviewer could raise, each with the strongest case on both sides, what Deloitte's own leaders have said, a defensible stance you can adapt, and how to hold it under challenge. Start with the five-step method in Figure 39.

The method: how to build an opinion in 45 seconds

Interviewers do not mark you on whether your view matches theirs. They mark whether you can commit to a position, explain the mechanism, back it with a dated fact, concede the strongest counterpoint and say what it means. A view without a mechanism sounds like a guess. A view without a concession sounds naive.

DON'T SAY THIS

"There are pros and cons on both sides." It is true and it scores nothing. Give both sides, then choose. "It depends" is acceptable only if you immediately say on what, and which way you lean.

Debate 1: Will AI break the consulting pyramid?

Case that it will

AI drafts the research, slides and code that juniors used to produce. Big Four UK graduate intakes fell between 2023 and 2024 (KPMG -29%, Deloitte -18%) [S143] [Reported]. A recruiter says the Big Four want AI "to replicate junior work more cost-effectively" [S162]. Queen's Tower Advisory, founded by Mark Bunker, formerly of Deloitte, reportedly targets teams of "20 per cent humans, 80 per cent AI agents" [S164] [Reported]. Deloitte UK's fee-earners fell about 5% in FY26 while revenue rose 2% [S93] [Confirmed].

Case that it won't

Clients' main AI barriers are data security (40%), skills (34%) and governance (28%), which need people [S240] [Reported]. Deloitte still hired more than 1,950 graduates, apprentices and interns in FY26 [S407]. MCA members raised graduate hiring 10% in 2025 [S150]. A commentator found that despite billions spent, firms still staff and bill the old way [S163] [Reported]. Firms need juniors today to have managers in eight years [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. Darren Graves: "As AI makes intelligence increasingly abundant, the role our people play will matter more, not less. AI can't replace lived experience or replicate the judgement and unwritten know-how built..." [S407] [Confirmed]. Hayley McKelvey: "The challenge is no longer getting people to use GenAI, it's how businesses meet this growing demand in a way that is secure, responsible and creates lasting value" [S243].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "AI reshapes the pyramid rather than breaking it. The base gets narrower and the junior job changes from producing first drafts to checking and judging them. Deloitte UK grew profit 14% on 2% revenue while fee-earners fell about 5%, which is that shift starting. The risk I'd watch is training: if juniors never build the analysis themselves, where do future managers come from?"

How to hold it if challenged. If told "AI will replace analysts entirely", go back to mechanism: clients pay for accountable judgement and for change that sticks inside their organisation, and Deloitte Australia's refund shows what happens when AI output goes unchecked [S316]. Concede that numbers at the base will probably keep falling.

Debate 2: Should the Big Four split audit from consulting?

Case for splitting

Independence rules block consulting at PIE audit clients: a whitelist and a 70% fee cap [S180] [Confirmed]. Conflicts are the challengers' main pitch: Unity Advisory sells "no conflicts" [S167]. Regulators already forced operational separation inside the firms by 30 June 2024 [S183, S184]. A split would let consulting raise outside capital for AI, as PE-backed rivals do [S168] [Inferred].

Case against

The mix smooths income: in FY25 Deloitte UK consulting fell 10% but total revenue fell only 1% because tax and audit grew [S305] [Reported]. EY's attempt ("Project Everest") was abandoned on 12 April 2023 after a reported US$600m cost [S141] [Reported]. The UK dropped the Audit Reform Bill on 20 January 2026, so no law is forcing a split [S185]. Breadth is a recruiting and client selling point [S308].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. Global CEO Joe Ucuzoglu, after EY's split collapsed in 2023, said keeping the model was "not even a close call" and that previous separations had "never once played out as intended" [S308] [Reported]. In 2022 Deloitte called split reports "categorically untrue" [S309] [Reported].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "I'd keep them together, because the portfolio works: FY25 showed audit and tax cushioning a 10% consulting fall. The cost is real, since consulting cannot serve many large audit clients, but operational separation already deals with the quality concern, and EY's failed split shows how hard and expensive unwinding is."

How to hold it if challenged. If pushed on conflicts, accept the cost openly and name it precisely (whitelist, 70% cap, separation). Then argue it is a constraint to manage, not a reason to split. Do not claim the conflict is imaginary.

Debate 3: Is the government right to cut consultancy spend?

Case for cutting

Test and Trace paid Deloitte staff about £1,000 a day each, some seniors up to £6,624 [S332] [Confirmed], while only 17% of people got results within 24 hours against a 90% target [S332] [Reported]. Consultants were still 45% of central staff in April 2021 [S333]. The NAO says government "does not collect data on how it uses consultants" [S173]. Mazzucato and Collington argue over-reliance weakens state capability [S337] [Reported].

Case against

The NAO found 86% of survey respondents said consultants made valuable contributions [S334] [Confirmed]. Government explained use by "skills shortages" and "comparatively low salaries in the civil service" [S333]. Cuts may shift spend into "professional services" or "contingent labour" (Cat Little) [S172]. Spend did not actually fall on some measures: £1.44bn of contracts in Labour's first year [S174]; £3.7bn record public sector spend in 2024/25 [S335] [Reported].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. No direct Deloitte UK leadership comment on the spend controls was found in the research. Use the firm's results framing instead: growth came from technology delivery, ERP and cyber [S407].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Right aim, blunt tool. Cutting advice that civil servants could do is sensible. But departments still need data and digital delivery they cannot hire fast enough, which is why the Home Office still bought up to £200m of data work from Deloitte. The better test is whether each contract leaves capability behind. I'd want Deloitte to be measured on that."

How to hold it if challenged. If told "you would say that, applying to Deloitte", agree that the incentive exists, and point to the evidence on both sides, including Test and Trace's poor 24-hour figure. Credibility comes from conceding the bad example.

Debate 4: Is outcome-based pricing good for clients?

Case for

It aligns incentives: the firm earns when the client gains. McKinsey reportedly ties a quarter to a third of fees to outcomes [S163, S164] [Reported]. Government frameworks already allow risk-and-reward pricing (MCF4) [S117] [Confirmed]. If AI makes work faster, day rates reward slowness; outcome fees do not [Inferred].

Case against

Outcomes are hard to measure and attribute: did revenue rise because of the project or the market? Firms price in risk, so outcome deals can cost more [Inferred]. Progress is "sluggish" [S163]. Deloitte's accounts show even fixed-fee work is managed by timesheets at charge-out rates [S93] [Confirmed], so the internal model is still time-based.

What Deloitte's leaders have said. No public Deloitte UK statement on outcome pricing was found. Deloitte does sell multi-year managed services ("Operate"), named as an area of significant growth [S120] [Confirmed].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Good for clients where the outcome is measurable and within the firm's control, such as cost savings on a process Deloitte runs. Risky where outcomes depend on the client's own decisions. So I'd expect a mix: outcome fees on managed services and automation, time-based fees on strategy and complex change."

How to hold it if challenged. If asked "so should all fees be outcome-based?", say no and give a mechanism: a firm cannot accept risk for results it does not control, and if it does, it will charge a premium for that risk.

Debate 5: Offshore delivery centres or UK regional centres?

Case for offshore

Lower cost. Capgemini runs 66% of its staff offshore [S132] [Reported]. Deloitte's US-India organisation (USI) is reported at over 100,000 people [S324] [Reported]. Big Four firms are moving more work to India, Malaysia and the Philippines [S162] [Reported]. Revenue per head shows the effect: Accenture about US$91k vs Deloitte about US$149k globally [S129, S120] [Inferred].

Case for UK regional centres

Security clearance and data sovereignty for public sector and defence; same time zone; cheaper than London. Deloitte opened Belfast, Cardiff, Manchester and Newcastle centres from June 2025, starting with 750 technologists and aiming for 1,500 within three years [S254, S323] [Confirmed]. 44% of the 2026 early-career intake is outside London [S231] [Reported]. The EU AI Act and data rules raise the value of local delivery [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. Then T&T managing partner Rob Cullen said the centres accelerate "our ambition to become the largest onshore technology capability in UK" [S323] [Reported]. Whether UK engagements use USI was not established in the research.

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Both, by work type. Commodity build and testing will keep going offshore on cost. But Deloitte UK's biggest client is government, where clearance and data location matter, so onshore regional centres are a real competitive advantage, not only a cost move. They also widen who can join, which is good for hiring."

How to hold it if challenged. If told "onshore is just more expensive", accept it on unit cost, then argue total cost: rework, security vetting and time-zone delays. If you are willing to work in a regional centre, say so; it is a credible signal.

Debate 6: Should graduates fear AI?

Case for worry

Fewer seats: Big Four intakes fell [S143]; Deloitte's 2026 early-career intake of 1,353 is flat against about 1,400 in 2025 [S231, S232] [Reported]. UK CFOs say AI is the second-biggest dampener on graduate hiring after cost control [S224] [Confirmed]. Accountancy graduate adverts fell 44% [S162].

Case against

Firms are redesigning training, not abolishing it: juniors are pushed into judgement work earlier [S229]. Deloitte promoted over 6,000 of 25,000 people and raised in-grade pay over 4% [S407]. 93% of CFOs expect digital investment to rise [S224], which needs people to deliver it. Graduates who use AI well become more productive earlier [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. Graves: people will "matter more, not less" [S407]. Starters are introduced to the firm's GenAI platform, PairD, during induction [S232] [Confirmed].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Not fear, but adjust. The entry bar rises because the first job now starts at checking and judging, not producing. That favours graduates who can question an AI output, explain it to a client and spot what is missing. That's the skill I'm trying to show."

How to hold it if challenged. If asked "why hire you rather than use AI?", answer with accountability and context: AI produces drafts; a client needs someone who understands their organisation and will stand behind the answer.

Debate 7: Is Deloitte's EMEA merger smart?

Case for

Scale to invest: Deloitte EMEA combines 16 firms with 132,000 people, 6,000 partners and €20bn revenue from 1 June 2026, with more than €1.5bn of incremental investment over four years in GenAI, sovereign cloud and sector solutions [S124] [Confirmed]. Clients want cross-border delivery. KPMG merged its UK and Swiss firms in 2024 [S140], so rivals are consolidating too.

Case against

Bigger partnerships decide more slowly; EY's split failed partly on member-firm opposition [S310] [Reported]. EMEA growth was only 3.5% in FY26, behind Asia Pacific's 7.5% [S221] [Confirmed]. Local client relationships could weaken. Integration takes management attention in a difficult market [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. Richard Houston: "Deloitte EMEA uniquely strengthens our ability to invest at scale across borders to accelerate innovation in areas that matter most to our clients" [S124]. Joe Ucuzoglu: "Our clients expect leading expertise wherever it is based, alongside seamless cross-border and technology-enabled delivery at speed" [S306] [Reported]. Country firms keep "local market responsibility" [S306].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Smart on investment logic. AI and sovereign cloud need spending on a scale no single country firm can fund easily, and a partnership cannot raise outside capital. The test will be whether it can staff cross-border work quickly without losing the local relationships that win it. It's too early to judge results, since it only started on 1 June."

How to hold it if challenged. If told it is "just empire-building", point to the specific investment commitment and the partnership capital constraint. Concede that the benefits are promised, not yet proven.

Debate 8: Partnership or company: which funds AI better?

Case for outside capital

Listed or PE-backed firms can invest ahead of profits. Accenture returned US$11.5bn to shareholders in FY26 and still funds acquisitions [S129] [Confirmed]. Grant Thornton UK (Cinven) and Unity Advisory (up to $300m from Warburg Pincus) raised outside money [S168, S167]. Partners who retire soon may resist long-term investment that cuts their payouts [Inferred].

Case for partnership

No quarterly earnings pressure: the firm absorbed FY25's consulting fall without a share-price shock [S305] [Inferred]. Deloitte still committed US$3bn through 2030 to AI and modernisation [S221] and raised UK technology investment to £180m [S407] [Confirmed]. Owners are the people serving clients, which aligns incentives on quality [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. The US$3bn commitment and the EMEA €1.5bn plan are the firm's answer: invest from pooled partner profits at larger scale [S221, S124].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "A large partnership can fund AI, but only by pooling across countries, which is exactly what EMEA does. PE money moves faster, but it comes with an exit date. For a firm whose product is trust and independence, I think the partnership is the better long-term owner, provided partners accept lower payouts in heavy investment years."

How to hold it if challenged. If told "PE firms will out-invest you", compare scales: US$3bn committed globally against up to $300m for Unity. Concede that speed of decision is the partnership's weak point.

Debate 9: Should Deloitte push harder into managed services?

Case for

Recurring revenue: half of Accenture's US$74.18bn is managed services, which grew 6% while its consulting grew 3% [S129] [Confirmed]. Long contracts smooth the cycle that hurt Deloitte UK consulting in FY25 [S305]. AI makes running processes cheaper, so the margin goes to whoever runs them [Inferred]. Deloitte already names "Operate" as an area of significant growth [S120].

Case against

Independence rules bar many managed services at audit clients [S180] [Inferred]. Lower margins and heavy offshore staffing: Accenture's revenue per head is about US$91k against Deloitte's US$149k [S129, S120] [Inferred]. It ties up capital and people for years, and pulls the brand from advice toward outsourcing [Inferred].

What Deloitte's leaders have said. The FY26 global release highlights Operate growth; no UK-specific statement on managed services strategy was found [S120].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Suggested stance: "Yes, selectively: where Deloitte has built a system, running it afterwards keeps the relationship and the data, and AI will make that work more profitable. But it should not chase Accenture's volume model, because audit independence limits the market and its edge is advice plus delivery."

How to hold it if challenged. If asked "why not copy Accenture outright?", give the structural reason (audit independence limits who you can run services for) and the economic one (different revenue-per-head model).
