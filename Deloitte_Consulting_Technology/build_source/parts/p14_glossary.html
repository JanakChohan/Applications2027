Part 14

Glossary

Every term of art in this pack, in plain English, in one alphabetical list. Use it as a lookup while you read, and as a self-test before an interview.

Each entry gives a short definition. Where a term changes how you should talk or act at Deloitte, a line starting "Why it matters here" says how. Source markers appear on Deloitte-specific and regulatory facts; general consulting vocabulary carries none because it is standard usage. Dates and figures are as of 4 October 2026.

Terms A to Z

70% cap
The rule that non-audit fees from a public interest entity audit client cannot exceed 70% of the average audit fee over the previous three years [S182, S364]. Why it matters here: it limits how much consulting Deloitte can sell to companies it audits.
ACT method
Deloitte's Global Code frame for ethical dilemmas: Assess the situation, Consider and Consult, Take action. The Code adds "Speaking up is taking action!" [S340]. Why it matters here: it is a ready-made structure for any ethics scenario question.
Agentic AI
AI systems that take actions (send, book, update, approve) rather than only producing text. Deloitte's Tech Trends 2026 says only 11% of organisations have such systems in production [S244]. Why it matters here: the hard part is redesigning the process, which is consulting work.
Agile
A way of delivering projects in short cycles, testing with users and adjusting as you learn, instead of fixing every requirement up front. Scrum is the most common agile framework.
Alliance partner
A technology vendor whose software a consultancy implements. Deloitte UK lists 14, including SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, AWS, Google and Oracle [S319]. Why it matters here: much T&T graduate work runs on these platforms.
ARGA (Audit, Reporting and Governance Authority)
The regulator that was meant to replace the FRC. The government abandoned the Audit Reform Bill on 20 January 2026, so ARGA will not be created [S185, S186].
Audit & Assurance (A&A)
Deloitte's audit business. It gives independent opinions on whether company accounts are true and fair. UK revenue £998m in FY26 [S407].
Audit committee
A board committee of a company, mostly independent directors, that oversees its auditor. Non-audit services to a public interest entity need its approval [S364].
Audit Governance Board (AGB)
Deloitte UK's majority-independent board that oversees the audit practice, set up under operational separation. It continues under the new UK Board [S93, S94].
Basel 3.1
The UK version of the final global bank capital rules. The PRA's final rules take effect on 1 January 2027 [S196, S197]. Why it matters here: banks buy help with data, models and reporting to comply.
Bench
Consultants not currently staffed on a client project. Time on the bench is not chargeable, so long bench periods hurt utilisation.
Big Four
The four largest professional services networks: Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG. Deloitte is the largest by revenue, at US$74.5bn in FY26 [S70, S126].
BPSS (Baseline Personnel Security Standard)
The UK government's basic pre-employment check (identity, right to work, employment history, unspent convictions) for people with access to government assets. It is not a formal clearance but underpins SC [S369, S370]. Deloitte UK does not say it is required for all graduates.
BrightStart
Deloitte UK's school-leaver apprenticeship route. 182 BrightStart apprentices were among the September 2026 intake [S231, S260].
Bribery Act 2010, section 7
Makes a commercial organisation guilty if someone associated with it pays a bribe to win business for it, unless it had "adequate procedures" to prevent bribery [S355, S357]. Why it matters here: it is why gifts and hospitality rules are strict.
Chargeable hours
Hours recorded against a client code and billed. Deloitte's Code requires hours to be recorded "timely and accurately" [S340].
Change control
The process for logging, assessing and approving or rejecting any change to an agreed project scope [S377]. Why it matters here: the correct answer when a client asks for "one small extra thing".
Change management
Helping people adopt a new system or way of working through communication, training and support. Technology projects often fail here, not in the build.
Chartered Management Consultant (ChMC)
A professional accreditation run with the MCA. Over 2,800 holders in the 2026 MCA report [S150].
Chief AI Officer
A new seat on Deloitte UK's Executive, held by Hayley McKelvey from June 2026, responsible for AI strategy "across client services and internal operations" [S407].
Cloud migration
Moving a client's systems and data from its own data centres to a cloud provider such as AWS, Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud.
Conflict check
The test every proposed engagement goes through before acceptance. Deloitte declines work where a conflict "cannot be eliminated or reduced to an acceptable level" [S94].
Consumer Duty
FCA rules requiring retail financial firms to deliver good outcomes for customers. In force 31 July 2023 for open products and 31 July 2024 for closed products [S200].
Contextual recruitment
Judging grades against a candidate's school and circumstances. Deloitte UK announced it in September 2015 with Rare Recruitment, alongside school- and university-blind interviews [S267, S268]. Whether it still runs in the 2027 cycle is not confirmed.
Cutover
The planned switch from an old system to a new one, often over a weekend, with a rehearsed plan and a rollback option if it fails.
Data controller and data processor
Under UK GDPR the controller decides why and how personal data is used; the processor handles it on the controller's behalf. A processor must tell the controller about a breach without undue delay [S354]. A consultancy is often the processor.
Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA)
The UK law amending data protection rules. Royal Assent 19 June 2025; most changes in force from 5 February 2026 [S194, S195].
Delivery centre
A lower-cost site where project work is done at scale. Deloitte UK opened technology centres in Belfast, Cardiff, Manchester and Newcastle from June 2025, starting with 750 technologists and aiming for 1,500 [S254].
Deloitte Digital
Deloitte's brand for customer, marketing and creative work, built partly through agency purchases such as Heat (2016) and Acne (2017) [S405]. Why it matters here: it links creative design to technology delivery.
Deloitte EMEA
A combined firm of Deloitte LLP and other European, Middle Eastern and African member firms, live from 1 June 2026: about €20bn revenue, 132,000 people and over €1.5bn of planned investment [S93, S124]. Richard Houston is its CEO [S407].
Deloitte LLP
The UK firm, a limited liability partnership registered in England and Wales, number OC303675, at 1 New Street Square, London [S407].
Deloitte NSE (North and South Europe)
The regional holding entity that was Deloitte LLP's parent until 31 May 2026. It served no clients and has been replaced by Deloitte EMEA [S93, S94].
Deloitte University EMEA
Deloitte's residential learning campus, moved in 2024 from near Brussels to a purpose-built site near Paris [S283].
Distributable profit
The profit shared among equity partners, on a management accounts basis. £899m in FY26 for the UK and Switzerland [S93, S407].
DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)
EU regulation on ICT risk, incident reporting, resilience testing and oversight of technology suppliers in financial services. Applies from 17 January 2025 [S201].
DTTL (Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited)
The UK private company limited by guarantee that coordinates the Deloitte network. It "does not provide services to clients", and member firms cannot bind each other [S83].
ECCTA failure to prevent fraud
Section 199 of the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023: a large organisation commits an offence if an associated person commits fraud to benefit it, unless it had reasonable prevention procedures. In force 1 September 2025 [S361, S362].
Enabling Functions
Deloitte UK's name for its internal support teams, such as HR, IT, finance and Quality, Risk & Security, which runs conflict and independence checks [S330].
Engagement
One piece of paid client work, defined by a contract, a team and a fee. Also called a project.
Equity partner
An owner of the LLP who contributes capital and takes a share of profit. Deloitte UK had 784 at the 2026 year end, a record [S407].
ERP (enterprise resource planning)
Core business software for finance, HR and supply chain, such as SAP S/4HANA. ERP programmes are large, long and risky.
Ethical Standard
The FRC's rulebook on auditor independence. The current version took effect on 15 December 2024. Its test is whether an "objective, reasonable and informed third party" would see a threat [S364].
EU AI Act
The EU's risk-based AI law, in force 1 August 2024. The 2026 Digital Omnibus moved the main high-risk deadline to 2 December 2027 [S192, S193, S234]. Why it matters here: it creates AI governance work for UK firms selling into the EU.
Final Stage Assessment
Stage 4 of Deloitte UK's process. For graduates: an in-person Topic Discussion (30 to 40 minutes) and a Skills and Motivation Interview (about 50 minutes). The Summer Vacation Scheme final stage is a virtual interview only [S260].
Financial year (Deloitte)
Deloitte's year ends on 31 May, so "FY26" means June 2025 to May 2026 [S93].
Fixed fee
A contract with an agreed total price. Deloitte still tracks progress through hours at charge-out rates [S93]. Why it matters here: overruns come out of the firm's margin.
Framework agreement
A pre-approved list of suppliers that public buyers can call off work from without a full tender each time. MCF4 is the main one for consultancy [S191].
FRC (Financial Reporting Council)
The UK regulator of auditors and accountants. It sets the Ethical Standard, inspects audits and can fine firms. It fined Deloitte a record £15m over Autonomy in 2020 [S105].
GCA (Government Commercial Agency)
The UK government's central buying agency, renamed from Crown Commercial Service on 1 April 2026 [S190].
Generative AI (GenAI)
AI that produces new text, images or code from a prompt. Large language models are the main kind.
Global Principles of Business Conduct
Deloitte's global code of ethics (October 2025 edition), covering integrity, confidentiality, anti-corruption, respect and responsible AI. Each member firm adds local rules [S340].
Hallucination
When an AI model states something false with confidence, such as an invented citation. Deloitte Australia partly refunded a government report in 2025 after fabricated references were found [S113]. Why it matters here: you must check every AI-assisted source and number.
Human in the loop
A person reviews and owns AI output before it is used. Deloitte's own guidance says accountability for AI output is "squarely a human domain" [S347].
Hypothesis-driven
Starting with a likely answer and testing it with data, instead of collecting everything first. It saves time and keeps analysis focused.
Immersive Online Assessment
Stage 2 of Deloitte UK's process: a behavioural part (rank responses to workplace scenarios) and a cognitive part (numerical and verbal reasoning), with a strengths report afterwards [S260].
Independence
An auditor's freedom from interests that could bias its opinion. The Ethical Standard bans any service that would put the firm in a management role at an audit client [S364]. Why it matters here: consultants must not make decisions for an audit client.
Industry (Deloitte)
The client sector a professional aligns to: Consumer; Energy, Resources & Industrials; Financial Services; Government & Public Services; Life Sciences & Health Care; Technology, Media & Telecom [S301].
Inside information
Precise, non-public information about a listed company that would likely move its share price if published (UK MAR Article 7) [S358]. Sharing it outside normal duties is unlawful [S359].
Integrity & AI guidance
Deloitte UK's rules for applicants on using AI in assessments. Breaking them is treated as a breach of process [S261, S351]. Why it matters here: write your own answers.
ISQM (UK) 1
The standard requiring an audit firm to run a system of quality management. Deloitte UK's 2025 review found two deficiencies, "neither severe nor pervasive" [S94].
Issue tree
A diagram that breaks a big question into smaller ones. Diagnostic trees ask "why"; solution trees ask "how". Branches should be MECE [S376].
Job Simulation
Stage 3 of Deloitte UK's process: a recorded, one-way assessment with ranking, multiple-choice, written and video answers, including scenarios that mention AI [S260].
Lead Client Service Partner (LCSP)
The partner who owns Deloitte's whole relationship with a large client and coordinates account plans and pursuits across offerings [S322]. Sources describe the US firm; the UK use is assumed similar [Inferred].
Leverage
The number of staff per partner. Deloitte UK has about 16.5 staff per partner (FY26, own calculation) [S88, S93]. More leverage means more billable hours behind each partner's profit.
LLM (large language model)
An AI model trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate language, such as Anthropic's Claude or OpenAI's GPT models.
LLP (limited liability partnership)
A UK legal form where partners (members) own the business and share profit, but are not personally liable for its debts in the way old-style partners were [S93].
Local currency growth
Growth with exchange-rate effects stripped out. Deloitte's FY26 global revenue grew 3.8% in local currency but 5.7% in US dollars [S70].
Managed services
Running a system or process for a client on an ongoing contract, often for years. Revenue is recurring, and the provider carries operational risk.
MAR (UK Market Abuse Regulation)
The law banning insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information and market manipulation [S358, S360].
MCA (Management Consultancies Association)
The UK consulting trade body. It put UK consulting fee income at £21.8bn in 2025 [S150].
MCF4 (Management Consultancy Framework Four)
The government's main consultancy framework, run by the GCA from 29 July 2025 to 28 July 2027, with 10 lots and 219 suppliers [S117, S191].
MECE
"Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive": split a problem into parts that do not overlap and together cover everything [S375].
Member firm
A legally separate national or regional Deloitte firm in the DTTL network. Each is liable only for its own acts [S83].
Monitor Deloitte
Deloitte's strategy consulting brand, from the 2013 purchase of Monitor Group [S134]. Since the 2024 restructure it sits within SR&T [S307].
Multidisciplinary model
One firm offering audit, tax, consulting and deals advice under one brand. Deloitte kept consulting in 2003 when its rivals sold theirs [S104].
Non-audit services
Anything an audit firm sells to an audit client other than the audit. For public interest entities only a short permitted list is allowed [S364].
Offering
A Deloitte practice defined by what it does. UK T&T offerings include Enterprise, Technology & Performance; Engineering, AI & Data; Cyber; Customer; Human Capital; and Defence & Security [S320].
Offshore and nearshore delivery
Doing work in lower-cost countries far away (offshore, such as India) or nearby (nearshore). The Big Four are moving more work to India, Malaysia and the Philippines [S162].
Operate
Deloitte's managed-services offer. The FY26 global release names it an area of "significant growth" [S70].
Operational resilience
FCA and PRA rules requiring firms to keep important business services within set impact tolerances during severe disruption. Transition ended 31 March 2025 [S199].
Operational separation
The FRC requirement that the Big Four ring-fence their UK audit practices with separate governance and finances. All four met it by 2024 [S183, S184].
Outcome-based pricing
Fees linked to results delivered (cost saved, revenue gained) rather than hours worked. Growing slowly across consulting [S163].
PairD
Deloitte's internal generative AI assistant, rolled out in the UK from October 2023 and to 75,000 people in Europe and the Middle East by January 2024 [S328]. UK staff sent about 1 million prompts a month in FY25 [S85].
Partner annuity
A pension paid to retired Deloitte UK partners out of current profit. Closed to new entrants from 1 June 2021; liabilities £1,545m, payments mainly capped at 8% of operating profit [S93].
PEP (profit per equity partner)
Distributable profit divided by equity partners. Deloitte UK's average was £1.125m in FY26 [S407].
Personal independence
Checks every joiner must clear before starting, restricting financial interests in audit clients [S261]. Why it matters here: you may need to sell some shares.
PIE (public interest entity)
Listed companies, banks and insurers, which face the strictest audit rules, including the whitelist and the 70% cap [S182, S364].
PIRG (Public Interest Review Group)
Deloitte UK's panel for engagements with high public-interest or reputational risk. In FY25 it considered 32 matters and declined 9% [S94].
PMO (programme management office)
The team that runs plans, governance, reporting and risk logs for a large programme. A common first role for graduates.
Procurement Act 2023
The UK public procurement law, in force from 24 February 2025 [S190]. Buyers must identify and manage conflicts of interest [S366, S368].
Pyramid (staffing)
The shape of a consulting firm: few partners at the top, many juniors at the base. AI threatens the base by automating junior tasks [S162].
Pyramid Principle
Barbara Minto's rule for clear writing: put the main point first, then group supporting points beneath it [S374].
RACI
A table showing who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each task, ideally with one Accountable person [S379].
RAID log
A project list of Risks, Assumptions, Issues and Dependencies, reviewed regularly.
Rate card
The standard daily charge-out rate for each grade. Deloitte recognises revenue using hours at charge-out rates [S93]. Deloitte does not publish its rates.
Realisation
The share of the standard value of time that is actually billed and collected after discounts and write-offs.
RFP (request for proposal)
A buyer's formal invitation for firms to bid for a piece of work. The firm's reply, often with a presentation, is the pitch.
Salaried partner
A partner by title who is an employee, not an owner. Deloitte UK had 654 on an FTE basis in FY26 [S88].
SaaS (software as a service)
Software rented by subscription and run by the vendor in the cloud, such as Salesforce or Workday.
SC (Security Check)
UK government clearance for posts needing long-term access to SECRET material. It builds on BPSS [S370].
Scope
What a project will and will not deliver. Defining it clearly prevents later disputes [S378]. Scope creep is unpaid growth in that list.
SCQ (Situation, Complication, Question)
Minto's way to open a piece of communication by stating what is true, what has changed, and the question that raises [S374].
Scrum
An agile framework with a team of 10 or fewer, three roles (Developers, a Product Owner and a Scrum Master) and work in Sprints [S373].
Shared Values
Deloitte's five values: Lead the way; Serve with integrity; Take care of each other; Foster inclusion; Collaborate for measurable impact [S266].
Skills and Motivation Interview
The roughly 50-minute final-stage interview on "alignment with the capabilities we look for" [S260]. Expect strengths and "tell me about a time" questions.
SOW (statement of work)
The contract document that says what will be delivered, by when, by whom and for what fee.
Speak Up
Deloitte's third-party-run channel for raising ethics concerns, anonymously if needed. Retaliation is not tolerated [S340, S343].
Sprint
A fixed cycle of one month or less in Scrum. Changes that endanger the Sprint Goal are not made mid-sprint [S373].
SR&T Advisory (Strategy, Risk & Transactions)
One of Deloitte's two consulting businesses, covering strategy, risk and deals work. UK revenue £913m in FY26 [S407].
Stakeholder map
A grid plotting people by power and interest, to decide who to manage closely and who to keep informed [S380].
STAR (or STARR)
An answer structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result, plus Reflection. Prospects suggests preparing five to eight examples [S382].
Strengths-based interview
Questions about what you enjoy and do well naturally, rather than only past examples. Deloitte's online assessment is designed to find "your unique strengths" [S350].
Summer Vacation Scheme
Deloitte UK's summer internship, using the same process as graduate roles but with a virtual interview-only final stage [S260].
Systems integrator (SI)
A firm that makes several software products work together inside a client's organisation.
T+1
Settling share trades one business day after the trade. The UK plans to move to T+1 by 11 October 2027 [S198].
T&M (time and materials)
Billing for hours actually worked at agreed rates, plus expenses. The client carries the overrun risk [S93].
T&T (Technology & Transformation)
Deloitte's largest business, covering technology implementation, data, AI, cyber and transformation. UK revenue £1.75bn in FY26, up 4% [S407].
Target operating model (TOM)
A design for how an organisation should work in future: people, processes, technology and governance.
Tax & Legal
Deloitte's tax compliance, tax advice and legal business. UK revenue £1.36bn in FY26 [S407].
Topic Discussion
The first final-stage exercise: a 30 to 40 minute two-way discussion with an assessor on a topic you research in advance [S260].
Transparency report
An annual report the audit firm must publish on governance, audit quality and risk. Deloitte UK's 2025 report rates its reputational risk "Very High" [S94].
Trustworthy AI
Deloitte's framework for responsible AI, launched 2020. Its dimensions include fair and impartial, transparent and explainable, responsible and accountable, safe and secure, robust and reliable, and respectful of privacy [S346, S348].
UAT (user acceptance testing)
The final test in which business users check a system meets their needs before they accept it [S381]. Users, not the build team, sign it off.
UK Board
Deloitte UK's top governance body from 1 June 2026, replacing the UK Oversight Board. Jane Whitlock chairs it [S93, S407].
UK GDPR
The UK's main data protection law. Notifiable breaches go to the ICO within 72 hours; top fines are £17.5m or 4% of turnover [S353, S354].
UK SRS (UK Sustainability Reporting Standards)
UK versions of the ISSB climate and sustainability disclosure standards, published 25 February 2026 for voluntary use [S202].
USI (US-India delivery)
The US Deloitte firm's delivery organisation in India, reported at over 100,000 people [S324] [Reported]. Its role in UK work is not established.
Utilisation
The share of a consultant's available hours that are chargeable to clients. Deloitte does not publish its targets.
Whitelist
The short list of non-audit services an auditor may provide to a public interest entity audit client, mostly audit-related or required by law [S180, S364].
Zora AI
Deloitte's suite of AI agents for finance, HR, procurement and other functions, launched March 2025 with NVIDIA technology and sold on subscription [S327].
