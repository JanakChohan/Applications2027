KEY TERM

"Consulting" versus "Advisory": on the early-careers website, "Consulting" is the label for teams inside Technology & Transformation, and "Advisory" is the label for teams inside Strategy, Risk & Transactions [S12, S15, S57] [Confirmed]. Deloitte's own results footnote says its "advisory businesses include the Technology & Transformation (T&T) business as well as the Strategy, Risk & Transactions Advisory (SRTA) business" [S407] [Confirmed]. So the press may call both "advisory". In interviews, use the business names.

Offerings: the teams inside a business

Each business is split into offerings. An offering is a team that owns a type of service: its methods, its software partnerships and its people. Inside T&T, Deloitte UK's website lists these offerings [S12, S40] [Confirmed]:

  • Customer (Deloitte Digital): marketing, commerce, sales and service technology, such as customer relationship management systems.
  • Cyber: security strategy, defence and resilience, digital trust and privacy, and managed cyber operations.
  • Engineering, AI & Data (EAD): software engineering, data platforms and AI, plus Operations, Industry & Domain Solutions.
  • Enterprise Technology & Performance (ET&P): the big "back-office" platforms and the strategy around them. It includes Finance Transformation, SAP, Oracle, ServiceNow, Supply Chain & Network Operations, and Technology Strategy & Transformation (TST), the team in THE JD.
  • Human Capital: HR strategy, workforce and organisation change, HR systems, Major Programmes, and actuarial work.
KEY TERM

Persona: a recruiting family Deloitte uses to group early-careers roles by type of work. The current consulting personas are Business Transformation Consulting, Enterprise Applications Consulting, Technology Strategy Consulting, Finance Transformation Consulting and Risk & Controls Consulting [S2, S4, S7, S8, S9, S10] [Confirmed]. A persona tells you the kind of work. The team name after the dash tells you which offering you land in. "Technology Strategy Consulting - Technology Strategy & Transformation" means persona first, team second.

KEY TERM

Industry: the client sector a team serves. Deloitte UK's industries are Consumer; Energy, Resources & Industrials; Financial Services; Government & Public Services; Life Sciences & Healthcare; Technology, Media & Telecommunications; plus Private Equity [S12, S26] [Confirmed]. Offerings (what you do) and industries (who you do it for) cross like the rows and columns of a grid. That grid is called a matrix, and it decides who you work with.

KEY TERM

Engagement: one piece of paid client work with an agreed scope, team, price and end date. Consultants say "project" and "engagement" interchangeably. Analyst: Deloitte's name for a graduate joiner in T&T ("As a Graduate (we call you Analysts)") [S4] [Confirmed].

Two older names have faded. "Strategy & Operations" no longer appears as an early-careers team name. "Government & Public Services" now appears as an industry, and "Defence & Security" appears inside many experienced-hire job titles [S40, S49] [Confirmed]. If an older blog says "Deloitte Consulting has five service lines", it predates the four-business model.

What the firm is hiring for right now

Job postings are one of the few public windows into a private partnership. On 4 October 2026 Deloitte UK listed 591 experienced-hire postings. We parsed 551 of them and counted them by business and city [S49]. Figure 14 shows the result. Treat it as a sample: 40 postings could not be read, and a posting is a vacancy, not a person.

Two readings matter. First, T&T is where the hiring is: just over half of all parsed experienced postings [S49] [Confirmed] (our count). That shows where the firm is investing, not that T&T is half the firm. Second, London is the main T&T hub, but Manchester is a strong second, and Bristol, Belfast, Edinburgh and Glasgow all hire into T&T [S49] [Confirmed] (count) [Inferred] (reading). If a London interviewer asks whether you would work with colleagues in other cities, the honest answer is that you almost certainly will.

The JD decoded, line by line

The JD's lines collapse into ten duties, labelled D1 to D10. The same labels are used in the scenario playbook later in the pack, so you can trace any practice scenario back to a line of the posting. Each duty below has five parts: what it means day to day, who you deal with, the tools, what good looks like, and what the line reveals about the firm.

A word on confidence. Deloitte does not publish an analyst's diary. The "day to day" content is built from Deloitte's own job postings, staff profiles and service pages, and is marked [Inferred] where it is our reasoning. Quotes are from the postings themselves.

D1. Senior client engagement and rapport

"We work with their most senior leaders and top-level executives"; "How to build rapport with clients and ensure they have confidence in your capability" [S2]

Day to dayYou will rarely lead a conversation with a client's chief information officer (CIO) in year one. You prepare the pack the partner or director uses, sit in the room, take notes and chase the actions afterwards. Rapport at your level is built in small exchanges with the client's analysts and managers: answering a data question the same day, sending minutes within hours [Inferred].
Who you deal withClient: CIO, chief financial officer (CFO), chief operating officer (COO) and their direct reports. Deloitte: a partner who owns the relationship, a director or associate director who leads the engagement, a manager who runs the plan day to day [Inferred] (grade names [Confirmed] [S49, S66]).
ToolsPowerPoint meeting packs, Word minutes, Excel action logs, Microsoft Teams [Inferred].
What good looks likeReliability. Small promises kept. You know the client's organisation chart and who decides what. You never surprise your manager [Inferred].
What it revealsTST sells to the top of the client. Its strategy work can open the door to larger implementation work elsewhere in T&T [Inferred]. A T&T director describes a week with "a check-in with my client on a project, so we can manage any potential risks" and "preparing a bid to win new work" [S55] [Confirmed] (quote).

D2. The IT operating model

"helping organisations move away from IT, the department of computers, to technology as a business partner and enabler" [S2]

KEY TERM

Operating model: how a function is organised, staffed, funded, sourced (in-house or outsourced) and governed. A technology operating model answers "who does what in technology, who pays, and who decides". It is the first of TST's four market offerings [S2] [Confirmed].

Day to dayCurrent-state interviews with IT leaders; a capability map (a one-page grid of everything the IT function does); an IT cost baseline in Excel; summaries of supplier contracts; organisation charts in PowerPoint [Inferred].
Who you deal withThe CIO's leadership team, finance business partners who hold the IT cost data, procurement teams who hold the contracts [Inferred].
ToolsExcel for cost baselines and contract summaries, PowerPoint for maps and charts [Inferred].
What good looks likeA baseline that the client's finance team agrees with. If the starting numbers are disputed, every later recommendation is disputed too [Inferred].
What it reveals"Technology Sourcing & Commercial Management" is another TST offering [S2] [Confirmed]. Sourcing work (deciding what to outsource, to whom, on what contract) means TST analysts read contracts and compare suppliers [Inferred]. The old view of IT as a cost centre is what TST is paid to change.

D3. The digital core and the technology landscape

"create a digital core"; "Discovering how technologies support everything an organisation does, and which technologies work best for each part of a business" [S2]

KEY TERM

ERP (enterprise resource planning): one large software system that runs a company's core processes, such as finance, purchasing, supply chain and often HR. SAP and Oracle are the best-known vendors. The digital core is the ERP plus the other core platforms and data that everything else hangs off [Inferred] (from [S12, S40]).

Day to dayApplication inventories (a list of every system the client runs, with owner, cost and age); "keep, retire or replace" assessments; vendor comparisons [Inferred].
Who you deal withEnterprise architects on both sides; Deloitte's platform teams for SAP, Oracle, Workday or ServiceNow [Inferred].
ToolsExcel inventories, architecture diagrams, vendor documentation [Inferred]. Deloitte UK lists alliances with Adobe, Anaplan, Apple, AWS, Google, Informatica, MuleSoft, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, UiPath, Workday and Workiva [S12, S40] [Confirmed].
What good looks likeYou can explain in two sentences what each major system does and who depends on it [Inferred].
What it revealsDeloitte is both adviser and implementer. One posting claims "We are the number one global SAP delivery partner with over 200 implementations delivered to date" [S52] [Reported] (Deloitte's own claim). That raises a fair question: can an adviser with large software alliances recommend platforms objectively? Have a view ready [Inferred].

D4. The business case and value

"improving financial performance, accelerating speed to market, delivering continuous innovation, and entering exciting new fields like AI"; "Help assess technology investments, identify opportunities to improve value, agility, efficiency, and user experience" [S2]

KEY TERM

Business case: the document that justifies spending money on a change. It sets out costs, benefits, risks, timing and options, and usually a payback period (how long until the benefits repay the cost). Boards approve large technology programmes on the strength of it.

Day to dayBenchmarking IT spend against peers, application portfolio rationalisation, cost and benefit models in Excel, sensitivity tables ("what if the benefits arrive a year late?") [Inferred]. Deloitte UK's TST service page uses the same words as the JD: "increase the value, agility, efficiency and experience of technology investments" [S41] [Confirmed].
Who you deal withThe CFO's team and finance business partners, who will challenge every number [Inferred].
ToolsExcel models; benchmark data; PowerPoint summaries [Inferred].
What good looks likeEvery number traces to a source. Assumptions are written down and agreed. Each benefit has a named owner on the client side [Inferred].
What it revealsThe business case is the product. Clients spend millions only when someone shows the benefit. An analyst comfortable with Excel and basic finance is useful from week one [Inferred].

D5. Net zero and inclusive ways of working

"transforming their enterprises to make advances in sustainability and diversity and inclusion ... in ways that drive net zero and incorporate inclusive ways of working" [S2]

Day to dayLow daily weight for an Analyst. You might add an emissions line to a cloud migration case, or check that a new system works for users with accessibility needs [Inferred].
Who you deal withThe client's sustainability lead; Deloitte's Sustainability team, which sits in SR&T [S15] [Confirmed] (that it sits there).
ToolsThe same business case models, with sustainability measures added [Inferred].
What good looks likeYou can define net zero (cutting greenhouse gas emissions as far as possible and balancing the rest) and name one technology lever, such as retiring old data centres [Inferred].
What it revealsDeloitte wants people who connect technology to wider outcomes. It is also fertile ground for an opinion: where do technology and sustainability goals pull against each other, for example the energy demand of AI computing? [Inferred]

D6. Large-scale transformation, design through implementation

"you will work on large-scale technology transformations, from the design through to implementation" [S2]

KEY TERM

PMO (programme management office): the small team that keeps a large programme organised: plans, status reports, risks, budgets, governance meetings. RAID log: a running list of Risks, Assumptions, Issues and Dependencies, each with an owner and a date. Juniors often own the RAID log. Steering committee: the senior client and Deloitte group that meets regularly to take programme decisions.

Day to dayMost first-year time on large programmes goes to PMO work: RAID logs, status reports, plan updates, meeting packs, requirement logs and test tracking [Inferred]. Sister postings say it openly: "scheduling meetings and taking minutes, tracking deadlines, and preparing materials" (Cyber) [S9] and "materials for workshops, communications, project plans and progress reporting" (Customer Products) [S4] [Confirmed].
Who you deal withThe client programme director and workstream leads; your Deloitte manager; build teams on and offshore. An SAP posting asks for communication "with clients and colleagues both on and offshore" [S51]; a Workday posting mentions "engineering teams (onshore and offshore)" [S50] [Confirmed].
ToolsExcel, PowerPoint, Word, Jira or Azure DevOps for work tracking, Microsoft Teams [Inferred]. Deloitte UK advertises an "Atlassian Center of Excellence" (Atlassian makes Jira) [S49] [Confirmed].
What good looks likeThe RAID log is current, every action has an owner and a date, and the status report tells the truth even when the news is bad [Inferred].
What it revealsDeloitte sells the whole lifecycle, from the first strategy conversation to running the system afterwards. That is its pitch against pure strategy firms on one side and pure IT outsourcers on the other [Inferred].

The design-to-implementation span is why the same Analyst can have very different years. Figure 16 contrasts the two channels of work you could be staffed on.

D7. Requirements

"Working with individuals from our clients' business and IT teams to understand what the problems and needs are, and transforming those into technical requirements" [S2]

KEY TERM

Requirement: a precise, testable statement of what a system or process must do. "A manager must be able to approve expenses under £500 from a phone" is a requirement. "Make expenses easier" is not. A user story is a requirement written from the user's point of view: "As a manager, I want to ... so that ...".

Day to dayRun or support workshops; capture "as-is" processes (how work is done today); write requirements or user stories; map them to what the software can do [Inferred]. Finance Transformation describes "Getting to the bottom of client's problems through interactive workshops and labs" [S8] [Confirmed].
Who you deal withClient process owners (for example the head of accounts payable), client IT, Deloitte functional and technical leads [Inferred].
ToolsProcess maps, requirements trackers in Excel or Jira, workshop templates [Inferred].
What good looks likeRequirements that are testable, traced to a business objective and signed off by a named client owner [Inferred].
What it revealsTST is not purely strategy. Its graduates translate between business and technology people, which is the scarce skill on most programmes [Inferred].

D8. Documents and client materials

"Creating documents crucial to the delivery and execution of the programme, alongside materials presented back to clients" [S2]

Day to dayPowerPoint is the main output: steering committee packs, design documents, playbooks, training material [Inferred].
Who you deal withYour manager, who reviews; the client audience, who decides [Inferred].
ToolsPowerPoint and Word in Deloitte templates, with Excel behind every chart [Inferred].
What good looks likeOne message per slide, stated in the title. Numbers that tie to a source. No typos. Expect several rounds of review on early drafts [Inferred].
What it revealsClear writing is a core skill. The final-stage topic discussion and interview test the same thing: a clear point backed by evidence [S17] [Confirmed] (format).

D9. Firm-approved AI tools, used responsibly

"Using firm-approved AI tools responsibly to support client delivery" [S3, S2]

ToolsOnly tools the firm has approved and secured. Never a public chatbot for client material [Inferred] (from [S36, S345]).
What good looks likeYou check every fact, number and citation an AI tool produces before it reaches a client. A Deloitte article puts it plainly: "accountability is squarely a human domain" [S347] [Confirmed].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Prompt: TST or an implementation team such as HR Applications? For TST: broader exposure, senior clients, business-case skills that transfer anywhere. For an implementation team: a certifiable technical skill early, clearer daily output, strong demand for platform skills. Suggested stance: pick the one whose daily tasks you would enjoy in month three, not the one with the grander title, and say why in one sentence.

Day A: a technology operating model review (advisory)

The client is a retailer whose board thinks its IT function is too expensive and too slow. Deloitte has been hired for eight weeks to describe today's IT function and propose a better one. You are the Analyst on a team of four.

TimeWhat you doWhy it matters
08:30Travel to the client's head office. Client-facing staff "might be expected to attend client sites on certain days" [S2].Being on site builds trust and gets you answers faster.
09:00Team stand-up with your manager and two consultants. Your actions: finish interview notes, update the IT cost baseline.You leave knowing exactly what "done" means today.
09:30Sit in on an interview with the Head of Infrastructure. A senior consultant leads; you take notes.Your notes become the evidence base for the recommendations.
10:30Write up notes into the interview log and tag themes (sourcing, skills, cloud costs).Tagging lets the team see patterns across 20 interviews.
11:30Excel: reconcile IT spend from the client's finance team against supplier contracts. Flag gaps to the manager.D2 and D4: the baseline must survive the CFO's challenge.
13:00Lunch with the team.
13:45Build two slides for Thursday's steering committee: "current state on one page" and "cost by capability".D8: the slides carry the team's message to the decision makers.
15:00Manager reviews the slides. You rework them.Expect red pen. It is how quality is built, not a judgement on you.
16:00Desk research on peer benchmarks, using firm-approved tools [S2]. Save every source.D9: the AI output is a draft; you own the facts.
17:00Update the RAID log; send minutes from the morning interview.Same-day minutes are a quiet signal of reliability.
17:45Record today's hours against the engagement code. Accurate time recording is a Global Code commitment [S36].Fees and integrity both depend on it.
18:00Finish slides if comments arrived late. Days run longer near deadlines.

The compliance and control side of the job

Consulting at Deloitte comes with rules that do not exist at a pure consultancy. The reason is structural. Deloitte is also one of the UK's largest auditors, and an auditor must be independent of the companies it audits. That one fact shapes which clients T&T can serve, what you may own personally, and how a new engagement starts. Four other controls apply to everyone: conflicts, confidentiality, approved AI and honest timesheets.

Independence: why an audit firm's consultants have extra rules

KEY TERM

Independence: the requirement that an auditor be free of interests that could bias its audit opinion, and be seen to be free of them. Public interest entity (PIE): broadly, a listed company, bank or insurer, where the public relies heavily on the audited accounts. The rules for PIE audit clients are the strictest.

  • The standard. The UK auditing regulator, the Financial Reporting Council (FRC), sets the Revised Ethical Standard 2024, effective 15 December 2024 [S38] [Confirmed].
  • The whitelist for PIEs. For PIE audit clients, an audit firm "and, where the audit firm belongs to a network, any member of such network, shall not provide (either directly or indirectly) to the audited entity ... services other than those set out" in a permitted list, subject to audit committee approval [S39] (para 5.40) [Confirmed]. Prohibited services include "designing and implementing financial information technology systems" and playing "any part in the management or decision-making of the audited entity" [S364] [Confirmed]. In plain English: Deloitte consulting cannot sell most technology implementation to a listed company that Deloitte audits.
  • The fee cap. Non-audit fees for a PIE are capped at 70% of the average audit fee of the last three years [S39, S364] [Confirmed].
  • The management threat. Any service where a reasonable third party would conclude the firm is taking "a management role" for an audit-relevant entity is prohibited [S364] [Confirmed]. If a client that Deloitte audits says "just approve it for us", that is an escalation, not a favour [Inferred].
  • Personal independence. Partners and those closely associated must not hold a financial interest (other than an immaterial indirect one) in an entity relevant to an engagement of the firm [S39] (para 2.3); people on or able to influence an engagement have the same restriction [S39] (para 2.4) [Confirmed]. New joiners must "clear Personal Independence checks before you join us" [S261] [Confirmed]. Practically, expect to declare investments and to check a restricted list before buying individual shares [Inferred].

Conflict checks before any engagement

"All proposed engagements and business and financial relationships go through a conflict check before they are accepted." Where conflicts cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, "we decline the engagement" [S35] [Confirmed]. Engagements with high public-interest or reputational risk go to the Public Interest Review Group (PIRG): 32 matters in FY25, of which 9% were declined [S35, S345] [Confirmed]. For T&T and SR&T the firm set up a "Consultative Forum" and a "globally aligned client portfolio management process" [S94] [Confirmed]. A global acceptance and continuance platform is planned for UK implementation in FY2027 [S35] [Confirmed]. As an Analyst you will not run these checks, but you may be asked to declare your own conflicts, and you should never start work before the engagement is formally accepted [Inferred].

Confidentiality between clients

Deloitte's Global Principles of Business Conduct say: "We prohibit disclosure of confidential and personal information entrusted to us unless granted permission or there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose", and "We prohibit the use of confidential information about our clients for personal advantage or for the benefit of third parties" [S36] [Confirmed]. A consultant may work for two competitors in the same year. What you learn at one must never reach the other. The PwC Australia tax leaks scandal of 2023, in which confidential government information was used to win clients, shows the cost when this fails [S392] [Confirmed].

Government work adds another layer. Many T&T roles prefer or require UK security clearance: "preference will be for those who have SC clearance or eligibility to obtain SC Clearance" [S54] [Confirmed]. Graduates placed on public sector work may be asked to apply [Inferred].

Inside information on deal work

Some T&T work touches listed companies or deals, for example integration planning before an acquisition is announced. UK market abuse law defines inside information as precise, non-public information that would be likely to have a significant effect on prices if made public [S358] [Confirmed]. Disclosure is lawful only "in the normal exercise of an employment, a profession or duties" [S359] [Confirmed], and insider dealing, tipping and unlawful disclosure are prohibited [S360] [Confirmed]. In practice: do not trade, do not tip, and do not discuss the work in public places [Inferred].

Firm-approved AI only

THE JD makes responsible AI use a duty (D9). The Global Code commits Deloitte to "responsible development and deployment of new technologies, including artificial intelligence" [S36] [Confirmed]. Deloitte UK's 2025 Transparency Report rates generative AI a "Very High" priority risk, with controls including "use-case approval" and "data governance" [S345] [Confirmed]. The firm's governance base is its "Trustworthy AI Framework" [S35] [Confirmed]. And in recruitment, candidates must read Deloitte's "Integrity & AI guidance" before the assessments [S17] [Confirmed].

Timesheet integrity

"We record hours worked timely and accurately, and submit legitimate business-related expenses" [S36] [Confirmed]. This is not admin. Time and expense reporting made up 14% of ethics allegations at Deloitte UK in FY26 [S23] [Confirmed]. If a manager ever asks you to move hours to a different code or under-record them, that is a Code issue to raise, not a favour to do [Inferred].
