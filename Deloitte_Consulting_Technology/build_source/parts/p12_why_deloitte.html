Part 12

Why Deloitte, in three lengths, and the counter-case

A “why Deloitte” answer built on how the firm works, not on adjectives. Three versions to learn, a table to replace weak lines, an honest case for the alternatives, and a one-page figure to revise from.

The idea behind every version

Most candidates say why they like Deloitte. A stronger answer explains how Deloitte is built, shows evidence that the design works, and says what that means for the job you would do. This is a mechanism-based answer: it explains cause and effect.

The mechanism here is the multidisciplinary model. Deloitte keeps audit, tax, advisory and technology in one partnership. Deloitte's own description: “Our multidisciplinary model, deep domain and industry knowledge, and commitment to upholding quality and trust in the corporate reporting ecosystem allow us to provide integrated solutions” [S300]. Deloitte decided to keep its consulting business in 2003, when rivals sold or floated theirs [S104].

KEY TERMS

Partnership: the firm is owned by its equity partners, who share the profit; there are no outside shareholders. Distributable profit: the profit available to share among equity partners (£899m in FY26) [S407]. Independence: rules that stop an auditor selling most other services to the companies it audits, so the audit stays objective [S314].

The 30-second version

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

“I want to start where the shape of the firm helps me learn. Deloitte keeps audit, tax, advisory and technology in one partnership, and last year showed why that matters: in FY25 technology and transformation revenue fell 10%, but the firm fell only 1% [S89] [S305]. That stability is what pays for things like £180 million of technology investment and a new Chief AI Officer [S407]. And in FY26, Technology & Transformation was the fastest-growing UK business, on ERP, cyber and human capital work [S407]. That is the work I want to learn.”

Why it works: one mechanism, two numbers, one link to the job. Each claim has a source you could name if asked.

The 2-minute version

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

“I looked at why Deloitte is built the way it is, because that tells me what the job would really be like.

First, it is a portfolio. Deloitte UK has four businesses. In FY25 T&T revenue fell 10% but total revenue fell only 1%, because tax and audit kept growing [S89] [S305]. In FY26 revenue rose 2% and distributable profit 14% [S407]. So the firm can keep hiring and investing through a weak consulting year. It hired more than 1,950 graduates, apprentices and interns in FY26 [S407].

Second, it turns that stability into investment. UK technology investment rose to £180 million, and Hayley McKelvey joined the UK Executive as the first Chief AI Officer [S407] [S274]. Globally Deloitte has committed US$3 billion to AI through 2030 [S300] and rolled Claude out to more than 470,000 people, with 15,000 to be certified [S325].

Third, alliances. Deloitte UK works with 14 platform partners, including SAP, Workday, ServiceNow and Salesforce [S319]. Clients pay for certified people who can make those platforms work, and that is a concrete skill I can build early.

I also see the limits. Independence rules mean Deloitte often cannot consult for its own audit clients [S314]. And the Australian report case showed how one member firm's AI error affects the whole brand [S316]. That makes me care about quality checks as much as speed.

So my reason is specific: a firm whose structure lets it keep investing in technology delivery, in the business where I can learn a platform skill and see AI change delivery from the inside.”

The close: one specific thing you would work on

Interviewers often end with “What would you want to work on?” A precise answer signals that you have pictured the job.

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

“The thing I would most like to work on is responsible AI in delivery: helping a team in Engineering, AI & Data use tools like Claude safely, with the checks that would have caught the citation errors in the Australian report [S320] [S325] [S316]. Deloitte is clearly investing there, with a Chief AI Officer whose remit covers client work and internal operations [S407]. I would like to help that investment show up as better work for clients.”

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Choose your own “one thing”. Options: responsible AI in delivery (above); ERP and finance transformation, the largest named growth driver [S407]; cyber resilience [S269]; public sector data work, given the Home Office contract [S233]. For picking AI: it is where the firm is investing. Against: many candidates will say AI. A suggested stance: pick the one you have real evidence for in your own CV, and link it to AI only if you can do so honestly.

DON'T SAY THIS

“Deloitte UK made £5.81bn.” The £5.81bn figure is UK and Switzerland consolidated. Say “the UK and Swiss firm”, or use the UK business-line figures [S407].

The counter-case: why someone might reasonably choose elsewhere

Deloitte-specific risks to weigh honestly

  • Restated UK T&T revenue fell 10% in FY25, with several rounds of job cuts [S89] [S305].
  • Low attrition led to voluntary redundancy offers in UK audit in June 2026 [S227] [S407].
  • Reputational spillover from separate member firms, such as the Australian AI report [S316] and a US DEI settlement with no admission of liability [S242].
  • The FRC is investigating Deloitte's audits of Glencore and Stenn [S252] [S253].

None of these is a reason to avoid applying. Knowing them shows you have looked at the firm with open eyes.
