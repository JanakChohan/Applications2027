Part 3

Why Deloitte is built this way

The mechanism behind the firm: why audit, tax, advisory and technology sit in one partnership, how the pieces work together and where the rules force them apart, what clients really pay for, and the support machine behind the client teams. This is the raw material for a "why Deloitte" answer that nobody else in the room will give.

The short answer

Deloitte is a set of partner-owned firms that share one brand. Inside each country firm, four businesses with different economic rhythms share one profit pool. Audit is steady and regulated. Tax is steady. Technology consulting swings with clients' appetite for large programmes. Put together, they behave like an investment portfolio: when one falls, the others hold the firm up, and the shared profit pays for the next round of investment (today, mostly AI and delivery centres) [Inferred].

The price of that design is friction. Independence rules stop the consulting side selling most services to the companies the audit side audits. The UK regulator has ring-fenced audit inside the firm. A scandal in one country lands on every firm that carries the name. Deloitte's leaders have looked at this trade and decided, repeatedly and publicly, that it is worth it.

KEY TERM

Multidisciplinary partnership (MDP): a firm that combines several professions (here audit, tax, legal, strategy, risk, deals and technology consulting) under one ownership and one brand. Academics who studied the big accounting firms found this form "was vigorously contested" from the start, because regulators worried that auditors who also sell consulting may be conflicted [S315] [Reported].

What the structure actually is

"Deloitte" is a brand, not a single company. Deloitte describes itself as the brand under which "nearly 500,000" people "in independent firms throughout the world come together" [S302] [Confirmed]. Three layers matter.

Layer 1: the global umbrella (DTTL)

At the top is Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (DTTL), a private company limited by guarantee incorporated in England and Wales. It sets brand and quality rules. In Deloitte's words, "DTTL does not provide services to clients" [S302] [Confirmed]. Member firms pay DTTL subscription fees, which show up in the UK accounts as an operating cost [S93] [Confirmed]. The key legal line is that DTTL and each member firm "are liable only for their own acts and omissions, and not those of each other" [S302] [Confirmed].

Layer 2: the region (new in 2026: Deloitte EMEA)

Until 31 May 2026 the UK firm sat under Deloitte NSE LLP (North and South Europe), a holding entity that served no clients [S93] [Confirmed]. From 1 June 2026, Deloitte LLP and its Swiss practice combined with other member firms to form Deloitte EMEA: 16 firms, more than 80 countries, about €20bn of revenue, 6,000 partners and 132,000 people, with more than €1.5bn of planned extra investment over four years in generative AI, sovereign cloud and sector solutions [S124] [Confirmed]. Richard Houston, previously UK CEO, runs it [S124] [Confirmed]. The UK accounts give the reason: "strategic alignment across participating firms, thereby enhancing the ability to invest in innovation, technology platforms, and talent development at a regional level" [S93] [Confirmed]. The legal chain, verbatim from the UK results: Deloitte LLP's affiliate "is a shareholder in Deloitte EMEA BV (Deloitte EMEA). Deloitte EMEA is a member firm of Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (DTTL). DTTL and Deloitte EMEA do not provide services to clients" [S407] [Confirmed].

Layer 3: the UK firm and its four businesses

Deloitte LLP is a limited liability partnership registered in England and Wales, number OC303675, at 1 New Street Square, London EC4A 3HQ [S407] [Confirmed]. Since a global simplification that took effect on 1 October 2024, it runs four businesses: Audit & Assurance (A&A); Tax & Legal (T&L); Strategy, Risk & Transactions Advisory (SR&T-A); and Technology & Transformation (T&T) [S94] [S72] [Confirmed]. The old Consulting, Risk Advisory and Financial Advisory units were folded into the last two [S89] [Confirmed]. Deloitte calls T&T and SR&T together its "advisory businesses" [S407] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Limited liability partnership (LLP) and equity partner: an LLP is a partnership whose owners ("members") are not personally liable for the firm's debts. Equity partners own the firm. They are not paid a salary; they share what is left after staff and costs are paid. That residual is distributable profit: £899m in FY26, or £1.125m per equity partner on average [S407] [Confirmed]. Salaried partners carry the title but are employees [S88] [Confirmed].

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte UK made £5.81bn and T&T is 30% of it." The £5.81bn includes Switzerland. Deloitte's own footnote: "All revenue numbers presented herein, unless otherwise noted, represent UK and Switzerland consolidated" [S407]. UK-only revenue was £5,014m and the Swiss practice £795m [S88] [Confirmed]. The four business lines are UK-only, so T&T is about 35% of the UK firm [Inferred] (arithmetic).

Why one partnership: the theory and the numbers

There are six mechanisms that explain why the firm is built like this. Each is stated as a theory, then tested against evidence.

1. A portfolio of businesses that peak at different times

Audit is recurring and required by law: a listed company must have one every year. Tax work follows the tax calendar and changes in tax rules. Consulting depends on whether clients feel confident enough to start large programmes. These cycles do not line up, so combining them smooths partner income [Inferred].

The UK numbers for the last two years are the clearest live test. In FY25 (the year to 31 May 2025), UK T&T revenue fell 10% to £1,675m. Tax & Legal rose 7%, Audit & Assurance 3% and SR&T Advisory 3% [S88] [S85] [Confirmed]. The firm's total fell only 1%, its first fall in about 15 years [S101] [Reported]. The reason given was that "Clients delayed large-scale investment programmes amid economic uncertainty" [S305] [Reported]. Distributable profit still rose 4% [S85] [Confirmed]. In FY26, T&T recovered by 4% and profit jumped 14% on revenue growth of 2% [S407] [Confirmed].

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

"I looked at the last two years of UK results. In FY25, Technology & Transformation fell 10%, but tax grew 7% and audit 3%, so the firm only dipped about 1% and profit still went up. In FY26 T&T came back with 4% growth. To me that is the case for the multidisciplinary model in two numbers: it lets the firm keep investing through a weak consulting year."

2. Brand trust from audit, sold across the firm

Audit puts Deloitte inside the finance function of large companies and makes the name a signal of reliability. Deloitte's own framing: "Our multidisciplinary model, deep domain and industry knowledge, and commitment to upholding quality and trust in the corporate reporting ecosystem allow us to provide integrated solutions" [S300] [Confirmed]. The firm's origin story is the same idea: William Welch Deloitte, who set up in London in 1845, is described as the first person appointed independent auditor of a public company, the Great Western Railway [S103] [Reported]. [Inferred] The trust earned in audit helps every other business win work, even where the rules stop the audit team itself from passing on leads.

3. Partner capital and a long horizon

Partners own the firm and are paid out of distributable profit [S407]. Each equity partner puts in capital; total members' capital was £296m at 31 May 2026 [S93] [Confirmed]. There are no outside shareholders and no quarterly results [Inferred]. That let the firm ride out FY25 without a share-price shock, and it means investment comes out of partners' pockets. Deloitte globally has committed US$3bn to AI through 2030 [S300] [Confirmed]; the UK spent £180m on technology in FY26, up from £158m [S407] [Confirmed].

4. Liability containment

Because each member firm is "liable only for their own acts" [S302], a failure in one country should not bankrupt the others [Inferred]. This is the lesson of Arthur Andersen, which collapsed after Enron in 2001-02. Andersen's UK practice joined Deloitte UK in 2002 [S103] [Reported].

5. Scale for alliances and investment

Big technology vendors want partners who can put thousands of certified people on their platforms. A pooled network can do that; a single country firm cannot [Inferred]. Global CEO Joe Ucuzoglu, on the EMEA combination: "Our clients expect leading expertise wherever it is based, alongside seamless cross-border and technology-enabled delivery at speed" [S306] [Reported].

6. Breadth for careers

Ucuzoglu's own list of reasons for keeping the model (March 2023, in a video after EY's split collapsed) was that clients value the breadth, people value "the diversity of career paths", markets value the quality and communities value the impact. He called the decision "not even a close call" and said previous separations had "never once played out as intended" [S308] [Reported]. In June 2022 Deloitte had already called media reports of a split "categorically untrue", adding "we remain committed to our current business model" [S309] [Reported].

KEY TERM

Local currency growth: growth with exchange-rate effects stripped out. Deloitte's global revenue rose 5.7% in US dollars but 3.8% in local currency in FY26 [S300]. The local-currency figure is the "real" growth.

The decision that made Deloitte different: 2002-03

After Enron, regulators worried that auditors selling consulting were conflicted. The other big firms sold or spun off their consulting arms: Ernst & Young sold to Cap Gemini (2000), KPMG Consulting floated and became BearingPoint, and PwC Consulting was sold to IBM (2002) [S104] [Reported]. Deloitte announced in February 2002 that it would separate Deloitte Consulting as "Braxton". On 31 March 2003 it abandoned the plan, citing a "tight credit market and the uncertain state of the economy", and kept consulting "focused on nonaudit clients" [S104] [Reported]. That left Deloitte the only one of the big firms that still owned a large consulting practice [S104] [Reported]. By FY25, consulting (SR&T plus T&T) was US$43.7bn of Deloitte's US$70.5bn global revenue, about 62% [S81] [Confirmed]. [Inferred] Deloitte today is, by revenue, a technology and strategy consultancy attached to an audit firm.

SOURCE UNCERTAIN

The "only Big Four that kept consulting" history rests on one contemporary press report (InfoWorld, April 2003) [S104] and background knowledge; peer details are Reported, not checked against each firm's own records. PwC, EY and KPMG have all rebuilt large consulting businesses since. Say "Deloitte did not complete its planned spin-off, while the others sold theirs", not "Deloitte is the only firm with consulting".

What the structure costs

Cost 1: independence blocks selling

An auditor must be independent of the company it audits, or its opinion is worthless. UK rules turn that principle into hard limits. Under the FRC Revised Ethical Standard 2019 (effective 15 March 2020), auditors of UK public interest entities (PIEs: broadly listed companies, banks and insurers) may only provide a short "whitelist" of non-audit services, mostly work required by law or closely linked to the audit [S180] [Confirmed]. Non-audit fees from such clients are capped at 70% of the average audit fee of the previous three years [S182] [S180] [Confirmed]. Internal audit, secondments and contingent fees are banned for all audit clients [S180] [S181] [Confirmed]. The rules reach the whole network: services to a UK PIE's parent and worldwide subsidiaries are caught [S180] [Confirmed]. The current version is the Revised Ethical Standard 2024, effective 15 December 2024 [S178] [Confirmed].

[Inferred] In practice, if Deloitte audits a FTSE company, Deloitte's T&T team generally cannot sell it an ERP implementation. Every large Deloitte audit client is a client the consulting side must largely leave to Accenture, PwC, EY or KPMG, and the reverse is true for them.

Cost 2: the regulator has ring-fenced audit inside the firm

On 6 July 2020 the FRC published principles requiring the Big Four to operationally separate their UK audit practices, with full implementation by 30 June 2024 [S183] [Confirmed]. The required outcomes include audit governance that protects auditors from the rest of the firm, and audit partners' profit share not persistently exceeding what the audit practice itself contributes, so there is no cross-subsidy [S183] [Confirmed]. Press reports of the approved plans add that dealings between audit and the rest of the firm must be at arm's length, and the audit practice may not receive fees for introducing work to other parts of the firm [S312] [Reported]. On 10 October 2024 the FRC said all four firms had completed the transition, with independent audit boards and more transparency on transactions between audit and non-audit businesses [S184] [Confirmed]. Deloitte UK's Audit Governance Board said ring-fencing "has undoubtedly played an important part" in better audit quality [S94] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Operational separation: the audit practice stays inside the same LLP (one legal firm, one brand) but has its own board, its own financial accounting and limits on what money and work can flow to and from the rest of the firm [S183] [S184]. It is separation inside the firm, not a legal split [Inferred].

Cost 3: slow decisions across many partner-owned firms

Any structural change needs agreement across many partnerships. EY's attempt to split (below) failed partly for this reason [S310] [Reported]. [Inferred] A partnership can be patient, but it is rarely fast.

Cost 4: one brand, so one firm's mistake travels

In 2025 Deloitte Australia agreed to repay the final instalment of an AU$440,000 contract with Australia's Department of Employment and Workplace Relations, after a report on its welfare compliance framework was found to contain fabricated citations and an invented quote from a Federal Court judgment [S316] [Confirmed]. The revised report disclosed use of "a generative AI large language model (Azure OpenAI GPT-4o)" [S316]. It happened in a legally separate firm, yet the story ran worldwide under the Deloitte name, in the same week Deloitte announced its Claude deployment to 470,000+ people [S325] [S326]. Other cases make the same point: the US SEC fined Deloitte China US$20m in 2022 over audit failings [S111] [Confirmed], and Deloitte UK has its own FRC record, including the record £15m Autonomy fine in 2020 [S105] [Confirmed].

Deloitte UK's own risk register rates "Our reputation, role and future public interest impact" as a Very High residual risk, and lists "Increasing breadth and depth of non-audit offerings (including Operate)" as one of the threats [S94] [Confirmed]. In other words, the firm itself names the multidisciplinary model as a source of its top risk.

The natural experiment: EY's Project Everest

EY is the only one of the four to have seriously tried the alternative. Its plan, known as Project Everest, was to split audit from consulting and list the consulting side on the stock market. Its stated rationale was to address "conflicts of interest often associated with audit and advisory firms" and capture consulting growth [S310] [Reported]. It was abandoned on 12 April 2023 after the US executive committee withdrew, following a dispute over how much of the tax business should stay with audit, and opposition from member firms in China and Israel [S141] [S310] [Reported]. The reported cost was about US$600m [S141] [Reported].

What the experiment shows [Inferred]: the split had real attractions (freedom to sell to audit clients, outside capital), but unpicking a multidisciplinary partnership is very hard. Tax sits naturally on both sides. Partners in each country have a vote. Deloitte's response has been to go the other way: a larger combined regional firm, Deloitte EMEA, funded by partners rather than by outside investors [S124] [Confirmed].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Prompt: "Should the Big Four split audit from consulting?"

For a split: independence rules wall off the biggest clients from consulting; the UK regulator has already ring-fenced audit [S183]; Deloitte's own risk register rates its reputation as a Very High risk and names the breadth of non-audit work as a threat [S94]; a listed consultancy could raise capital without partners' pockets.

Against: FY25 showed the portfolio absorbing a 10% consulting fall [S88]; EY spent about US$600m and still could not agree the split [S141]; tax and risk work sit naturally with both sides; private capital in mid-tier firms (Grant Thornton) is a recent, unproven experiment [S144] [S145].

Suggested stance: "For Deloitte, the portfolio benefit and the investment capacity outweigh the lost access to audit clients today. The test I would watch is whether AI shrinks the hours on big implementation projects enough that consulting stops being the growth engine. Then the logic of keeping it tied to audit would weaken."

How the businesses work together

The matrix: offering by industry

Every Deloitte professional sits in an offering (what they do) and usually aligns to an industry (who they do it for). The six global industries are Consumer; Energy, Resources & Industrials; Financial Services; Government & Public Services; Life Sciences & Health Care; and Technology, Media & Telecommunications [S301] [Confirmed]. In the UK, Financial Services is the biggest industry at £1,735m of FY26 revenue, with Government & Public Services at £679m [S88] [Confirmed].

UK T&T offerings named on Deloitte's careers page are Enterprise, Technology & Performance; Engineering, AI & Data; Cyber; Customer; Human Capital; and Defence & Security [S320] [Confirmed]. FY26 demand was for "enterprise technology support, including ERP implementation, Cyber and Human Capital services" [S303] [Confirmed]. Deloitte's own summary of the idea: "Our multidisciplinary model allows us to bring together technology, tools, insights, and deep industry experience across all businesses to provide integrated solutions" [S301] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Offering: a practice built around a skill, such as Cyber or Human Capital (people and organisation). ERP (enterprise resource planning): the core software that runs a company's finance, supply chain and HR, for example SAP, Oracle or Workday. ERP implementations are long, large programmes, which is why they drive T&T revenue.

Account teams: where collaboration lives

For large clients Deloitte appoints a Lead Client Service Partner (LCSP). Deloitte US profiles and postings describe the role as leveraging Deloitte's networks to support major relationships, making sure account plans are "developed, monitored", and that pursuit processes are run "consistently ... across the account" [S322] [Reported]. These sources are from the US member firm; the UK term and practice are assumed similar but not verified. [Inferred] Cross-selling is organised around the client, not the offering: the LCSP sees the whole wallet and offering partners bring specialists.

Alliances: the vendor sells the licence, Deloitte sells the implementation

Deloitte UK lists 14 alliance partners: Adobe, Anaplan, Apple, AWS, Google, Informatica, MuleSoft, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, UiPath, Workday and Workiva [S319] [Confirmed]. Its own description: "We combine our award-winning consulting services, market sensing data and trusted relationships with some of the largest technology firms to help solve your most complex challenges" [S319] [Confirmed]. Globally, Deloitte was named NVIDIA Global Consulting Partner of the Year 2026 for the fifth year running and won six Google Cloud Partner of the Year awards [S300] [Confirmed]. On 6 October 2025 Deloitte and Anthropic announced Claude for more than 470,000 Deloitte people, a Claude Center of Excellence and certification for 15,000 professionals [S325] [Confirmed].

Delivery centres: where the building happens

Deloitte UK opened onshore technology centres in Belfast, Cardiff, Manchester and Newcastle from 2025, starting with 750 technologists and aiming for 1,500 within three years [S323] [Reported]. T&T managing partner Rob Cullen said the investment "accelerates our ambition to become the largest onshore technology capability in UK" [S323]. The FY26 results say the centres were "fully operational in FY26" [S407] [Confirmed]. Deloitte's US-India delivery organisation (USI) is reported at more than 100,000 people [S324] [Reported], but it belongs to the US firm and its role on UK engagements is not established. Deloitte UK audit uses an offshore audit delivery centre, Global Audit India [S94] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Delivery centre: a lower-cost location where teams build, test and run technology for engagements led elsewhere. Onshore means inside the client's country (useful for security-cleared public sector work); offshore means abroad, often India.

Where independence blocks collaboration

Deloitte's own rule, from its Audit Transparency Report: "All proposed engagements and business and financial relationships go through a conflict check before they are accepted." If conflicts cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, "we decline the engagement" [S94] [Confirmed]. The audit partner is always consulted where a potential conflict touches an audit client [S94] [Confirmed]. A Public Interest Review Group reviews engagements with high reputational risk: 32 matters in FY25, of which 9% were declined [S94] [Confirmed].

[Inferred] (from [S180] [S183] [S94]) So truly integrated, multi-business work happens mostly at clients that Deloitte does not audit (often audited by another Big Four firm), in the public sector, and in private companies below the PIE threshold.

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte can offer its audit clients the full package, from audit to tech implementation." For UK listed and other PIE audit clients, that is exactly what the rules forbid [S180]. Say instead: "The integrated model works best at clients Deloitte doesn't audit, which is why the conflict check comes before everything else."

What clients actually buy

Who the clients are

Deloitte serves "nearly 90% of the Fortune Global 500" [S300] [Confirmed]. Gartner ranks Deloitte the No. 1 consulting services provider worldwide by revenue; Deloitte's 2026 release says this is the ninth consecutive year [S125] [Reported] (Deloitte's release, Gartner report paywalled). In UK public procurement, Tussell data reported by Consultancy.uk shows UK public sector consulting spend at a record £3.7bn in 2024/25, up 6%, and Deloitte as the top supplier of key consulting contracts for five years running, with £400m+ in fees, about four times the next firm [S335] [Reported]. In late June 2026 Deloitte won a four-year Home Office data and analytics deal worth up to £200m [S233] [Reported]. "Up to" is a ceiling, not guaranteed spend [Inferred].

What they are buying

  1. Scale at short notice. On NHS Test and Trace in 2020-21, Deloitte had around 900 to 1,035 consultants working at peak [S332] [S333] [Confirmed]. Few organisations can find a thousand people in weeks.
  2. Certified platform skills. 14 UK alliances [S319] and, globally, 15,000 people to be certified on Claude [S325]. Deloitte US cites more than 36,000 SAP-focused professionals [S146] [Confirmed].
  3. Sector depth from the industry side of the matrix [S301].
  4. A defensible choice. A board or minister can justify hiring the firm Gartner ranks No. 1 [S125] [Inferred].
  5. Risk transfer. A contract with a large balance sheet behind it. The Australian refund shows the mechanism working in reverse: the client got money back [S316] [Inferred].

Why they accept the price

Rates disclosed to Parliament: Deloitte staff on Test and Trace cost about £1,000 a day each, close to £1m a day in total, with some senior consultants up to £6,624 a day [S332] [S333] [Confirmed]. Deloitte's contracts were worth £298m, with £174m spent by end-March 2021 [S332] [S333]. The government's explanation was "skills shortages in certain areas of the civil service" and "comparatively low salaries in the civil service" [S333] [Reported]. The National Audit Office (NAO, Parliament's spending watchdog) found in November 2025 that 86% of survey respondents said consultants made valuable contributions [S334] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Day rate: the price a client pays per consultant per day. It covers salary, the firm's overheads, time between projects and partner profit, which is why it is far higher than the consultant's own daily pay. Deloitte recognises revenue on time-and-materials or on fixed fees managed internally through "actual inputs of time at charge-out rates" [S93] [Confirmed].

The counter-case, with numbers

  • Performance. During heavy consultant use on Test and Trace, only 17% of people got test results within 24 hours against a 90% target [S332] [Reported].
  • Dependence. In April 2021 Test and Trace had 2,239 external workers; consultants were still 45% of central staff [S333] [Reported].
  • Visibility. The NAO said "Government does not have consistent data on how much individual departments are spending on external consultants" [S334] [Confirmed]. HM Treasury estimated central government spend at about £1.36bn in 2022-23; the government targeted £550m of savings in 2024-25 and £700m by 2028-29 [S334] [Confirmed]. Yet Tussell measured wider public sector consulting at a record £3.7bn in 2024/25 [S335] [Reported]. The two figures use different scopes, so do not compare them directly.
  • Ideas. Mariana Mazzucato and Rosie Collington's book The Big Con (2023) argues that over-reliance on consultancies weakens state capability, charges well above equivalent public staff and creates conflicts of interest, naming the Big Four including Deloitte [S337] [Reported] (summary only, book not read). Critics said its idea of public value is loosely defined [S337].
  • Quality failure. The Australian AI report [S316].

Prompt: "Is the government right to keep buying from firms like Deloitte?"

Yes: the civil service cannot hire a thousand people in weeks; the NAO found most users valued the work [S334]; data and digital skills are scarce, which is why the Home Office still bought up to £200m of data work after pledging cuts [S233].

No: £1,000+ a day per person on Test and Trace, 45% of central staff from outside, and weak results against targets [S332] [S333]; the state cannot even count what it spends [S334]; The Big Con argues it hollows out capability [S337].

The non-core side: the centre and internal AI

Enabling Functions

Deloitte UK calls its internal support business "Enabling Functions". Its own line: "In Enabling Functions we support our colleagues to do that well, so the firm can continue to make a positive impact" [S330] [Confirmed]. Two early-careers routes are named on Deloitte's pages. The Leadership Support Centre in Cardiff supports senior leaders. Quality, Risk & Security Services (Cardiff and London) covers client vetting, conflict checks, anti-money laundering and cyber security, with sub-teams for Financial Crime, Independence, Deloitte Business and Security, Strategy and Operations, and Contentious Matters [S330] [Confirmed]. Search results also name People and Performance (HR), Growth, IT Services, Finance, Administration and Corporate Responsibility, with Cardiff described as the centre for centralised business operations [S331] [Reported] (snippet only). No headcount for Enabling Functions was found.

[Inferred] The Independence team is where the rules in Figure 7 are enforced day to day. Before T&T can start work, Quality, Risk & Security checks whether the client is an audit client and whether there is a conflict. The friction point of the multidisciplinary model has been turned into a job.

Internal AI

  • PairD. Deloitte's own generative AI assistant, built by its AI Institute, rolled out in the UK from October 2023 and to 75,000 people across Europe and the Middle East by January 2024 [S328] [Confirmed]. Staff had to complete training and were warned output may be inaccurate [S328]. UK staff were sending it 1 million prompts a month in FY25 [S85] [Confirmed], and new graduates were introduced to it at induction in 2025 [S232] [Confirmed].

Prompt: "Will AI break the consulting pyramid?"

It will: UK fee-earning staff fell 5% in FY26 while revenue rose 2% and profit 14% [S93] [S407]; the firm offered voluntary redundancy in audit because too few people were leaving [S407]; T&T was the slowest-growing business globally at 2.5% [S300].

It won't, yet: Deloitte still hired 1,950+ graduates, apprentices and interns in FY26 [S407]; the UK CEO's line is that "the role our people play will matter more, not less" [S407]; large ERP and cyber programmes still need many people.

Suggested stance: "The base of the pyramid gets narrower and the work at the base changes from producing to checking and judging. That is why I'd want a firm that trains people on the tools early."
