Part 2

The firm as a business, from the inside

How Deloitte UK earns a pound, who keeps it, who is protected from whom, and what has gone wrong. Read this once and you can talk about the firm the way a partner thinks about it, with the numbers and the dates.

Most candidates describe Deloitte from the careers website: big, global, "purpose-led". Interviewers hear that all day. This Part explains the firm as a business. It traces a client's fee from the invoice to a partner's profit share, shows the legal wiring that holds nearly 500,000 people under one brand, and lists the failures you should know before anyone asks. Every number has a source and a date. Where a number is our own arithmetic, it says so.

Two housekeeping points first, because they trip up almost everyone:

  • "Deloitte UK" headline numbers include Switzerland. Deloitte's own footnote says: "All revenue numbers presented herein, unless otherwise noted, represent UK and Switzerland consolidated" [S407] [Confirmed]. A. The founding insight: an independent check on other people's numbers

William Welch Deloitte set up as an accountant in Basinghall Street, London, in 1845. He is described as the first person appointed as independent auditor of a public company, the Great Western Railway [S103] [Reported]. That is the founding idea, and it still runs the business: investors and lenders will put money into a company only if someone independent has checked the accounts. The auditor sells trust.

KEY TERM

Audit: an independent check that a company's published accounts are fair. The auditor signs an "opinion". Investors rely on it, so the auditor must be independent of the company it checks. That need for independence is the source of many of Deloitte's rules today (Section D).

The decision that made today's firm: keeping consulting

In 2001 the US energy company Enron collapsed. Its auditor, Arthur Andersen, also earned large consulting fees, and regulators asked whether an auditor that sells consulting can stay tough on its client. The US passed the Sarbanes-Oxley Act in 2002, which tightened auditor independence. The other big accounting firms got rid of their consulting arms. Ernst & Young sold to Cap Gemini (2000). KPMG Consulting floated and became BearingPoint (2001). PwC Consulting was sold to IBM (2002) [S104] [Reported].

Deloitte planned to do the same. In February 2002 it announced that Deloitte Consulting would be separated as a partner-owned firm called "Braxton". On 31 March 2003 it dropped the plan, citing a "tight credit market and the uncertain state of the economy" [S104] [Reported]. It committed to keep consulting "focused on nonaudit clients", in line with the new rules [S104]. Some clients were reported to have walked away during the uncertainty [S104] [Reported].

So the choice was partly forced by market conditions, not a grand strategic vision. But its effect has been huge. In FY2025, consulting services were US$43.7bn of Deloitte's US$70.5bn global revenue, about 62% [S81] [Confirmed]. Gartner ranks Deloitte the number one consulting provider worldwide by revenue, for the eighth year running [S301] [Confirmed]. Deloitte has had more than 20 years to build a multidisciplinary firm while rivals rebuilt their consultancies from scratch. Part 3 explains why that structure works and what it costs; this Part sticks to how it makes money.

KEY TERM

Multidisciplinary firm: one firm that sells audit, tax, consulting and deal advice under one brand. The upside is breadth and steadier income across the economic cycle. The downside is constant policing of conflicts between audit clients and advisory work. See Part 3 for the full mechanism.

Purpose statement

Deloitte's stated purpose is "to make an impact that matters, for our clients, our people, and society" [S82] [Confirmed] (read on the Global Facts and Figures page, 4 October 2026). The UK audit business has its own: "Protecting the public interest and building trust and confidence in business" [S94] [Confirmed] (Audit Transparency Report 2025).

DON'T SAY THIS

"Deloitte has always been a consulting firm." It started as an audit firm, nearly gave consulting away in 2002-03, and kept it partly by accident of timing [S104]. Knowing that history shows you understand why audit independence rules still shape what consultants can sell.

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

"Deloitte is the only Big Four firm that kept its consultancy after Sarbanes-Oxley. It nearly spun it out as Braxton and pulled back in 2003. Today consulting is about 62% of global revenue, and Technology & Transformation alone was US$33.4bn in FY2025." [S104, S81]

B. How the firm makes money: from a client's fee to a partner's share

Deloitte UK is a partnership. That one fact explains most of its economics, so start there.

KEY TERM

Limited liability partnership (LLP): a business owned by its partners (legally called "members") rather than by shareholders. Partners share the profit. Unlike an old-style partnership, members are generally not personally liable for the firm's debts. Deloitte LLP is registered in England and Wales as OC303675 [S93, S407] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Equity partner vs salaried partner: an equity partner owns a share of the firm, puts in capital and takes a share of profit. A salaried partner carries the partner title but is an employee on a salary and bonus. In FY2026 Deloitte UK averaged 799 equity partners and 654 salaried partners (full-time-equivalent averages, 1,453 in all) [S88] [Confirmed].

The business model in one paragraph

Clients pay fees for work done by teams of people. The firm pays those people salaries, pays sub-contractors, and pays for offices, IT and its share of the global network. Whatever is left is profit, and it all belongs to the equity partners. There are no outside shareholders and no retained-earnings growth story. So the core question for the partnership every year is: how much fee income can a given number of partners generate, through how many staff, at what cost?

KEY TERM

Distributable profit: the profit available to share among equity partners, measured on Deloitte's internal "management accounts" basis. It uses different accounting policies from the statutory accounts, which is why it was £899m while statutory profit for the year was £769m [S93] [Confirmed]. Press releases quote distributable profit.

The engine underneath: rate card, utilisation, realisation, leverage

The accounts show totals. The mechanism that produces them is the same across all large professional-services firms. Deloitte does not publish its internal targets, so what follows is the standard model [Inferred], tied to what the accounts do confirm.

KEY TERM

Charge-out rate (the rate card): the standard price per hour or day for each grade, from analyst to partner. Utilisation: the share of a person's available hours that are chargeable to a client. Realisation: the share of the standard value of those hours that the client actually pays, after discounts and write-offs. Leverage: how many staff work under each partner. Profit rises with all four.

The FY2026 accounts confirm the mechanism in Deloitte's own words. Fixed-fee contracts are recognised "based upon the value of the actual services provided ... determined based on the actual inputs of time at charge-out rates, via timesheet reporting" [S93] [Confirmed]. In plain English: even when the client pays a fixed price, Deloitte measures progress by hours times rates. Your timesheet is how the firm knows how much revenue it has earned.

The operating model in layers

Money is one flow. Figure 2 shows the others: what moves between clients, the four client-facing businesses, the firm's own centre, and the global network. Leverage: how many people per partner?

Leverage is the shape of the pyramid. The more juniors each partner leads, the more chargeable hours feed each partner's share. Using Deloitte's own numbers [S88, S93] and our arithmetic [Inferred]:

  • UK group FY2026: 23,970 FTE employees across 1,453 partners is about 16.5 staff per partner. Per equity partner it is about 30.
  • Counting only fee-earners (20,346 FTE), it is about 14 per partner.
  • Deloitte global FY2025: 473,050 people across 21,183 partners, principals and managing directors is about 22 per partner [S81].
  • Revenue per head, UK group FY2026: about £235k per person (employees plus equity partners) [Inferred]. Average employee cost: about £122k per FTE including employer pension and social security, bonuses and salaried partners. That is not a typical salary; senior grades and Swiss pay pull it up [S93] [Inferred].

The most important number in the FY2026 accounts is easy to miss. Average fee-earning employees fell from 21,471 to 20,346 FTE, down about 5%, while revenue rose 2% [S93] [Confirmed]. More revenue from fewer people pushed distributable profit up 14%. That is leverage and productivity working in the partners' favour, likely helped by delivery centres, AI tools and lower hiring [Inferred]. Section E and Figure 41 come back to this.

The partner deal, from the accounts

  • Capital. Each member's capital is set by their profit "units" and is repayable after retirement. Total members' capital was £296m at 31 May 2026 (2025: £293m) [S93] [Confirmed]. Across 809 members that averages about £366k, though individual amounts vary [Inferred] (our arithmetic).
  • Profit share. In Deloitte's words: "Members' share of profits is based upon a comprehensive evaluation of their individual and team contribution to, among others, quality, risk, performance, and leadership" [S93] [Confirmed]. The UK Executive allocates units with oversight from the Partnership Council. For audit partners, the Audit Non-Executives observe the process to test the link to audit quality [S93].
  • Cash. Partners draw monthly advances against expected profit. The firm holds back tax and pays it to HMRC for them. The average effective tax rate on equity partners was 49% in FY2026 [S88] [Confirmed].
  • Annuities. A partner annuity scheme pays retired partners. It was closed to new entrants on 1 June 2021. Liabilities are £1,545m. In Deloitte's words: "The annual payments are mainly capped at 8% of applicable Group operating profit before annuity charges in any financial year" [S93] [Confirmed]. In effect, today's partners fund past partners' retirement out of today's profit [Inferred].
  • Voting. The Senior Partner and Chief Executive is elected by partner vote, after soundings by the Partnership Council [S93, S96] [Confirmed].
  • Conversion. Salaried partners become equity partners in a separate step: 29 in 2024, 77 in 2025, 68 on 1 June 2026 [S84, S85, S95] [Confirmed].
KEY TERM

Partner annuity: a retirement income paid by the firm to former partners, funded from current profits rather than a separate pension pot. Deloitte UK closed its scheme to new partners in 2021 and caps the annual cost at mainly 8% of operating profit before annuity charges [S93]. Why you care: it is a cost the current partners carry, so it shapes how hard they push for profit.

The staff deal: promotion, pay and the narrowing pyramid

  • On 1 June 2026, over 6,000 people were promoted out of about 25,000, average in-grade salary rose 4.2%, and the bonus pool rose 14% [S95, S407] [Confirmed].
  • A year earlier the mood was different. In May 2025 UK staff were reportedly told the firm would "promote fewer people and reduce average salary rises" because profits were "below our original plan" [S101] [Reported].
  • New partners: 48 promoted on 1 June 2026, after 60 in 2025 and 80-81 in 2024 [S95, S85] [Confirmed].
  • Promotions to partner and director fell from 501 (FY2023) to 485 (FY2024) to 341 (FY2025) [S89] [Confirmed]. The top of the pyramid is narrowing.
  • Voluntary attrition (people choosing to leave) was 10.0% in FY2025, after 9.4% in FY2024 and 11.1% in FY2023 [S89] [Confirmed].
  • Low attrition has a cost. In FY2026 Deloitte offered voluntary redundancy on enhanced terms to "less than 3% of our A&A business" because too few people were leaving [S407] [Confirmed].
KEY TERM

Up or out: the norm in big professional firms that people are expected to move up through the grades on a rough timetable, or leave. Deloitte does not publish an up-or-out policy. Treat it as an industry norm [Inferred], visible in the promotion numbers above. Low voluntary attrition breaks the model, because the pyramid only works if people leave the lower grades at a steady rate.

D. The risk and control architecture

Deloitte's biggest asset is trust in one brand. Its biggest liability is that thousands of partners in separately owned firms around the world can damage that brand. The firm manages this with three layers of control: legal separation between firms, governance inside the UK firm, and gates on every engagement.

Layer 1: the network is a brand, not one company

The UK firm's legal position, verbatim from Deloitte's FY2026 release:

"Deloitte LLP is a limited liability partnership registered in England and Wales with registered number OC303675 and its registered office at 1 New Street Square, London EC4A 3HQ ... Deloitte LLP's affiliate (Deloitte Management Services Limited) is a shareholder in Deloitte EMEA BV (Deloitte EMEA). Deloitte EMEA is a member firm of Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (DTTL). DTTL and Deloitte EMEA do not provide services to clients." [S407] [Confirmed]

DTTL is "a private company limited by guarantee incorporated in England and Wales" [S83] [Confirmed]. The network's own legal language explains the logic. DTTL and each member firm "are legally separate and independent entities, which cannot obligate or bind each other in respect of third parties", and are "liable only for their own acts and omissions, and not those of each other" [S83] [Confirmed].

KEY TERM

Member firm: a separate legal business, owned by its own local partners, that has signed up to use the Deloitte brand and follow DTTL's standards. Company limited by guarantee: a company with no share capital, owned by members who guarantee a nominal sum; common for coordinating bodies that do not trade.

Why this matters to you [Inferred]: a failure in Deloitte China or Deloitte Australia does not legally bind Deloitte UK. But it lands on the same brand and the same search results. Section I shows that this is not theoretical.

Layer 3: gates on every engagement

This is where consultants meet the control system. The Audit Transparency Report describes the gates [S94] [Confirmed]:

  1. Conflict check before acceptance. "All proposed engagements and business and financial relationships go through a conflict check before they are accepted." The audit partner is always consulted if a potential conflict touches an audit client. "Where potential conflicts cannot be eliminated or reduced to an acceptable level, we decline the engagement."
  2. Public Interest Review Group (PIRG). Reviews engagements with high public-interest or reputational risk. It has looked at more than 800 matters over its history. In FY2025 it formally considered 32 matters and declined 9%; in FY2024, 38 matters with 19% declined.
  3. Tax Review Panel. Considers reputational risk in complex tax work.
  4. Consulting forum. For SR&T and T&T, a "Consultative Forum" and a "globally aligned client portfolio management process".

KEY TERM

Conflict of interest: a situation where the firm's duty to one client clashes with its interest or its duty to another, for example advising a bidder while auditing the target. Independence: the rules that stop an auditor having financial or business ties with its audit client. For a consultant, independence decides which clients you are allowed to work for at all. How Deloitte rates its own risks

The firm publishes a principal risks register. Two entries matter for consulting [S94] [Confirmed]:

  • "Our reputation, role and future public interest impact": Very High residual risk. Threats listed include "Increased government focus on the reputation and role and public sector use of professional services organisations" and "Increasing breadth and depth of non-audit offerings (including Operate)".
  • "Client Portfolio": High. It cites "Reputational risks associated with client selection" and notes that "Reputation is at risk from the 'company we choose to keep' and the projects we choose to deliver".

"Operate" means running services for clients on an ongoing basis, such as a benefits system or a finance function, rather than advising and leaving. The firm itself flags it as a source of risk. Rhode Island, in Section I, shows why.

Quality management

Audit firms must run a system of quality management under the standard ISQM (UK) 1. Deloitte UK's evaluation at 31 May 2025 found "two deficiencies identified (neither severe nor pervasive)" and concluded the system gives reasonable assurance; one was fixed by the report date [S94] [Confirmed]. The FRC rated 95% of sampled Deloitte audits "good or limited improvements required" (94% the year before) [S94]. The audit practice is "operationally separated" from the rest of the firm, an FRC requirement for the Big Four, and the transition period ended during FY2025 [S94] [Confirmed]. Deloitte UK also uses an offshore audit delivery centre, Global Audit India [S94].

E. Business mix and growth over time

Global: doubled in eleven years

Deloitte's global revenue grew from US$35.2bn in FY2015 to US$74.5bn in FY2026, about 7% a year compounded [S80, S70] [Inferred] (our arithmetic). The fastest years were FY2022 (+19.6% in local currency) and FY2023 (+14.9%), the post-COVID boom in digital and consulting work [S74, S73] [Confirmed]. Growth then slowed to 3-5% a year [S70, S71, S72]. Headcount roughly doubled too, from over 225,000 to nearly 500,000 [S80, S70].

The business mix changed too. On 1 October 2024 Deloitte globally cut its five businesses to four, folding Consulting, Risk Advisory and Financial Advisory into T&T and SR&T [S94, S72] [Confirmed]. Global CEO Joe Ucuzoglu announced it in the FY2024 release: "over the course of FY2025, we are aligning our capabilities into the following business units: Audit & Assurance, Tax & Legal, and two units housing our Consulting services: Strategy, Risk & Transactions and Technology & Transformation" [S72] [Confirmed]. Fewer staff, more equity partners

UK group FTE employees rose from 18,405 in FY2019 to a peak of 26,824 in FY2024, then fell to 23,970 in FY2026, down about 11% in two years [S92, S89, S88] [Confirmed] (percentage [Inferred]). Over the same two years, FTE equity partners rose from 749 to 799 [S88] [Confirmed]. Fewer staff, more owners, higher profit per owner: that is the shape of the last two years.

There are two ways to read this, and you should be able to hold both [Inferred]. The optimistic reading: the firm over-hired in the FY2023 boom (6,800 hires and 4,109 experienced hires that year [S87, S89]), then corrected, and new technology lets fewer people deliver more. The cautious reading: if AI and delivery centres keep raising output per head, the firm needs fewer juniors per partner for good. Entry-level hiring fell from 2,767 in FY2023 to 1,908 in FY2025 [S89] [Confirmed], with "more than 1,950" in FY2026 [S407]. Part 4 turns this into how you stand out.

UK: steady revenue, rising profit, one dip

Deloitte UK (with Switzerland) grew from £3,950m in FY2019 to £5,809m in FY2026, about 5.7% a year [S92, S88] [Inferred] (our arithmetic). FY2025 was the exception: revenue fell 1% to £5,676m, the first fall in about 15 years [S101] [Reported]. T&T fell 10%. UK government and public services revenue fell 14%, from £761m to £652m. Deloitte's footnote gives the reason: "The reduction in FY25 revenue was primarily due to the early announcement of the general election which resulted in delays to procurement decision making, contract award decisions and start dates of projects" [S89] [Confirmed]. Even in that year, distributable profit rose 4% to £789m [S85] [Confirmed]. That is the multidisciplinary model doing its job: tax and legal grew 7% while T&T shrank.

F. What "private partnership" means for your research

Deloitte UK has no shares on a stock market. That changes where the numbers come from.

What you cannot get

  • No share price, no analyst forecasts, no quarterly results [Inferred].
  • No margin by business. You cannot tell whether T&T is more profitable than audit [S93].
  • No day rates, utilisation or realisation targets.
  • No client names in the accounts and no individual partner pay.

What is public, and where

  • LLP accounts at Companies House (OC303675), audited by BDO: income statement, costs, partners, capital, annuities, governance [S93].
  • Business and ESG performance metrics PDF each September: revenue by business and industry, profit per partner, FTEs, attrition, promotions [S88, S89, S90, S91, S92].
  • Audit Transparency Report: governance, quality, PIRG, risk register [S94].
  • Global revenue release and Global Impact Report each September [S70, S71, S81].

Three traps sit in these sources [S93, S88, S407] [Confirmed]:

  1. The UK headline includes Switzerland. Business-line figures do not.
  2. The fiscal year ends 31 May, so FY2026 is mostly calendar 2025.
  3. Partner and people numbers come in headcount and FTE-average versions. Always say which.
KEY TERM

FTE (full-time equivalent): a way of counting staff where two half-time people count as one. An "FTE average" is the average over the year, so it differs from the headcount on a single day. Deloitte UK's "25,000 people" is a rounded headcount; 23,970 is the FTE average [S88, S407].

ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY OF THIS PART

Deloitte is a brand shared by legally separate firms. The UK firm is an LLP owned by about 800 equity partners. It turned £5.81bn of FY2026 fees (UK and Switzerland) into £899m of distributable profit: half of each pound pays staff, about a quarter passes through to sub-contractors and expenses, and about 15p is left for the owners. Revenue grew 2% while fee-earning staff fell 5%. Since 1 June 2026 it sits inside Deloitte EMEA under a new CEO and a new Chief AI Officer. Its biggest risk, by its own rating, is reputation, and the cases that matter most for a consultant are about AI quality and Operate work [S93, S88, S407, S94].
