What is happening right now

The eleven stories from April to early October 2026 that a Deloitte Consulting or Technology interviewer is most likely to know, each explained from scratch, with why it matters to the firm and to you, and one question or opinion you could raise. Then the latest CFO Survey and Tech Trends in brief.

How to use this part

"Tell me about something in the news" is a near-certain question. The interviewer is testing three things: that you follow the business, that you can explain it simply, and that you can say what it means. Each story below is built in that order. Pick three you find genuinely interesting and practise them out loud. The first two (the results) are the ones you must know cold.

Story 1 · Deloitte UK FY26: revenue +2% to £5.81bn, profit +14%30 Sep 2026
Outlet and source

Deloitte UK press release, "Deloitte UK publishes 2026 financial results", 30 September 2026 [S407, S220]; FY26 metrics document [S88]. Bloomberg's "Deloitte UK Partner Pay Grows to $1.5 Million on AI Demand" (29 Sep) is paywalled and was not read [S223].

In plain English

Deloitte UK's year ends on 31 May. In the year to 31 May 2026 revenue rose 2% to £5.81bn (FY25 £5.68bn) [S407] [Confirmed]. Careful: that headline is UK and Switzerland consolidated. Deloitte's footnote says "All revenue numbers presented herein, unless otherwise noted, represent UK and Switzerland consolidated" [S407]. The split is UK £5,014m and Switzerland £795m [S88] [Confirmed]. The four UK businesses: Technology & Transformation (T&T) £1.75bn (+4%), Tax & Legal £1.36bn (+2%), Audit & Assurance £998m (+3%), Strategy, Risk & Transactions (SR&T) Advisory £913m (+1%) [S407] [Confirmed]. Distributable profit, the pool shared by the owning partners, rose 14% to £899m. Average profit per equity partner rose to £1.125m (FY25 £1.051m). Equity partners rose 6.5% to a record 784 [S407] [Confirmed]. Context: FY25 had been the first revenue fall in about 15 years, with UK consulting down 10% [S123, S305] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

Profit grew seven times faster than revenue. Average fee-earning staff fell about 5% while revenue rose 2% [S93] [Confirmed], so the gain came from efficiency and leverage, not volume [Inferred]. The firm says its advisory businesses "returned to profitable growth", led by enterprise technology, ERP, cyber and human capital work [S407, S303] [Confirmed]. Technology investment rose to £180m from £158m [S407].

Why it matters to the role

T&T, the business you are most likely applying to, is the largest UK business and grew fastest. The firm hired 3,500 people including more than 1,950 graduates, apprentices and interns; over 6,000 of 25,000 people were promoted; the bonus pool rose 14% and average in-grade pay rose over 4% [S407] [Confirmed].

Raise this

"Profit rose 14% on 2% revenue growth while fee-earners fell about 5%. How much of that margin came from AI-enabled delivery, and does it change how T&T staffs a typical project?"

SAY THIS IN THE INTERVIEW

"£5.81bn is the UK and Switzerland figure. The UK alone was about £5.0bn, and UK T&T was £1.75bn, up 4%." Getting this precision right signals you read the release itself, not a headline.

Story 2 · Deloitte global FY26: US$74.5bn, but T&T grew only 2.5%24 Sep 2026
Outlet and source

Deloitte Global press release, "Deloitte reports FY2026 revenue", 24 September 2026 [S221, S120]; City AM, "Deloitte's tech consulting division weighs on growth", 24 September 2026 [S222].

In plain English

The whole network (independent firms in about 150 countries under Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited) reported US$74.5bn for the year to 31 May 2026. That is +5.7% in US dollars and +3.8% in local currency, which strips out exchange-rate effects [S221] [Confirmed; the local-currency total comes from a secondary summary, Reported]. By business, in local currency: Tax & Legal +5.8%, Audit & Assurance +5%, SR&T +4.4%, T&T +2.5%. By region: Asia Pacific +7.5%, EMEA +3.5%, Americas +3.2% [S221] [Confirmed]. Headcount is "nearly 500,000", and Deloitte committed US$3bn through 2030 to modernise operations and build AI-enabled products [S221] [Confirmed].

Why it matters to Deloitte

City AM framed slow T&T growth as a sign of "industry-wide pressure from artificial intelligence" [S222] [Reported]. The irony: the business that should gain most from AI grew slowest, plausibly because AI lets clients do more themselves and compresses hours on big implementations [Inferred]. Accenture, now almost the same size at US$74.18bn, grew 5% in local currency [S129] [Confirmed].

Why it matters to the role

Globally T&T was the slowest business; in the UK it was the fastest. That contrast is a sharp, specific talking point few applicants will have.

Raise this

"Globally T&T grew slowest, but in the UK it grew fastest. Is that the ERP and cyber mix, the public sector, or the regional technology centres?"

Leadership, people and the pyramid

Story 3 · Darren Graves becomes UK CEO; Deloitte UK appoints its first Chief AI Officer1 Jun 2026
Outlet and source

Deloitte UK press release, "Deloitte UK appoints first Chief AI Officer", 1 June 2026 [S226]; FY26 results [S407]; Accountancy Today, 1 April 2026 (headline only) [S251].

In plain English

Darren Graves became CEO and senior partner of Deloitte UK on 1 June 2026. His predecessor Richard Houston became CEO of the new Deloitte EMEA firm. Jane Whitlock became Chair of the UK Board [S407] [Confirmed]. Graves joined as a tax graduate in 2001 [S251] [Reported]. Hayley McKelvey joined the UK Executive as the first Chief AI Officer, "responsible for driving the firm's AI strategy across client services and internal operations" [S407] [Confirmed]. Hammad Rafique was named managing partner of T&T and Dave Sharman of SR&T [S226] [Confirmed].

Why it matters to Deloitte

Putting AI on the executive committee makes it a whole-firm question, not one service line. A CEO from tax rather than consulting is also worth noticing.

Why it matters to the role

Hammad Rafique leads the business you are applying to. Graves's official line on AI and jobs is "As AI makes intelligence increasingly abundant, the role our people play will matter more, not less" [S407]. McKelvey: "Harnessing the power of AI responsibly and strategically, both for our clients and in our own operations, is my priority" [S226].

Raise this

"The Chief AI Officer covers both client work and internal operations. In practice, which moves first: changing how Deloitte delivers, or selling AI to clients?"

Story 4 · Deloitte UK offers voluntary redundancy to up to 175 auditors16 Jun 2026
Outlet and source

City AM, "More Big Four blues as Deloitte plans to slash UK audit roles", 16 June 2026 [S227]; Bloomberg the same day (paywalled, headline only) [S228].

In plain English

Deloitte offered voluntary redundancy on enhanced terms to up to about 175 auditors, under 3% of Audit & Assurance and under 1% of the UK firm. The reason given was "low rates of attrition" [S227, S407] [Confirmed]. Big Four firms rely on people leaving each year to make room for promotions and new graduates. When fewer leave in a weak job market, the pyramid clogs, so firms pay people to go [Inferred]. KPMG cut about 600 UK roles in March 2026 [S227] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

It shows how the FY26 profit jump was achieved, and that the people model is under strain.

Why it matters to the role

Low attrition means fewer entry seats, more competition for promotion and a more selective intake.

Raise this

"If attrition stays low, does the classic pyramid still work, or does the shape move toward a diamond, with fewer juniors and more mid-level specialists?"

Story 5 · 1,353 early-career starters at Deloitte; Big Four overhaul graduate training for AI10 and 23 Sep 2026
Outlet and source

Consultancy.uk, "2026 intake of early-career staff to hit 1,500 at Deloitte", 10 September 2026 [S231]; Resultsense summary of City AM, "How AI is changing Big Four graduate training in the UK", 23 September 2026 [S229]; Deloitte UK, 1 September 2025 (prior year) [S232].

In plain English

Deloitte UK said 1,353 graduates, apprentices and industrial placement students would join across 21 UK offices, 44% outside London, including 182 BrightStart apprentices (the headline says 1,500; the body says 1,353) [S231] [Reported]. In 2025 the intake was about 1,400 across 17 locations, and starters met Deloitte's GenAI platform PairD in induction [S232] [Confirmed]. The Big Four told City AM they are front-loading exams, pushing trainees into judgement work earlier and coaching communication and teamwork, because AI now does much of the data work juniors used to do [S229] [Reported]. KPMG's chief people officer: "Graduates will become more involved in reviewing and exercising judgement rather than simply producing outputs" [S229].

Why it matters to Deloitte

It must keep a pipeline of future managers while AI absorbs junior tasks. Cut too hard now and there are no seniors in eight years [Inferred].

Why it matters to the role

Entry is flat, not growing. Year-one work will be more judgement-based and client-facing than for past cohorts, so communication and critical thinking weigh more in assessment.

Raise this

"If AI handles first drafts and data work, how does Deloitte make sure analysts still learn the fundamentals the drafts are built on?"

Clients, government and the market

Story 6 · Deloitte wins a Home Office data deal worth up to £200m despite the spend pledge1 Jul 2026
Outlet and source

The Register, "UK.gov vows to cut consultancy spending, then hands up to £350M to consultancies", 1 July 2026 [S233].

In plain English

Deloitte won a four-year Home Office data and analytics deal worth up to £200m, and PA Consulting one worth up to £150m [S233] [Reported]. "Up to" means a ceiling, not guaranteed spend [Inferred]. It came despite the government's pledge to cut consultancy spend (Part 6 sets out the conflicting data).

Why it matters to Deloitte

Government is one of Deloitte UK's largest client sectors, and Deloitte is the largest UK government consultancy supplier [S174, S335] [Reported]. Government says "fewer consultants" but still needs data, digital and AI skills it cannot hire fast enough [Inferred].

Why it matters to the role

Many T&T analysts start on public sector work; some programmes need security clearance.

Raise this

"Is the real trend 'less advice, more delivery'? And how does Deloitte help departments build their own capability so they need it less over time?"

Story 7 · UK firms cut consulting spend as they buy AI tools directly9 Sep 2026
Outlet and source

City AM, "UK firms slash consulting spend amid rising AI adoption" (Business Spend Pulse), 9 September 2026 [S239].

In plain English

Card-spend data from fintech Capital on Tap (mostly small firms) showed median consulting fees down 1.9% year on year in August 2026 while total corporate spending rose 2.2%. About one in seven firms now pays AI companies such as Anthropic and ElevenLabs, up from one in thirteen a year earlier. The share of businesses paying professional services firms fell to 18.6%, "the steepest drop in any category" [S239] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

This is the AI substitution risk in numbers. Caveat: it is small-business data; Deloitte's clients are mostly large companies and government, so read it as direction of travel [Inferred].

Why it matters to the role

The work that survives is the work AI cannot easily do: complex integration, change management, regulated and high-trust decisions.

Raise this

"If small firms substitute AI tools for consultants, which parts of a large T&T engagement are most and least exposed?"

Story 8 · MCA: 2025 growth only 3%; clients want help making AI work23 Jan, 6 May, 6 Jul 2026
Outlet and source

Consultancy.uk on MCA data: 23 January 2026 [S241], 6 May 2026 [S240] and 6 July 2026 [S150].

In plain English

The MCA cut its 2026 forecast to 5.7% in January, then reported in July that UK fee income grew 3% to £21.8bn in 2025, with 6% forecast for 2026 and 8% for 2027 [S241, S150] [Reported]. Its May client survey of 350+ leaders found two in five organisations seeking help with digital and technology transformation; top AI barriers were data security and privacy (40%), lack of skilled internal teams (34%) and governance problems (28%) [S240] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

The barriers clients name map onto Deloitte's offer: data engineering, cyber, risk and change management.

Why it matters to the role

"AI" alone is not the product. Getting data, security and governance right is.

Raise this

"If clients' biggest AI barrier is data security, are cyber and data teams now the front door to AI work?"

Story 9 · Accenture FY26 US$74.2bn; PwC UK revenue +2%1 Oct and Sep 2026
Outlet and source

Accenture Q4 FY26 results (SEC filing), 1 October 2026 [S238]; PwC UK results via Accountancy Today, 21 September 2026 [S246, S138]; Irish Times headline, 16 September 2026 [S247].

In plain English

Accenture reported FY26 revenue of US$74.2bn (+5% local currency), split almost evenly between consulting (US$36.9bn) and managed services (US$37.3bn), with about 814,000 people, and guided 3% to 6% growth for FY27 [S238] [Confirmed]. Managed services means running clients' systems for years. PwC UK revenue rose 2% to £4.365bn and profit per partner 8% to £935,000; its wider UK and Middle East group fell 3% to £6.155bn [S246] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

Accenture and Deloitte are now almost the same size (US$74.2bn vs US$74.5bn), but Accenture grows faster and half its revenue recurs [S221, S238] [Inferred comparison].

Why it matters to the role

"Why Deloitte, not Accenture?" is a classic question. One honest answer: Deloitte combines technology delivery with audit, tax, risk and deals expertise in one firm [Inferred].

Raise this

"Accenture earns half its revenue from managed services. Should Deloitte push harder into that, given independence rules limit it at audit clients?"

Rules and reputation

Story 10 · EU delays AI Act high-risk deadlines; UK drops its Audit Reform Bill24 Jun 2026 / Jan 2026
Outlet and source

Morgan Lewis, "EU Approves Delays and Other Amendments to Certain EU AI Act Obligations", 24 June 2026 [S234]; ICAEW, "Government abandons Audit Reform Bill", January 2026 [S235]; Scottish Financial News, 20 January 2026 [S185].

In plain English

Through the "Digital Omnibus", the EU moved stand-alone high-risk AI duties from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, and AI in regulated products to 2 August 2028; bans and AI-literacy duties still apply [S234] [Confirmed by several law-firm summaries]. One tracker gives 2 August 2027 for the high-risk date (see Part 6) [S193]. In the UK, the government confirmed on 20 January 2026 that it would not proceed with the Audit Reform and Corporate Governance Bill, so ARGA will not be created [S185, S235] [Confirmed]. The FRC's July 2026 inspections rated 90% of Deloitte's inspected audits as good or needing limited improvement (KPMG 95%) [S236] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

The AI Act delay may slow urgent compliance work but extends the advisory pipeline into 2027. No audit bill means no forced split of audit and consulting in the UK, which keeps the multidisciplinary model intact [Inferred].

Why it matters to the role

Regulation creates consulting work (AI governance, risk, assurance), and independence rules shape which clients you can serve.

Raise this

"Do clients treat the December 2027 deadline as breathing room, or are leaders using it to build AI governance properly?"

Story 11 · Reputation and risk: US settlement, Australian AI refund, FRC investigationsAug 2026 and ongoing
Outlet and source

US Department of Justice, 25 August 2026 [S242]; The Register, 6 October 2025 [S316]; Accountancy Age, 23 July 2025 [S252]; Accountancy Today, 10 July 2025 [S253].

In plain English

Deloitte's US firm agreed to pay US$21.5m to settle allegations that its diversity programmes breached anti-discrimination terms in federal contracts. The DOJ states these "are allegations only and there has been no determination of liability" [S242] [Confirmed]. In October 2025 Deloitte Australia partly refunded a government department for a report with fabricated references, after disclosing use of a generative AI model [S316] [Confirmed]. In the UK the FRC is investigating Deloitte's audits of Glencore (2013 to 2020) and of collapsed fintech Stenn [S252, S253] [Reported].

Why it matters to Deloitte

Each is a trust risk, and trust is what clients buy. The member firms are legally separate, but the brand is shared [S302] [Confirmed].

Why it matters to the role

You will use AI tools as an analyst, and you will be accountable for checking every source and number.

Raise this

Neutrally: "After the Australian report, what quality checks does Deloitte UK apply when GenAI is used in client deliverables?"

Tech Trends 2026 in brief

Deloitte's 17th annual Tech Trends report (10 December 2025) says the question has moved from "what can we do with AI?" to "how do we move from experimentation to impact?" [S244] [Confirmed]. Five trends:

  1. AI goes physical: AI in robots and autonomous devices that act in the real world.
  2. The agentic workforce: AI agents that take actions. Only 11% of organisations have them in production; the hard part is redesigning processes "agent-first" rather than automating old workflows.
  3. The AI infrastructure reckoning: unit costs fall but total AI bills rise with use; firms mix cloud, on-premises and edge computing.
  4. The great rebuild: AI reshapes how IT functions are organised; leaner teams tied to business outcomes.
  5. The AI dilemma in cyber: AI creates new weaknesses but also stronger defences, such as automated red-teaming (simulated attacks).

Quote to use: Kelly Raskovich, "the distance between emerging and mainstream is collapsing" [S244]. A related September 2026 Deloitte study of 25,000 UK workers estimated they spend about £958m a year of their own money on GenAI tools for work, and nearly a third of users use it without their employer knowing ("shadow AI") [S243] [Reported].

YOUR OPINION GOES HERE

Prompt: "Which story are you following, and what do you think?" Pick one where you can add a mechanism. Example stance: "Tech Trends says only 11% of organisations have agentic AI in production, and Deloitte's own survey found a third of GenAI users hide it from their employer. Both point the same way: the bottleneck is process redesign and governance, not the models. That is consulting work, and it is why I think T&T's growth depends on change management as much as engineering." Counterpoint to acknowledge: SME data show some clients buying AI tools instead of advisers [S239].
