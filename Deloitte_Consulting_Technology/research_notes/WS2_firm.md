## 1. Findings

### A. Founding insight and the decision that shaped today's firm

**Origins.** William Welch Deloitte set up in London in 1845 (Basinghall Street). He is described as the first person appointed independent auditor of a public company, the Great Western Railway [S103, Reported; Wikipedia, tertiary]. The founding idea: an independent professional checks the numbers that investors rely on. That "trust" idea still sits at the centre of the brand.

**Mergers that built the name** [S103, Reported]:
- 1952: merged with Haskins & Sells (US) to form Deloitte Haskins & Sells.
- 1989: combined with Touche Ross in the US to form Deloitte & Touche.
- 1993: international firm renamed Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu (Tohmatsu was the Japanese partner firm).
- 2002: Arthur Andersen's UK practice joined Deloitte UK, after Andersen collapsed over Enron.
- 10 January 2003: Deloitte LLP (the UK firm) was incorporated as a limited liability partnership [S93, Confirmed, primary accounts].

**The 2002-03 decision not to split off consulting.** After Enron (2001) and the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (2002), regulators worried that auditors who also sold consulting were conflicted. The other big firms sold or spun out their consultancies: Ernst & Young sold to Cap Gemini (2000), KPMG Consulting floated and became BearingPoint (2001), PwC Consulting was sold to IBM (2002), Andersen's consulting unit was sold in pieces (2002) [S104, Confirmed for Deloitte facts; Reported for peer details]. Deloitte announced in February 2002 that it would separate Deloitte Consulting as "Braxton", owned by its partners. On 31 March 2003 it abandoned the plan, citing a "tight credit market and the uncertain state of the economy". Deloitte became the only Big Four firm to keep its consulting practice, and committed to keep it "focused on nonaudit clients" in line with Sarbanes-Oxley and SEC rules [S104, Reported]. Clients such as General Motors and Vodafone were reported to have walked away during the uncertainty [search snippet of InfoWorld and Accountancy Age, 2003; treat as Reported].

**Why it matters now [Inferred].** Keeping consulting gave Deloitte a 20-year head start in building a multidisciplinary firm. By FY2025, consulting services were US$43.7bn of US$70.5bn global revenue, about 62% [S81, Confirmed]. Deloitte reports it has been ranked No. 1 consulting provider by revenue for the eighth consecutive year (Gartner basis) [S81 page summary, Reported]. The trade-off is permanent: the firm must police independence between audit and advisory clients every day (see section D). For a candidate, "one firm with audit, tax, consulting and deals under one roof" is a structural fact, not marketing.

**Stated purpose.** "To make an impact that matters, for our clients, our people, and society" [S82, Confirmed; text on the Deloitte Global Facts and Figures page and the 2025 Global Impact Report landing page, read 2026-10-04]. The UK Audit Transparency Report frames the audit business purpose separately as "Protecting the public interest and building trust and confidence in business" [S94, Confirmed].

Key points:
- Partner profit is not a salary cost. In an LLP, partners (members) are owners. What is left after paying staff and costs is the profit pool. Distributable profit of £899m divided by 799 FTE equity partners gives the £1.125m average [S84, S88; arithmetic Inferred].
- Distributable profit is on a "management accounts" basis, using different accounting policies to the statutory statements [S93, Confirmed]. That is why £899m differs from the £769m statutory profit.
- About a quarter of revenue is pass-through (subcontractors and recoverable expenses). Revenue includes "recoverable expenses incurred on assignments", including sub-contractor costs [S93, Confirmed].
- Average fee-earning employees fell from 21,471 to 20,346 FTE (-5%) while revenue rose 2% [S93, Confirmed]. More revenue from fewer people pushed profit up 14%. [Inferred: this is the leverage-and-productivity lever working in partners' favour, likely helped by offshore delivery centres, AI tooling and lower hiring.]

#### B4. Engagement economics: the money flow, step by step

How fees are earned. Deloitte's accounts state revenue is recognised either on time-and-materials (time charged to date) or, for fixed-fee work, "based upon the value of the actual services provided ... determined based on the actual inputs of time at charge-out rates, via timesheet reporting" [S93, Confirmed]. Contingent fees are not recognised until the contingency resolves. Transaction-based fees (for example data processing) are "per unit" [S93, Confirmed]. So even fixed-price work is managed internally through hours times charge-out rates. That is the "rate card".

The chain [Inferred, standard professional-services model; Deloitte does not publish its internal targets]:
1. **Rate card**: each grade (analyst/consultant, senior consultant, manager, senior manager, director, partner) has a standard charge-out rate per day.
2. **Utilisation**: share of available hours that are chargeable to clients. Juniors are expected to be highly chargeable. Partners spend more time selling and managing.
3. **Realisation**: share of the standard value of time that is actually billed and collected after discounts and write-offs. Fixed-fee overruns reduce realisation.
4. **Leverage (the pyramid)**: number of staff per partner. More juniors per partner means each partner's profit pool draws on more billable hours.
5. **Cost**: staff pay, pensions, social security, subcontractors, property, IT, DTTL network fees.
6. **Profit pool**: what remains is divided among equity partners by units.

Sourced leverage numbers [Inferred arithmetic on Confirmed inputs]:
- Deloitte UK FY2026: 23,970 FTE employees / 1,453 partners = about 16.5 staff per partner. Per equity partner: about 30. Fee-earners only (20,346): about 14 per partner [S88, S93].
- Deloitte global FY2025: 473,050 people / 21,183 partners, principals and managing directors = about 22 per partner [S81].
- Revenue per FTE (people plus equity partners), UK group FY2026: about £235k. Global FY2025: about US$149k per person [Inferred; S81, S88, S93]. The gap partly reflects price levels (UK/Swiss versus global offshore delivery).
- Average employee cost per FTE, UK group FY2026: about £122k including employer social security, pensions and bonuses, and including salaried partners who count as employees [Inferred; S93]. This is not a typical salary; it is skewed by senior grades and Swiss pay.

Day rates. The UK government buys consultancy through the Management Consultancy Framework Four (MCF4, RM6309), run by the Government Commercial Agency (formerly Crown Commercial Service). It runs 29 July 2025 to 28 July 2027, has 219 suppliers across 10 lots, and allows time-and-materials, fixed or risk-and-reward pricing [S117, Confirmed]. Supplier rate cards sit in a filtering tool and pricing schedules I could not open. I did not verify Deloitte's MCF4 day rates. Gap: do not quote a number.

Utilisation and realisation targets: no sourced Deloitte figure found. Gap.

### C. The "contracts": what each party gives and gets

**Equity partners (members of Deloitte LLP)** [S93, Confirmed unless marked]:
- Give capital. Each member's capital is set by their profit "units"; it is repayable after retirement. Total members' capital £296m at 31 May 2026 (2025: £293m). That is roughly £366k per member on average across 809 members [Inferred arithmetic; individual amounts vary].
- Get a discretionary profit share, "based upon a comprehensive evaluation of their individual and team contribution to, among others, quality, risk, performance, and leadership". The UK Executive allocates units, with oversight from the Partnership Council. Audit Non-Executives observe the process for audit partners to test the link to audit quality.
- Are paid by monthly drawings (advance on expected profit), with tax retentions paid to HMRC on their behalf. Average effective tax rate on equity partners 49% in FY2026 [S88].
- Retirement: a partner annuity scheme exists but was closed to new entrants from 1 June 2021. Annuity and related liabilities are £1,545m. Annual payments are mainly capped at 8% of group operating profit before annuity charges [S93, Confirmed]. [Inferred: current partners are, in effect, funding past partners' pensions out of today's profit.]
- Liability: as members of an LLP (Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000), partners are not personally liable for the firm's debts in the way old general partnership partners were [S93 confirms LLP status; the general legal point is Inferred/standard]. The firm also carries professional indemnity insurance [S93].
- Elect leaders. The Senior Partner and Chief Executive is chosen by partner vote following soundings by the Partnership Council [S93, S96].
- **Salaried partners** carry the title but are employees, included in employee numbers (654 in FY2026) [S88, Confirmed]. Converting to equity is a separate step: 68 converted on 1 June 2026, 77 in 2025, 29 in 2024 [S84, S85, S95, Confirmed].

**Staff** [S84, S89, S95, Confirmed; norms labelled]:
- Give time, chargeable hours and mobility. Get salary, bonus, training and promotion.
- 1 June 2026: over 6,000 promoted, average in-grade salary rise 4.2%, bonus pool +14% [S95].
- May 2025 message to UK staff (reported): the firm would "promote fewer people and reduce average salary rises" because profits were "below our original plan" [S101, Reported].
- Voluntary attrition 10.0% in FY2025 (9.4% FY24, 11.1% FY23) [S89, Confirmed].
- L&D spend £64.2m in FY2025, about £2,473 and 35 hours per FTE [S89, Confirmed].
- Norm [Inferred, not a Deloitte-published policy]: Big Four careers run on an "up or out" logic, where people are expected to progress through grades on a rough timetable, and professional qualifications (ACA, CTA, CIMA, or technology certifications in T&T) are often sponsored. Promotion numbers and the cut in partner and director promotions (501 in FY23, 485 in FY24, 341 in FY25) show the top of the pyramid narrowing [S89, Confirmed numbers].

**Clients** [Inferred, standard]:
- Give fees (time-and-materials, fixed fee, contingent or per-unit) and access to their people and data [S93 for fee types, Confirmed].
- Get expertise, extra capacity, a brand that reassures boards and regulators, and someone accountable for delivery. They also take risk if delivery fails, as the Rhode Island and Australian cases show (section I).

### D. Risk and control architecture

**The global network** [S83, S82, Confirmed]:
- Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (DTTL) is "a private company limited by guarantee incorporated in England and Wales". DTTL "does not provide services to clients".
- "DTTL and each DTTL member firm ... are legally separate and independent entities, which cannot obligate or bind each other in respect of third parties" and are "liable only for their own acts and omissions, and not those of each other."
- Each member firm pays DTTL subscription fees (listed in UK "other operating expenses") [S93, Confirmed].
- Why it matters [Inferred]: a scandal in one country (China, Australia, US) does not legally bind the UK firm, but it hits the shared brand.

**UK structure, 2003 to May 2026** [S93, Confirmed]:
- Deloitte LLP (OC303675), registered office 1 New Street Square, London EC4A 3HQ. Ultimate parent to 31 May 2026: Deloitte NSE LLP (North and South Europe), a holding entity that served no clients. NSE was described as the second-largest member firm with "over 75,000 people and 4,000 partners across 30 countries" [S94, Confirmed].
- Governance to 31 May 2026: Senior Partner and Chief Executive plus UK Executive (management); UK Oversight Board (UKOB) with four Independent Non-Executives (oversight, public interest, Audit Firm Governance Code); Audit Governance Board (AGB, majority independent, chaired by an Audit Non-Executive); UK and Swiss Partnership Council (fairness between partners, soundings for leadership elections) [S93, S94].

**UK structure from 1 June 2026** [S93, Confirmed]:
- Deloitte LLP, with its Swiss practice, combined with 14 other member firms to form **Deloitte EMEA**. Deloitte NSE LLP ceased to be the parent.
- The UKOB was replaced by a **UK Board**, which sets long-term strategy with the UK Executive and oversees risk, reputation, public interest, quality and ethics. Committees: Audit and Risk, Remuneration, Nomination, and the Partnership Council. The AGB continues.
- Designated members: Darren Graves and Heather Bygrave from 1 June 2026, plus Martin Reilly from 1 September 2026.
- Auditor of Deloitte LLP: BDO LLP. Audit fee £914k for FY2026 [S93].

**Audit quality (ISQM (UK) 1)** [S94, Confirmed]:
- ISQM (UK) 1 is the standard that requires an audit firm to run a "system of quality management". Deloitte UK's evaluation at 31 May 2025 found "two deficiencies identified (neither severe nor pervasive)" and concluded the system gives reasonable assurance. One deficiency was remediated by the report date.
- FRC inspection: "95% of sampled audits were rated as good or limited improvements required" (FY2024: 94%).
- Output: 200 public-interest-entity audit opinions in FY2025 (FY24: 216); 400 assurance opinions including 34 sustainability assurance opinions.
- "Operational separation" (ring-fencing the audit practice from the rest of the firm, an FRC requirement for the Big Four) completed its transition period during FY2025. The AGB said ringfencing "has undoubtedly played an important part" in improved quality.
- Deloitte UK uses an offshore audit delivery centre, Global Audit India.

**Consulting-relevant controls** [S94, Confirmed]:
- "All proposed engagements and business and financial relationships go through a conflict check before they are accepted." If conflicts cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, "we decline the engagement."
- The audit partner is always consulted where a potential conflict touches an audit client.
- **Public Interest Review Group (PIRG)** reviews engagements with high public-interest or reputational risk. Over 800 matters reviewed in its decade-plus history. FY2025: 32 matters formally consulted, 9% declined (FY2024: 38 matters, 19% declined).
- A Tax Review Panel considers reputational risks in complex tax work.
- For SR&T and T&T, the firm set up a "Consultative Forum" and a "globally aligned client portfolio management process" [S94, Confirmed].
- Principal risks register rates "Our reputation, role and future public interest impact" at **Very High** residual risk. Threats listed include "Increased government focus on the reputation and role and public sector use of professional services organisations" and "Increasing breadth and depth of non-audit offerings (including Operate)". "Client Portfolio" risk is rated High, citing "Reputational risks associated with client selection" and the "company we choose to keep" [S94, Confirmed].

### E. Business mix and growth over time

Global revenue grew from US$35.2bn (FY2015) to US$74.5bn (FY2026), about 7.1% a year compounded [Inferred; S70-S80]. The fastest years were FY2022 (+19.6% local currency) and FY2023 (+14.9%), the post-COVID digital and consulting boom [S73, S74]. Growth then slowed to 3-5% a year from FY2024 [S70-S72].

Deloitte UK (including Switzerland) grew from £3,950m (FY2019) to £5,809m (FY2026), about 5.7% a year [Inferred; S88, S92]. UK Consulting peaked under the old structure at £1,596m in FY2023 [S90]; restated T&T peaked at £1,857m in FY2024 then fell 10% in FY2025 [S88, S89].

UK FTE employees rose from 18,405 (FY2019) to a peak of 26,824 (FY2024), then fell to 23,970 (FY2026), down 11% in two years [S88, S89, S92, Confirmed; % Inferred]. Over the same two years, equity partners rose from 749 to 799 [S88]. Fewer staff, more equity partners, higher profit per partner: that is the shape of the last two years.

### I. Skeletons in the cupboard (verified only)

**UK audit regulator (FRC) sanctions on Deloitte UK:**
1. **Autonomy, 17 September 2020.** Record £15m fine and severe reprimand for audits of Autonomy Corporation plc covering January 2009 to June 2011. Former partners Richard Knights (£500,000 fine, five-year exclusion from ICAEW) and Nigel Mercer (£250,000, severe reprimand). Deloitte also paid about £5.6m of FRC costs. The tribunal cited "wholesale nature of the failure of professional scepticism" [S105, Confirmed, FRC primary].
2. **Serco, 16 July 2019.** £4.2m fine (after settlement discount) over audits of Serco's Geografix tagging division in 2011 and 2012. Partner Helen George fined £97,500 [S106, Reported].
3. **Mitie, 21 April 2022.** £2m fine, reduced to £1.45m, over 2016 goodwill impairment testing; partner John Charlton fined. The FRC said there was "no suggestion that breaches were intentional, dishonest or reckless" [S107, Reported].
4. **SIG, 22 December 2022.** £1.25m, discounted to £906,250, for audits of SIG's 2015-16 results (supplier rebates and cheque payments) [S108, Reported].
- **Open: Glencore.** FRC investigation (opened March 2025, announced July 2025) into Deloitte's Glencore audits for 2013-2020. Glencore pleaded guilty to bribery in 2022 [S109, Reported]. Outcome not known as of 2026-10-04.
- Context: over five years to June 2025, Big Four pre-discount FRC fines were KPMG £81.8m, PwC £34m, Deloitte £19m, EY £18m [S110, Reported].
- **Not verified as Deloitte matters, so excluded:** Kier, Ted Baker and Patisserie Valerie. My understanding is these involved other auditors, but I did not verify that in this session. Do not attribute them to Deloitte.

**Deloitte Australia AI report refund (October 2025)** [S113, Reported]. Australia's Department of Employment and Workplace Relations paid AU$440,000 for a review of the welfare Targeted Compliance Framework (commissioned December 2024, published July 2025). A University of Sydney academic, Dr Christopher Rudge, found fabricated references and a misquoted Federal Court judgment. The corrected report disclosed the use of "a generative AI large language model (Azure OpenAI GPT-4o)". Deloitte agreed to refund the final instalment. Why it matters: it is the cautionary tale for AI-assisted consulting, and it broke the same week as the Anthropic deal.

**US federal cuts (DOGE), 2025** [S114, Reported]. At least 129 Deloitte contracts were terminated or cut back, more than double any other consultancy. Government contracts were about US$3.2bn of Deloitte US revenue in 2024. Deloitte US made "modest personnel actions" in its government consulting business in April 2025. Why it matters: public-sector consulting carries political risk. The UK saw a milder version, with UK GPS revenue down 14% in FY2025 around the election [S89].

**Rhode Island RIBridges breach** [S115, Reported]. Deloitte Consulting ran Rhode Island's benefits system. The Brain Cipher ransomware gang got in using a Deloitte employee's stolen credentials (from July 2024, discovered December 2024), affecting more than 650,000 people [Bleepingcomputer snippet for credentials detail, Reported]. A final settlement in May 2026 brought total state recovery to US$12m, plus about US$6m in system support. Deloitte denied wrongdoing. Why it matters: in "Operate" and managed services, the firm owns ongoing operational and cyber risk, not just advice.

**Deloitte China** [S111, S112]:
- SEC, 29 September 2022: US$20m penalty. Deloitte China staff let audit clients select their own samples and prepare audit documentation (2016-2018 audits) [S111, Confirmed, SEC primary].
- China Ministry of Finance, 17 March 2023: 211.9m yuan (about US$31m) fine and three-month suspension of Deloitte's Beijing office over audits of China Huarong Asset Management [S112, Reported].

**Earlier US matter.** Rhode Island 2020: a US$50m settlement over the original RIBridges system design failures [S103, Wikipedia only; treat as Reported, low confidence].

**Why each matters to a consulting candidate [Inferred]:**
- They show how the firm's two biggest assets, trust and one brand, are exposed. A failure in one country or business damages all of them.
- They explain why engagement acceptance, independence checks and the PIRG are taken seriously (section D).
- The Australia and Rhode Island cases are the live risks of the AI-and-operate model that T&T is selling. A candidate who can talk about controls, not just hype, stands out.

## 6. Interview angles

1. "Deloitte is the only Big Four firm that kept its consulting arm after Sarbanes-Oxley. It nearly spun it out as Braxton in 2002 and pulled back in 2003. Today consulting is about 62% of global revenue, and T&T alone is US$33.4bn." [S104, S81]
2. "The UK headline revenue includes Switzerland. UK-only revenue was £5.0bn in FY2026, of which T&T was £1.75bn, the biggest single business." [S88]
3. "FY2025 was the first UK revenue fall in about 15 years, driven by a 10% fall in T&T and a slowdown in government work around the election. FY2026 T&T is back to plus 4%. I'd be curious what has changed in demand: is it AI-led work replacing large ERP-style programmes?" [S89, S84]
4. "Profit rose 14% on 2% revenue growth while fee-earning staff fell about 5%. That says productivity and leverage are shifting. How is that changing what a first-year analyst actually does?" [S93]
5. "I read in the accounts that even fixed-fee work is tracked through timesheets at charge-out rates. So utilisation still matters to the economics even when the client buys an outcome." [S93]
6. "The Public Interest Review Group declined 9% of the 32 matters it formally reviewed last year. How does that kind of gate show up for consultants day to day?" [S94]
7. "Deloitte EMEA went live on 1 June 2026: about €20bn revenue, 6,000 partners and €1.5bn of planned investment. For a graduate in London, does that mean more cross-border staffing?" [S98, S93]
8. "Darren Graves came up through tax and transactions and talks about reinventing the firm for the next generation. Hayley McKelvey joined the UK Executive as Chief AI Officer. What does reinvention mean for T&T specifically?" [S96, S97, S84]
9. "Deloitte rolled Claude out to 470,000 people in October 2025, and the same week the Australian refund showed the risk of unchecked AI output. I think the winners will be firms whose quality controls scale as fast as their AI use." [S116, S113]
10. "Public-sector consulting is a political risk. US DOGE cuts hit at least 129 Deloitte contracts, and UK government revenue fell 14% around the 2024 election before recovering. How does the GPS practice manage that cycle?" [S114, S89]
11. "Partner economics: about 800 equity partners sharing £899m, with capital repayable on retirement and an old annuity scheme capped at 8% of profit. The pyramid is narrowing at the top: partner and director promotions fell from 501 to 341 over two years." [S93, S89]
12. "In the Transparency Report, 'Our reputation, role and future public interest impact' is rated Very High residual risk, partly because of growing non-audit and Operate work. Rhode Island showed what operational risk looks like in managed services." [S94, S115]
13. Ask: "Entry-level hiring went from 2,767 in FY23 to about 1,900 to 1,950 in FY25 and FY26. Where in T&T is the intake growing, and where is it shrinking?" [S89, S84]
