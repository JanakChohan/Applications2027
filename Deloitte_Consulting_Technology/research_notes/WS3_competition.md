#### A2. What each firm's latest results say

**Deloitte global.** Deloitte reported US$74.5bn for the year to 31 May 2026. Growth was 3.8% in local currency and 5.7% in US dollars [S120] [Confirmed]. By business, in local currency: Audit & Assurance +5%, SR&T +4.4%, T&T +2.5%, Tax & Legal +5.8% [S120] [Confirmed]. So the two consulting-type businesses grew more slowly than audit and tax this year. That is a reversal of the usual pattern. By region: Asia Pacific +7.5%, EMEA +3.5%, Americas +3.2% [S120]. Headcount is "nearly 500,000" [S120]. Deloitte also announced a US$3bn investment through 2030 to modernise delivery and operations [S120] [Confirmed]. The year before (FY25) was US$70.5bn, +4.8% LC, with T&T +4.7% and SR&T +5.5%, and more than 470,000 people [S121] [Confirmed]. Going Concern notes Deloitte "was the first Big 4 to cross the $70 billion revenue threshold" [S120] [Reported].

**Deloitte UK.** Revenue for the year to 31 May 2026 rose 2% to £5.81bn (FY25: £5.68bn) [S122] [Confirmed]. By business: T&T £1.75bn (+4%), SR&T Advisory £913m (+1%), Tax & Legal £1.36bn (+2%), Audit & Assurance £998m (+3%) [S122] [Confirmed]. Those four lines add up to about £5.02bn, not £5.81bn. I could not find what the other ~£0.8bn is. Do not quote a split without checking (see Gaps). The UK had 3,500 new hires, including "more than 1,950 graduates, apprentices and interns", and "Over 6,000 of our 25,000 people were promoted" [S122] [Confirmed]. Average profit per equity partner rose 7% to £1.125m; equity partners rose to 784 [S122] [Confirmed, via fetched summary]. The UK CEO quote: "Our advisory businesses returned to profitable growth" [S122]. Context: in FY25 Deloitte UK revenue fell 1% to £5.68bn, its first fall in 15 years, and consulting revenue fell 10% to £1.68bn [S123] [Reported]. TechMarketView says ERP and cyber demand drove the FY26 T&T recovery [S400] [Reported].

**Deloitte EMEA (new structure).** On 23 February 2026 Deloitte announced an EMEA firm, effective 1 June 2026. It combines 16 firms including the UK, with 6,000 partners, 132,000 people, more than 80 countries and €20bn of revenue [S124] [Confirmed]. It plans more than €1.5bn of incremental investment over four years in GenAI, sovereign cloud and sector solutions [S124] [Confirmed]. Richard Houston (previously UK and North & South Europe CEO) is CEO [S124]. Darren Graves became UK CEO from 1 June 2026 [S122].

**PwC.** Global revenue US$56.9bn for the year to 30 June 2025, +2.7% LC. Advisory grew 4.5% to US$24.3bn; Assurance +1.7% to US$19.8bn; Tax & Legal +1.0% to US$12.7bn. Headcount 364,000. PwC said it invested US$1.5bn to scale AI [S126] [Reported; PwC's own page returned 403]. FY26 global figures were not found as of today (see Gaps). PwC UK FY26: UK revenue +2% to £4.365bn; the wider UK group (UK, Middle East, Channel Islands) fell 3% to £6.155bn; group consulting revenue fell to £1.818bn from £2.015bn [S138] [Reported, snippet]. The prior year, PwC UK cut 123 partners and saw consulting fall 3% [S401] [Reported].

**EY.** Global revenue US$53.2bn for FY25 (to June 2025), +4.0% LC. Consulting US$16.4bn (+5.2%), Assurance US$17.9bn (+3.5%), Tax US$12.7bn (+5.5%), Strategy and Transactions (EY-Parthenon) US$6.2bn (flat). Headcount about 406,000. AI-related revenue grew 30% [S127] [Confirmed]. EY UK FY25: fee income £3.78bn (+2%); Strategy & Transactions +10%; consulting -6% [S139] [Reported]. EY FY26 global and UK results were not yet found.

**KPMG.** Global revenue US$39.8bn for the year to 30 September 2025, +5.1% LC: the fastest Big Four growth that cycle. Advisory grew only 2.9%; Tax & Legal 7.5%; Audit 6.0%. Headcount 276,030 [S128] [Confirmed]. KPMG UK merged with KPMG Switzerland on 1 October 2024. The UK/Swiss group made £3.6bn (+2%) in FY25, with Advisory down 3% and profit per partner £880,000 [S140] [Confirmed].

**Accenture.** FY26 (to 31 August 2026) revenue US$74.18bn, +5% LC. Consulting US$36.88bn (+3% LC). Managed Services US$37.30bn (+6% LC). New bookings US$84.54bn. About 814,000 people. GAAP operating margin 15.4%. FY26 business optimisation (mainly severance) cost US$307.5m [S129] [Confirmed]. FY27 guidance: 3% to 6% LC growth [S129]. Important for the "size" debate: Accenture's FY26 revenue (US$74.18bn) is within about US$0.3bn of Deloitte's FY26 (US$74.5bn). But only about half of Accenture is consulting. The other half is managed services. In 2025 Accenture ran an US$865m restructuring and exited staff it could not reskill for AI. Headcount fell from 791,000 to 779,000 in the quarter to August 2025 [S130] [Reported].

**IBM Consulting.** 2025 revenue US$21.055bn, +1.8% reported, +0.4% at constant currency. Segment profit margin 11.7% [S131] [Reported, from 10-K snippet].

**Capgemini.** 2025 revenue €22.465bn, +3.4% at constant currency. Operating margin 13.3%. Headcount 423,400, up 24% because of the WNS acquisition; 66% offshore [S132] [Reported].

**McKinsey.** Bloomberg (via Slashdot summary) reported in December 2025 that McKinsey planned a "roughly 10% headcount reduction in support functions" over 18-24 months. Headcount is about 40,000, down from 45,000 in 2022. Revenue has "hovered around $15 billion to $16 billion for the past five years". It "still plans to hire consultants" [S135] [Reported].

**BCG.** 2025 revenue US$14.4bn, +7%, the 22nd straight year of growth. 33,500 people. AI- and tech-focused services are over 40% of revenue, with AI services up 25% [S133] [Confirmed].

#### A3. Mix, pricing and positioning (evidence-based where possible)

- **Deloitte.** Four businesses: Audit & Assurance, SR&T, T&T, Tax & Legal [S120]. Strategy brand Monitor Deloitte (from the 2013 Monitor Group purchase) [S134]. Deloitte also sells "Operate" services. The FY26 release names Operate as an area of "significant growth" [S120] [Confirmed]. Operate is Deloitte's managed-services offer, so Deloitte prices across time and materials, fixed fee and multi-year managed services. Exact pricing mix is not disclosed [Inferred].
- **Accenture.** Exactly half consulting, half managed services by revenue [S129] [Confirmed]. Managed services are long contracts, typically priced per outcome or per unit of service. This is why Accenture can report bookings and a book-to-bill ratio (1.1 overall) [S129]. Deloitte does not report bookings.
- **IBM Consulting.** Split into Strategy & Technology and Intelligent Operations (the managed-services half) [S131]. Strongly tied to IBM's own software and Red Hat [Inferred].
- **Capgemini.** Two-thirds offshore staff [S132]. Its model is volume technology delivery from India and other low-cost centres [Inferred from S132].
- **PwC / EY / KPMG.** Same multidisciplinary shape as Deloitte. PwC has the largest advisory line disclosed in dollars among the three (US$24.3bn) [S126]. EY publishes Consulting and EY-Parthenon separately [S127].
- **McKinsey / BCG / Bain.** Pure-play advice, mostly project fees. BCG says over 40% of revenue is now AI- and tech-focused [S133], so the gap with "implementation" firms is narrowing.

### C. Design axes: where each firm sits

Scale: 1 = left end, 5 = right end.

**Axis 1. Audit-anchored (1) vs independent of audit (5).**
- Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG: 1. All four combine audit with consulting [S120][S126][S127][S128]. Trade-off: audit independence rules stop them selling many consulting services to their own audit clients. In return they get a trust brand and audit-led client relationships.
- Evidence the constraint bites: EY's "Project Everest" tried to split audit from consulting to remove these conflicts. It was abandoned on 12 April 2023 after the US executive committee withdrew [S141] [Reported]. KPMG's new US law firm (approved in Arizona, 27 Feb 2025) may not serve KPMG audit clients [S142] [Reported].
- Accenture: 5. It was spun out of Arthur Andersen and is fully separate from audit (background). IBM, Capgemini, McKinsey, BCG, Bain: 5.

**Axis 2. Strategy-to-execution breadth (1) vs specialism (5).**
- Deloitte: 1. Strategy (Monitor Deloitte), technology implementation (T&T), risk, transactions, tax, audit and managed services (Operate) under one brand [S120][S134].
- Accenture: 1-2. Strategy through managed services, but no audit or tax [S129].
- PwC, EY, KPMG: 1-2. Same breadth as Deloitte, smaller consulting scale [S126][S127][S128].
- IBM Consulting, Capgemini: 3. Technology-led, lighter on strategy [S131][S132].
- McKinsey, BCG, Bain: 4-5. Strategy specialists moving into implementation; BCG's 40% AI/tech share shows the move [S133].

**Axis 3. Partnership (1) vs public company (5).**
- Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, McKinsey, BCG, Bain: 1. Partners own the firm. Investment comes from partners' profits. Deloitte's US$3bn AI programme and EMEA's €1.5bn plan are funded this way [S120][S124].
- Accenture, IBM, Capgemini: 5. Shareholders, quarterly guidance, buybacks. Accenture returned US$11.5bn to shareholders in FY26 [S129].
- Middle ground: private equity in mid-tier firms. Grant Thornton US sold about 60% of its non-audit business to New Mountain Capital in 2024 [S144]. Grant Thornton UK took Cinven investment in 2025 [S145]. Baker Tilly merged with Moss Adams in June 2025 with Hellman & Friedman support [S403]. None of the Big Four has done this.
- Deloitte EMEA is a different move: larger regional partnership, not outside capital [S124].

**Axis 4. Pyramid shape and offshore leverage.**
- Revenue per head is a simple proxy (US$ thousands, my calculation): BCG ~430; McKinsey ~375-400; PwC ~156; Deloitte ~149; KPMG ~144; EY ~131; Accenture ~91; Capgemini ~€53k [Inferred from S120, S126-S129, S132, S133, S135]. Low revenue per head means a wide pyramid and heavy offshore use. Capgemini is 66% offshore [S132].
- Deloitte sits in the middle: wider than strategy houses, narrower than Accenture.
- All are thinning the base. Big Four UK graduate cuts 2023-24 [S143]. McKinsey support-staff cuts [S135]. Accenture exits [S130].

**Axis 5. Alliance-led (1) vs vendor-neutral (5).**
- Deloitte: 1. Deloitte US lists alliances across SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday and others, with "over 36,000 SAP-focused professionals" [S146] [Confirmed].
- Accenture: 1. Also deeply alliance-led (background; not researched).
- IBM Consulting: 2. Partner-led, but with a pull to IBM's own software [Inferred].
- McKinsey, BCG, Bain: 4-5. Traditionally vendor-neutral. But Bain now describes "a vibrant ecosystem of technology partners" on its own About page [S406], so even strategy firms now talk about partners.

**Axis 6. Time & materials (1) vs managed services / outcome-based (5).**
- Accenture: 4. Half of revenue is managed services [S129].
- IBM: 3-4. Intelligent Operations line [S131].
- Capgemini: 3-4. Offshore run services [S132].
- Deloitte: 2-3. Mostly project work, but "Operate" is growing [S120].
- PwC, EY, KPMG: 2.
- McKinsey, BCG, Bain: 1-2. Mostly project fees; some outcome-linked deals (not verified here).

### D. Industry events, verified and dated

1. **EY Project Everest (2022-23).** EY planned to split audit and consulting and list the consulting side. It was abandoned on 12 April 2023 after the US executive committee withdrew, following a dispute about how much of tax stayed with audit. Reported cost about US$600m [S141] [Reported]. Lesson: the audit-plus-consulting model is hard to unwind.
2. **KPMG Law US (2025).** The Arizona Supreme Court licensed KPMG Law US LLC as an Alternative Business Structure on 27 February 2025. It is the first Big Four law firm in the US. It may not serve clients KPMG audits [S142] [Reported, Arizona court release seen as snippet].
3. **PwC disposals.** PwC sold its global mobility tax and immigration business to CD&R for US$2.2bn, announced October 2021 (now Vialto Partners). It was PwC's largest disposal in nearly two decades [S147] [Confirmed]. PwC also sold SME audit/advisory businesses in Sweden and Norway (snippets only). In October 2024 PwC UK restructured consulting, deals, risk and tax, affecting about 2,700 staff and partners and creating a digital delivery unit of about 900 technologists [S404] [Reported].
4. **Private equity in mid-tier firms.** Grant Thornton US: New Mountain Capital majority stake (about 60%), announced March 2024, closed mid-2024; audit stays in a separate CPA firm [S144] [Reported]. Grant Thornton UK: Cinven deal closed 2025 [S145] [Reported]. Baker Tilly and Moss Adams combined on 3 June 2025, about US$7bn, with Hellman & Friedman [S403] [Reported].
5. **Accenture slowdown and AI pivot.** US$865m restructuring in 2025; more than 11,000 exits; CEO Julie Sweet said the firm would exit people who could not be reskilled [S130] [Reported]. FY26 growth 5% LC, FY27 guidance 3-6% [S129] [Confirmed]. Consulting grew only 3% LC in FY26 while managed services grew 6% [S129].
6. **McKinsey shrinkage.** About 10% cut in support functions over 18-24 months (Bloomberg, Dec 2025). Headcount ~40,000 vs 45,000 in 2022; revenue flat at US$15-16bn for five years [S135] [Reported].
7. **Big Four UK graduate cuts.** 2023 to 2024 intakes fell at all four (KPMG -29%, Deloitte -18%, EY -11%, PwC -6%) [S143] [Reported]. Deloitte UK FY26: 1,950+ graduates, apprentices and interns among 3,500 hires [S122] [Confirmed]. MCA members overall raised graduate hiring 10% in 2025 [S148] [Reported]. So the Big Four squeeze is not the whole market.
8. **UK consulting dip and recovery.** FY25 UK consulting falls: Deloitte -10% [S123], EY -6% [S139], KPMG Advisory -3% [S140], PwC consulting -3% [S401]. FY26 recovery at Deloitte T&T +4% [S122]; PwC UK group consulting still down [S138].
9. **Network consolidation.** KPMG UK + Switzerland (Oct 2024) [S140]. Deloitte EMEA (June 2026, 16 firms) [S124]. Firms are building bigger regional units to fund AI.

### E. What can be said about Deloitte that cannot be said about any rival

1. **Largest professional services network by revenue (FY26).** US$74.5bn vs PwC US$56.9bn, EY US$53.2bn, KPMG US$39.8bn [S120][S126][S127][S128]. Accenture is close at US$74.18bn [S129], but its year ends three months later and it is a different kind of firm. Safe phrasing: "the largest of the Big Four by a wide margin, and roughly level with Accenture as the largest professional services firm". [Confirmed]. Why it matters: scale funds investment (US$3bn AI programme [S120]) and wide sector coverage for graduates.
2. **No. 1 in Gartner's consulting services market share for nine years running (14 times in total).** [S125] [Reported, Deloitte's release]. Why it matters: an outside analyst, not Deloitte, measures it, and it covers consulting only, not audit.
3. **Strategy through operate under one brand, plus audit.** Monitor Deloitte (acquired 2013 from the bankrupt Monitor Group, founded by Michael Porter and others) [S134], T&T, SR&T, Operate [S120], and Audit & Assurance. Accenture has no audit; MBB have no audit or large-scale implementation; PwC/EY/KPMG are smaller in consulting. [Confirmed for components; "only one" claim is Inferred.] Why it matters: a graduate can see a problem from diagnosis to running the fix.
4. **Deloitte Digital creative agencies.** Deloitte bought Heat (San Francisco, announced 29 Feb 2016) and Acne (Stockholm, August 2017) [S405] [Reported]. Why it matters: links brand and marketing creative to technology delivery. Note: Accenture Song has a similar strategy, so this is a differentiator against the Big Four, not against Accenture [Inferred].
5. **Deloitte EMEA: the first single Big Four firm of this scale in Europe.** 16 firms, 132,000 people, €20bn, from 1 June 2026, with €1.5bn of planned incremental investment [S124] [Confirmed]. KPMG's UK-Swiss merger is much smaller [S140]. "First" is my inference; verify before saying it. Why it matters: UK graduates join a firm that can staff and invest across borders.
6. **Deep enterprise-software alliances.** "over 36,000 SAP-focused professionals" [S146] [Confirmed]. ServiceNow Partner of the Year awards (snippet only, unverified). Why it matters: most T&T graduate work runs on these platforms.
7. **UK partner economics recovered first.** Deloitte UK FY26 profit per equity partner £1.125m [S122], above EY UK FY25 £787k [S139] and KPMG UK/Swiss FY25 £880k [S140] [Confirmed]. Careful: different years. Why it matters: shows financial strength after the FY25 dip.

### F. Where Deloitte does NOT lead

- **Growth.** Deloitte FY26 grew 3.8% LC [S120]. Accenture grew 5% LC [S129]. BCG grew 7% [S133]. KPMG grew 5.1% in its FY25 [S128]. Deloitte's T&T grew only 2.5% LC, the slowest of its four businesses [S120].
- **UK consulting in FY25.** Deloitte UK consulting fell 10%, the worst of the Big Four declines found [S123][S139][S140].
- **Strategy prestige.** McKinsey, BCG and Bain own the top-strategy brand. BCG's revenue per head (~US$430k) is nearly three times Deloitte's (~US$149k) [Inferred from S120, S133].
- **Pure technology services and managed services scale.** Accenture's managed services alone are US$37.3bn [S129]. Accenture also reports bookings, which show future work; Deloitte does not.
- **Audit quality (UK).** FRC Annual Review of Audit Quality 2025 (July 2025): 86% of Tier 1 audits inspected needed no more than limited improvements (2023/24: 74%). Five of six Tier 1 firms scored 90%+; BDO scored 50% [S136] [Confirmed]. That means Deloitte, EY, KPMG and PwC were all at 90%+ in 2024/25 [Inferred from S136]. In the FRC 2026 review (22 July 2026): KPMG 95%, Deloitte 90%, EY 90%, PwC 89% [S137] [Reported]. So in 2026 Deloitte ties for second, behind KPMG. Search snippets gave different market-wide totals (74% vs 79%); I could not read the 2026 PDF.
- **UK size vs PwC group.** Deloitte UK £5.81bn [S122] vs PwC UK group £6.155bn (UK, Middle East, Channel Islands) [S138]. On a pure UK basis Deloitte (£5.81bn) is bigger than PwC UK (£4.365bn), but the scope of each figure differs. Compare with care.

## 6. Interview angles

1. "Deloitte is the largest of the Big Four by a clear margin: US$74.5bn last year against PwC's US$56.9bn. But I noticed T&T grew only 2.5%, the slowest of the four businesses. How is the UK T&T team thinking about growth now that AI is changing implementation work?" [S120][S126]
2. "Accenture and Deloitte are now almost exactly the same size, around US$74bn. The difference is that half of Accenture is managed services and Deloitte also has audit and tax. I'm drawn to the breadth: I could work on strategy, technology and the 'Operate' side in one firm." [S120][S129]
3. "Gartner has ranked Deloitte No. 1 in consulting services by revenue for nine years in a row. That's an outside measure, which I find more convincing than a firm's own claims." [S125]
4. "EY tried and failed to split audit from consulting in 2023. That told me the multidisciplinary model is sticky. Deloitte's answer seems to be to go the other way, building Deloitte EMEA with 132,000 people and a €1.5bn investment plan." [S141][S124]
5. "The UK consulting market dipped in 2025: Deloitte UK consulting fell 10%, EY UK consulting 6%, KPMG advisory 3%. Deloitte's T&T came back with 4% growth this year. What changed in client demand?" [S123][S139][S140][S122]
6. "MCA data says UK consulting grew 3% to £21.8bn in 2025, and Source forecasts 6% for 2026, mostly in implementation work. That plays to a firm that can both advise and deliver." [S148][S149]
7. Question to ask: "With Deloitte EMEA now live, how does a London graduate in T&T get staffed on work in Germany or the Middle East?" [S124]
8. "Strategy houses have a different shape. BCG makes about US$430k per person, Deloitte about US$149k. That tells me Deloitte runs bigger delivery teams, so graduates get exposure to real delivery early." [S133][S120] (state as your own calculation)
9. "McKinsey is cutting support roles and Accenture exited 11,000 people it couldn't reskill. The whole industry is reshaping its pyramid around AI. How is Deloitte UK training graduates differently because of that?" [S135][S130]
10. "On audit quality, the FRC's 2026 review put KPMG at 95% and Deloitte at 90%. For a consulting applicant that matters because the brand trust that wins consulting work starts with the audit." [S137] (Reported; check before quoting)
11. "Deloitte has over 36,000 SAP-focused professionals. A lot of T&T graduate work must sit on platforms like SAP, Salesforce and ServiceNow. Which platforms does the UK practice see most demand for?" [S146]
12. "Private equity now owns big stakes in Grant Thornton in the US and UK. The Big Four have stayed partnerships. Deloitte funds investment like its US$3bn AI programme from partners' profits instead." [S144][S145][S120]
13. Avoid saying: "Deloitte is the biggest consulting firm on every measure." Accenture is about the same size overall and much bigger in managed services [S129].
