## 1. Findings

### A. The actual JD

**A1. Timing matters: the London consulting roles open tomorrow.**
- Deloitte UK's 2026/27 application timeline says Consulting, Advisory, Enabling Functions and Real Estate open on "5th October"; Technology opens partly on 28 September and partly on 5 October; Scotland opened on 21 September [Confirmed, S14].
- So on 2026-10-04 the live early-careers board shows 168 programmes, mostly Audit, Tax and IT Audit, plus a first wave of Consulting roles in Belfast and Edinburgh only [Confirmed, S1]. London Consulting graduate postings were not yet live when I checked. Action for the candidate: re-check the board on 5 October and be early, because Deloitte says it closes roles early when volumes are high [Confirmed, S13, S14].
- One application per year. Deloitte runs "a strict one application per year policy across our Early Careers opportunities" [Confirmed, S14, S2]. Choosing the right team is a real decision, not a formality.

"Government & Public Services" appears as an industry, and "Defence & Security" appears inside many experienced-hire T&T job titles [Confirmed, S40, S49].

**A5. Selection process (short, for context; other workstreams cover it in depth)** [Confirmed, S17]
1. Application. 2. "Immersive Online Assessment": behavioural (rank actions in workplace scenarios) plus cognitive (numerical and verbal reasoning), some items timed. 3. "Job Simulation": ranking, written and recorded video responses; "Some scenarios may refer to workplace tools like AI" but no AI skill is needed. 4. Final Stage Assessment: in person for graduates at the linked office. Two exercises: a 30 to 40 minute discussion of a pre-researched topic, and a ~50 minute "skills and motivation interview". Summer Vacation Scheme candidates do only the interview, virtually. Candidates must read Deloitte's "Integrity & AI guidance" first.

### B. Decoding THE JD line by line

Each line: what it means day to day for a first-year Analyst, who you deal with, tools, what good looks like, and what it reveals. All "day to day" content is [Inferred] unless a source is cited.

1. **"enabling our clients to be the best that they can be ... work with their most senior leaders and top-level executives"** [S2].
   - Day to day: you will rarely lead a CIO conversation. You will prepare the pack that the partner or director uses with the CIO, and sit in the room taking notes.
   - Counterparties: client CIO, CFO, COO and their direct reports; on the Deloitte side a partner (relationship owner), a director or associate director (engagement lead), a manager (runs the plan) [Inferred; grade names Confirmed, S50-S54, S66].
   - Reveals: TST sells to the C-suite. It is a "door-opener" offering that can lead to larger implementation work [Inferred].
2. **"move away from IT, the department of computers, to technology as a business partner and enabler"** [S2].
   - Meaning: operating model work. How the IT function is organised, funded, sourced and governed.
   - Matches TST's four market offerings: "Technology Operating Model", "Enterprise Transformation & Agility", "Technology Strategy, Vision and Architecture", "Technology Sourcing & Commercial Management" [Confirmed, S2].
   - Junior tasks: current-state interviews, capability maps, IT cost baselines in Excel, vendor contract summaries, org charts in PowerPoint [Inferred].
3. **"create a digital core"** [S2].
   - "Digital core" means the ERP and core platforms (finance, supply chain, HR) that everything else hangs off. This links TST to ET&P's SAP, Oracle, Workday and ServiceNow practices [Inferred from S12, S40].
4. **"helping COOs, CFOs, CIOs ... improving financial performance, accelerating speed to market ... entering exciting new fields like AI"** [S2].
   - Reveals: the business case is the product. Analysts help quantify benefits (cost out, time saved) [Inferred].
5. **"net zero and ... inclusive ways of working"** [S2].
   - Reveals: ESG is part of the pitch. Expect sustainability KPIs inside business cases. Low daily weight for an Analyst [Inferred].
6. **"you will work on large-scale technology transformations, from the design through to implementation"** [S2].
   - Day to day: most first-year time on large programmes goes to PMO (project management office) work: RAID logs (risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies), status reports, plan updates, meeting packs, requirement logs, test tracking [Inferred]. The Cyber posting lists similar junior tasks openly: "scheduling meetings and taking minutes, tracking deadlines, and preparing materials" [Confirmed, S9].
   - Counterparties: client programme director and workstream leads; Deloitte manager; offshore or nearshore build teams. An experienced-hire SAP JD asks for good communication "with clients and colleagues both on and offshore" [Confirmed, S51]; a Workday JD mentions guiding "engineering teams (onshore and offshore)" [Confirmed, S50]. I could not confirm from a primary page which offshore centres UK T&T uses (gap).
   - Tools: Excel, PowerPoint, Word, Jira or Azure DevOps for backlogs, Microsoft Teams, Visio or similar for process maps [Inferred]. Atlassian is visible: Deloitte UK advertises an "Atlassian Center of Excellence" [Confirmed, S49].
7. **"Learning how to engage with clients to solve their most complex problems"** [S2].
   - Good looks like: you know the client's org chart, you write clear minutes within hours, you follow up actions, you never surprise your manager [Inferred].
8. **"Working with individuals from our clients' business and IT teams to understand what the problems and needs are, and transforming those into technical requirements"** [S2].
   - This is the business analyst core. Run or support workshops, capture "as-is" processes, write user stories or requirement statements, map them to system capabilities [Inferred]. Finance Transformation describes the same line [Confirmed, S8].
   - Good looks like: requirements that are testable, traced to a business objective, and agreed by the client owner [Inferred].
9. **"Creating documents crucial to the delivery and execution of the programme, alongside materials presented back to clients"** [S2].
   - Day to day: PowerPoint is the main output. Steering committee packs, design documents, playbooks [Inferred].
   - Good looks like: one message per slide, numbers that tie back to a source, Deloitte template, no typos. Managers review your drafts; expect several rounds [Inferred].
10. **"Discovering how technologies support everything an organisation does, and which technologies work best for each part of a business"** [S2].
    - Means vendor landscape knowledge. Deloitte UK lists alliances with Adobe, Anaplan, Apple, AWS, Google, Informatica, MuleSoft, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, UiPath, Workday, Workiva [Confirmed, S12 nav, S40].
    - Reveals: Deloitte is both an adviser and an implementation partner. An SAP JD claims "We are the number one global SAP delivery partner with over 200 implementations delivered to date" [Reported, Deloitte's own claim, S52]. Independence of advice versus alliance revenue is a fair question to think about (see F).
11. **"Using firm-approved AI tools responsibly to support client delivery"** [S2, S3].
    - Two words carry weight: "firm-approved". You do not paste client data into public AI tools. Deloitte's Global Code commits to "responsible development and deployment of new technologies, including artificial intelligence" [Confirmed, S36]. Deloitte UK appointed its first Chief AI Officer, Hayley McKelvey, in June 2026 [Confirmed, S22, S28].
    - Reveals: AI use is now a stated junior duty, not a perk [Inferred].
12. **"Help assess technology investments, identify opportunities to improve value, agility, efficiency, and user experience"** [S2].
    - Day to day: benchmarking IT spend, application portfolio rationalisation (which systems to keep, retire, replace), vendor comparisons in Excel [Inferred]. The UK TST service page uses the same words: "increase the value, agility, efficiency and experience of technology investments" [Confirmed, S41].
13. **"working with teams across our wider Technology and Transformation practice"** [S2].
    - Reveals the matrix. You are hired into an offering (TST) but staffed on engagements led by industry teams and other offerings (see E) [Inferred].
14. **"How to build rapport with clients and ensure they have confidence in your capability"** [S2].
    - Good looks like: reliability. Small promises kept [Inferred].
15. **Development section of a sister posting** [S4]: graduates are called "Analysts", supported by a "Consulting Early Experience team", attend an in-person "Analyst Induction Programme (AIP)", then business-area training "for a few more weeks", and meet their "People Leader and Resource Manager" [Confirmed, S4]. The Resource Manager matters: they allocate you to projects (staffing) [Inferred from title].

### C. A worked day for a first-year Analyst [Inferred]

Grounding used: JD duty lists [S2, S4, S8, S9]; a T&T Director's described week ("a check-in with my client on a project, so we can manage any potential risks", "preparing a bid to win new work", "invoicing the client") [S55]; a T&T Senior Manager's week ("I have a client workshop, where we try and work out what they need for a new project", Monday team planning, Friday "project wrap up call to identify any key risks") [S56]; hybrid working with client-site days [S2]. No Glassdoor or Reddit pages could be read (blocked), so there are no anecdotes here. Times are typical, not sourced.

**(i) Strategy / operations engagement (for example, a technology operating model review for a retailer's IT function)**
- 08:30 Travel to client site (client-facing roles "might be expected to attend client sites on certain days" [S2]).
- 09:00 Team stand-up with manager and two consultants. Your actions: finish interview notes, update the IT cost baseline.
- 09:30 Sit in on a stakeholder interview with the client's Head of Infrastructure. You take notes. Senior consultant leads.
- 10:30 Write up notes into the interview log. Tag themes (sourcing, skills, cloud costs).
- 11:30 Excel: reconcile IT spend data from the client finance team against vendor contracts. Flag gaps to the manager.
- 13:00 Lunch with the team.
- 13:45 Build two slides for Thursday's steering committee: "current state in one page" and "cost by capability".
- 15:00 Manager review of slides. Rework.
- 16:00 Desk research using firm-approved tools on peer benchmarks; save sources [S2 on AI tools].
- 17:00 Update RAID log and send minutes from the morning interview.
- 17:45 Time entry for the day against the engagement code (accurate time recording is a Global Code commitment [S36]).
- 18:00 to 19:00 Finish slides if review comments came late. Longer near deadlines.

**(ii) Technology implementation (for example, a Workday HR or SAP finance rollout)**
- 08:45 Join the daily stand-up for your workstream (client process owner, Deloitte functional lead, offshore build team on the call).
- 09:15 Update the requirements tracker in Jira or Excel after yesterday's design workshop.
- 10:00 Support a design workshop: you run the screen, capture decisions and open questions. Finance Transformation describes this as "interactive workshops and labs" [S8].
- 11:30 Configure or test a simple element in the sandbox under guidance (HR Applications Analysts "configure the chosen HR application" [S6]).
- 13:00 Lunch.
- 13:45 Write test scripts for user acceptance testing (UAT).
- 15:00 Defect triage call. Log defects, assign owners, chase fixes with the build team.
- 16:00 Prepare the weekly status report for the programme management office.
- 17:00 Data migration reconciliation check in Excel.
- 17:45 Time entry, plan tomorrow. Go-live weeks are longer and can include weekend cutover support.

### D. Activity types, grades, performance, qualifications

**D1. Taxonomy of consulting activity (typical lifecycle)** [Inferred, labels grounded where cited]
1. Business development and bids: proposals, orals, pricing. Director's week includes "preparing a bid to win new work" [S55]; HR Applications Analysts may work on "bids for new client opportunities" [S6]; the board has a "Bid Advisor" and a "Pricing Manager, Technology and Transformation" [S49].
2. Discovery and current-state assessment: interviews, data requests, process maps.
3. Strategy and options: target operating model, sourcing strategy, roadmap (TST offerings [S2]).
4. Business case: costs, benefits, risks. Value tracking.
5. Design: future-state process and system design, requirements ("Designing the future state of a finance function" [S8]).
6. Build / configure / integrate: SAP, Oracle, Workday, Salesforce, ServiceNow, cloud and data engineering [S6, S12, S49].
7. Testing: test scripts, UAT, quality engineering (QE roles exist on the board [S49]).
8. Deployment and cutover; data migration.
9. Programme and project management (PMO): "Major Programmes" is a named Human Capital team [S5, S40].
10. Change management, training and adoption: the TA NI change offering [S5].
11. Managed services ("as a Service / Operate"): ET&P, EAD, Human Capital and Cyber each have an "as a Service/Operate" line on the UK site [Confirmed, S12 nav]. Customer Products sells "multi-year process, platform, and data operate services" [S4].

**D2. Grade structure** [Confirmed for names]
- Analyst (graduate entry; "As a Graduate (we call you Analysts)") [S4]. Consultant. Senior Consultant. Manager. Senior Manager. Associate Director. Director. Partner (salaried or equity) [S49 titles, S66 named staff, S25].
- Partnership numbers: 1,308 partners overall from 1 June 2026, of which 784 equity [Confirmed, S25]. The FY26 metrics show "All partners = 1,453" as a financial-year figure including 654 salaried partners [Confirmed, S23]; the two differ in date and basis.
- T&T promoted 15 of the 48 new partners in 2026 (largest share), SR&T 10 [Confirmed, S25].
- What each grade is judged on [Inferred, typical Big Four]: Analyst: accuracy, speed, reliability, learning. Consultant: owning a workstream piece, client trust. Senior Consultant: running a small workstream, coaching juniors. Manager: day-to-day delivery, budget, quality, client relationship at working level. Senior Manager / Associate Director: multiple workstreams, selling extensions. Director / Partner: client relationships, sales, practice building.
- Time per grade: not found on any primary page. Common market pattern is roughly 1 to 3 years per grade below Manager [Inferred, unverified]. Firm-level signal: over 6,000 promotions in 2026 out of about 25,000 people [Confirmed, S25, S22], i.e. almost a quarter [S30].
- Pay: average in-grade salary rise 4.2% for FY27, bonus pool up 14% [Confirmed, S25].

### F. Compliance and control dimension

**F1. Independence (why an audit firm's consultants have rules others do not)**
- The FRC's Revised Ethical Standard 2024 is current, effective 15 December 2024 [Confirmed, S38].
- For public interest entity (PIE) audit clients, an audit firm "and, where the audit firm belongs to a network, any member of such network, shall not provide (either directly or indirectly) to the audited entity, to its UK parent undertaking or to its worldwide controlled undertakings, services other than those set out" in a permitted list, subject to audit committee approval [Confirmed, S39, para 5.40]. In plain English: Deloitte consulting cannot sell most consulting or technology implementation to a listed bank or company that Deloitte audits. There is also a 70% non-audit fee cap for certain entities [Confirmed that the cap exists, S39].
- Scale: in FY25, Deloitte UK earned £155m from non-audit services to entities it audits, versus £3,819m from non-audit services to entities it does not audit [Confirmed, S35]. About 96% of non-audit revenue comes from non-audit clients [Inferred arithmetic]. So the consulting business is built mostly on clients Deloitte does not audit.
- Personal independence: partners and those closely associated must not hold a financial interest (other than an immaterial indirect one) in any entity "relevant to an engagement of the firm" [Confirmed, S39, para 2.3]. "Covered persons" (people on or able to influence an engagement) have the same restriction for their engagements [Confirmed, S39, para 2.4]. Deloitte runs "85 educational resources on independence" a year and quarterly videos; monitoring shows impact "particularly in matters of personal independence" [Confirmed, S35]. Practical meaning for a joiner [Inferred]: expect to declare shareholdings and to check a restricted-entity list before buying shares; ISAs and pension funds in diversified funds are usually fine; individual shares in audit clients may not be.
- Conflict checks: "All proposed engagements and business and financial relationships go through a conflict check before they are accepted"; where conflicts cannot be reduced "we decline the engagement" [Confirmed, S35]. Higher-risk engagements go to the Public Interest Review Group (PIRG): 32 matters in FY2025 [Confirmed, S35].
- A global "end-to-end acceptance and continuance platform" is planned for UK implementation in FY2027 [Confirmed, S35].

- Objectivity: "We do not allow bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence, to alter our interactions, or to override our professional judgments" [Confirmed, S36].

## 6. Interview angles

1. "T&T is Deloitte UK's largest business at about £1.75bn, a third of UK revenue. It dipped about 10% in FY25 and came back 4% in FY26, driven by ERP, cyber and Human Capital work. I want to be in the part of the firm that is rebuilding growth." [S23, S22, S30]
2. "UK T&T grew 4% while global T&T grew 2.5%, so the UK practice is outperforming the network average." [S23, S34]
3. Ask: "With Deloitte EMEA launched in June, how much of a TST graduate's work now involves cross-border teams in EMEA?" [S24, S34]
4. "Your TST offerings are operating model, enterprise agility, strategy and architecture, and sourcing. Sourcing interests me because it is where the business case meets the contract." [S2]
5. "The JD says 'firm-approved AI tools'. To me that means client data stays in approved systems, and I check outputs before they reach a client. That matches the Global Code commitment on responsible AI." [S2, S36]
6. "About 96% of Deloitte UK's non-audit revenue comes from companies it does not audit. Independence rules shape which clients consulting can serve, so conflict checks come before the work." [S35, S39]
7. Ask: "How does the Resource Manager balance what I want to learn with where the practice needs me?" [S4]
8. "Financial services is about 35% of UK revenue. I would expect many early projects to be in banking or insurance, and the new T&T leader comes from insurance consulting." [S23, S27]
9. Ask: "Hammad Rafique joined from Accenture's insurance practice. What has changed in how T&T sells large programmes since June?" [S27] (check name and title on the day)
10. "I like that Deloitte sits across strategy and implementation: TST can shape a decision and the SAP, Oracle or Workday teams can deliver it." [S2, S6, S52]
11. Ask: "Belfast is the UK delivery centre. How do London TST analysts work with Belfast and offshore build teams day to day?" [S21, S50, S51]
12. "Time recording is in the Global Code. I would treat accurate timesheets as part of integrity." [S36, S23]
13. Ask: "Which qualification do TST analysts usually take: CIMA certificate, the MSc in Digital and Business Risk Management, or platform certifications?" [S18, S6]
14. "Equity partners hit a record 784, and T&T made the most new partners this year (15 of 48). That tells me the firm is investing in T&T leadership." [S25]
15. Ask: "The public sector share of revenue has fallen since FY24, yet many T&T roles need SC clearance. Where do you see GPS demand heading?" [S23, S54]
