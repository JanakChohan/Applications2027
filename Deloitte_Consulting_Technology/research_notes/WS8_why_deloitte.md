# WS8: Why Deloitte, built from mechanism

Workstream WS8. Source range S300-S339. Accessed 2026-10-04.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Why is Deloitte structured this way?

**What the structure is, in plain terms**

- "Deloitte" is a brand, not one company. Deloitte describes itself as the brand under which "nearly 500,000" people "in independent firms throughout the world come together" [S302] [Confirmed, primary].
- At the top sits Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (DTTL), a UK private company limited by guarantee. DTTL "does not provide services to clients" [S302] [Confirmed, primary]. It owns the brand rules and quality standards. The member firms do the client work.
- Each member firm is liable only for its own work. Deloitte's wording: DTTL and each member firm "are liable only for their own acts and omissions, and not those of each other" [S302] [Confirmed, primary].
- The UK firm is Deloitte LLP, an England and Wales limited liability partnership, registered number OC303675 [S339] [Reported: seen in Deloitte consent letters and filings via search snippet, not on Companies House directly in this workstream]. Older filings describe it as the UK affiliate of "Deloitte NWE LLP" [S339]. That wording is out of date (see next bullet). Verify the current parent wording on Companies House or the deloitte.com/uk legal page.
- The regional layer keeps changing. Deloitte North and South Europe (NSE) grouped the UK, Switzerland, the Nordics, the Netherlands, Belgium and others [S306] [Reported]. In early 2026 Deloitte announced that its European and Middle East firms would combine into "Deloitte EMEA" from 1 June 2026, covering about 80 countries, €20bn of revenue, 6,000 partners and 132,000 professionals, with a €1.5bn four-year investment commitment, led by Richard Houston as CEO [S306] [Reported, single outlet; dates in the outlet's text are 23 Feb / 2 Mar 2026]. Country firms keep "local market responsibility" [S306].
- Inside each firm there are four businesses since a 2024 global restructure: Audit & Assurance; Tax & Legal; Strategy, Risk & Transactions (SR&T); Technology & Transformation (T&T). Before that there were five. Consulting, Financial Advisory and Risk Advisory were folded into SR&T and T&T. The stated aim was to reduce complexity and "address duplication in services" [S307] [Reported]. The UK FY26 results use these four names [S303] [Confirmed, primary].

**The mechanisms that hold it together (theory, then evidence)**

1. **Risk pooling across businesses that peak at different times.** Audit is recurring and regulated. Consulting depends on clients' appetite for big programmes. Combining them smooths partner income.
   - Evidence, UK FY25 (year to 31 May 2025): consulting revenue fell 10%, but tax and legal rose 7%, audit 3%, deals 3%. Total UK revenue fell only 1% to £5.68bn [S305] [Reported]. The reason given was that "Clients delayed large-scale investment programmes amid economic uncertainty" [S305].
   - Evidence, global FY26 (year to 31 May 2026): Technology & Transformation grew slowest at 2.5% in local currency, while Audit & Assurance grew 5% and Tax & Legal 5.8% [S300] [Confirmed, primary]. Total grew 3.8% to US$74.5bn [S300].
   - Evidence, UK FY26: revenue up 2% to £5.81bn; distributable profit up 14% to £899m; average profit per equity partner up 7% to £1.125m [S303] [Confirmed, primary; matched by S304].
   - [Inferred] The pooled model let Deloitte UK grow profit in a year when its largest business (T&T) grew only 4%. A standalone consultancy would not have had audit and tax to lean on.

2. **Brand trust from audit, sold across the firm.** Audit puts Deloitte inside the finance function of large companies and makes its name a signal of reliability. Deloitte's own framing: "Our multidisciplinary model, deep domain and industry knowledge, and commitment to upholding quality and trust in the corporate reporting ecosystem allow us to provide integrated solutions" [S300] [Confirmed, primary].
   - Academic framing: Greenwood and Suddaby (Academy of Management Journal, 2006) studied how the big accounting firms pioneered the "multidisciplinary practice" (MDP), a firm combining accounting with consulting and sometimes law. They found the form "was vigorously contested" [S315] [Reported, abstract via snippet; full paper not read].

3. **Breadth for clients and careers.** Deloitte Global CEO Joe Ucuzoglu, in a March 2023 video after EY's split collapsed, said keeping the model was "not even a close call". His four reasons: clients value the breadth, people value "the diversity of career paths", markets value the quality, communities value the impact [S308] [Reported]. He also said previous separations had "never once played out as intended" [S308].
   - In June 2022 Deloitte called media reports of a split "categorically untrue" and said "we remain committed to our current business model" [S309] [Reported].

4. **Scale for investment and alliances.** The FY26 global release commits US$3bn to AI through 2030 [S300]. The EMEA combination is explicitly about pooled investment: €1.5bn over four years on generative AI, sovereign cloud and sector solutions [S306]. Global CEO Ucuzoglu: "Our clients expect leading expertise wherever it is based, alongside seamless cross-border and technology-enabled delivery at speed" [S306] [Reported].

5. **Liability containment.** Because each firm is "liable only for their own acts" [S302], a failure in one country should not bankrupt the others. [Inferred] This is the lesson of Arthur Andersen, which collapsed after Enron; the separate-firm structure is the network's firewall. (Andersen history is general knowledge, not sourced in this workstream.)

6. **Partnership capital and horizon.** Partners own the firm and are paid out of distributable profit [S303]. [Inferred] There are no outside shareholders demanding quarterly growth, which lets the firm absorb a bad consulting year (FY25) without a share-price shock. The trade-off is that investment comes out of partners' pockets.

**The costs of the structure**

1. **Independence blocks selling.** Under the FRC Revised Ethical Standard 2019 (in force 15 March 2020), auditors of UK public interest entities can only provide a short "whitelist" of non-audit services closely linked to the audit or required by law. Non-audit fees for these clients are capped at 70% of the average audit fee of the previous three years [S314] [Confirmed: ICAS, ACCA and accountancy press snippets agree]. [Inferred] In practice: if Deloitte audits a FTSE company, Deloitte's T&T business generally cannot sell it an ERP implementation. Every large audit client is a client consulting must largely leave to Accenture, PwC, EY or KPMG.

2. **Regulatory pressure to separate.** The FRC set 22 principles in July 2020 requiring the Big Four to operationally separate their audit practices by 30 June 2024 at the latest [S313] [Reported]. Key rules: an independent audit board; dealings between audit and the rest of the firm on arm's-length terms; and the audit practice may not receive fees for introducing work to other parts of the firm [S312] [Reported]. The FRC approved the firms' plans in February 2021 [S312]. [Inferred] This weakens the "audit as a door-opener for consulting" mechanism in the UK by rule.

3. **Coordination costs and partner consensus.** EY's attempt to split ("Project Everest") shows how hard it is to change a global partnership. EY's stated rationale was to address "conflicts of interest often associated with audit and advisory firms" and capture consulting growth [S310]. It was abandoned in April 2023 after disputes over how much of the tax business should stay with audit, and opposition from member firms in China and Israel [S310] [Reported; S311 snippet consistent]. [Inferred] Any Deloitte structural decision needs agreement across many partner-owned firms, which is slow.

4. **Reputational contagion across countries.** In 2025 Deloitte Australia agreed to repay the final instalment of an AU$440,000 contract with the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations after a report on the welfare Targeted Compliance Framework was found to contain fabricated citations and an invented quote from a Federal Court judgment [S316] [Confirmed: The Register, with Fortune/Washington Post snippets S317]. The revised report disclosed use of "a generative AI large language model (Azure OpenAI GPT-4o)" [S316]. This happened in a legally separate firm, yet the story ran worldwide under the Deloitte name. The same week, Deloitte globally announced its Claude deployment to 470,000+ staff [S325] [S326].
   - UK example: Deloitte received four FRC sanctions in five years, with fines over £18.5m after discount; across the Big Four, KPMG had the largest total (£81.8m pre-discount) [S318] [Reported]. The FRC is investigating Deloitte's audits of Glencore for 2013-2020 [S318 adjacent snippet] [Reported].

**Net view [Inferred]:** The model works as a portfolio. It trades lower consulting access to audit clients and slower decisions for steadier income, a trusted brand, and enough pooled capital to fund AI and alliances. The FY25 to FY26 numbers are the clearest live test: consulting fell, the firm held.

### 1.2 How do the businesses and offerings work together?

**The matrix**
- Every Deloitte professional sits in an offering (what they do) and usually aligns to an industry (who they do it for). Global industries: Consumer; Energy, Resources & Industrials; Financial Services; Government & Public Services; Life Sciences & Health Care; Technology, Media & Telecommunications [S301] [Confirmed, primary].
- UK Technology & Transformation offerings named on Deloitte's careers page: Enterprise, Technology & Performance; Engineering, AI & Data; Cyber; Customer; Human Capital; Defence & Security [S320] [Confirmed, primary]. Engineering, AI & Data combines "Business Architects, Engineers and Data Scientists underpinned by our deep sector" expertise [S320].
- UK T&T revenue was £1.75bn in FY26, up 4%, with demand for "enterprise technology support, including ERP implementation, Cyber and Human Capital services" [S303] [Confirmed, primary]. That is about 30% of UK revenue [Inferred arithmetic: 1.75 / 5.81].

**Account teams: where collaboration lives**
- For large clients Deloitte appoints a Lead Client Service Partner (LCSP). Deloitte US profiles and postings describe the LCSP as leveraging Deloitte's networks to support major relationships, making sure account plans are "developed, monitored", and that pursuit processes are run "consistently ... across the account" [S322] [Reported, snippets from deloitte.com profiles and apply.deloitte.com postings; US member firm]. Examples include a global LCSP for the International Olympic Committee relationship [S322].
- [Inferred] So cross-selling is organised around the client, not the offering. The LCSP sees the whole wallet; offering partners bring specialists.

**Alliances**
- Deloitte UK lists 14 alliance partners: Adobe, Anaplan, Apple, AWS, Google, Informatica, MuleSoft, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, UiPath, Workday, Workiva [S319] [Confirmed, primary]. Its own description: "We combine our award-winning consulting services, market sensing data and trusted relationships with some of the largest technology firms to help solve your most complex challenges" [S319].
- Global alliances also include NVIDIA and HPE [S301]. Deloitte was named NVIDIA Global Consulting Partner of the Year 2026 for the fifth year running and received six Google Cloud Partner of the Year awards [S300] [Confirmed, primary].
- Anthropic: on 6 October 2025 Deloitte and Anthropic announced Claude for 470,000+ Deloitte people, a Claude Center of Excellence, and a certification programme for 15,000 professionals, targeting financial services, healthcare, life sciences and public services [S325] [Confirmed: Anthropic page plus CNBC S326]. Deloitte's Ranjit Bawa: "enterprise AI should be both powerful and principled" [S325].
- [Inferred] How alliances make money: the vendor sells the licence; Deloitte sells the implementation. Certified headcount is the asset clients buy.

**Delivery capacity**
- UK onshore technology centres opened from June 2025 in Belfast, Cardiff, Manchester and Newcastle, starting with 750 technologists and aiming for 1,500 within three years; Belfast alone to add 500 jobs [S323] [Reported]. T&T managing partner Rob Cullen: the investment "accelerates our ambition to become the largest onshore technology capability in UK" [S323].
- Offshore: Deloitte's US-India delivery organisation (USI) is reported at over 100,000 people across seven Indian cities, with about 40,000 in Hyderabad [S324] [Reported, trade press snippets; USI belongs to the US firm, so its role for UK engagements is not established here].

**Teaming language in job descriptions**
- 2027 UK graduate postings (via Bright Network and Prosple snippets) describe analysts who "collaborate across disciplines – working alongside colleagues in strategy, design, engineering, data, and sales to co-create practical, technology-enabled solutions", and teams that "work collaboratively with other elements of the Cyber, Data, and Digital team" [S321] [Reported: snippets only; Bright Network returned 403 on fetch].
- The UK T&T careers page references links to Audit, Assurance, Tax, Legal and M&A capabilities [S320].

**Where independence blocks collaboration [Inferred from S312, S314]**
- If the client is a UK audit client that is a public interest entity, consulting is limited to the whitelist and the fee cap.
- The audit practice cannot be paid for introductions to the rest of the firm.
- So true "integrated" work happens mostly at non-audit clients (often audited by another Big Four firm), in the public sector, and in private companies below PIE scale.

### 1.3 What do clients actually buy from Deloitte?

**Who the clients are**
- Deloitte serves "nearly 90% of the Fortune Global 500" [S300] [Confirmed, primary].
- Gartner ranks Deloitte the No. 1 consulting service provider worldwide by revenue, eighth consecutive year [S301] [Confirmed, primary citation of Gartner].
- UK public sector: Tussell data (via Consultancy.uk, 26 May 2026) shows UK government consulting spend at a record £3.7bn in 2024/25, up 6%, and Deloitte as the top supplier of key consulting contracts for five years running, with £400m+ in fees, about four times the next firm (Palladium, £102m) [S335] [Reported]. A separate Tussell case study says Deloitte outperforms the other Big Four combined in winning work via public frameworks [S336] [Reported, landing page].

**What they are buying [Inferred, each tied to evidence]**
1. Scale delivery capacity at short notice. Test and Trace 2020-21: around 900 to 1,035 Deloitte consultants at peak [S332] [S333].
2. Platform certification. Alliance status and certified staff on SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday and now Claude [S319] [S325].
3. Sector depth from the industry matrix [S301].
4. Brand and legitimacy. A board or minister can defend hiring the No. 1 ranked firm [S301].
5. Risk transfer. A contract with a large balance sheet behind it. The Australian refund shows the mechanism working in reverse: the client recovered money [S316].

**Why clients accept the price**
- Rates disclosed in Parliament: Deloitte staff on Test and Trace at about £1,000 a day each, close to £1m a day in total; some senior consultants up to £6,624 a day [S332] [Confirmed: S332 and S333 agree]. Deloitte contracts were worth £298m with £174m spent by end-March 2021 [S332] [S333].
- The government's own explanation was "skills shortages in certain areas of the civil service" and "comparatively low salaries in the civil service" [S333] [Reported]. A DHSC spokesperson said the work built expertise inside the department [S332].
- The NAO (21 November 2025) found 86% of survey respondents said consultants made valuable contributions [S334] [Confirmed, primary].

**The counter-case, with numbers**
- Performance: during the period of heavy consultant use, only 17% of people got test results within 24 hours against a 90% target [S332] [Reported].
- Dependence: in April 2021 Test and Trace had 2,239 external workers; consultants were still 45% of central staff [S333] [Reported].
- Data: the NAO said "Government does not have consistent data on how much individual departments are spending on external consultants" [S334] [Confirmed, primary]. HM Treasury's estimate for central government was about £1.36bn in 2022-23 [S334]. The government targeted £550m savings in 2024-25 and £700m by 2028-29 [S334]. Yet Tussell measured total public sector consulting at a record £3.7bn in 2024/25 [S335].
- Ideas: Mariana Mazzucato and Rosie Collington, The Big Con (Penguin, 2023), argue that over-reliance on consultancies weakens state capability, involves rent-seeking (charging well above equivalent public sector staff), and creates conflicts of interest; they name the Big Four including Deloitte. They recommend conflict disclosure, rebuilding in-house capacity, and better contract evaluation [S337] [Reported via Wikipedia summary; book not read]. Critics noted thin evidence from the Global South and a loosely defined idea of public value [S337].
- Quality failure: the Australian AI report [S316].

### 1.4 The non-core side: Enabling Functions and internal AI

**Enabling Functions**
- Deloitte UK calls its internal support business "Enabling Functions". Its line: "In Enabling Functions we support our colleagues to do that well, so the firm can continue to make a positive impact" [S330] [Confirmed, primary].
- Early-careers routes named: Leadership Support Centre (Cardiff), supporting senior leaders; and Quality, Risk & Security Services (Cardiff and London), covering client vetting, conflict checks, anti-money laundering and cyber security, with sub-teams for Financial Crime, Independence, Deloitte Business and Security, Strategy and Operations, and Contentious Matters [S330] [Confirmed, primary].
- Wider teams named in search results: People and Performance (HR), Growth, IT Services, Finance, Administration, Corporate Responsibility, information services; Cardiff is described as the centre for centralised business operations and the largest office outside London [S331] [Reported, snippet].
- How it wires to client work [Inferred]: the Independence team is where the audit restrictions in 1.1 are enforced. Before T&T can start work, Quality, Risk & Security checks whether the client is an audit client and whether there is a conflict. That is the friction point of the MDP made into a job.
- Headcount of Enabling Functions: not found (see Gaps).

**Internal AI tools**
- **PairD (verified).** Built by Deloitte's AI Institute, rolled out in the UK from October 2023 and to 75,000 people across Europe and the Middle East by January 2024 [S328] [Confirmed: Consultancy.uk, ICAEW and AI Magazine snippets agree]. Uses include emails, code and PowerPoint drafts. Staff must complete training and were warned output may be inaccurate [S328]. It is based on an OpenAI model, with Deloitte building the security and data layers [S328 adjacent snippets] [Reported].
- **Sidekick (verified, but US).** An enterprise generative AI assistant of the US member firms, reported at "170,000+ users", "110 million+ uses" and "about two hours saved per user each week" [S329] [Confirmed, primary, US firm]. Not established as a UK tool.
- **Zora AI (verified, client product with internal use).** Launched 18 March 2025 at NVIDIA GTC: a portfolio of AI agents for finance, HR, supply chain, procurement, sales and customer service, sold on subscription. Deloitte said it would use Zora AI for Finance internally for expenses, targeting 25% lower costs and 40% higher productivity [S327] [Reported, Deloitte press release; targets not outcomes].
- **Claude** for 470,000+ people globally [S325].
- UK governance: the UK FY26 results name Hayley McKelvey as new Chief AI Officer, "responsible for driving the firm's AI strategy across client services and internal operations" [S303] [Confirmed, primary]. UK technology investment rose to £180m from £158m [S303].
- UK CEO Darren Graves: "As AI makes intelligence increasingly abundant, the role our people play will matter more, not less" [S303].

### 1.5 Draft "Why Deloitte" answers

All three are built from the mechanisms above. Plain English. Each sentence maps to a source.

**30 seconds**
> "I want to start my career where the shape of the firm helps me learn. Deloitte keeps audit, tax, advisory and technology in one partnership, and last year showed why that matters: when UK consulting fell 10%, tax and audit kept the firm steady [S305]. That stability is what pays for things like the four UK technology centres and the £180m tech investment [S303] [S323]. In Technology & Transformation I would be working on the ERP, cyber and AI programmes that grew the business this year [S303]."

**2 minutes**
> "I looked at why Deloitte is built the way it is, because that tells me what my job would really be.
> First, it is a portfolio. Deloitte UK has four businesses. In FY25 consulting fell 10% while tax rose 7% and audit 3%, so total revenue moved only 1% [S305]. In FY26 the firm grew profit 14% while technology grew 4% [S303]. That means the firm can keep investing in people and tools through a weak consulting cycle.
> Second, it puts that money into scale. There is a new Chief AI Officer, a £180m technology budget, and onshore technology centres in Belfast, Cardiff, Manchester and Newcastle aiming for 1,500 people [S303] [S323]. Globally, Deloitte has rolled Claude out to 470,000 people and is certifying 15,000 on it [S325].
> Third, alliances. Deloitte UK works with 14 platform partners, from SAP and Workday to ServiceNow [S319]. Clients buy certified people who can make those platforms work. As an analyst that is a concrete skill I can build.
> I also understand the limits. Independence rules mean Deloitte often cannot consult for its own audit clients [S314], and the Australian AI report showed how one firm's mistake hits the whole brand [S316]. That makes me care about quality controls, not just speed.
> So my reason is specific: a firm whose structure lets it keep investing in technology delivery, in a business where I can learn a platform skill and see how AI changes delivery from the inside."

**The close (one specific thing to work on)**
> "If I joined, the thing I would most want to work on is responsible AI in delivery: helping teams in Engineering, AI & Data use tools like Claude and Zora safely, with the kind of checks that would have caught the citation errors in Australia [S316] [S320] [S325] [S327]. Deloitte is clearly investing there, with a Chief AI Officer and a 15,000-person certification [S303] [S325]. I would like to be part of making that investment show up as better work for clients."

**Honest counter-case: why someone might reasonably choose elsewhere**

- **Accenture.** A larger pure technology services business: US$69.7bn revenue and about 779,000 people in fiscal 2025 [S338] [Reported, snippet of Accenture filings]. No audit independence limits, so it can sell to any company. If you want only technology delivery at maximum scale, it is a fair choice.
- **MBB (McKinsey, BCG, Bain).** Smaller teams, more pure strategy work, higher early responsibility on board-level questions. Deloitte's strategy arm (Monitor Deloitte, now inside SR&T per the 2024 restructure [S307]) is a smaller share of the firm. [Inferred]
- **PwC.** Same model, similar size in the UK: £6.35bn revenue in the year to June 2025, consulting £1.98bn [S338] [Reported]. A candidate who values a slightly larger UK consulting business per se could pick PwC. PwC's consulting fell 3% that year [S338], so it faces the same cycle.
- **EY.** Same model, but having nearly split, it has thought hardest about the conflicts [S310]. Someone who wants to be part of a firm still debating its shape may find that interesting.
- **A boutique (specialist consultancy).** Faster responsibility, deeper niche, no independence red tape. Less scale and fewer alliance certifications. [Inferred]
- **Industry or the civil service.** The Big Con argument is that capability should be built inside organisations, not rented [S337]. Someone motivated by building a lasting team, not moving between projects, could reasonably choose an in-house digital team. Test and Trace's 45% consultant share is the cautionary example [S333].
- **Deloitte-specific risks to weigh:** UK consulting fell 10% in FY25 with several rounds of staff cuts [S305]; reputational spillover from other member firms [S316]; FRC sanctions [S318].

## 3. Verbatim quotes actually read

> "Our multidisciplinary model, deep domain and industry knowledge, and commitment to upholding quality and trust in the corporate reporting ecosystem allow us to provide integrated solutions." [S300]

> "Our multidisciplinary model allows us to bring together technology, tools, insights, and deep industry experience across all businesses to provide integrated solutions." [S301]

> "DTTL and each DTTL member firm and each of its related entities, are liable only for their own acts and omissions, and not those of each other." [S302]
