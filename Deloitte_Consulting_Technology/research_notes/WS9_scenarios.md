## 1. Findings

### A. Deloitte's own rules a junior consultant must follow

#### A1. Deloitte Global Principles of Business Conduct ("Global Code"), October 2025 edition

- **What it is.** It is a 15-page PDF titled "Global Principles of Business Conduct. It starts with integrity". It is dated October 2025 and signed by Joe Ucuzoglu (Deloitte Global CEO) and Anna Marks (Deloitte Global Chair) [S340] [Confirmed]. It "outlines Deloitte's ethical commitments and expectations for Deloitte people globally". Each member firm has its own code "based on the Global Code" that adds local requirements [S340] [Confirmed]. So Deloitte UK people follow the Global Code plus a UK code. The UK code is not public (see Gaps).
- **Applies on and off the clock.** The Code says behaviour "both inside and outside of the Deloitte workplace, in both a professional and a personal capacity" can affect colleagues and reputation [S340] [Confirmed]. Scenario use: what you post on social media about a client counts.
- **Dilemma method.** When facing a dilemma, people "should consider all available information... use professional judgment, consult with relevant stakeholders as appropriate and, where necessary, take action, including applying appropriate safeguards" [S340] [Confirmed]. Deloitte names this the **ACT method**: **A**ssess the situation, **C**onsider and Consult, **T**ake action. The Code adds: "Speaking up is taking action!" [S340] [Confirmed]. This is the single most useful frame for scenario answers.
- **Consultation culture.** "No one at Deloitte should have to navigate a difficult situation alone." People are encouraged to consult "team leaders or others in their chain of command, Ethics, or Talent/HR". Formal channels allow anonymous reporting [S340] [Confirmed].
- **Duty to report.** "All Deloitte people are responsible for reporting any noncompliance or suspected noncompliance with applicable laws and regulations, or potential fraud, illegal or unethical act, or other violation of Deloitte policy". This applies whether the person involved is "a colleague, client, supplier, contractor, or others associated with Deloitte" [S340][S343] [Confirmed]. Scenario use: noticing a client doing something wrong is still a reporting matter. It is not "not my business".
- **Non-retaliation and consequences.** "Retaliation against those who raise ethical concerns is not tolerated." Breaching the Code can lead to consequences "up to and including dismissal" [S340] [Confirmed].
- **Integrity.** "We are straightforward and honest in our professional opinions and business relationships." "We are truthful about the services we provide, the knowledge we possess, and the experience we have" [S340] [Confirmed]. Scenario use: never overstate your experience to a client. Say "I will check and come back to you."
- **Objectivity, conflicts, gifts.** "We do not allow bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence, to alter our interactions, or to override our professional judgments." On gifts: "We do not offer, accept or solicit any gifts, entertainment, or hospitality that may improperly influence, or be perceived to improperly influence, business decisions or impair objectivity" [S340] [Confirmed]. Note the words "or be perceived to". Appearance matters, not only intent.
- **AI and technology.** "We prioritize responsible development and deployment of new technologies, including artificial intelligence, aligned with our Ethical technology guiding principles, and take measures to identify, understand, and mitigate possible associated risks" [S340] [Confirmed].
- **Learning integrity and AI.** "We engage in learning honestly and complete assessments independently without seeking or accepting help from other people, sharing assessment questions or answers, or inappropriately using artificial intelligence tools" [S340] [Confirmed]. This matches the recruitment rules in A6.
- **Confidentiality, privacy and data protection.** There are three commitments. (1) Protect confidential and personal information in line with internal policy, law and professional standards. (2) "We prohibit disclosure of confidential and personal information entrusted to us unless granted permission or there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose." (3) "We prohibit the use of confidential information about our clients for personal advantage or for the benefit of third parties" [S340] [Confirmed].
- **Fair business practices.** "We record hours worked timely and accurately, and submit legitimate business-related expenses" [S340] [Confirmed]. Scenario use: a manager asks you to move hours to another code, or under-record hours. That is a Code issue.
- **Anti-corruption.** "We are against corruption and neither make bribes nor accept them, nor induce or permit any other party to make or receive bribes on our behalf" [S340] [Confirmed]. Deloitte also commits to comply with anti-money laundering, export control and sanctions law [S340] [Confirmed].
- **Respect.** Deloitte does "not tolerate discrimination or harassment of any nature" and supports "a psychologically and physically safe work environment", and expects "clients, and other third parties... to do the same" [S340] [Confirmed]. Scenario use: harassment by a client is still reportable.
- **Independence.** The October 2025 Global Code does not have a separate "independence" heading. Independence is handled through firm policies and professional standards (see A4 and B6) [S340][S345] [Confirmed that it is absent from the Code text; Inferred that it sits in policy].
- **Speak Up channels named in the Code.** "Deloitte Speak Up" for non-US firms (deloittespeakup.ethicspoint.com) and the "Integrity Helpline" for US firms [S340][S341] [Confirmed]. The global speak-up page repeats that people can raise concerns with the ethics officer for the geography or through "the third party-operated ethics helpline" [S343] [Confirmed].

#### A5. Trustworthy AI framework and Deloitte UK's own GenAI controls

- **Dimensions.** The framework launched in 2020 as a "six-part framework". The launch release lists fair and impartial, transparent and explainable, responsible and accountable, safe and secure, robust and reliable, and respectful of privacy [S348] [Confirmed]. Deloitte US material now lists "fair and impartial; robust and reliable; transparent and explainable; safe and secure; accountable and responsible; and respectful of privacy" [S347] [Confirmed]. Some Deloitte pages split "responsible" and "accountable" into two, which makes seven descriptions [S346][S347] [Confirmed]. Safest answer in interview: "six dimensions, sometimes shown as seven when responsible and accountable are separated."
- **Definitions (Deloitte UK page text) [S346] [Confirmed]:**
  - Privacy: "User privacy is respected, and data is not used or stored beyond its intended and stated use and duration; users are able to opt-in / out of sharing their data."
  - Transparent/explainable: "Users understand how technology is being leveraged... these decisions are easy to understand, auditable, and open to inspection."
  - Fair/impartial: "designed and operated inclusively in an aim for equitable application, access, and outcomes."
  - Responsible: "created and operated in a socially responsible manner."
  - Accountable: "Policies are in place to determine who is responsible for the decisions made or derived with the use of technology."
  - Robust/reliable: "produces consistent and accurate outputs, withstands errors, and recovers quickly from unforeseen disruptions and misuse."
  - Safe/secure (US page): "protected from risks that may cause individual and / or collective physical, emotional, environmental, and / or digital harm" [S347].
- **Accountability point for scenarios.** Deloitte's own GenAI article says: "Because an AI model cannot be meaningfully held accountable for its outputs, accountability is squarely a human domain." It also says "the organization deploying the tool is accountable for its outputs" [S347] [Confirmed]. This is the rule a junior should apply to any AI-drafted deliverable. You own it. You check it.
- **Deloitte UK's internal GenAI governance (public).** The 2025 Transparency Report lists GenAI as a "Very High", "NEW" priority risk. Mitigations include "GenAI use-case governance including risk thresholding, triage and clearing house processes" and a "Globally consistent Trustworthy AI framework and guidance embedded in the firm's global technology operating model". The firm's approach prioritises "skills training and fluency, use-case approval, data governance, ethical considerations, regulatory compliance, and cybersecurity" [S345] [Confirmed].
- **Approved tools only.** Audit & Assurance uses an internal GenAI tool, **PairD**. Over 75% of A&A professionals use it, with "around two million PairD prompts" in a year. It lets people "analyse, summarise, explain, and translate confidential data, all within its secure digital ecosystem" [S345] [Confirmed]. Inference for scenarios: client confidential data goes only into firm-approved, secured tools. It never goes into a public chatbot [Inferred from S340 confidentiality rule plus S345].
- No public Deloitte UK policy document specifically on "GenAI with client data" was found beyond the above (see Gaps).

#### A6. Deloitte UK recruitment rules on integrity and AI (directly relevant to the candidate)

- **Process.** The same structure applies to Summer Vacation Scheme, Industrial Placement and Graduate programmes: (1) application form, (2) Immersive Online Assessment, (3) Job Simulation, (4) Final Stage Assessment, (5) outcome [S350] [Confirmed].
- **Academic bar.** Graduate and work-experience programmes usually need "a 2:1 or higher". Extenuating circumstances can be declared [S350] [Confirmed].
- **Immersive Online Assessment.** It is "designed to understand your unique strengths". There are two parts. The behavioural part uses "realistic workplace scenarios" where you rank actions from most to least likely. The cognitive part covers numerical and verbal reasoning, and some items are timed [S350] [Confirmed]. Recruiter tip: "go with your gut instinct, don't try to second guess what it is we might be looking for" [S350] [Confirmed]. Candidates get a strengths feedback report [S350] [Confirmed].
- **Job Simulation.** It includes ranking, selecting responses, written responses and video responses. "Some scenarios may refer to workplace tools like AI... We're interested in how you think and make decisions, how you solve problems, and consider using AI responsibly in a professional setting, not your technical AI knowledge" [S350] [Confirmed]. This is why the playbook needs AI-ethics scenarios.
- **Final Stage Assessment.** Graduate and Industrial Placement candidates attend in person at their office. The Summer Vacation Scheme stage is virtual [S350] [Confirmed]. Graduate candidates do two exercises. The first is a topic discussion based on mandatory pre-research, "a two-way discussion" of about 30-40 minutes. The second is a "skills and motivation interview" of about 50 minutes on "alignment with the capabilities we look for". The Summer Vacation Scheme has only the skills and motivation interview [S350] [Confirmed].
- **AI rules [S351] [Confirmed]:**
  - AI may be used "responsibly in your preparation".
  - For final-stage topic research, Deloitte asks candidates to "use GenAI tools, such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Microsoft Copilot, alongside other research methods" and to check that AI content "is accurate and can be supported by reliable sources".
  - Online assessments "must be completed independently and without AI assistance". Interviews "must be completed without AI assistance".
  - "Do not share assessment details with AI tools."
  - Breach leads to removal from the process and possibly a report to the university. If found after hire, it can mean disciplinary action "up to and including termination of employment".
  - Candidates record "a short integrity statement" on camera before the online assessment.
  - Paid "assessment insights" websites are "not endorsed". Using third-party answers breaches the Integrity Guidelines.
- **Reapplying.** One application per intake or academic year (September to August) [S350][S352] [Confirmed].

### B. External rules

#### B1. UK GDPR principles and breach reporting

- **Seven principles (Article 5)** [S353] [Confirmed]: (a) lawfulness, fairness and transparency; (b) purpose limitation; (c) data minimisation; (d) accuracy; (e) storage limitation; (f) integrity and confidentiality (security); plus (2) accountability: "The controller shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1".
- **Fines.** Infringing the basic principles falls in the highest tier: "up to £17.5 million, or 4% of your total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher" [S353] [Confirmed].
- **Live change.** The ICO notes the guidance is "under review" because of the Data (Use and Access) Act. Purpose-limitation guidance has already been updated [S353] [Confirmed]. The Act also moved PECR breach reporting from 24 to 72 hours [S354] [Confirmed].
- **Definition.** "A personal data breach means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data." This covers accidental causes too [S354] [Confirmed]. An email to the wrong recipient can be a breach.
- **72 hours.** "You must report a notifiable breach to the ICO without undue delay, but not later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it. If you take longer than this, you must give reasons for the delay." Information can be given "in phases" [S354] [Confirmed]. A breach must be reported unless it is "unlikely to result in a risk" to people's rights and freedoms. If there is "high risk", individuals must also be told "without undue delay". All breaches must be documented, including ones not reported [S354] [Confirmed].
- **Processor rule (key for consultants).** "If your organisation uses a data processor, and this processor suffers a breach, then under Article 33(2) it must inform you without undue delay as soon as it becomes aware." The ICO's own example is an IT services firm that detects an attack and "promptly notifies" the controller [S354] [Confirmed]. Scenario rule: a junior who mis-sends client personal data does not call the ICO. They escalate immediately inside Deloitte, through their manager and the firm's incident or data-protection route. The firm (often a processor) then informs the client (often the controller), and the 72-hour clock is running [Inferred from S354 + S340].

#### B2. Bribery Act 2010

- **s.1 (bribing).** It is an offence to offer, promise or give "a financial or other advantage" intending to induce or reward "improper performance of a relevant function or activity". It applies whether done "directly or through a third party" [S356] [Confirmed].
- **s.7 (failure to prevent).** A "relevant commercial organisation" is guilty if an "associated" person bribes another intending to obtain or keep business or an advantage for it. "But it is a defence for C to prove that C had in place adequate procedures designed to prevent persons associated with C from undertaking such conduct" [S355] [Confirmed]. Partnerships formed under UK law that carry on business are covered, and "a trade or profession is a business" [S355] [Confirmed]. So Deloitte LLP is in scope [Inferred].
- **Adequate procedures: six principles** in the Ministry of Justice guidance (Act in force 1 July 2011) [S357] [Confirmed]: proportionate procedures; top-level commitment; risk assessment; due diligence; communication (including training); monitoring and review.
- **Hospitality.** "Bona fide hospitality and promotional, or other business expenditure which seeks to improve the image of a commercial organisation... is recognised as an established and important part of doing business and it is not the intention of the Act to criminalise such behaviour." But "hospitality and promotional or other similar business expenditure can be employed as bribes". "The more lavish the hospitality... the greater the inference that it is intended to influence" [S357] [Confirmed]. Scenario rule: a modest team lunch is fine. Lavish tickets during a live bid or procurement are a red flag. Check the gifts register and policy, and ask [Inferred].

#### B3. UK Market Abuse Regulation (consultants on listed clients or M&A)

- **Inside information (Art. 7).** "information of a precise nature, which has not been made public, relating, directly or indirectly, to one or more issuers or to one or more financial instruments, and which, if it were made public, would be likely to have a significant effect on the prices" [S358] [Confirmed]. "Significant effect" means information "a reasonable investor would be likely to use as part of the basis of his or her investment decisions" [S358]. In a protracted process (for example a deal), "intermediate steps" can themselves be inside information [S358] [Confirmed].
- **Unlawful disclosure (Art. 10).** Disclosure is unlawful unless it is made "in the normal exercise of an employment, a profession or duties" [S359] [Confirmed]. So need-to-know sharing within the engagement team is allowed. Telling a friend or a flatmate is not.
- **Prohibitions (Art. 14).** A person shall not engage in insider dealing, recommend or induce it, or "unlawfully disclose inside information" [S360] [Confirmed].
- Scenario rule: on an M&A or listed-client project, do not trade or tip, and do not discuss it in public places. Follow any insider-list or wall-crossing instructions [Inferred; Art. 18 insider lists not fetched].
- Criminal insider dealing also exists under the Criminal Justice Act 1993 Part V [Inferred, not fetched].

#### B4. Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023: failure to prevent fraud

- **In force 1 September 2025: verified.** The CPS says the offence "comes into force on 1 September 2025" [S362] [Confirmed]. Government guidance to organisations was published 6 November 2024 [Reported via search snippets of law-firm summaries; the gov.uk page itself was not fetched].
- **Offence (s.199).** "A relevant body which is a large organisation... is guilty of an offence if... a person who is associated with the body... commits a fraud offence intending to benefit (whether directly or indirectly)" the body, or a person to whom the associate provides services on the body's behalf [S361] [Confirmed]. Note the second limb. A consultant's fraud intended to benefit the consultancy's client can be caught [Inferred from text].
- **Defence.** The body had "such prevention procedures as it was reasonable in all the circumstances to expect", or it was not reasonable to expect any [S361] [Confirmed].
- **Associated person.** An "employee, agent or subsidiary undertaking", or someone who performs services for or on its behalf [S361] [Confirmed].
- **Large organisation.** Two or more of: more than 250 employees, more than £36m turnover, more than £18m balance sheet [Reported, law-firm summaries via search; ss.201-202 not fetched].
- The CPS and SFO encourage self-reporting [S362] [Confirmed].
- Scenario use: being asked to backdate a document, inflate a benefits figure in a business case, or misstate progress to the client to secure the next phase is a fraud risk, and now a corporate one.

#### B5. Computer Misuse Act 1990

- **s.1.** It is an offence to cause a computer to perform any function "with intent to secure access to any program or data held in any computer", where the access "is unauthorised", and the person "knows at the time" that it is [S363] [Confirmed]. The maximum on indictment is two years [S363] [Confirmed].
- Scenario use: using a colleague's login, keeping system access after you roll off, or browsing client data beyond your project role. Lend no passwords. Ask for proper access [Inferred].

#### B6. FRC Ethical Standard (independence when Deloitte audits the client)

- The Revised Ethical Standard became "effective on 15 December 2024" [S364] [Confirmed]. Its test is the "objective, reasonable and informed third party" [S364] [Confirmed].
- **Management threat (core rule).** "The provision of any service where it is probable that an objective, reasonable and informed third party would conclude that it would require the firm or any covered person to undertake a management role, or play any part in management decision making in an entity relevant to an engagement is prohibited, as the resulting threat to the independence... is so great that it cannot be safeguarded" [S364] [Confirmed]. Where advice is allowed, the firm must check there is "informed management". That means client management, not the firm, makes the judgments and decisions [S364] [Confirmed].
- **Other threats named:** self-interest, self-review, management and advocacy [S364] [Confirmed].
- **Public interest entities (PIEs).** For PIE audit clients the Standard works as a whitelist. The firm "and... any member of such network, shall not provide... services other than those set out" in para 5.40, subject to audit committee approval [S364] [Confirmed]. Appendix B lists prohibited services. They include tax services; "services that involve playing any part in the management or decision-making of the audited entity"; bookkeeping; payroll; "designing and implementing internal control or risk management procedures related to the preparation and/or control of financial information or designing and implementing financial information technology systems"; valuation; certain legal services; internal audit services; financing and capital structure services; and some HR services including "structuring the organisation design" and "cost control" [S364] [Confirmed].
- **Fee cap.** Non-audit fees for a PIE are capped at 70% of the average audit fee of the last three years [S364] [Confirmed].
- Scenario use for a consultant: if your client is also a Deloitte audit client, you may not make its decisions, run its processes or own its deliverables as if you were management. A request like "just approve it for us" or "can you sit in as interim finance lead" needs immediate escalation to the engagement partner and Independence [Inferred from S364 + S345].

#### B7. Procurement Act 2023: conflicts of interest

- **s.81.** A contracting authority "must take all reasonable steps to identify, and keep under review" conflicts and potential conflicts. "Interest" includes "a personal, professional or financial interest" that may be "direct or indirect". Anyone who "influences a decision" is treated as acting in the procurement [S366] [Confirmed].
- **s.82.** The authority must take reasonable steps so a conflict "does not put a supplier at an unfair advantage or disadvantage". This "may include requiring a supplier to take reasonable steps". If an unfair advantage cannot be avoided, the supplier is treated as excluded [S367] [Confirmed].
- **s.83.** Before publishing a tender or transparency notice, the authority must prepare a "conflicts assessment" and keep it under review [S368] [Confirmed]. The Act's regime applied from 24 February 2025 [Reported: date taken from legislation.gov.uk modification notes, not a commencement order].
- Scenario use: a consultant who helped a department write requirements, and whose firm then wants to bid, creates an unfair-advantage risk. Disclose it and let the bid team and Risk decide. Never use non-public information from advisory work in a bid [Inferred; compare the McKinsey/FDA and PwC cases in F].

#### B8. Security vetting for government work (BPSS and above)

- **BPSS.** The current standard is version 7.0, June 2024. It covers "civil servants, members of the armed forces, temporary staff and government contractors". It applies to people who have "access to government assets" [S369] [Confirmed].
- BPSS "is not a formal security clearance" but "underpins" CTC, Level 1B, SC and DV [S370] [Confirmed]. Checks: "Identity (ID); Right to work (RTW) in the UK; Employment history; and Criminal record (unspent convictions)". It allows access to "UK OFFICIAL information and assets" [S370] [Confirmed].
- **SC.** For posts needing "long-term, frequent and uncontrolled access to SECRET assets and/or occasional, supervised access to TOP SECRET assets". SC is formally reviewed after 10 years, or 7 for non-FSC contractors [S370] [Confirmed].
- Scenario use: on a government engagement you may not see some material until cleared. Do not work around it. Holding a clearance brings ongoing "aftercare" duties [S370] [Confirmed].
- The Official Secrets Acts were partly replaced by the National Security Act 2023 [Inferred, not fetched].

#### B9. ICAEW Code of Ethics: five fundamental principles

- The latest Code "came into force on 1 July 2026" [S371] [Confirmed]. Para 110.1 A1 [S372] [Confirmed]:
  - **Integrity**: "to be straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships."
  - **Objectivity**: "to exercise professional or business judgement without being compromised by: (i) Bias; (ii) Conflict of interest; or (iii) Undue influence of, or undue reliance on, individuals, organisations, technology or other factors."
  - **Professional Competence and Due Care**: attain and maintain knowledge and skill, and "Act diligently and in accordance with applicable technical and professional standards."
  - **Confidentiality**: "to respect the confidentiality of information acquired as a result of professional and business relationships."
  - **Professional Behaviour**: comply with laws, act in the public interest, and "Avoid any conduct that the professional accountant knows or should know might discredit the profession."
- Note "undue reliance on... technology". The Code itself names over-reliance on tools such as AI as an objectivity threat [S372] [Confirmed].
- When principles conflict, the Code suggests consulting, "on an anonymous basis if necessary", with others in the firm, those charged with governance, a professional body, a regulator or legal counsel [S372] [Confirmed]. This mirrors Deloitte's "Consider and Consult".

### C. Consulting craft norms

- **Pyramid Principle and SCQ.** Barbara Minto's principle: "your thinking will be easy for a reader to grasp if you present the ideas organized as a pyramid under a single point". Her Situation, Complication, Question (SCQ) Framework is for "identifying the question in your reader's mind" [S374] [Confirmed]. The Minto textbook "has become a standard text used by all of the major consulting firms" [S374] [Confirmed, self-description]. Practical rule: lead with the answer, then group 3 or so supporting reasons, each backed by evidence [Inferred].
- **MECE.** "Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive". "Strategy consultants use MECE problem structuring to break down client problems into logical, clean buckets of analysis that they can then hand out as work streams" [S375] [Reported, Wikipedia]. It is linked to Minto and the SCQA storytelling frame [S375]. Known criticism: forcing everything to be MECE can be limiting [S375].
- **Issue trees.** "A graphical breakdown of a question that dissects it into its different components". There are two types. Diagnostic trees break down a "why" question. Solution trees break down a "how" question. Branches should be MECE. Issue trees "are used to answer questions in case interviews" [S376] [Reported, Wikipedia citing Chevallier and McKinsey sources].
- **Hypothesis-driven problem solving.** Start with a best-guess answer, build the issue tree around it, and test it with data. I could not fetch a primary McKinsey source because mckinsey.com blocked all requests (see Gaps) [Inferred].
- **Scope and change control.** APM: "Clearly defining what is in and out of scope prevents the risk of misunderstanding at a later point" [S378] [Confirmed]. Change control is "the process through which all requests to change the approved baseline... are captured, evaluated and then approved, rejected or deferred" [S377] [Confirmed]. The steps are: log the request in a change register; initial evaluation; detailed evaluation of impact on "benefits, scope, quality, time, resources, costs, risks"; recommendation to sponsor or board to approve, reject or defer; update the plan if approved; implement and monitor [S377] [Confirmed]. "Formal change control is required when tolerances are breached" [S377]. Scenario rule: a client asks a junior for "one more small analysis" that is out of the SOW. Be helpful, but say "let me check with my manager how we fit that in". Do not silently absorb it [Inferred].
- **RACI.** Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. Accountable people "sign off and approve work", and many sources say there should be only one Accountable per task [S379] [Reported, Wikipedia].
- **Power/interest grid (Mendelow's matrix).** Stakeholders are plotted by power and interest. The usual quadrant strategies are manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, and monitor [S380] [Reported, Wikipedia; quadrant labels Inferred as common usage].
- **RAID log.** Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies. APM explains that issues can turn into change requests [S377] [Confirmed for the issue-to-change link]. No authoritative single source for "RAID" was fetched [Inferred].
- **Scrum (Scrum Guide 2020, Schwaber and Sutherland, November 2020)** [S373] [Confirmed]:
  - "Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems."
  - It is founded on empiricism and lean thinking. The pillars are transparency, inspection and adaptation. The values are "Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage".
  - The Scrum Team is "typically 10 or fewer people". It has three accountabilities: Developers, a Product Owner ("one person, not a committee") and a Scrum Master.
  - Sprints are "one month or less". During a Sprint "No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal", but scope "may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner".
  - Timeboxes: Sprint Planning up to 8 hours for a one-month Sprint; Daily Scrum "a 15-minute event for the Developers"; Sprint Review up to 4 hours; Retrospective up to 3 hours.
  - The commitments are Product Goal, Sprint Goal and Definition of Done.
  - Scenario rule: a stakeholder asks a developer directly to add a feature mid-sprint. Route it to the Product Owner and the backlog [Inferred from S373].
- **UAT.** "Formal testing with respect to user needs, requirements, and business processes conducted to determine whether a system satisfies the acceptance criteria and to enable the user, customers or other authorized entity to determine whether to accept the system" (ISTQB definition quoted) [S381] [Reported, Wikipedia]. Scenario rule: business users, not the build team, sign off UAT. Do not mark failed tests as passed to hit a go-live date [Inferred; links to B4 fraud risk and Code integrity].
- **Cutover.** This is the planned switch from old to new system, usually with go/no-go criteria and a rollback plan. No authoritative source was fetched [Inferred].
- **Escalation norm.** Deloitte's own Code is the best source: consult "team leaders or others in their chain of command, Ethics, or Talent/HR" and "No one at Deloitte should have to navigate a difficult situation alone" [S340] [Confirmed]. Practical pattern: raise early, bring facts and a proposed option, and escalate one level at a time unless it is an ethics or legal matter, which can go straight to Ethics or Speak Up [Inferred].

### D. Behavioural interviewing and assessment

- **What Deloitte UK says it assesses.** The online assessment targets "your unique strengths". The final interview is a "skills and motivation interview" on "alignment with the capabilities we look for" [S350] [Confirmed]. Deloitte does not publicly label it "strengths-based" or "competency-based" on the pages read. Expect both "tell me about a time" and "what do you enjoy" style questions [Inferred].
- **STAR/STARR.** Prospects recommends "the STARR (situation, task, action, result and reflection) method". It advises having "five to eight STARR examples", focusing on "what you specifically did and decided", always including the result, ideally "tangible outcomes", and shifting emphasis to fit the question [S382] [Confirmed]. CAR (Context, Action, Result) is a shorter variant [Inferred].
- **How assessors score at assessment centres.** "Assessors, who are usually a mix of HR consultants and line managers, score your actions against competency frameworks". "All, several, one or none of the candidates could be successful". This means candidates are not competing against each other in a group exercise [S383] [Confirmed]. Typical skills: adaptability, analytical thinking, commercial awareness, communication, creativity, decision-making, leadership, negotiation, organisation, persuasion, planning, teamwork, time management [S383] [Confirmed]. Assessment centres "allow you to compensate for poor performance in one task by excelling in another" [S383] [Confirmed].
- **Group exercise implication.** Because scoring is against criteria, not ranking, the right behaviours are: include quieter people, build on others' points, keep time, summarise, and steer to a decision [Inferred from S383].
- **Deloitte final-stage topic discussion.** This is the closest current Deloitte UK format to a "case". It is a researched topic discussed two-way with an assessor for about 30-40 minutes. Deloitte asks candidates to use GenAI in that research but to verify sources [S350][S351] [Confirmed]. I found no current Deloitte UK "case interview prep" page (see Gaps).
- **Recruiter reassurance (quotable).** "Our assessors are here to get the best out of you, they are not trying to trip you up or trick you" [S350] [Confirmed].

### E. Composure under pressure (evidence)

- **Reappraise anxiety as excitement.** Brooks (2014), *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 143(3):1144-58. Most people believe "trying to calm down is the best way to cope". But across karaoke, public speaking and maths tasks, people who reappraised anxiety as excitement "feel more excited and perform better". Minimal strategies such as saying "I am excited" out loud worked. They led to "an opportunity mind-set (as opposed to a threat mind-set)" [S384] [Confirmed]. Mechanism: "arousal congruency". Excitement and anxiety are both high-arousal states, so it is easier to relabel than to calm down.
- **Arousal reappraisal.** Jamieson et al. (2010), *J Exp Soc Psychol* 46(1):208-212. Students told that "arousal improves performance" outperformed controls on GRE maths, both in the lab and on the real GRE 1-3 months later [S386] [Confirmed].
- **Exhale-focused breathing (cyclic sighing).** Balban et al. (2023), *Cell Reports Medicine* 4(1):100895. This was a randomised controlled study over one month comparing 5-minute daily practices. "Exhale-focused cyclic sighing" gave greater improvement in mood and reduction in respiratory rate than mindfulness meditation (p < 0.05) [S385] [Confirmed]. Caveats: it measured daily practice over a month, not one breath before an interview. One author disclosed an advisory role with WHOOP [S385] [Confirmed]. Practical use: a few long slow exhales in the waiting room is a reasonable, low-cost step [Inferred].
- **Expressive writing before high-stakes tests.** Ramirez and Beilock (2011), *Science* 331:211-3. A brief writing exercise about worries right before an exam "significantly improved students' exam scores, especially for students habitually anxious about test taking" [S387] [Confirmed]. Note: Science published an erratum in 2014 [S387].
- **Playbook combination [Inferred]:** 10 minutes of writing down worries the night before or that morning; slow exhales before entering; tell yourself "I'm excited" rather than "calm down"; then use a structure (STAR, Minto) so working memory is not spent on organising.

### F. Real cautionary cases (date, source, lesson)

1. **Deloitte Australia, AI-assisted report refund (October 2025).** The Department of Employment and Workplace Relations commissioned a review costing about A$440,000. The report was published 4 July 2025. It was found to contain nonexistent references and an erroneous summary of a court case (Amato). The revised version disclosed use of "Azure OpenAI GPT – 4o". Deloitte agreed to repay the final instalment and said "the matter has been resolved directly with the client" [S388] [Confirmed; FT coverage S389 PAYWALLED-NOT-READ]. **Lesson:** you own every line of an AI-assisted deliverable. Verify every citation, disclose AI use as the contract requires, and remember that human review is the control.
2. **PwC Australia tax leaks (story broke 2023; CEO stood down 8-9 May 2023).** Partner Peter-John Collins had signed confidentiality agreements while advising the Australian government on multinational tax law. He shared that knowledge inside PwC, which used it to win clients. The Treasurer called it "a shocking breach of trust". CEO Tom Seymour stepped down [S392] [Confirmed]. **Lesson:** information from one client (especially government) is ring-fenced. Using it for another client or for business development is a career-ending breach.
3. **McKinsey and the FDA (reported 2021).** McKinsey advised the FDA's drug-regulation division from 2008 while also advising opioid makers such as Purdue. According to the FDA, it "never disclosed its pharmaceutical company clients" despite contract clauses requiring "immediate and full disclosure" of potential conflicts [S391] [Confirmed]. **Lesson:** disclose potential conflicts early and in writing. Let the client and your firm decide. Silence is the failure.
4. **McKinsey opioid settlement (13 December 2024).** McKinsey paid $650 million to resolve federal civil and criminal probes, with a deferred prosecution agreement. A former senior partner agreed to plead guilty to obstruction for deleting Purdue-related files. The DOJ called it "the first time a management consulting firm has been criminally responsible for advice it has given resulting in the commission of a crime by a client" [S390] [Confirmed]. **Lesson:** advice can carry legal liability, and destroying records turns a problem into a crime. Never delete files when a matter may be investigated.
5. **Deloitte global email breach (disclosed 25 September 2017).** Attackers reached Deloitte's global email server through an administrator account that "required only a single password and did not have 'two-step' verification". The attack was discovered March 2017, with access possibly since late 2016. Client emails were exposed [S393] [Confirmed]. **Lesson:** basic hygiene (MFA, least privilege) protects client confidentiality. Report anything odd at once.
6. **Capita data breach and ICO fine (fine announced 15 October 2025; attack March 2023).** The ICO fined Capita £14m (cut from a proposed £45m) after a 2023 attack exposed data of 6.6 million people. Containment was not enacted "for 58 hours" after an alert. Its security centre missed a high-priority alert [S394] [Confirmed]. Capita is an outsourcer, not a consultancy, but it is the clearest recent UK example of processor-side failure. **Lesson:** speed of escalation matters. A junior who sees something wrong should raise it now, not after the weekend.

## 6. Interview angles

1. "Deloitte's Global Code gives a simple method I'd use on day one, ACT: assess, consider and consult, take action. It also says speaking up is taking action." [S340]
2. On AI: "Your own GenAI guidance says an AI model can't be held accountable, so accountability is human. If I used a tool to draft something, I'd verify every source before it left my hands. The Australian DEWR report shows why." [S347][S388]
3. On client data and AI: "I'd only put client data into firm-approved tools. The Transparency Report describes PairD working inside a secure ecosystem, and the Code bans disclosing client information without permission." [S340][S345]
4. Question to ask: "The 2025 Transparency Report lists GenAI as a 'Very High' new priority risk with a use-case clearing house. How does that show up for a first-year consultant on a client project?" [S345]
5. Question to ask: "PIRG declined about 9% of the matters it reviewed last year, mostly on values grounds. How often does that kind of review touch Consulting work?" [S345]
6. On independence: "If my client were also a Deloitte audit client, I know the FRC standard bars anything that looks like taking a management role. So I'd give options and analysis, and the client's informed management would decide." [S364]
7. On data breaches: "If I mis-sent personal data, I'd escalate inside the firm straight away, because the client may have only 72 hours to tell the ICO and processors must tell controllers without undue delay." [S354]
8. On gifts: "The Bribery Act guidance accepts reasonable hospitality but says the more lavish it is, the stronger the inference of influence. Deloitte's Code adds 'or be perceived to'. So during a live bid I'd decline and check policy." [S357][S340]
9. On scope creep: "I'd be helpful but route new asks through change control, which APM describes as log, evaluate, recommend, approve or reject, and update the plan. That protects the client and the team." [S377]
10. On agile delivery: "In Scrum, scope can be renegotiated with the Product Owner, but nothing should endanger the Sprint Goal. So a mid-sprint request goes to the backlog, not straight to a developer." [S373]
11. On conflicts: "The McKinsey/FDA story is about non-disclosure. The fix is to disclose early in writing and let the firm decide." [S391]
12. On confidentiality: "PwC Australia showed that knowledge from one client, especially government, can't be reused for another. That is exactly the Code's ban on using client information for third parties." [S392][S340]
13. On composure (for the candidate, not to say aloud): reframe nerves as excitement (Brooks 2014), do slow exhale-focused breathing (Balban 2023), and write worries down beforehand (Ramirez and Beilock 2011). [S384][S385][S387]
14. On Deloitte's process: "I understand the final stage is a topic discussion plus a skills and motivation interview. You ask candidates to use GenAI for the research but verify sources, which is how I'd want to work anyway." [S350][S351]
15. On the ICAEW Code 2026: "Objectivity now explicitly includes not relying unduly on technology, which is a useful rule for AI-assisted consulting." [S372]
