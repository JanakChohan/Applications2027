## 1. Findings

### 4A. The industry from scratch

#### 4A.1 What consulting actually sells

A consultancy sells people's time and judgement to an organisation that has a problem it cannot or will not solve alone. Strip away the branding and clients buy five things [Inferred]:

1. **Expertise.** Knowledge the client does not have in house, for example how to move a bank's systems to the cloud or how to meet a new regulation.
2. **Capacity.** Extra hands for a fixed period. A client may know what to do but lack the people to do it. Much "consulting" spend is this.
3. **Independence.** An outside view that is not tied to internal politics. The 2013 HBR article notes that clients who are less sure of the question are "more tempted to use a big brand" [S160] [Confirmed].
4. **Legitimacy.** A known firm's name on a recommendation helps a board or minister defend a decision.
5. **Implementation.** Actually building and running the change: systems, processes, training. This is where most money now flows. Source Global Research says the global *technology* consulting market alone is projected to pass $400bn in 2026 [S157] [Reported].

Christensen, Wang and van Bever (HBR, October 2013) describe the classic model this way: very smart outsiders go into an organisation for a finite period and recommend solutions to its hardest problems. They call integrated firms "solution shops" [S160][S161] [Confirmed].

**Who buys it.** Large corporates (often the CFO for finance and cost work, the CIO or CTO for technology, the COO for operations), banks and insurers (heavily driven by regulation), and the public sector (central government, NHS, local government, defence) [Inferred]. Source says digital transformation projects "are most likely to be commissioned by CxOs, so companies are prepared to pay more for them" [S157] [Reported]. Older MCA data put financial services at about a third and the public sector at about a fifth of UK consulting fee income, but that dataset is old (it cites forecasts "through 2018") so treat the shares as indicative only [S159] [Reported, dated].

#### 4A.3 How consulting differs from neighbouring businesses

- **Audit.** A statutory opinion on whether financial statements give a true and fair view. Legally required, regulated by the FRC, and the auditor must be independent. Consulting is optional advice and delivery. The key point for Deloitte: a firm that audits a company is heavily restricted in what consulting it can sell to that same company (see 4B.1) [S180] [Confirmed].
- **Tax.** Compliance and advisory on tax. Often sits next to consulting in the Big Four but is a separate business line.
- **Deal advisory / transaction services.** Due diligence, valuation, restructuring around a specific transaction. Time-bound and tied to a deal.
- **Investment banking.** Raises capital and advises on M&A, paid largely by success fees on deals. Regulated by the FCA. Consultants are usually paid for time or deliverables, not for completing a deal.
- **Private equity.** Invests its own and its investors' money to own companies. PE firms are big *buyers* of consulting (due diligence, value creation), and increasingly *owners* of professional services firms (see 4A.7).

#### 4A.4 Core vocabulary (plain English)

- **Engagement / project:** a piece of paid work for a client.
- **SOW (statement of work):** the contract document that says what will be delivered, by when, by whom, for how much.
- **Rate card:** the agreed daily or hourly price for each grade of consultant.
- **Utilisation:** share of a consultant's available hours that are billed to clients. High utilisation means more revenue per person.
- **Chargeable hours:** the hours actually billed.
- **Leverage:** the ratio of junior staff to partners. More juniors per partner means more revenue per partner.
- **Pyramid:** the staffing shape that leverage produces: few partners at the top, many analysts and consultants at the base. AI is the main threat to this shape (see 4A.6).
- **Bench:** consultants not currently on a client project.
- **Pitch / RFP (request for proposal):** the competitive bid process to win work.
- **Framework agreement:** a pre-approved list of suppliers a buyer (often government) can call off work from without running a full tender each time. Example: the UK government's Management Consultancy Framework Four (MCF4) [S191].
- **Managed services:** the consultancy runs a process or system for the client on an ongoing basis, often for years. Revenue is recurring.
- **Offshore / nearshore delivery:** doing work in lower-cost locations (India, Malaysia, Philippines are named in reporting on the Big Four) or nearby cheaper countries [S162] [Reported].
- **Alliance partners:** tech vendors (for example SAP, Oracle, Salesforce, Microsoft, AWS, Google) whose software the consultancy implements. Reporting says each big firm picked an AI partner, for example Deloitte with Anthropic's Claude and PwC with OpenAI [S164] [Reported].
- **Systems integrator (SI):** a firm that makes multiple software products work together inside a client.
- **ERP (enterprise resource planning):** core business software for finance, HR, supply chain (for example SAP S/4HANA). ERP projects are big, long and risky.
- **Cloud migration:** moving systems from a client's own data centres to cloud providers.
- **Change management:** helping people actually adopt a new way of working.
- **PMO (programme management office):** the team that runs governance, plans and reporting for a large programme.
- **Target operating model (TOM):** a design of how an organisation should work in future (people, process, technology, governance).

#### 4A.5 Market size

**Global.** Source Global Research estimated the global consulting market at $275bn in 2025, 6% growth on "a disappointing 2024" (Emma Carroll, 10 June 2025). By service line: technology and innovation $69.2bn (5% growth); strategy just over $60bn (6%); risk and financial management $58.7bn (7%); cybersecurity about $36bn (9%, the fastest). Operations (4%) and HR / change (2%) lagged [S156] [Reported]. Source counts only "big consulting" (firms with 50+ consultants) and excludes outsourcing and managed services [S157]. Separately, Source puts global *technology* consulting (a broader definition) above $400bn in 2026, with growth of 4% in 2024, 6% in 2025, 7% forecast for 2026 [S157] [Reported]. These two numbers use different definitions and should not be added together.

**UK (MCA).** The Management Consultancies Association (MCA), the UK trade body, publishes an annual report with Oxford Economics.
- 2024: UK consulting industry estimated at £20.4bn, exports about £5.9bn (MCA, 7 July 2025) [S152][S153] [Confirmed].
- 2025: fee income up 3% to £21.8bn. MCA members alone £10.9bn. Exports up 9%, now 31% of fee income. Published July 2026 [S150][S151] [Confirmed].
- Growth was uneven: 52% of firms grew, 46% declined, and the report says the 3% average "masks a mixed performance across the sector" [S151] [Reported].
- Forecasts have been cut repeatedly. 2026 growth was forecast at 8.7% (January 2025), then 7.8% (mid-2025), then 5.7% (early 2026), and the July 2026 report says 6% for 2026 and 8% for 2027 [S154][S150] [Reported].
- Hiring in 2025: 5,400+ new hires, graduate recruitment up 10%, apprentice hiring up 31% across MCA members [S150] [Reported]. Note this contrasts with Big Four graduate cuts (4A.6). The difference is plausibly mix: smaller and mid-sized MCA members growing hiring while the largest firms cut [Inferred].
- Chartered Management Consultants (ChMC): 1,800+ in the 2025 report, 2,800+ in the 2026 report [S153][S150] [Reported].
- Regional: 39% of consultants based outside London in the 2025 report [S153] [Confirmed].

**UK (Source Global Research).** Source uses a narrower definition and put the UK market at £14.9bn in 2024 [S154] [Reported]. Its February 2026 report forecast 6% UK growth in 2026, based on a survey of 200 senior UK buyers. Nick Jotischky, Head of Market Trends: "There's a cautious optimism that there's going to be growth in the market" [S155] [Confirmed].

**Public sector share.** No current, freely readable MCA figure on public sector share was found. Older MCA-derived data say 21% [S159] [Reported, dated]. Central government consultancy spend is covered under 4A.6.

#### 4A.6 Structural forces now

**(a) Generative and agentic AI.**
- *Pyramid pressure.* If AI drafts research, slides and code, firms need fewer analysts at the base. A Patrick Morgan recruiter told City AM: "The Big Four are looking at AI very seriously to replicate junior work more cost-effectively" [S162] [Reported].
- *New AI-native competitors.* An FT Big Read (May 2026, not read: paywalled) is summarised as saying AI erodes the Big Four's scale advantage. Mark Bunker, ex-Deloitte founder of Queen's Tower Advisory, reportedly targets teams that are "20 per cent humans, 80 per cent AI agents". KPMG UK advisory head Lisa Fernihough is quoted: "I want this organisation, us, to still exist … That's how disruptive I believe AI will be" [S164][S165] [Reported, second-hand summary of FT].
- *AI labs as consultancies.* The Telegraph (26 May 2026) reports OpenAI and Anthropic launched consulting ventures that month, and OpenAI bought UK consultancy Tomoro AI. A consultancy insider called it "definitely a direct play against the traditional consulting firms" [S166] [Reported].
- *Firms' AI investment.* Secondary reporting lists Deloitte's $2bn "Industry Advantage" initiative (early 2024), PwC $1bn (2023), KPMG a $2bn Microsoft alliance, EY $1.4bn; McKinsey's internal tool Lilli used by about 72% of 45,000 staff [S163] [Reported, secondary blog; verify against firm press releases].
- *Pricing shift to outcomes.* McKinsey is reported to have about a quarter to a third of fees linked to outcomes [S163][S164] [Reported, figures differ between sources]. Christensen et al. in 2013 quoted a partner expecting value-based pricing to go "from the high single digits to a third of the business within 20 years" [S160] [Confirmed].
- *AI risk on the delivery side.* Deloitte Australia agreed in October 2025 to partially refund the A$440,000 paid by the Australian government for a 237-page report that contained a fabricated court quote and references to non-existent papers. The revised version disclosed use of Azure OpenAI [S171] [Reported, AP via search snippet; full page not loaded]. This is the clearest public example of why AI quality control matters for a consultancy brand.

**(b) Demand slowdown 2023 to 2025, and partial recovery.** Source called 2024 "disappointing" and saw 6% global growth in 2025 [S156]. UK growth was only 3% in 2025 with 46% of firms shrinking [S150][S151]. Forecasts for 2026 to 2027 are 6% to 8% [S150][S155]. Source's December 2025 outlook said "It's the clients with the shakiest business outlook that are likely to spend the most in 2026" [S158] [Reported]. MCA members said AI is the industry's leading challenge (52%), and 77% had integrated AI into systems [S154] [Reported].

**(c) Graduate hiring cuts.** City AM (23 June 2025): KPMG's graduate intake fell 29% (1,399 to 942); Deloitte cut its scheme by 18%; EY 11%; PwC 6%. Accountancy graduate adverts fell 44% year on year [S162] [Reported; the exact years compared are not fully clear in the article, see Gaps]. PwC's global headcount reportedly fell by 5,600 [S164] [Reported].

**(d) UK government consultancy spend controls.**
- 10 October 2023: then shadow Chancellor Rachel Reeves said Labour "will aim to cut consultancy spending in half over the next parliament" and that departments "must demonstrate the value-for-money case. And if they cannot, then that request will be denied" [S176] [Confirmed].
- From July 2024 in government: stop non-essential consultancy spend and halve consultancy spend in 2025-26; target £550m savings in 2024-25, rising to £700m a year by 2028-29 [S173][S177] [Confirmed].
- Outcome claimed: of £1.5bn planned spend July 2024 to March 2025, actual spend was £910.6m, a reduction of £602.7m, beating the £550m target. Biggest spenders: DHSC £251.9m, DESNZ £160.9m, DfT £153.1m. Civil service COO Cat Little noted concern that cuts should not "simply lead to an increase in 'professional services' or 'contingent labour'" [S172] [Reported].
- Counter-evidence: City AM's Tussell analysis (22 July 2025) found management consultancy contract spend *rose* to £1.44bn in Labour's first year, with Deloitte the largest supplier at £431m [S174] [Reported]. Different definitions (contracts awarded versus spend) explain part of the gap [Inferred].
- NAO (21 November 2025): "The government does not collect data on how it uses consultants, only what it spends." Central government spend was about £1.36bn in 2022-23 per HM Treasury, but "other sources suggest the figure could be significantly higher" [S173] [Confirmed].
- Why it matters for Deloitte: Deloitte is the single largest UK government consultancy supplier by contract value in the Tussell data [S174], so these controls hit it directly [Inferred].

**(e) US DOGE cuts (2025).** The Pentagon cancelled $5.1bn of contracts (claimed $4bn savings) in April 2025, naming Accenture, Booz Allen Hamilton and Deloitte; $1.8bn at the Defense Health Agency involved all three [S169] [Confirmed by Nextgov and Washington Technology]. Fortune reported DOGE had eliminated more than 120 Deloitte contracts, more than twice any other consultancy [S170] [Reported, snippet]. Booz Allen said it would lay off about 2,500 staff [S206] [Reported, snippet]. This is the US firm, not Deloitte UK, but it shows public-sector concentration risk [Inferred].

**(f) Offshoring.** Big Four firms are pushing more work to India, Malaysia and the Philippines [S162] [Reported].

**(g) Private equity in professional services.** Cinven completed a buyout of Grant Thornton UK in 2025, reportedly valuing it around £1.5bn, the largest such UK deal; average partner payout reported at £682,000 [S168] [Reported]. Unity Advisory launched (April 2025) with up to $300m from Warburg Pincus, led by ex-EY UK chair Steve Varley and ex-PwC COO Marissa Thomas. It offers no audit [S167] [Confirmed]. Varley: "people are looking for a proposition that is super client-centric, has really low administrative cost, is AI-led rather than based on legacy infrastructure and, crucially, has no conflicts" [S167].

**(h) Audit / consulting separation pressure.** The FRC's operational separation (4B.2) ring-fences audit inside the Big Four. Challengers such as Unity sell "no conflicts" as a feature [S167]. Ernst & Young's global attempt to split audit and consulting ("Project Everest") is well known but was not re-verified in this workstream [Not verified].

#### 4A.7 Three-plus competing views on where the industry goes (3 to 5 years)

1. **"Disruption is coming, slowly then suddenly" (Christensen, Wang, van Bever, HBR, October 2013).** Consulting is shifting from integrated "solution shops" to modular providers; knowledge is being "democratized"; clients are disaggregating work. They predicted consolidation at the top, that the real story "will begin with smaller clients", blurring of professional-service boundaries, and that "the steady invasion of hard analytics and technology (big data) is a certainty". Closing line: "there may be nothing as vulnerable as entrenched success" [S160] [Confirmed]. AI is the strongest version yet of the force they described [Inferred].
2. **"AI breaks the pyramid and scale advantage" (FT Big Read and City AM, May 2026, as summarised; Telegraph, May 2026).** AI-native boutiques and AI labs take work; the Big Four's leverage model weakens; graduate intakes fall; KPMG's Fernihough frames the threat as existential [S164][S166] [Reported].
3. **"Cautious recovery, driven by implementation" (Source Global Research, MCA).** Clients are moving from uncertainty to execution and need outside help to deliver; UK growth of about 6% in 2026 and 8% in 2027; AI, cyber and transformation drive demand [S155][S150][S157] [Confirmed]. 81% of tech buyers intend to increase reliance on external consultants [S157] [Reported].
4. **"Conflict-free, PE-backed, outcome-priced challengers" (Unity Advisory / Steve Varley; Grant Thornton / Cinven).** Capital from PE plus no audit conflicts plus AI-first cost base lets challengers undercut and use "different fee models" such as value sharing [S167][S168] [Reported].
5. **"Old pyramid persists despite billions spent" (Marin Ivezic, January 2026).** Firms have spent heavily on AI but still staff and bill traditionally; the shift to outcome pricing is "sluggish" [S163] [Reported, single commentator].

A balanced candidate view [Inferred]: demand for *implementation* (AI, cloud, cyber, regulation) is real and growing, while demand for *junior analytical hours* shrinks. Firms with strong delivery, alliances and trust (regulated sectors, government) are best placed, but must change pricing and junior roles.

### 4B. Regulation of the sub-industry

Consulting itself has no licence or statutory regulator. Deloitte UK consultants are constrained mainly because they sit inside a firm that also audits. Below are the instruments that matter, in order.

#### 4B.1 Auditor independence: EU Audit Regulation and the FRC Ethical Standard

- **Regulation (EU) No 537/2014** (EU Audit Regulation) applied from **17 June 2016**. It introduced a **70% cap**: non-audit fees to a public interest entity (PIE) audit client cannot exceed 70% of the average statutory audit fee over the previous three years. It also lists prohibited non-audit services (Article 5) and mandatory audit firm rotation (Article 17) [S182][S180] [Confirmed]. It is retained in UK law after Brexit [Inferred; not separately verified].
- **FRC Revised Ethical Standard 2016** gave effect to these rules in the UK [S179][S180] [Confirmed].
- **FRC Revised Ethical Standard 2019**: released **17 December 2019**, effective **15 March 2020** [S180] [Confirmed]. Key changes, per Deloitte's own February 2020 briefing:
  - Move from a "blacklist" to a **"whitelist"** of permitted non-audit services for UK PIEs. Permitted services are largely those required by law and regulation, loan covenant reporting, other assurance closely linked to the audit, and reporting accountant work [S180] [Confirmed].
  - Complete bans for all audited entities on internal audit services, secondments, and contingent fees [S180][S181] [Confirmed].
  - Extraterritorial effect: all services by the firm and its network to UK PIEs, their UK parents and worldwide subsidiaries are subject to the whitelist [S180].
  - New category **Other Entities of Public Interest (OEPI)** (large AIM companies, Lloyd's syndicates, largest pension schemes, largest private companies with more than 2,000 employees, or turnover over £200m and balance sheet over £2bn). Whitelist applies to OEPIs for periods from **15 December 2020**, but the 70% cap does not [S180][S181] [Confirmed].
  - Stronger "objective, reasonable and informed third party" test [S180].
- **FRC Revised Ethical Standard 2024**: published **15 January 2024**, effective **15 December 2024**. Simplified; aligned with IESBA Code changes; new restriction on fees from entities related by a single controlling party; dropped some proposals after consultation. Mark Babington: "The revised ethical standard has been simplified to ensure auditors are clear as to the high ethical standards expected" [S178] [Confirmed].

**Why it matters to a Deloitte consultant.** Before a Deloitte consulting team can work for a company, the firm must check whether Deloitte (anywhere in the network) audits that company or a related entity. If it is a PIE audit client, most consulting is simply not allowed. This is why "independence checks" and conflict clearance are a daily part of the job, and why rivals like Unity pitch "no conflicts" [S180][S167] [Inferred from Confirmed rules].

#### 4B.2 FRC operational separation of audit practices

- **6 July 2020**: FRC published principles requiring the Big Four (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) to operationally separate their UK audit practices. Implementation plans due **23 October 2020**; full implementation by **30 June 2024** [S183] [Confirmed].
- Outcomes required: audit governance that protects auditors from the rest of the firm; audit partners' profit share not persistently exceeding the audit practice's own contribution (no cross-subsidy); an audit culture of challenge and scepticism; auditors acting in the public interest [S183] [Confirmed]. Sir Jon Thompson (then FRC CEO): "Operational separation of audit practices is one element of the FRC's strategy to improve the quality and effectiveness of corporate reporting and audit in the United Kingdom" [S183].
- **10 October 2024**: FRC announced all four firms had concluded the transition period and met the deadline, with independent audit boards chaired by Audit Non-Executives and more transparency on financial transactions between audit and non-audit businesses. FRC will publish annual compliance assessments [S184] [Confirmed].
- **What it means for Deloitte UK** [Inferred]: Deloitte UK's audit practice has its own board and separate financial accounting inside the LLP. Consulting cannot rely on audit profits, and audit cannot be run to sell consulting. Deloitte remains one LLP (registered OC303675) [S204], so this is separation *inside* the firm, not a legal split.

#### 4B.3 Audit reform: white paper to abandonment

- **December 2018**: Kingman Review recommends replacing the FRC (after Carillion, BHS, Patisserie Valerie) [S187] [Reported].
- **March 2021**: BEIS white paper "Restoring trust in audit and corporate governance" [S187] [Confirmed by Wikipedia; government response of May 2022 not re-verified].
- **July 2024**: King's Speech announced a Draft Audit Reform and Corporate Governance Bill to create ARGA [S185][S186] [Confirmed].
- **July 2025**: Minister told the Business and Trade Committee pre-legislative scrutiny would not happen this session [S186 snippet] [Reported].
- **20 January 2026**: Government abandoned the Audit Reform Bill. Business Secretary Peter Kyle confirmed; Business Minister Blair McDougall wrote to the select committee. The reason given was growth and lower burdens on large firms. ARGA is dropped. The government still intends to put the FRC on a statutory footing with stronger investigative powers and mandatory industry funding. ICAEW's Alan Vallance called it a "false dawn"; FRC CEO Richard Moriarty said the FRC would continue its work "regardless" [S185][S186] [Confirmed by two outlets].
- **13 May 2026**: King's Speech 2026 background notes contain no audit reform bill and no mention of the FRC or ARGA (checked by text search of the 129-page gov.uk PDF) [S188] [Confirmed]. Deloitte's own FS blog on the speech also did not mention audit reform [S189].
- **Status as of October 2026** [Inferred from above]: ARGA is dead. FRC statutory footing is a stated intention without a bill in the 2026 session. Rules come from the FRC's existing powers.

#### 4B.5 Data and AI rules

- **UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018**: the base regime [Inferred].
- **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025**: Royal Assent **19 June 2025**. Staged commencement: some provisions on Royal Assent; first commencement regulations from **20 August 2025**; most data protection changes in force **5 February 2026**; new complaints duty from **19 June 2026** (organisations must acknowledge data protection complaints within 30 days) [S194][S195] [Reported, two sources agree on Royal Assent date].
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)**: in force **1 August 2024**; prohibitions and AI literacy from **2 February 2025**; general-purpose AI (GPAI) obligations from **2 August 2025**; most remaining provisions from **2 August 2026** [S193] [Confirmed].
- **Digital Omnibus on AI** (amending the AI Act): provisional agreement 7 May 2026; Parliament approval 16 June 2026; Council adoption 29 June 2026; published in the Official Journal late July 2026 (Orrick says 29 July 2026; another summary says entry into force 27 July 2026) [S192] [Reported]. New deadlines: **Annex III high-risk systems (employment, credit scoring, education, biometrics, critical infrastructure) by 2 December 2027**; Annex I (AI in regulated products) by **2 August 2028**; Article 50 synthetic content transparency by **2 December 2026**; new bans on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM from **2 December 2026**; public authority systems by **2 August 2030**. Relief for "small mid-caps" (under 750 staff) [S192] [Reported]. **Conflict flag:** artificialintelligenceact.eu lists Annex III as 2 August 2027 [S193]. The law-firm source written after publication (Orrick) says 2 December 2027. Use 2 December 2027 but check the OJ text.
- **UK AI approach**: no AI bill. AI Opportunities Action Plan published **13 January 2025** (50 recommendations) [S203] [Reported]. King's Speech 2026 (13 May 2026) has no AI bill. It includes a **Regulating for Growth Bill** with a strengthened statutory Growth Duty for regulators and cross-economy "sandboxing powers", explicitly including "cross-cutting AI sandboxes" [S188] [Confirmed]. It also lists a **Cyber Security and Resilience Bill** (DSIT) [S188] [Confirmed].

#### 4B.6 Financial-services regulation consultants work on

- **DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554)**: applies from **17 January 2025**. ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing, third-party oversight and a register of ICT providers. UK firms with EU entities, and ICT providers to EU financial firms (including IT consultancies), are caught [S201] [Reported].
- **FCA PS21/3 and PRA SS1/21 operational resilience**: transition ended **31 March 2025**. Firms must be able to stay within impact tolerances for each important business service in a severe but plausible disruption [S199] [Confirmed by FCA page title and multiple firms].
- **Consumer Duty (FCA PS22/9)**: in force **31 July 2023** for open products; **31 July 2024** for closed products, also the date of the first annual board report [S200] [Confirmed].
- **Basel 3.1**: PRA delayed by one year (announced January 2025) to **1 January 2027** [S197]. Final rules PS1/26 published **20 January 2026**, effective 1 January 2027, with FRTB internal models delayed to **1 January 2028** [S196] [Confirmed].
- **UK T+1 settlement**: HMT, FCA and Bank of England confirmed on **19 February 2025** support for moving to T+1 by **11 October 2027** [S198] [Confirmed by multiple outlets].
- **UK Sustainability Reporting Standards (UK SRS S1 and S2)**: ISSB-based; government consulted 25 June to 17 September 2025 and published final standards on **25 February 2026** for **voluntary** use [S202] [Reported]. Mandatory application had not been confirmed in the sources read.
- **King's Speech 2026 FS bill**: an Enhancing Financial Services Bill delivering the 2025 "Leeds Reforms": FOS reform, consolidating the Payment Systems Regulator into the FCA, SM&CR changes [S188][S189] [Confirmed].

#### 4B.7 Professional bodies

- **Management Consultancies Association (MCA)**: UK trade body for consulting firms; publishes the annual industry report; runs "Consulting Excellence" (the quality hallmark) and the Chartered Management Consultant (ChMC) accreditation [S205][S153] [Confirmed]. MCA had 91 member firms per the 2025 executive summary [S153].
