1How to use this guide

The secret about product names

You have seen this before. Products get grand names, and behind each name is a simple idea. Rubrik is the same.

"Preemptive Recovery Engine" means "we work out which backup is safe before you need it". "Quorum Authorization" means "two people must agree before anything is deleted".

The one idea behind every product

Something valuable gets broken, and Rubrik puts it back the way it was before it broke.

Each product is that same idea applied to a different valuable thing: files, cloud systems, email, logins, or the work of an AI assistant.

How each product is explained

Every product in this guide follows the same pattern, so you always know where to look.

  • The name, and what it means in plain English.
  • The problem. What goes wrong for a customer without it.
  • How it works. In steps, with any technical word translated.
  • A real-life example. A real incident where I have one, a made-up one where I do not. I say which.
  • Say it in one sentence. How you would explain it in an interview.

A test for real understanding

For any product, ask yourself: "What would go wrong for the customer if this did not exist?" If you can answer that, you understand it. The name and the features follow from the answer.

What a backup is

A backup is a spare copy of data, kept in case the original is lost or damaged. If the original breaks, you copy the spare back. That copying back is called a restore or recovery.

Think of the save points in a video game. You save before a hard level. If it goes wrong, you reload the save.

What ransomware is

Ransomware is malicious software that scrambles a company's files so they cannot be read. The criminals then demand money for the key to unscramble them.

The technical word for scrambling is encryption. Scrambled files are useless without the key, so tills stop, websites go down and staff cannot work.

How a modern attack unfolds

This is the most useful thing to understand in the whole guide. Attacks follow a pattern, and each Rubrik product answers one step of it.

The key insight: step 3

Attackers delete the backups before they scramble anything. A victim with working backups just restores and ignores the ransom demand. A victim with no backups has to pay.

So the whole game is whether the backups survive. Rubrik was designed around that one fact.

Where Rubrik sits among security companies

  • Alarm companies try to keep attackers out and spot them inside. Examples: CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Microsoft Defender.
  • Rubrik assumes the alarm will sometimes fail. It makes sure you can get back up afterwards. The industry phrase is cyber resilience.
  • The two are partners. Rubrik feeds what it finds into the alarm companies' tools.

3One bad week: the whole range in a story

This story is made up, but each step is based on how real attacks and real recoveries work. Follow it once and you have seen every product do its job.

Meet Harlow & Finch, a UK clothing retailer with 300 shops. They bought Rubrik last year.

  1. Months before: quiet copying. Every few hours, Rubrik takes a sealed copy of the tills system, the warehouse system, email and the login system. Product: Data Protection.
  2. Monday: the phone call. A criminal rings the IT help desk pretending to be a manager who is locked out. The help desk resets the password. The criminal is in. Rubrik does not stop this.
  3. Tuesday: looking around. The intruder copies the company's password list and quietly creates two hidden administrator accounts for later.
  4. Wednesday: going for the backups. The intruder finds the backup system and tries to delete every copy. It fails. The copies cannot be edited, and deleting needs two named people to approve. Product: the locks.
  5. Thursday 2am: the scramble. Ransomware locks the servers and the login system. Shops cannot take card payments.
  6. Thursday 2:20am: the alarm. Rubrik's next copy looks wrong. 400,000 files changed overnight and they look random. It raises an alert. Product: Anomaly Detection.
  7. Thursday 6am: getting in. Staff cannot log in to anything, because the login system is locked. But Rubrik's control screen has its own separate sign-in, so the recovery team can start work.
  8. Thursday morning: which copy is clean? The intruder has been inside since Monday. Rubrik searches every copy for the attacker's files and marks the last clean one. Product: Threat Hunting.
  9. Thursday afternoon: logins first. Nothing else can come back until staff can sign in. Rubrik rebuilds the login system on a brand-new machine, leaving the intruder's hidden tools behind. Product: Identity Recovery.
  10. Friday: tills back in minutes. Rubrik starts the tills system running straight from the backup copy, then restores the rest in a planned order. Products: Live Mount and recovery plans.
  11. Saturday: what did they see? The company must tell its regulator what was exposed. Rubrik's map shows which stolen folders held customer card details. Product: sensitive-data mapping.
  12. Three months later: AI assistants. Harlow & Finch gives its software team AI coding assistants. It adds Rubrik's newest product to watch them and undo their mistakes. Product: Agent Cloud.
What the story shows

No ransom was paid. The shops were shut for about a day and a half, not a month. And every product had one clear job at one clear moment.

4The products that protect data

This is the foundation. Most customers start here, and every other product depends on it.

4.1 Backup and Recovery

In plain English

Regular spare copies of everything, with a way to put them back. Rubrik's name for the family is "Data Protection".

The problem

Systems break, people delete things, and attackers scramble files. Without a recent copy, the data is gone.

How it works

  • The customer writes one simple rule. For example: "copy the tills system every four hours, keep copies for a year, and hold a second copy in another country".
  • Rubrik calls this rule an SLA Domain. Think of it as a standing order. You say what you want, and the software works out how.
  • Only changes are copied. After the first full copy, each run collects just what is new. This keeps it quick.
  • Each copy is one moment in time. The industry word is snapshot. You can go back to any of them.

Real-life example

Northumbria Healthcare NHS Trust, a real Rubrik customer, says it can now recover a system in about five minutes. For a hospital, that is the difference between a delay and a cancelled clinic.

Say it in one sentence

"It takes regular copies of a company's systems, based on one simple rule the customer sets, so anything can be put back to how it was."

4.2 The locks on the vault

In plain English

Four protections that stop anyone, including a thief with a stolen password, from destroying the copies.

The problem

Ordinary backups are just files on the network. Anyone with an administrator's password can delete them. That is step 3 of the attack.

How it works

Fancy nameWhat it really meansEveryday comparison
Immutable storageCopies are written once and can never be edited afterwards.Ink, not pencil.
Logical air gapCopies cannot be reached through the normal file-sharing routes on the network.A safe with no door on the street side.
Retention LockOnce you say "keep for a year", nobody can shorten it.A time-lock on a bank vault.
Quorum AuthorizationTwo named people must both approve before copies are deleted.Two keys turned at once.

Real-life example

A made-up but typical one. An attacker steals the IT manager's login and types the command to delete all backups. With ordinary backups, they are gone in seconds.

With these locks, the command fails. The copies cannot be edited, the keep-until date cannot be moved, and a second person would have to approve.

Say it in one sentence

"Even if an attacker steals an administrator's password, they still cannot change or delete the backups."

4.3 Live Mount

In plain English

Running a system straight from its backup copy, without waiting to copy it all back first.

The problem

Copying a large system back can take hours or days. The business is shut the whole time.

How it works

  • Rubrik switches the system on where the copy already sits, inside Rubrik's storage.
  • Staff start working on it within minutes.
  • In the background, the data is moved back to its proper home. Nobody notices the switch.

Real-life example

It is the difference between streaming a film and downloading it. With streaming, you press play now. With downloading, you wait for the whole file first.

Say it in one sentence

"Instead of waiting hours to copy a system back, Rubrik runs it directly from the backup so people are working again in minutes."

4.5 Rubrik Cloud Vault

In plain English

An extra copy kept in an account that Rubrik owns and runs, with completely separate logins.

The problem: if the customer controls every copy, an attacker who takes over the customer's accounts can reach every copy.

How it works: Rubrik holds one more copy in its own account at a cloud provider. The customer's passwords do not open it, so stealing them does not help.

Think of it as a safety deposit box at a different bank. A burglar with your house keys still cannot get to it.

Say it in one sentence

"It's a last-resort copy held by Rubrik, out of reach of anyone who has stolen the customer's own passwords."

5The products that find and fix an attack

Having copies is not enough. After an attack, you need to know that you were hit, which copy is safe, and in what order to rebuild. Rubrik's name for this family is "Cyber Recovery" or "Threat Analytics".

5.1 Anomaly Detection

In plain English

A smoke alarm inside the vault. It notices when a new copy looks very different from the last one.

The problem

Attackers often switch off the security software on a machine before they scramble it. So the usual alarm stays silent.

How it works

  • After each backup, Rubrik makes a short summary of what changed. Only the summary is analysed, not the customer's actual files.
  • Check one: is this normal for this system? It compares the number of files added, deleted or changed with the usual pattern.
  • Check two: do the files suddenly look random? Scrambled files look like noise. The technical word for that randomness is entropy.
  • If both checks fire, it raises an alert and marks the copies taken before the change.

Real-life example

A made-up one. On a normal night, 2,000 files change on the warehouse system. Tonight, 400,000 changed and they all look like random noise. That is not a busy night. That is ransomware.

Why it is clever: the check happens on the backup, away from the attacked machine. Switching off the machine's security software does not switch this off.

An honest limit: a rival argues that very slow, partial scrambling could stay under the alarm level.

Say it in one sentence

"It compares each new backup with the last one and raises the alarm if files have suddenly been scrambled."

5.2 Threat Hunting and Turbo Threat Hunting

In plain English

Searching every backup for the attacker's files, to find the last copy from before they arrived.

The problem

Attackers are usually inside for days or weeks before they strike. So recent backups contain the attacker's tools. Restore one of those and you bring the attacker back.

How it works

  • Every file has a fingerprint: a short code worked out from its contents. Same file, same fingerprint. The technical word is hash.
  • Security experts publish the fingerprints of known malicious files.
  • The slow way: open each backup in turn and scan it. With thousands of backups, that takes days.
  • Rubrik's way: it records every file's fingerprint when it takes the copy. Searching is then like using the index of a book, not reading every page.
  • Infected copies are quarantined, meaning fenced off, so nobody restores one by mistake.

Real-life example

Rubrik's own test: 75,000 backups checked in under a minute. This figure is Rubrik's claim, not an independent test.

Think of police who know a burglar's fingerprint. They do not search every house in the country. They look it up in the records.

Say it in one sentence

"It finds the last backup from before the attacker got in, quickly, so you don't restore the attacker along with your data."

In plain English

A rehearsed plan for bringing systems back in the order the business needs them.

The problem

A company has hundreds of systems that depend on each other. Restore them in the wrong order and nothing works. And in a crisis, nobody remembers the right order.

5.4 Sensitive-data mapping (DSPM)

In plain English

A map showing where the valuable information is kept, and who has a key to it. The full name is Data Security Posture Management.

The problem

Big companies lose track of their own data. Customer card numbers end up in a spreadsheet on a shared drive that 4,000 people can open.

How it works

  • It scans stored data and labels what is sensitive: card numbers, health records, passport details.
  • It lists which people and apps can open each item.
  • It flags anything too widely shared.

Real-life example

Before an attack: a company switches on an AI helper such as Microsoft Copilot. The helper can read anything its user can read. If old salary files are open to everyone, the helper will happily quote them. The map finds those files first.

After an attack: the regulator asks "what personal data was exposed?" The map answers in hours.

Say it in one sentence

"It shows a company where its sensitive data is and who can get to it, which matters before turning on AI tools and after a break-in."

9Deep dive: the product in your application answer

The answer I recommended was built on Identity-First Recovery. This section teaches it properly, so you can explain every word of what you wrote.

If you submitted a different product, tell me and I will write the same deep dive for that one.

Step 1: what Active Directory actually is

Active Directory is Microsoft's login system for organisations. It has been around since 2000 and most large companies still depend on it.

It does two jobs:

  • It checks who you are. When you type your password at work in the morning, Active Directory decides whether to let you in.
  • It decides what you may open. Finance staff can open the payroll folder. You cannot.

Think of it as the staff directory and the key cabinet in one. Every person, every computer and every permission is listed in it.

Step 2: the four words in your answer

WordWhat it meansEveryday comparison
Domain controllerA server that runs Active Directory. A company has several, and they keep each other up to date.A security desk. Big buildings have one at each entrance.
NTDS.ditThe single database file holding every account and a scrambled version of every password.The master register behind the desk.
SYSVOLA folder of rules pushed out to every computer, such as "lock the screen after five minutes".The rule book handed to every guard.
ForestThe company's entire Active Directory: every domain controller and everything in it.All the security desks and registers together.

Remember this: NTDS.dit and SYSVOL together are the login system. Everything else on a domain controller is just Windows, the ordinary operating system.

Step 3: why attackers go for it

  • It holds every password. Steal NTDS.dit and you can work on cracking the passwords of the whole company at your leisure.
  • It controls every door. Whoever controls Active Directory can give themselves access to anything.
  • Locking it stops everything. Scramble the domain controllers and no one can sign in.

Real example: reporting on the Marks & Spencer attack says the intruders first took the NTDS.dit file in February 2025, two months before the ransomware struck in April. That is the exact file your answer names.

Step 4: why getting it back is so hard

There are three separate traps. Your first question targets the first one.

Trap one: the locked-out rescuers

Many backup tools check passwords against Active Directory. So when Active Directory is down, the people who should restore it cannot sign in to the tool that restores it.

That is why your question works. "What would your backup admins log in with?" has a factual answer, and most teams have never checked.

Trap two: the infected machine

The old way is to restore the whole domain controller from a backup: Windows and all. But attackers hide tools inside Windows. Restore the whole machine and you restore their way back in.

Trap three: the copies that talk to each other

Domain controllers constantly update each other. If you restore one clean controller while infected ones are still running, the infected ones "update" the clean one with the attacker's changes.

Microsoft's own guide for doing this by hand runs to 29 steps and more than 100 pages, according to Rubrik.

Step 5: how Identity-First Recovery solves it

  1. Sign in to Rubrik. Its control screen is hosted by Rubrik, outside the company, with its own accounts. That escapes trap one.
  2. Pick a clean copy. Rubrik has already checked the login-server backups for the attacker's known files.
  3. Build a fresh Windows server. Brand new, never touched by the attacker.
  4. Lift out only the login data. Rubrik takes NTDS.dit and SYSVOL from the backup and places them on the new server. The old Windows stays behind. That escapes trap two.
  5. Forget the old servers. By default, Rubrik removes the records of all the other domain controllers, so the new one does not take "updates" from infected ones. That escapes trap three.
  6. Grow it back. More clean domain controllers are added, and they copy from the clean one.
The whole idea in one line

Take the register, leave the building. The valuable part is the list of accounts. The dangerous part is the machine it sat on. Identity-First Recovery separates the two.

Step 6: "clean or current?" and Roll Forward

Your answer ends with a second question. Here is what it means.

  • Clean means going back to a copy from before the attacker arrived. Safe, but you lose every change since: new starters, leavers, password resets.
  • Current means keeping today's version. Up to date, but the attacker's hidden accounts are still in it.

Identity Roll Forward tries to give both. It starts from the clean copy, then replays the genuine changes and leaves out the attacker's.

"Why not just restore the whole server?"

"Because attackers hide tools in the operating system. Restore the whole server and you restore their way back in."

"What is NTDS.dit?"

"It's the database file that holds every account and password in Active Directory. Reports say it's the file the attackers took in the M&S case."

"Why the Head of Infrastructure, not the security chief?"

"Because they run both the login servers and the backups, so the gap between the two sits on their desk. They can go and check the answer the same day."

"What if they said: we have a separate emergency account?"

"Good. Then I'd ask when they last tested a full rebuild of Active Directory from scratch, and how long it took."

"What about companies that don't use Active Directory?"

"Rubrik also covers the two main online login systems, Microsoft Entra ID and Okta. The idea is the same: back it up and be able to put it back."

"Who else does this?"

"Specialists such as Semperis and Quest, and Rubrik's backup rivals Commvault and Cohesity are adding it. Rubrik's argument is that it does data and logins in one place."

"Is Roll Forward available now?"

"It was announced in June 2026 as a private preview, so I'd want to check its current status before telling a customer they can buy it."

"How fast is it?"

"Rubrik says hours instead of days or weeks. On an investor call, the chief executive described a customer who needed recovery in hours where their old supplier took seven days. I'd treat those as Rubrik's claims."

Two limits worth knowing

Same version needed. The new server must run the same major version of Windows Server as the old one.

Rubrik's own claims. Most of the detail here comes from Rubrik's blogs and papers. It has not been independently tested. Saying so shows judgement.

10Talking about products in the interview

The three levels

For any product, be ready at three depths. Start at level one and only go deeper if asked.

LevelWhat it isExample: Anomaly Detection
1. One lineWhat it does, as you would tell a friend."It's a smoke alarm for backups."
2. The problemWhy a customer needs it."Attackers switch off security software before they strike, so the usual alarm stays quiet."
3. How it worksThe mechanism, in two steps."It compares each backup with the last. A sudden wave of scrambled files sets it off."

Four habits that make you sound like you understand

  • Lead with the problem, not the product. "Attackers delete backups first, so..." beats "Rubrik has immutable storage".
  • Translate as you go. If you say "immutable", add "meaning it can't be changed".
  • Use one example. A real incident or a simple comparison. One, not three.
  • Say what you don't know. "I haven't checked whether that's on full sale yet" is a strong answer.

If she asks "what does Rubrik sell?"

A 30-second answer

"Rubrik sells software that gets a company back on its feet after an attack. It keeps protected copies of their systems that attackers can't delete, works out which copy is clean, and rebuilds from it."

"It does that for three things: data, the login system, and now AI assistants, where it can undo their mistakes."

If she asks "which product interests you most?"

Pick the one you can explain best. For most people that will be the one from the application answer. Use the three levels.

What you do not need

This is a first conversation with a recruiter, and the advert says "we will teach the tech". You do not need to recite every product name. You need to show you understand what problem the company solves and can explain one product clearly.

Self-test

Cover the right-hand column. If you can answer in your own words, you understand it.

QuestionAnswer
1. Why do attackers delete backups before scrambling anything?So the victim cannot simply restore and ignore the ransom demand.
2. What stops a thief with an admin password deleting Rubrik's copies?Copies cannot be edited, the keep-until date is locked, and two people must approve a deletion.
3. Does Rubrik stop attackers getting in?No. It assumes they will, and focuses on getting back up.
4. How does Rubrik notice an attack from a backup?The new copy shows far more changed files than normal, and they look random.
5. Why not just restore the most recent backup?The attacker was inside for days, so recent copies contain their tools.
6. Why is Turbo Threat Hunting fast?File fingerprints are recorded at backup time, so searching is a lookup, not a scan.
7. Does Microsoft back up a company's Microsoft 365 data?Not in the way people assume. It keeps the service running. Protecting the content is the customer's job.
8. Why must the login system be restored first?Nobody can sign in to anything else until it works.
9. What does Identity-First Recovery move, and what does it leave?It moves the login data (NTDS.dit and SYSVOL). It leaves the old Windows, where attackers hide.
10. What is "clean or current"?Go back to before the attack and lose changes, or keep changes and risk keeping the attacker.
11. What are the four jobs of Agent Cloud?Watch, permit, stop, undo.
12. What is the one idea behind every product?Something valuable breaks, and Rubrik puts it back how it was.
