# The Aon scales numerical playbook

Everything here comes from Phase 1 research (`research/FINDINGS.md`), which cites its sources and rates confidence. This is preparation only — it teaches the *skills*, never how to cheat a live test.

---

## What you are actually being tested on

Aon **scales numerical** shows you ~6 data displays (charts/tables) as **tabs**, then a series of statements you judge **True / False / Cannot Say** against that data. Employers use it to measure **numerical reasoning + decision-making under time pressure** — can you pull the right figure from the right display, do a quick correct calculation, and know when you *can't* answer. Difficulty scales with role seniority. *(Sources: JobTestPrep, MConsultingPrep.)*

**Forms:** the real test is **37 tasks in 12 minutes** (long) or **18 tasks in 6 minutes** (short) — about **20 seconds per item**. Your remembered "18 in 12 minutes" is gentler than reality; train at 20s/item. *(JobTestPrep, AssessmentDay, Practice Aptitude Tests — High confidence.)*

---

## How it is scored — and what that means tactically

- **Negative marking.** Correct **+1**, wrong **−1**, blank **0**. *(psychometrictests.org, fintest.io, psychometric-success, MConsultingPrep — High confidence.)*
- **Therefore: blind guessing is negative-EV.** A random 1-in-3 guess averages a loss. **Skip beats guess** unless you can eliminate at least one option (then a narrowed guess is roughly break-even). Leave truly blind items blank.
- **You are not expected to finish.** The test is deliberately over-length. A strong result is a high *correct* count with few penalties, **not** reaching the last item. **Accuracy dominates coverage.** *(psychometric-success, JobTestPrep, AssessmentDay — High.)*
- **Norm-referenced.** No fixed pass mark; your score becomes a **percentile + Stanine (1–9)** against a comparison group, and each employer sets its own cut-off. *(Practice Aptitude Tests, psychometric-success — High. The app's Stanine/percentile is a clearly-labelled synthetic stand-in, since Aon's real norms are confidential.)*
- **Wrong-tab quirk.** Submitting while the **wrong data tab is displayed reportedly costs points even if your answer is right.** Confirm the correct tab is on screen before you submit. *(JobTestPrep, GraduatesFirst, MConsultingPrep — Medium-High.)*
- **Not classic-adaptive.** Standard scales numerical is a randomized fixed-form (you can navigate back and change answers), not an item-by-item adaptive test. *(fintest.io, GraduatesFirst — Medium-High.)*

**The one-line strategy:** *Maximise correct answers, skip rather than guess, watch the units and the tab, and never over-infer.*

## The decision rule — True vs False vs Cannot Say

From Aon's own worked examples *(aon.com practice PDF — High confidence)*:

- **TRUE** — every figure you need is shown, and the data **confirms** the statement, exactly.
- **FALSE** — every figure you need is shown, and the data **contradicts** it. *"The figures are close, but the statement was not asking for an approximation"* → close-but-not-exact is **False**.
- **CANNOT SAY** — a figure the statement needs is **not shown on any tab** (a missing period, a missing category, or the wrong *type* of quantity). You can neither confirm nor deny it.

> Operational test: *Can I prove this 100% true or 100% false from what's on the tabs?* If a single required number is missing → **Cannot Say**. If all are present but disagree → **False**.

**Over-inference to True/False when the honest answer is Cannot Say is the single biggest score-killer.** Never use outside knowledge; answer only from the displays.

## The traps (with the fix for each)

1. **Units — thousands vs millions.** Read the axis/footnote unit *first*. A table "in $ thousand" showing 7,256 means **$7.26m**, not $7,256. Convert into the statement's units before comparing. *(Aon PDF, AssessmentDay Tip 9.)*
2. **Close but not exact = False.** If the statement doesn't say "about/approximately", any mismatch is False, however small. *(Aon PDF Ex.4.)*
3. **Missing period/row/year = Cannot Say.** If the statement names FY3 and the tab only shows FY4–FY8, it's Cannot Say — don't estimate. *(Aon PDF Ex.3, Ex.5.)*
4. **Percent vs percentage points.** A margin going 10% → 15% is **+5 percentage points** but **+50% relative**. Decide which the statement means: "points" → subtract the rates; "%" → divide the change by the **original**. Never subtract two percentages and call it a % change. *(Practice Aptitude Tests.)*
5. **Data-type mismatch = Cannot Say.** "Units sold" when the tab shows "revenue" (or share) — a revenue figure can't answer a units question. *(JobTestPrep worked sample.)*
6. **Combine two displays.** Some items need a figure from tab A and a figure from tab B (e.g. share % × total, or revenue − costs). Identify every figure first, and mind that the two tabs may use different units. *(Aon PDF Ex.2.)*
7. **Cumulative / YTD vs per-period.** A "Total" or year-to-date column is not a single period. Match the statement's granularity (month/quarter/year) to the column. *(Practice Aptitude Tests.)*
8. **Index / base-100.** Index points aren't absolute quantities. 100 → 130 is +30% (because base 100), but "was 130 units" is Cannot Say. Check the axis for "index/base 100/rebased". *(AssessmentDay Tip 7.)*
9. **Total-row distractor.** A bold Total sits next to line items — read the exact row label you were asked about. *(Aon PDF.)*
10. **Compound % isn't additive.** +12% then −8% is not +4%; chain ×1.12 ×0.92 = −0.96%. *(testsolve.ai.)*
11. **Biggest absolute ≠ biggest %.** The largest raw move often comes off the largest base — divide each change by its own base before ranking. *(AssessmentDay.)*

## Time budgeting (~20s/item)

- **Orient briefly:** in the first few seconds, note which tab holds what. Don't deep-read all six.
- **Per item:** read the statement → identify the exact figure(s) and tab(s) → open only what you need → verify → **confirm the right tab is showing** → answer.
- **Skip rule:** if you haven't cracked it by ~30s (1.5× budget), leave it (blank, no penalty) and move on. Revisit if time allows.
- **Guess rule:** only if you can eliminate an option; otherwise blank.
- **Calculator:** scales numerical provides an on-screen one, and pen & paper are allowed. But **compare before you compute** — many True/False items only need "is A bigger than B?", not an exact quotient.

---

## The per-item mental checklist

1. What is the statement literally claiming? (exact entity, period, quantity type)
2. Which tab(s) hold the figure(s)? Is the quantity type even shown?
3. Is every needed figure present? If not → **Cannot Say**.
4. What are the **units**? Convert to match the statement.
5. Points or percent? Cumulative or per-period? Index or absolute?
6. Compute (or just compare) — exactly, no rounding unless asked.
7. **Right tab showing?** Then answer. Don't over-infer.

---

## What "getting better" looks like

Track it on the Progress screen: your **accuracy on attempted items** should rise while your **wrong-tab** and **missed-Cannot-Say** counts fall. Coverage (how many you reach) matters less than staying accurate. Use **Adaptive drill** to over-serve whatever category you're weakest in.
