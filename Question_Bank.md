How to use this file: these are scaffolds, not scripts. Evidence base: Millennium's own student page and job postings, regulator documents, trade press, and anecdotes from candidate forums (labelled). ## What Round 1 almost certainly is

- Millennium describes its process as Screening, then Assessments "to match with hiring teams", then "technical and behavioral interviews" [S261]. For a non-investing seat, "technical" means: can you explain what corporate access is, why a PM wants a management meeting, how you would run a request list in Excel, and why compliance cares.
- Thirty minutes with two desk-level staff (both appear to be Corporate Access Associates on the London desk, medium confidence [S54][S68]) breaks down roughly as: 2 to 3 minutes introductions; 5 minutes CV, why us, why this role; 12 to 15 minutes of three to five behavioural or role-understanding questions; 5 minutes for your questions; buffer.
- Anecdotes for other Millennium non-investing intern seats cluster at two to three conversations after the assessment, mostly behavioural, sometimes with a question on market conditions [S262][S263]. Expect one, possibly two, further rounds. Ask about next steps at the end.

Use STAR-R for behavioural answers: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection. Keep Situation to one sentence.

## A. Motivation

F: You fit the Dec 2027 to Jun 2028 graduation window; "Humanities and Social Sciences are a plus" [S260] if that is you. X: Chronological life story. Anything over 90 seconds.

**2. Why Millennium?**
T: Have you done more than read the homepage. S: Three specific reasons, then one personal one. (i) The platform model: hundreds of independent PM teams, so corporate access is a service business inside the firm with internal clients [S260][S42]. (ii) The team is structured: two channels (broker-sourced and originated), three regional desks, a proprietary portal, a semi-annual broker vote [S40][S42][S43]. (iii) Stated intent to hire the analyst class from interns, and London is the largest hub outside the US [S261][S271b]. (iv) Your own reason. X: Quoting AUM with false precision (private firm; press estimates vary). Saying "prestige". Confusing Millennium with other firms of the same name.

**3. Why corporate access, and not an investing seat?**
T: Is this a fallback. S: Say what you like about the work itself: organising scarce access, broker relationships, data and reporting, being close to the investment process without being the stock-picker. F: The team exists "to increase the quantity and quality of their industry meetings" [S260]. The desk exists because since 2014 UK rules stopped access being paid for through trading commission, so it must be sourced and allocated deliberately [S2]. X: Implying you plan to move to the investing side; the desk hears "you'll leave".

**4. Why a hedge fund rather than an investment bank or a long-only manager?**
T: Buy side versus sell side. S: On the sell side corporate access is a product sold to clients. On the buy side it is procured and allocated for internal clients; you are the client side of the broker relationship ("Build and maintain relationships with brokers" [S260]). At a multi-manager, many teams compete for the same meeting, so allocation and process matter more than at a single-PM fund. X: Disparaging banks. Your interviewers may have come from them.

**5. Why this internship, and what do you want from it?**
T: Realism. S: Three things from the JD you want to learn (portal, allocation, consumption reporting) and one thing you bring (Excel, a European language). F: Programme includes financial markets training and desk project work [S261]. X: "Exposure to PMs" as the main draw.

**6. How did you hear about the role / what else have you applied for?**
T: Honesty. S: Be straight. Note the two-application cap [S261]. X: Claiming this is your only application if it is not.

**7. What do you know about what our team does day to day?**
T: Preparation. S: Paraphrase the JD's day-to-day line (databases, email, communication channels, coordinating meetings, reports, efficiencies), then the two channels [S260]. X: Describing sell-side roadshow logistics as if that were the buy-side job.

**8. Where do you see yourself in five years?**
T: Fit with the conversion goal. S: Full-time on the platform side, growing into owning a set of broker relationships or the reporting and portal side; open to where the firm needs you. F: "To hire our analyst class from our internship program" [S261]. X: "Portfolio manager."

## B. Commercial awareness

**9. What is happening in markets right now that would matter to our PMs' meeting requests?**
T: Are you awake. S: One live EMEA theme you can talk about for 45 seconds, linked to demand for meetings (results season, sector rotation, a shock). F: Ops and FX intern anecdotes mention "questions about global market conditions" [S263]. See the guide's Part 6 for the current backdrop. X: Bluffing numbers. Say "as of this week" and keep it qualitative.

**10. What is a multi-manager or platform hedge fund, in your own words?**
T: Basic model. S: Many independent teams under central risk limits and central shared services; the centre provides resources so teams can focus on ideas; costs passed through to investors. X: Reciting risk limits you cannot source.

**11. Why do buy-side firms have their own corporate access teams now?**
T: The 2014 and 2018 shift. S: FCA 2014 said corporate access is not research and cannot be bought with dealing commission [S2]; MiFID II 2018 made it a "discrete service" across the EU [S13]; companies prefer direct contact [S4]; brokers withdrew from small and mid caps [S34]. So firms source and pay for access deliberately, centrally. X: "MiFID II banned corporate access." It did not. It changed how it is paid for.

**12. What do brokers get out of giving us meetings?**
T: Incentives. S: Trading flow and goodwill; our consumption data feeds their broker vote, which shapes how much business and value they get [S39][S43]. X: Implying meetings are bought per slot.

**13. What could reduce the value of corporate access over the next few years?**
T: Critical thinking. S: Virtual meetings lowering scarcity; direct company-to-investor platforms; AI note-taking commoditising the "capture" stage; tighter inducement rules; fewer listed companies. X: Answering only "AI".

**14. Who are Millennium's peers and how do they organise corporate access?**
T: Breadth. S: Citadel, Point72, Balyasny, ExodusPoint, Schonfeld. Point72 runs a corporate access intern programme with "Compliance-approved" brokers and events [S274]; Balyasny's head owns both broker relations and the commission wallet [S65]. X: Peers' AUM figures you cannot source.

**15. Why is London important to Millennium?**
T: Local awareness. F: 57 of 239 open roles in January 2025 [S271b]; Co-Head of Global Equities relocated to London in late 2025 [S62]; 50 Berkeley Street pre-let as European HQ [S63]; the JD remit is "primarily across EMEA" [S260]. X: Asserting an address; public sources show two.

## D. Role understanding

**28. What is corporate access?**
T: Baseline. S: FCA definition: "arranging or bringing about contact between an investment manager and an issuer" [S2]. Formats: 1x1, group, NDR, conference, site visit, virtual. Two channels at Millennium [S260]. X: Calling it investor relations; that is the company's side of the table.

**29. What does a PM or analyst want from a management meeting?**
T: Understanding the client. S: Answers the filings do not give; management's tone and consistency; whether the thesis is tracking; colour on competitors and the cycle. Never inside information. X: Implying they want an information edge.

**30. How would you allocate 1x1 slots at a conference when demand exceeds supply?**
T: Process judgement. S: (i) collect ranked requests with reasons; (ii) apply a rule: relevance to the book, who has not met the company recently, whether a group format works; (iii) publish the rule; (iv) escalate real ties to the desk head; (v) record the outcome for the consumption report. F: Sell-side allocators use "investor tier, AUM, and fund mandate fit" [S275], so the firm's request list must look coherent to the broker. Booking windows open four to six weeks out [S30]. X: "First come first served" alone.

**31. Two PMs want the same CFO slot and both say it is critical.**
T: Stakeholder handling. S: Ask the broker for a second slot or a group meeting; check who has the larger or more time-sensitive need; loop in the desk head; never promise both. X: Choosing by who shouted loudest.

**32. What is MNPI and why does a corporate access team care?**
T: Compliance basics. S: Material non-public information: not yet public, and a reasonable investor would consider it important. Trading on it is market abuse under UK MAR. The desk puts investors and executives in a room, so it routes meetings through approved channels, logs them, and knows the escalation path. The FCA has said analysts are "not the public" and that private issuer-shareholder calls count as dissemination [S21][S22]. X: "Interns would never encounter this."

**33. An executive says something in a meeting that sounds material and non-public.**
T: Instinct. S: The attendee stops, reports to Compliance immediately, does not trade or share; your role is to know the path, not to filter the decision. F: The Einhorn and Greenlight case: refusing to be wall-crossed was no defence [S19]. X: "I'd ask the PM what to do."

**34. What is a consumption report and why would a strategy session use it?**
T: Reading the JD. S: A record of which meetings each team used, by broker, sector and format; used for coverage gaps, broker value (feeds the vote), and next quarter's conference plan [S260][S43]. X: Not having read this line.

**35. What is a broker vote?**
T: Vocabulary. S: Periodic scoring of each broker's services by the buy side; corporate access is a large component; Millennium's is semi-annual [S43][S39]. X: Over-claiming how Millennium runs it.

**36. Broker-sourced versus Millennium-originated access: why both?**
T: Reading the JD. S: Broker channel gives volume and conferences; origination gives control, reach into names brokers do not bring, and independence. Origination is run by a sectorised Sector Specialist Origination team [S42][S47]. X: Implying originated access is free; it costs relationships and time.

**37. What would you put in a weekly forward-calendar email to analysts?**
T: Practical thinking. S: Conferences by date and sector, request deadlines, NDRs in London, "new this week", who to contact. Scannable. F: "Forward-looking event marketing" [S260]. X: No deadlines.

**38. What is an NDR?**
T: Vocabulary. S: Non-deal roadshow: management meets investors without raising capital; usually broker-hosted; about 65% in a large US sample [S38]. X: Confusing with an IPO roadshow.

**39. How would you measure whether our team is doing a good job?**
T: Outcome thinking. S: Meetings per team and quality feedback; hit rate on requests; time from request to confirmation; error rate; PM satisfaction. Millennium's own senior posting names "access quality, utilization, costs, and team feedback" [S25]. X: Volume only.

## Reading the interviewer type in the first three minutes

Both named interviewers appear to be Associates on the London Corporate Access desk (medium confidence). Do not assume; listen.

Practitioner signals: introduces themselves by desk; asks how you would do something; uses vocabulary (1x1, NDR, broker, conference season, portal); follows up with "and then what?". Adapt: be concrete and operational; use their vocabulary once you have heard it; admit gaps; ask questions 1 to 6; keep answers short; show respect for brokers and for admin precision, because they own those relationships and that error rate.

Talent Acquisition signals: introduces the process; same competency questions in the same order; notes on a template; asks about eligibility and dates. Adapt: full STAR-R with signposting; map answers to the JD's exact competencies; use the firm's published values vocabulary sparingly and accurately; ask questions 7 to 10.

Two-interviewer dynamics: one leads, one observes. Address the asker, bring the second in with eye contact at the end of each answer. If they say "ten minutes left", cut answers to 45 seconds and keep two questions for them.

Webex: test it the day before (the browser version works if the app fails); camera at eye level; one page of notes off-camera; say their names on hand-over; if audio drops, say so calmly.
