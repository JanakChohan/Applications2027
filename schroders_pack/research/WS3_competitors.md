# WS3 - Competitive landscape: Schroders vs peers

Prepared 2026-09-29 for the Schroders Client Group Internship Programme 2027 (London, Sales). Source IDs S120-S149.

> **Read this first: the landscape changed this year.** Nuveen (owned by TIAA) announced a recommended £9.9bn cash offer for Schroders on 12 Feb 2026 [S121][S125]. Shareholders approved it in April with more than 99% in favour [S120]. The Court sanctioned the scheme **today, 29 Sep 2026**. The last day of dealings in Schroders shares is **30 Sep 2026**, the scheme is expected to take effect on **1 Oct 2026**, and the shares will be de-listed by 8am on **2 Oct 2026** [S122]. As a result:
> - **Several classic Schroders talking points stop being true from this week.** These are "family-controlled since 1804", "FTSE 100 constituent" and "UK's largest listed standalone manager".
> - **Some things carry on.** The Schroders brand is kept and London becomes the combined group's non-US headquarters and largest office [S121]. Richard Oldfield stays CEO and will report to Nuveen CEO William Huffman [S126].
> - **The interview framing should change to match.** Lead with "a 222-year-old London franchise that is now the international hub of a ~$2.5tn active, public-to-private manager".
>
> Two peers also changed ownership this year. Janus Henderson was taken private by Trian, General Catalyst and QIA (completed 30 Jun 2026) [S138]. Jupiter completed its acquisition of CCLA in Feb 2026 [S134].

### 1c. Design-choice spectra: where Schroders sits

1. **Active vs passive.** Schroders is fully active, pitched through its "Active Edge" campaign [S121]. Its closest thing to passive-style economics is "Core solutions" at 6bp (£112.1bn) [S120]. It has launched European active ETFs, which reached $3.5bn in under 12 months and a top-10 UCITS active ETF position [S120]. BlackRock and L&G AM sit at the passive end, with Amundi between the two (€624bn ETF & index) [S130][S133][S136]. Jupiter, Janus Henderson and Ninety One are fully active.
2. **Public vs private markets mix.** Schroders Capital is £74.1bn, about 9% of group AUM, at a 57bp margin [S120]. That is smaller than M&G (£83bn) and L&G (£79bn) [S131][S133]. After the combination, the group will have $414bn of private markets assets [S125]. That scale is the core strategic rationale ("public-to-private platform") [S126].
3. **Asset manager vs wealth manager integration.** Schroders runs both under one roof: a Wealth Management segment of £129.8bn, including Cazenove Capital. It is selling Benchmark, its UK advice platform, to focus on Cazenove Capital in the UK and Schroders Wealth Management internationally [S120]. M&G and L&G pair asset management with an insurance balance sheet. aberdeen pairs it with platforms. SJP and Rathbones are wealth-led.
4. **Family-controlled vs widely held.** Until this week Schroders was family-controlled (~42%) [S123]. It now moves to ownership by a US pension-insurer (TIAA via Nuveen) [S124]. Fidelity International remains family-controlled and private. Janus Henderson is now PE/activist-owned. The rest are widely held or parent-owned.
5. **Global vs UK-centric.** Schroders is global: "40+ markets" [S123][S126], Bank of Communications JVs in China [S120], and a US$1.1tn dollar-equivalent AUM [S126]. Jupiter is UK-centric and growing in Europe. aberdeen and SJP/Rathbones are UK-centric.
6. **Integrated client group vs channel-based sales.** Schroders has one "Client Group" under a Global Head who reports to the CEO, with regional heads [S129]. The Global Head's previous role covered "product strategy, marketing, and sales" [S129 search snippet], which matches how the internship describes the group. M&G, Jupiter and Amundi report and organise by channel. Janus Henderson uses a "Global Distribution" function. aberdeen organises by business line.

### 1d. Things that can be said about Schroders and no listed rival (with evidence)

1. **Heritage.** Founded 1804 [S128]. Founding-family control of ~42% lasted until 2026, described as "ending 200 years of family control" and a "222-year reign" [S123][S125]. No other peer here pairs a founding date this old with continuous family control. *Caveat:* this ends on 1 Oct 2026.
2. **One brand spans institutional asset management and a UK private bank (Cazenove Capital).** Cazenove & other Wealth holds £90.1bn, with 5% annualised NNB in H1 26 [S120]. Among the main peers, M&G, L&G, Jupiter, Janus Henderson, BlackRock and Amundi lack a discretionary private-client bank. aberdeen's wealth offer is platforms, not a private bank. Rathbones and SJP are wealth-led, not global institutional managers.
3. **The international HQ of a ~$2.5tn active, public-to-private manager owned by TIAA.** London is to be the "non-US headquarters and largest office" and the Schroders brand is retained [S121]. No UK peer has this position.
4. **The largest London-listed independent asset/wealth manager by AUM, up to its delisting.** £867.8bn vs aberdeen £579.4bn AUMA and M&G £387bn [S120][S131][S132]. This is my inference from the reported figures. L&G AM is bigger but sits inside an insurer. *Caveat:* it lapses on 2 Oct 2026.
5. **Transparent margin disclosure for each capability and a turnaround on record.** It reports margins for six business lines [S120]. The adjusted cost:income ratio has fallen from 75% (FY24) to 68% (H1 26), below the 70% target within 18 months [S120][S121].
6. **Owns an organisational unit literally called the "Client Group", which integrates product, marketing and sales globally under one CEO report** [S129]. This is the internship's own framing; peers generally use "distribution".
7. **China JVs with Bank of Communications, in both fund management and wealth management**, which added +£4.1bn of flows in H1 26 [S120]. This is distinctive among UK peers; Amundi also has large Asian JVs.

### 1e. Where Schroders does NOT lead (state this plainly)

- **Organic flows.** H1 26 NNB was -£8.3bn ex-JVs [S120], against Amundi +€56.4bn [S130], BlackRock ~$321bn [S136], M&G +£2.2bn [S131], Jupiter +£0.7bn [S134] and aberdeen's ii +£6.8bn [S132]. Equities alone lost £8.7bn in H1 26 [S120].
- **Share price.** The shares were down about 25% over the five years before the bid [S125]. Value was realised through a sale, not a re-rating.
- **Scale.** Schroders is roughly a third of Amundi and under a tenth of BlackRock [S130][S136], and smaller than L&G AM's £1.2tn [S133]. Reuters notes the combined group still trails BlackRock, Vanguard and Amundi [S127].
- **Passive/ETF.** There is no index franchise at scale. Its active ETF range is $3.5bn [S120], compared with Amundi ETFs >€400bn [S130] and iShares $178bn of inflows in one quarter [S136].
- **Private markets.** Schroders Capital at £74.1bn is smaller than M&G (£83bn) or L&G (£79bn) [S120][S131][S133], and H1 26 NNB fell to £0.9bn from £2.3bn [S120].
- **Margin pressure.** The equities margin slipped from 46bp (FY24) to 44bp (FY25 exit), and multi-asset fell from 25bp to 23bp over the same period [S121]. Institutional fees across the industry are falling ~3% a year [S143].
- **Independence.** It chose to sell rather than consolidate. Jupiter (CCLA) and Ninety One (Sanlam assets) took the buy-side route [S134][S140].

### 1f. Industry context

- **Active vs passive.** In 2025 European funds took a record €696.3bn. Active equity funds lost €40.8bn (the fourth straight year of outflows), while passive equity took €227.4bn. Active bond funds took a record €310bn [S145]. Index funds' share of UK AUM reached 35% in 2024 [S146 snippet]. In the US, the top-10 passive providers captured more than 90% of net inflows [S143][S144].
- **Fee compression and margins.** Institutional fees are "declining by 3% annually". Industry revenues grew 5.1% a year between 2010 and 2025, while costs grew 5.4%. Profit margins stayed "close to 30%" [S143]. More than 80% of 2025 revenue growth came from market appreciation [S143]. Active ETFs are gaining share at fee levels below the vehicles they replace [S143 search snippet].
- **UK/European consolidation, 2024-26:**
  - Nuveen–Schroders: £9.9bn, announced Feb 2026, effective Oct 2026 [S121][S122].
  - Trian/General Catalyst/QIA take Janus Henderson private: $7.8bn, completed 30 Jun 2026 [S138].
  - Jupiter–CCLA: announced Jul 2025, completed Feb 2026 [S134].
  - Dai-ichi takes ~15% of M&G (May 2025) [S149].
  - Ninety One–Sanlam asset take-on: £18.3bn [S140].
  - Generali–Natixis JV (~€1.9tn) was planned, then fell through [S127].
  - Amundi–AllianzGI talks stalled over control [S127].
  - Analysts name Jupiter, Liontrust, GAM, Comgest and Berenberg as potential targets. Oliver Wyman expects ~1,500 deals over 4-5 years [S127].
- **The UK industry as a whole** managed a record £11.1tn in 2025. 53% of it is managed for overseas clients [S146].

## 6) Interview angles

1. **Open with the news.** For example: "I've been following the Nuveen combination. The scheme was sanctioned today, and it becomes effective on 1 October." Then ask how Client Group expects distribution to integrate with Nuveen's US wealth and institutional coverage, and whether Schroders becomes the international distribution hub.
2. **Why active, why now.** In 2025 European active bond funds took a record €310bn while active equity lost €40.8bn [S145]. Schroders' fixed income and multi-asset returned to inflows in H1 26 (+£1.9bn, +£2.6bn) while equities saw outflows [S120]. Argue that the sales story is moving towards outcome, income and multi-asset solutions and active ETFs ($3.5bn in under a year).
3. **Channel mix is margin mix.** Losing a 6bp core-solutions mandate (£6.6bn) hurts less than it looks, while intermediary gross inflows grew more than 40% [S120]. Show you understand that flows should be weighted by margin, not just counted.
4. **Distribution is the battleground.** Use BCG's line "Distribution now defines who wins" [S144]. Link it to why a Client Group that integrates product, marketing and sales matters, and to the Nuveen deal buying distribution reach.
5. **Wealth plus asset management as a differentiator.** Cazenove Capital has a 5% annualised NNB rate, the Benchmark sale sharpens the focus, and graduate intake is up [S120]. Ask how Client Group works with Cazenove, for example on private-markets access for wealth clients.
6. **Be honest about the weak spots.** These include five-year share price underperformance, H1 outflows, scale relative to Amundi and BlackRock, and the lack of passive. Then pivot to the transformation: cost:income down from 75% to 68%, with 72/71/69% of assets outperforming.
7. **Consolidation literacy.** Contrast Schroders (sold to scale) with Jupiter (bought CCLA), Janus Henderson (taken private) and M&G (strategic minority stake from Dai-ichi). Mention the "bulk up or sell out" framing [S127].
