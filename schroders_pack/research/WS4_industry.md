## 1) FINDINGS

### 4A. THE INDUSTRY

**What asset management is (plain English).** An asset manager invests other people's money for a fee, usually a percentage of assets under management (AUM), not a share of profits. It does not own the money: clients (pension schemes, insurers, individuals) own it, and a separate custodian/depositary holds the assets. The manager's job has two halves: (1) investment (fund managers, analysts, traders) and (2) the "Client Group"-type functions: product (designing funds and share classes), marketing, and sales/distribution plus client service. The internship sits in the second half.

**Who the clients are.**
- *Institutional*: defined benefit (DB) pension schemes (promise a pension; now mostly closed, de-risking and handing liabilities to insurers via buy-ins/buyouts); defined contribution (DC) schemes (master trusts, group personal pensions; members bear investment risk; growing via auto-enrolment); the Local Government Pension Scheme (LGPS, now six pools); insurers (life books, annuity portfolios, bulk annuity assets); sovereign wealth funds and central banks (reserves); endowments, foundations, charities.
- *Wholesale / intermediary*: independent financial advisers (IFAs), discretionary fund managers and wealth managers, private banks, platforms (Hargreaves Lansdown, AJ Bell, Transact etc.), model portfolio service (MPS) providers, fund-of-funds/multi-managers. Here the "buyer" is a professional fund selector acting for many end investors.
- *Direct retail*: individuals buying directly or through D2C platforms (ISA, SIPP).
- UK data: IA members manage £3.8trn of UK institutional client assets; pension funds are ~51% of UK institutional AUM (down from 56%) and insurers 28% [S150]. In 2024 retail became the largest client group (28% of total AUM) overtaking pensions (27%) for the first time [S150].

**How asset managers differ from neighbours.**
| Type | What it does | How it earns | Typical client |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset manager (e.g. Schroders, L&G, abrdn, BlackRock) | Runs pooled funds and segregated mandates in public (and increasingly private) markets | % of AUM (bps); sometimes performance fees | Institutions and, via intermediaries, retail |
| Wealth manager (e.g. Schroders Wealth/Cazenove Capital, SJP, Rathbones, Quilter) | Advises and manages whole portfolios for individuals/families, including financial planning | % of client assets plus advice/planning fees | Individuals, families, charities |
| Hedge fund | Absolute-return strategies using leverage, shorting, derivatives | Management fee plus performance fee (historically "2 and 20") | Institutions, UHNW, funds of funds |
| Private equity / private markets manager | Buys stakes in private companies, private credit, infrastructure, real estate via closed-end (or semi-liquid) funds | Management fee on committed capital plus carried interest | Pensions, insurers, SWFs, increasingly wealth |
| Investment bank | Advises companies (M&A, capital raising), trades securities (sell side), sells research | Advisory fees, trading spreads, underwriting | Corporates, governments, and asset managers themselves (buy side is the IB's client) |

Asset managers are the "buy side"; investment banks and brokers are the "sell side".

**UK industry size (IA Investment Management Survey 2024-25, data at end-2024, published Oct 2025) [S150, FETCHED].**
- Total AUM by IA members: record £10.0trn, +10% in 2024 (after +3% in 2023 and -12% in 2022).
- 51% managed for overseas clients (up from 40% a decade ago), surpassing UK clients for the first time; European clients £3.0trn (+20%), North American £990bn.
- UK-managed fund assets £4.9trn; 69% domiciled overseas; Ireland (35%) overtook the UK (31%) as largest domicile, driven by Irish ETFs.
- Institutional 71% / retail 28% of AUM. Equities 49% of AUM, fixed income 28%.
- Indexing 35% of AUM (33% in 2023); active "nearly two-thirds".
- LDI down to £860bn, over 40% below the 2019 peak of £1.5trn; DB pension transfers to insurers ~£45-50bn in 2024.
- Institutional exposure to private markets only 2.7% (1.7% in 2022). Cash ~21% of institutional assets.
- UK retail FUM £1.49trn; net retail sales ~-£2bn in 2024; record +£28bn into index trackers vs -£29bn from active funds; UK equity funds ~-£12.5bn; Cash ISA inflows record £50.6bn.
- Operating margin fell to a new low of 18% (31% in 2019): revenue +10% but costs +14%.
- 123,300 jobs supported, 44,900 direct; top 10 firms manage 60% of assets; North American groups oversee 54% of UK-managed AUM, UK-headquartered share 35%; North American firms run ~three-quarters of UK-managed tracker assets.
- Three themes named by the IA: technology/AI and tokenisation; supporting UK growth (Mansion House Accord); democratising investing (retail investment campaign, targeted support).

**UK retail flows in 2025 (IA, 5 Feb 2026) [S152, FETCHED].** Net retail outflow £2.3bn (same as 2024); trackers +£12.8bn (down from £27.6bn record); active -£15.1bn (improved from -£29.9bn); UK equities -£11.1bn (worst); money market funds +£6.9bn (record); mixed asset +£4.5bn; fixed income +£1.1bn; FUM £1.62trn.

**Global industry.**
- BCG Global Asset Management Report 2026 (28 Apr 2026) [S154, FETCHED]: global AuM $147trn in 2025, +11%; profit margins "close to 30%", roughly where they were in 2010; revenues +5.1% a year vs costs +5.4%; "more than 80% of gross revenue growth in 2025 was driven by market appreciation"; retail investors accounted for 61% of global expansion 2020-2025; institutional fees -3% a year; AI could cut costs 25-35% over 3-5 years and lift coverage per relationship manager three- to five-fold.
- BCG 2025 report [S155, SNIPPET]: $128trn AuM in 2024 (+12%); >70% of $58bn revenue growth from markets not flows; semi-liquid private funds >$300bn NAV, grown more than fivefold in four years.
- McKinsey "Asset management 2025: The great convergence" [S156, SNIPPET/blocked 503]: margins tight as costs rise; traditional and alternative managers converging (semi-liquid/evergreen funds, public-private model portfolios); $6trn-$10.5trn of "money in motion" over five years.
- PwC "Asset and Wealth Management Revolution 2025: The profitability paradox" [S157, SNIPPET/403]: profit per $bn of AUM fell from $1.68m (2021) to $1.18m (2024).
- Morningstar Europe 2025 [S158, SNIPPET]: €696.3bn European net inflows (record), €15.4trn assets; ETFs record €337.5bn inflows, €2.72trn assets; active ETFs €22.3bn inflows (6.6% of ETF flows), €73.6bn assets, JPMorgan ~49% share; equities favour passive but bonds shifted to active. ETF Express (Feb 2026) reports European active ETFs tripled in size in two years [S159, SNIPPET].

**Structural forces (what is changing and why sales cares).**
1. *Passive shift and fee compression*: trackers took record UK retail flows in 2024 [S150]; institutional fees falling ~3%/yr [S154]; UK margins at 18% [S150]. Sales must justify active fees with outcomes, not stories.
2. *Private markets growth and democratisation*: LTAF regime (FCA; retail access broadened by PS23/7 [S199]; LTAFs eligible for stocks and shares ISAs from April 2026 per AIMA/search snippet [S197]); ELTIFs in the EU; semi-liquid funds. But the Bank of England FSR (7 July 2026) warns "Redemption requests have been elevated in several retail funds, with some limiting redemptions" [S196].
3. *Active ETFs in Europe*: fast growth from a small base [S158, S159]; also record active ETF closures globally in 2025 (~150 shut) [S160].
4. *DB endgame*: buy-ins/buyouts > £40bn a year for a third consecutive year in 2025, ~350 deals [S191]; LDI shrinking [S150]. DB mandates run off; insurer mandates grow.
5. *DC consolidation*: Pension Schemes Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026) requires multi-employer DC default "megafunds" of £25bn by 2030 (easement to 2035 for £10bn+ providers with a credible plan) [S183]; fewer, bigger, more price-sensitive buyers with in-house capability.
6. *LGPS pooling*: pools cut from eight to six (ACCESS and Brunel plans rejected); funds to be pooled by 31 March 2026; pools must build in-house strategic advice [S185, S186]. Asset managers now sell to six large pools, not ~86 individual funds.
7. *Mansion House Accord* (13 May 2025): 17 DC providers aim for at least 10% of DC default funds in private markets by 2030, with 5% in the UK; at least £252bn in scope; voluntary [S181]. Backed by a reserve mandation power in the 2026 Act [S183].
8. *Retail investment push*: cash ISA limit cut to £12,000 for under-65s from 6 April 2027 (overall £20,000 unchanged) [S187]; targeted support live 6 April 2026 [S170]; "Invest for the Future" campaign launched 23 April 2026 - Schroders is a founding member firm [S189]; FCA package of 8 Dec 2025 (CCI, client categorisation, disclosures) [S169].
9. *Wealth and model portfolios*: UK platform MPS assets >£214bn at end-2025, grown 20-fold in a decade, >25% of advised platform assets (Platforum via Money Marketing, 21 May 2026) [S193]; UK wealth M&A record ~£20bn deal value in 2025 [S195, SNIPPET].
10. *AI and technology*: IA names AI and tokenisation as top theme [S150]; BCG sees 25-35% cost cuts [S154].
11. *Manager consolidation*: top 10 UK firms 60% of AUM [S150]; BCG 2025 report subtitle "Staying relevant in a consolidating market" [S155].

**Where the industry goes in 3-5 years - competing views.**
- *View 1: "Barbell - scale and specialism win, the middle gets squeezed."* Passive giants (mostly US) and private-markets specialists take share; mid-sized active houses must merge or niche. Evidence: North American share of UK AUM up 7pp in four years; top-10 concentration 60%; margins 18% [S150]; institutional fees -3%/yr [S154].
- *View 2: "Great convergence - public and private blend and move into wealth/DC."* Growth comes from packaging private assets for wealth, DC and insurers (semi-liquid funds, LTAFs, public-private model portfolios): $6-10.5trn money in motion [S156]; Mansion House Accord 10% target [S181]; retail drove 61% of growth 2020-25 [S154].
- *View 3: "Caution - private markets are over-sold, and active public strategies (especially bonds, active ETFs) recover."* BoE warns on private credit valuations, liquidity and retail fund redemption limits [S196]; bonds shifting to active in Europe [S158]; 81% of fund selectors surveyed by Rathbones expect a bigger role for active fixed income managers [S194, SNIPPET].
- *View 4: "Tech-led operating model reset."* AI lowers cost-to-serve and lets one salesperson cover 3-5x more clients (BCG [S154]); the winners are those who personalise at scale (direct indexing, model portfolios, digital distribution).
A good interview answer: these are not mutually exclusive; a diversified manager (like Schroders, with public, private and wealth businesses) is effectively betting on 2 and 4 while defending against 1.

### 4B. THE SUB-INDUSTRY: ASSET MANAGEMENT DISTRIBUTION / SALES

**History in five steps.**
1. *Commission era (pre-2013)*: fund houses paid advisers initial and trail commission; salespeople competed partly on commission levels.
2. *RDR (Retail Distribution Review), in force 31 December 2012*: "removed commission payments to advisers and platforms from product providers", raised adviser qualifications, improved charge transparency [S177, S178]. FCA 2014 review: "commission is no longer a driving factor in advisers' recommendations" [S177].
3. *Platform rebate ban*: PS13/1 - from 6 April 2014 platforms paid only by explicit charge; cash rebates >£1/month/fund banned for new business; legacy payments ended April 2016 ("sunset clause") [S178, SNIPPET]. Result: "clean" share classes, fee pressure, fund houses selling on merit to fund selectors rather than on commission.
4. *Rise of gatekeepers*: investment consultants (Mercer, WTW, Aon, LCP, Hymans Robertson, Isio, Barnett Waddingham etc.) advise trustees on strategy and manager selection; their manager-research "buy ratings" decide which strategies make RFP shortlists [S198, SNIPPET]. On the wholesale side, centralised fund-selection teams at discretionaries, platforms and MPS providers act as gatekeepers; MPS now >£214bn on platforms [S193].
5. *Scrutiny of gatekeepers*: CMA investment consultants market investigation (final report Dec 2018; Order made 10 June 2019). Remedies: trustees must competitively tender fiduciary management covering 20%+ of scheme assets (at least three unrelated candidates), set strategic objectives for consultants, standardised fee and performance reporting, separate marketing of fiduciary management; tendering obligations in force by 10 December 2019 [S179, S180]. These duties later moved into DWP/TPR pensions regulation (knowledge-based; see Gaps).

**Channels.**
- *Institutional*: segregated mandates or pooled funds for pensions, insurers, SWFs, LGPS pools. Long sales cycles (6-18 months), led by consultant ratings and formal RFPs; sales roles = business development + client relationship management; heavy on reporting, due diligence questionnaires (DDQs), stewardship evidence.
- *Wholesale/intermediary*: IFAs, wealth managers, private banks, platforms, MPS and multi-managers. Sold through fund-selector research meetings, platform "best buy"/panel lists, model portfolio inclusion; needs ratings (Morningstar, FE, Square Mile/RSMR), share-class availability on platforms, marketing content, adviser events.
- *Wealth / own wealth arm*: vertically integrated groups sell through their own wealth managers (captive distribution) - a debate under Consumer Duty value rules.
- *Direct*: D2C platforms, workplace DC default funds (via scheme provider), and ETFs listed on exchanges.

**How an institutional RFP typically works** (general industry practice; not from a single fetched source): trustee sets objective -> consultant screens its rated-manager universe -> RFI/RFP with DDQ (performance, team, process, risk, fees, ESG/stewardship, ops) -> shortlist of 3-6 -> finalist presentations ("beauty parade") -> fee negotiation -> legal (IMA) -> onboarding. Consultant "buy" ratings, 3-5 year track record, team stability and competitive fees are gating factors. Since the CMA Order, fiduciary management deals over the 20% threshold must be tendered [S179].

**What external clients / fund buyers value today.** (a) Clear value for fee - reinforced by Consumer Duty price-and-value and Assessment of Value [S161, S164]; (b) outcomes and consistency, not star managers; (c) private market access with credible liquidity terms (but with BoE warnings [S196]); (d) active fixed income (Rathbones survey [S194]); (e) MPS-friendly building blocks, low OCFs, platform availability [S193]; (f) sustainability evidence that survives anti-greenwashing rules [S165]; (g) scale and operational robustness (DC megafunds and LGPS pools increasingly insource and negotiate hard [S183, S185]); (h) service: data, reporting, education, targeted support tools.

**What internal clients (fund managers/product) value from sales.** Accurate client intelligence (what buyers need, fee sensitivity, competitor moves), fair representation of the strategy, well-qualified pipeline, sticky assets (not hot money), feedback for product development (e.g. active ETF share classes, LTAF wrappers), and compliance-clean communication (COBS 4 financial promotions, SDR naming rules).

**Current issues in distribution (2025-26).**
- Persistent UK retail outflows from active and UK equity funds [S152].
- Fund rationalisation: record active ETF closures globally [S160]; SDR renaming and fund mergers (FCA let firms contact supervisors on mergers/wind-ups) [S166].
- Concentration of buying power: MPS top 10 >£115bn [S193]; LGPS six pools [S185]; DC megafunds [S183].
- Platform/adviser consolidation and vertical integration (FCA multi-firm review of consolidation in advice/wealth, late 2025 [S195, SNIPPET]).
- Retail disclosure reset: CCI replaces PRIIPs KID/UCITS KIID [S168].
- Targeted support and the ISA change open a new mass-market funnel [S170, S187].

**Competitive map (suppliers in distribution, UK lens).** Passive/ETF giants (BlackRock iShares, Vanguard, L&G, Amundi, State Street, Invesco, DWS Xtrackers); UK/European active houses (Schroders, abrdn/aberdeen, M&G, Jupiter, Janus Henderson, Baillie Gifford, Fidelity International, Artemis, Royal London AM, Liontrust); US active and active-ETF leaders (JPMorgan AM ~49% of European active ETF assets [S158], Capital Group, T. Rowe); private markets platforms (Blackstone, Apollo - first UK LTAF announced March 2026 [S197], KKR, Partners Group, and in-house private arms of traditional managers); MPS/discretionary providers (Quilter, Tatton, Brooks Macdonald etc. [S193]); consultants as fiduciary managers (Mercer, WTW, Aon, LCP-linked, Isio) who compete with and gatekeep asset managers.

| Date | Instrument | What it did | Why it matters to a sales role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Dec 2012 | RDR (FSA) | Banned product-provider commission to advisers and platforms; raised adviser qualifications [S177] | Sales moved from paying for distribution to winning on merit with fund selectors |
| 6 Apr 2014 (legacy to Apr 2016) | PS13/1 platform rules | Platforms paid by explicit charges; cash rebates banned [S178] | Clean share classes; fee transparency; platform negotiating power |
| Jan 2018 | MiFID II (UK: PROD 3, inducements, research unbundling) | Manufacturers must define a target market and share info with distributors; research paid separately from execution [S176, S175] | Every fund has a target market; sales must sell within it and feed back distribution data |
| 10 Jun 2019 (tendering from 10 Dec 2019) | CMA Investment Consultancy and Fiduciary Management Market Investigation Order 2019 | Mandatory tendering for fiduciary management (20%+ of assets), consultant objectives, fee/performance standards [S179, S180] | Consultants are held to account, which makes their manager ratings and RFPs more formal |
| 30 Sep 2019 | PS18/8 Assessment of Value (COLL 6.6.20R) | Authorised fund managers must assess annually whether charges are justified by value; independent directors on AFM boards [S163, S164] | Sales must be able to explain the AoV report and value case for each fund |
| 31 Jul 2023 / 31 Jul 2024 | FCA Consumer Duty (PS22/9, pub. 27 Jul 2022) | New Consumer Principle; four outcomes (products & services, price & value, consumer understanding, consumer support); open products 31 Jul 2023, closed 31 Jul 2024 [S161] | Manufacturers and distributors share responsibility for retail outcomes; fair value evidence is part of every retail pitch |
| 31 May 2024 | SDR anti-greenwashing rule (PS23/16) | Sustainability claims must be fair, clear, not misleading and consistent with product characteristics [S165, S167] | Every piece of sales/marketing copy with ESG language is in scope |

| 2 Dec 2024 (flex to 5pm 2 Apr 2025) | SDR naming and marketing rules | "Sustainable", "sustainability", "impact" only in names of labelled funds; temporary flexibility for qualifying funds to 2 Apr 2025 [S165, S166] | Many funds renamed or merged; sales must explain name changes to clients |
| 13 May 2025 | Mansion House Accord (voluntary) | 17 DC providers: 10% of default funds in private markets by 2030, 5% UK; £252bn in scope [S181] | Creates demand for private-market DC products |
| 3 Jun 2025 (effective 1 Jan 2026) | FRC UK Stewardship Code 2026 | Streamlined reporting: Policy and Context Disclosure every 4 years plus annual Activities and Outcomes Report [S190, SNIPPET] | Stewardship evidence is standard in institutional DDQs |

### GLOSSARY (48 terms)
1. **Asset management**: investing money on behalf of clients for a fee.
2. **AUM (assets under management)**: total market value of money a manager runs; drives revenue.
3. **Net flows (net sales)**: new money in minus money withdrawn; growth that is not from markets.
4. **Fund**: a pooled vehicle where many investors own units/shares of one portfolio.
5. **OEIC / ICVC**: UK open-ended investment company; shares created/cancelled at net asset value.
6. **Unit trust**: UK trust-based open-ended fund; unitholders, trustee and manager.
7. **UCITS**: EU/UK regulated retail fund standard with diversification and liquidity rules.
8. **ETF (exchange-traded fund)**: fund whose shares trade on a stock exchange during the day.
9. **Active ETF**: an ETF run by a manager making active choices rather than tracking an index.
10. **Investment trust**: UK closed-ended listed company that invests; can trade at a discount/premium to NAV.
11. **NAV (net asset value)**: value of a fund's assets minus liabilities, per share.
12. **Mandate**: the instructions (objective, benchmark, limits, fee) under which a manager runs a client's money.
13. **Segregated account**: a portfolio run for one client alone, as opposed to a pooled fund.
14. **Pooled fund**: a fund shared by many clients.
15. **Benchmark**: an index or target a portfolio is measured against.
16. **Alpha**: return above the benchmark attributable to the manager's skill.
17. **Beta**: return from market exposure itself.
18. **Tracking error**: how much a portfolio's return varies from its benchmark; a measure of active risk.
19. **Active vs passive**: stock-picking/judgement versus replicating an index at low cost.
20. **bps (basis points)**: hundredths of a percent; 0.75% = 75bps.
21. **OCF (ongoing charges figure)**: annual running cost of a fund as % of assets.
22. **AMC**: annual management charge - the manager's fee component.
23. **Share class (clean/institutional)**: versions of a fund with different fees/terms; "clean" = no commission built in.
24. **LDI (liability-driven investment)**: using gilts and derivatives so DB scheme assets move with liabilities.
25. **DB (defined benefit)**: pension promising a set income; employer bears risk.
26. **DC (defined contribution)**: pension pot built from contributions; member bears investment risk.
27. **Default fund**: the fund DC members are placed in if they make no choice (most members).
28. **Master trust**: multi-employer trust-based DC scheme (e.g. Nest).
29. **Buy-in / buyout (bulk annuity)**: a DB scheme insuring its pensions with an insurer.
30. **LGPS pool**: one of six vehicles consolidating local government pension assets.
31. **Fiduciary management**: outsourcing investment decisions of a pension scheme to a provider (often a consultant).
32. **Investment consultant**: adviser to trustees on strategy and manager selection; gatekeeper.
33. **Buy rating**: consultant/research house approval that puts a strategy on shortlists.
34. **RFP / RFI**: request for proposal/information - formal tender from a client.
35. **DDQ**: due diligence questionnaire covering team, process, risk, operations, ESG.
36. **Fund selector / fund buyer**: professional who chooses funds for a wealth manager, platform or MPS.
37. **Platform**: online service holding investors' funds/ISAs/SIPPs (e.g. HL, AJ Bell).
38. **MPS (model portfolio service)**: ready-made risk-graded portfolios run by a discretionary manager on platforms.
39. **IFA**: independent financial adviser, paid by client fees since RDR.
40. **Discretionary fund manager (DFM)**: wealth manager allowed to make investment decisions without asking the client each time.
41. **Private markets**: unlisted assets - private equity, private credit, infrastructure, real estate.
42. **Semi-liquid / evergreen fund**: open-ended private-markets fund with periodic, capped redemptions.
43. **LTAF (Long-Term Asset Fund)**: UK authorised open-ended fund for illiquid assets, aimed at DC and (since PS23/7) wider retail.
44. **ELTIF**: EU equivalent long-term investment fund.
45. **Consumer Duty**: FCA rule requiring firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers.
46. **Assessment of Value (AoV)**: annual report where UK fund managers judge whether fees are justified.
47. **SDR / investment labels**: FCA sustainability disclosure regime and four fund labels.
48. **CCI (Consumer Composite Investments)**: new UK retail disclosure regime replacing PRIIPs KID/UCITS KIID.
49. **Target market**: MiFID II/PROD definition of who a product is designed for.
50. **Inducements**: payments/benefits from third parties that could bias advice; heavily restricted.
51. **Targeted support**: new FCA regime of suggestions for groups of consumers with similar characteristics.
52. **Stewardship**: using voting and engagement to look after investee companies on clients' behalf.
53. **SM&CR**: Senior Managers and Certification Regime - individual accountability in FCA firms.
54. **Financial promotion (COBS 4)**: FCA rules that marketing must be fair, clear and not misleading.
55. **Fee compression**: long-run fall in fees as passive and competition grow.
56. **Distribution**: everything that gets a product to clients - sales, marketing, platforms, intermediaries.

## 6) INTERVIEW ANGLES
1. **"Explain asset management to a friend."** "We run other people's money - pensions, insurers, savers - for a fee on assets. Schroders' Client Group is the bridge between the fund managers and the clients: it designs the products, markets them and wins and keeps the clients."
2. **The UK is a global hub, but not a UK-owned one**: 51% of AUM for overseas clients, 54% run by North American groups [S150]. Angle: a UK-headquartered active manager has to win on differentiated capability and service.
3. **Know the squeeze**: margins 18% vs 31% in 2019 [S150]; >80% of global revenue growth from markets not flows [S154]. Sales that brings *net new money* is what matters.
4. **Retail is the growth story in the UK now**: retail 28% of AUM > pensions 27% [S150]; ISA reform April 2027 [S187]; targeted support April 2026 [S170]; Invest for the Future, with Schroders a founding member [S189]. Good question to ask: "How is Client Group preparing for the post-April 2027 ISA flows and targeted support providers?"
5. **Institutional buyers are consolidating**: six LGPS pools, DC megafunds of £25bn, buyout insurers. Talking point: fewer, bigger, more expert clients mean deeper, solutions-led selling and lower fees.
6. **Private markets for DC and wealth - with balance**: Mansion House Accord 10%/5% [S181] vs BoE warnings on semi-liquid fund redemptions [S196]. Showing you can see both sides impresses.
7. **Regulation is part of the sales job**: Consumer Duty price and value / Assessment of Value; SDR naming rules; CCI product summaries by 8 June 2027; PROD target market. Say: "I'd expect to make sure every client conversation is fair, clear and not misleading and inside the fund's target market."
8. **Gatekeepers**: in institutional, consultants' ratings and RFPs; in wholesale, fund selectors and MPS (> £214bn [S193]). Ask: "How does the team balance consultant relationships with direct asset-owner coverage now pools and megafunds have in-house teams?"
9. **Active vs passive is not binary**: trackers +£12.8bn vs active -£15.1bn in UK 2025 [S152], but bonds going active and active ETFs growing in Europe [S158, S159]. Angle: active ETFs as a new wrapper for active skills.
10. **Three future scenarios** (Section 1): barbell, convergence, caution/active rebound, plus AI. Pick one, defend it, acknowledge the others.
11. **History in one line**: "RDR in 2012 removed commission, so fund houses now win on performance, value and service to professional selectors - which is why Consumer Duty and Assessment of Value matter so much to sales today."
