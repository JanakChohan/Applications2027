# WS2: Schroders as a business, from the inside

Researched 2026-09-29. Source IDs S70 to S108. Tags: **[Confirmed]** means read in a primary source (RNS, annual report, company page, regulator). **[Reported]** means read in press or a secondary source. **[Inferred]** means my own calculation or reading between the lines, flagged as such.

> **HEADLINE FOR THE CANDIDATE (read this first).** Schroders plc is being **taken over by Nuveen**, the asset manager owned by TIAA in the US. The offer is 590p cash plus up to 22p of permitted dividends per share, about **£9.9bn** in total. The deal was announced on 12 Feb 2026 [S71][S76] and more than 99% of shareholders approved it in April 2026 [S70][S77]. **The High Court sanctioned the scheme today, 29 Sep 2026** [S75]. The last day of dealings is **30 Sep 2026**. The scheme takes effect and trading is suspended on **1 Oct 2026**, and the shares are delisted by **8:00am on 2 Oct 2026** [S75]. Schroders leaves the FTSE 100 at the start of trading on 1 Oct 2026, and Balfour Beatty replaces it [S84][S85]. By the time of an interview for the 2027 internship, Schroders will be **a private subsidiary of Nuveen/TIAA**, no longer a listed family-controlled plc. Every "listed company" and "family shareholder" point below has to be read in that light.

## 1) Findings

### A. Founding, heritage, ownership, purpose

- **1804 origins.** Johann Heinrich (JH) Schröder became a partner in J.F. Schröder & Co, a London firm his brother JF Schröder had founded. The AR 2025 says: "The history of Schroders began in 1804 when JH Schröder became a partner in J.F. Schröder & Co, a London-based firm founded by his brother JF Schröder." [S72] [Confirmed]. **J. Henry Schröder & Co** was established in London in 1818 [S81] [Reported].
- **Merchant bank era.** The history page says that "over the next 80 years Schroders became one of London's leading merchant banks, arranging bonds for transformative infrastructure". Milestones: an 1870 bond for Japan's first railway, the first investment trust in 1924 (when it "started managing investments"), an Investment Department in 1926, the first UK pension fund mandate in 1947, and an LSE listing in 1959 that was "oversubscribed eighteen times". [S80] [Confirmed]. Wikipedia adds a less flattering fact: Schroders issued £3m of bonds for the Confederacy in 1863 [S81] [Reported].
- **2000 pivot.** Schroders sold its investment banking arm to Citigroup for about £1.35bn (US$2.2bn, announced 18 Jan 2000). Around 3,000 staff moved to Citi, where the business traded as Schroder Salomon Smith Barney from 2000 to 2003 [S81][S82] [Reported]. Schroders' own history page says the sale was to focus on "asset and wealth management" [S80] [Confirmed].
- **Build-out through acquisitions** (selected): 2005 JV with Bank of Communications (China) [S80]; Cazenove Capital in 2013 for about £424m [S81] [Reported]; BlueOrchard majority stake in 2019 [S80]; River & Mercantile's UK solutions business, EV £230m, announced Oct 2021 and completed 31 Jan 2022 (about £42bn AUM), which became Schroders Solutions [S106] [Reported]; 75% of Greencoat Capital for an initial £358m plus an earn-out capped at £120m, announced Dec 2021 [S105] [Reported]; Future Growth Capital partnership with Phoenix in 2024 [S80] [Confirmed].
- **Family ownership.** The **Principal Shareholder Group** (PSG) is made up of descendants of the late Helmut Schroder, their trustee companies, investment companies and a family charity. It "currently holds 713,021,408 ordinary shares (44.27% of the issued ordinary shares excluding treasury shares)". Of this, 40.96% is held via four private trustee companies: Vincitas, Veritas, Alster and Treva [S72] [Confirmed]. In the Nuveen deal, the family trustee companies (about 41%) plus directors gave irrevocable undertakings covering about 42% of the shares [S76][S78] [Reported].
- **2022 share class unification.** The non-voting shares were enfranchised 1:1 into ordinary shares, and ordinary holders received a bonus issue of 3 for every 17 held. Before the change, the PSG held 47.93% of the voting shares and 20.44% of the non-voting shares. Afterwards it held about 43.11% [S83] [Reported, from the company press release snippet]. The AR 2025 still refers to a Takeover Panel waiver that caps the PSG at 47.93% [S72] [Confirmed].
- **Purpose, vision and values, in the firm's own words (AR 2025, p.3)** [S72] [Confirmed]:
  - Purpose: "Creating prosperity together."
  - Vision: "We partner with our clients to provide trusted advice and invest in the assets and markets that matter to them, building their future prosperity through delivering excellent investment outcomes."
  - Values: "We strive for excellence. We promote innovation and teamwork. We have passion and integrity."
  - Brand positioning since 2025 is the **"Active Edge"** campaign [S71][S72].
  - Note: the brief's suggested wording ("provide excellent investment performance to clients through active management") is an older formulation. The current words are the ones above.
- **Regulated entity.** Schroder Investment Management Limited has registered office 1 London Wall Place, London EC2Y 5AU, Companies House no. 1893220, and **FCA FRN 119348** [S104] [Reported, from the Schroders legal page via search snippet; I did not open the register page directly]. The FCA register lists it as authorised since 01/12/2001 [S104] [Reported].

### B. How it makes money (the money-flow)

**Mechanics, in the company's words:** "We generate revenue by charging fees as a percentage of the assets we manage, and for the use of our platform or advisory services, where relevant. We also sometimes earn performance-based revenues, transaction fees and returns on seed and co-investments." (AR 2025, p.11) [S72] [Confirmed]

**Money-flow, FY2025 (year to 31 Dec 2025)**, all [Confirmed] from [S71] unless marked:

1. **Client capital in.** Gross inflows were £142.0bn (excluding JVs). NNB was +£11.2bn excluding JVs, or +£6.0bn including JVs. FY2024 NNB was −£10.8bn.
2. **AUM.** £823.7bn including JVs and associates, which is a record and up 6% (FY24: £778.7bn). Average AUM excluding JVs was **£686.2bn**.
3. **Fees in bps** (net operating revenue margin excluding performance fees, FY25): Equities 45bps, Fixed income 34, Multi-asset 24, Core solutions 6, **Schroders Capital 57**, Cazenove Capital & other Wealth 48, Benchmark 21 [S71]. The implied blended group margin is about **36.5bps** including performance fees (£2,504.3m ÷ £686.2bn), or about 35.3bps excluding them [Inferred, my calculation].
4. **Revenue.** Net operating revenue was **£2,504.3m**, including £79.4m of performance fees and carried interest. Adding JV/associate profits of £49.3m and other income gives **adjusted net operating income of £2,589.8m** (+6%). Statutory net operating income was £2,700.5m.
5. **Costs.** Adjusted operating expenses were **£1,833.2m** (flat). Of this, compensation was £1,138.5m and non-compensation £694.7m. The **adjusted cost:income ratio was 71%** (FY24: 75%). The compensation ratio was about **44%** of adjusted net operating income [Inferred: 1,138.5 ÷ 2,589.8].
6. **Profit.** Adjusted operating profit was **£756.6m** (+25%). **Statutory profit before tax was £673.8m** (+21%). This included a one-off £113.3m gain on the SPW sale and £152.5m of transformation and portfolio restructuring costs. Adjusted operating EPS was 36.6p (+29%).
7. **Dividends.** The full-year dividend was 21.5p (interim 6.5p, final 15.0p), unchanged. Dividends paid in 2025 totalled **£335.8m**, and the 2025 final dividend comes to £234.9m [S73] [Confirmed]. The Chair says the dividend is being held flat "as we seek to bring the dividend back in line with the 50% payout policy target" [S72] [Confirmed].
8. **To the family.** 44.27% of £335.8m is roughly **£149m a year** to the Principal Shareholder Group [Inferred]. On the Nuveen exit, 713.0m shares × 590p is about **£4.2bn** in cash, rising to about £4.4bn with permitted dividends. The press figure is "roughly £4.4 billion" [S77] [Inferred/Reported].

### D. Risk and control architecture (AR 2025, "Risk management", pp.25–31) [S72] [Confirmed]

- **The Board** has "ultimate accountability for identifying, managing and monitoring the firm's principal risks". The **Audit and Risk Committee** reviews key risks through a quarterly dashboard. Note that it is a combined audit and risk committee, not a separate Board Risk Committee. The **Remuneration Committee** covers conduct and risk in pay.
- **The Chief Risk Officer chairs the Group Risk Committee (GRC)**, which meets five times a year. Its sub-committees are the Group Conflicts Committee, the Financial Crime Committee and the Information Security Risk Oversight Committee.
- **Three lines of defence**:
  - The 1st line is the business functions, supported by the Risk and Control Assessment (RCA) process.
  - The 2nd line was narrowed in 2025 to **Risk and Compliance only**. People & Culture, Legal, Tax and Finance moved into the 1st line.
  - The 3rd line is Internal Audit.
- **Principal risks for 2025** (strategic, business and operational):
  - Business model disruption. This now absorbs fee attrition, changing investor requirements and product strategy risk.
  - Financial risk
  - Information security & technology
  - Investment performance
  - Operational process
  - People & conduct
  - Regulatory, which now includes financial crime
  - Sustainability including climate
  - **Third party risk**, new this year "as a result of our increased use of outsourcing" (UST partnership)
  - Transformation risk
  - Reputational risk was removed as a standalone risk.
- **Specific risk work.** The Liquidity Risk framework was updated for **open-ended private-market vehicles** (evergreen funds, LTAFs). Oversight of cross-investments (public funds holding private fund units) was tightened. A counterparty model is aligned to Basel 3.1.
- **H1 2026.** Principal risks for H2 2026 are "expected to be substantially the same" [S70] [Confirmed].
- **Provisions.** There are no material contingent liabilities (FY25 and FY24). There is a provision for **remedial building works at the London head office** (a £20m unanticipated charge in 2025, partly insured) [S71][S73] [Confirmed].

### E. Strategy and business mix under Richard Oldfield

- **CEO.** Richard Oldfield (previously CFO) was named CEO in Sep 2024, succeeding Peter Harrison. He took office in Nov 2024, when Meagen Burnett became interim CFO from 8 Nov 2024 [S81][S90] [Reported].
- **Early cuts.** Before the formal plan, about 3% of the workforce (about 200 roles, mostly technology) was cut in Jan 2025, and the executive committee was more than halved [S88][S89] [Reported].
- **Strategy update, 6 Mar 2025** ("simplify, scale and deliver") [S86][S87]:
  - £150m annualised net cost savings by end-2027, about 8% of the cost base. Restructuring costs were about £200m [Reported].
  - Adjusted cost:income below 70% (from 75%).
  - Stabilise Public Markets revenues by end-2027.
  - Schroders Capital: £20bn cumulative NNB over three years.
  - Wealth Management: 5–7% NNB rate per year, 2025–27.
  - Up to £500m of seed and co-investment for Schroders Capital [S71].
  - Nine "leading capabilities" in Public Markets [S71].
  - [Confirmed from company page S86 plus RNS S71; headcount and restructuring figures from Reuters/Yahoo S87]
- **Delivery.**
  - Cost savings: £75m net in-year in 2025 and £102m annualised by FY25 [S71]. More than 98% of the £150m had been delivered by H1 26 [S70]. Cost:income was 68% at H1 26 [S70] [Confirmed].
  - Headcount KPI was −10% in 2025 [S72] [Confirmed]. The monthly average number of employees was 6,110 (2024: 6,385) [S73] [Confirmed].
- **Operating model.** Technology and operations were outsourced further to **UST**. Over 16% of the Public Markets fund range is being merged or closed. Client Group was restructured: "a new sales and marketing model where central support is focused on fewer core propositions" [S71][S72] [Confirmed].
- **Exits and simplification.**
  - Closed the convertibles desk.
  - Exited Munich real estate and Australian private credit.
  - Exited Brazil, completed H1 26.
  - Sold the Indonesian business, completed H1 26.
  - Restructured South Korea.
  - China wholly-owned FMC funds transferred to a third party.
  - **Schroders Personal Wealth**: the 49.9% stake went to Lloyds in exchange for Lloyds' 19.1% of Cazenove Capital, with no cash. This was announced 9 Oct 2025 and gave a £113.3m gain. Schroders keeps multi-year asset management agreements for SPW and Scottish Widows assets.
  - **Benchmark** sale agreed on 4 Jul 2026 to **Söderberg & Partners** for about £215m [S70][S71][S94][S95] [Confirmed/Reported].
- **Growth bets.**
  - European **active ETFs**: launched Sep 2025, about $3.5bn AUM within 12 months, a top-10 UCITS active ETF position [S70]. Europe and Japan equity ETFs were added Sep 2026 [S97].
  - **LTAFs**: a bespoke Mercer LTAF (Dec 2025) and LTAFs on Hargreaves Lansdown [S71][S72].
  - **Apollo partnership** (9 Feb 2026): public/private hybrid credit for UK wealth, and a US DC Collective Investment Trust [S96].
  - A 40-person Schroders Capital business development team [S71].
  - Cazenove "significantly increased its graduate intake for 2026" [S70] [Confirmed].
- **The endgame is the Nuveen combination.** About $2.5trn combined AUM. The Schroders brand is retained. **London becomes the combined group's non-US HQ and largest office, with more than 3,100 professionals.** Oldfield will report to Nuveen CEO William Huffman and join Nuveen's Executive Management Team. Schroders is to be run as a separate business for at least 12 months [S76][S77][S78] [Reported, from the announcement text].

### F. Listing status and what it means for research

- Schroders plc (LSE: SDR) was a **FTSE 100** constituent until the takeover. FTSE Russell confirmed on 24 Sep 2026 that it will be deleted from the FTSE 100/350/All-Share with effect from the start of trading on 1 Oct 2026, subject to court sanction, which was granted 29 Sep 2026 [S84][S85][S75] [Reported/Confirmed].
- I could not confirm whether it dropped out of the FTSE 100 at any point in 2024–25, when shares fell 25% in 2024 [S87]. An Investment Week headline "Schroders exits FTSE 100" exists but is undated in results. **See Gaps.**
- **Implications** [Inferred]:
  - The HY 2026 results (30 Jul 2026) are very likely the **last public results**.
  - There will be no FY2026 annual report as a listed plc, although UK subsidiaries such as SIM Ltd will still file accounts at Companies House.
  - Future disclosure comes through Nuveen/TIAA reporting.
  - The 590p cash price anchors valuation: about 17x adjusted post-tax operating profit [S78].
  - A candidate should talk about "Schroders as part of Nuveen" rather than share price.

### I. Skeletons, told straight

- **Hong Kong SFC, 2016.** The SFC reprimanded and fined Schroder Investment Management (Hong Kong) **HK$1.8m** for failing to disclose notifiable interests in HK-listed shares held in client portfolios from Aug 2005 to Jan 2013. Schroders had legal advice defining "interest" broadly but "failed to properly follow the advice". The SFC credited Schroders for self-reporting and cooperating [S102] [Reported, trade press quoting SFC].
- **Insider dealing by an employee.** Former Schroders equities trader Damian Clarke traded on inside information about M&A between 2003 and 2012 (profits of at least £155k). He was sentenced to 2 years at Southwark Crown Court, and the FCA page dates this to 2016 [S103] [Confirmed, FCA]. This was individual misconduct. The FCA release does not say how it was detected.
- **Confederate bonds (1863).** A historic reputational point: Schroders issued £3m of bonds for the Confederacy [S81] [Reported].
- **SEC / US.** I searched for SEC enforcement (including off-channel communications sweeps) against Schroders' US entities and **found no SEC settlement**. I am not claiming one exists. **See Gaps.**
- **SPW.** I found **no FCA fine or redress scheme specific to SPW** in my searches. SPW has now passed to Lloyds, so any legacy ongoing-advice-fee issues sit mainly with Lloyds [S94] [Inferred]. **See Gaps.**
- **Current disclosures.** No material contingent liabilities (FY25, FY24) [S73] [Confirmed]. The one live issue is the **London HQ remedial building works** provision (a £20m unanticipated charge in 2025) [S71] [Confirmed].
- **Performance, not misconduct.** The three-year share price decline to end-2024 [S87]. The 2024 net outflows of −£10.8bn [S71]. H1 26 NNB of −£8.3bn, including a £6.6bn institutional loss [S70]. Management is candid that Wealth NNB (2.7% in 2025) was "below our expectations" [S71].
- **Costs to staff.** About 200 roles were cut in Jan 2025, and the headcount KPI fell 10% in 2025 [S88][S72]. Nuveen has committed that Schroders will run separately for at least 12 months, but there are no long-term job guarantees beyond London "more than 3,100 professionals" [S76][S77] [Reported].

## 6) Interview angles (for a Client Group / Sales intern)

1. **Know the date.** The court sanctioned the Nuveen deal on 29 Sep 2026, and completion is 1 Oct 2026. Open with awareness of this, e.g. "Schroders is about to become part of a ~$2.5trn group with London as its non-US HQ. For Client Group that means Nuveen/TIAA's US distribution plus Schroders' European and Asian relationships." Ask how Client Group is thinking about cross-selling Nuveen capabilities (US municipals, real assets via Nuveen/Churchill) to Schroders clients, and the reverse.
2. **Talk margins, not just AUM.** Core solutions earns 6bps, Schroders Capital 57bps, Cazenove about 48–52bps. The £6.6bn H1 26 redemption hurt NNB headlines but was low-margin, and revenue still rose 24% in Public Markets. A sales intern who understands that "not all flows are equal" stands out.
3. **The intermediary story is Client Group's win.** Intermediary gross inflows were up more than 40% in H1 26. The Q4 2025 intermediary flows were the strongest since Q1 2021. European active ETFs reached about $3.5bn and a top-10 position within 12 months. LTAFs are on Hargreaves Lansdown. This is concrete evidence to cite on why active distribution still wins.
4. **Client Group was restructured in 2025.** There is a new Global Head (Matt Oomen, ex-BNY) and a "new sales and marketing model where central support is focused on fewer core propositions". A good question: "How has focusing on the nine leading capabilities changed what a salesperson brings to a client meeting?"
5. **Public–private convergence is the strategic thread.** It runs through Schroders Capital's £20bn NNB target, the Apollo partnership, the Mercer LTAF, and the Nuveen "scaled public-to-private investment platform". Be ready to explain an LTAF or an evergreen private-markets fund, and the liquidity-risk issues the AR flags.
6. **Transformation with honesty.** Name the changes precisely: cost:income went from 75% to 68%, the £150m savings are more than 98% delivered, headcount fell 10% in 2025, and there was UST outsourcing and a string of exits (SPW, Benchmark, Brazil, Indonesia). Ask how the firm protected client service through it. The AR cites retention of more than 95% of highly rated Public Markets staff and average client duration up from 4.3 to 4.6 years.
7. **Family and heritage angle.** After 222 years the Schroder family (44.27%) chose to sell. A mature answer: this shows that scale economics in active management now beat independence. The brand, London and culture were the family's stated asks, and all three are in the deal commitments.
8. **Risk culture.** The AR narrowed the 2nd line to Risk and Compliance, and added a new third-party (outsourcing) principal risk. The historic Hong Kong disclosure fine was self-reported. For a client-facing role, mention conduct, suitability and Consumer Duty as part of the sales job.
