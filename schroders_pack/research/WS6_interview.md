### 1.2 Stage-by-stage detail

**Stage 1 (Cognitive, about 20 min).** Numerical, critical and diagrammatic reasoning. The Candidate Zone has three practice tiles, one per type ("similar questions to those you will experience in Stage 1") [S262]. The FAQ says Stages 1 and 2 are untimed "so you can complete them at a pace that works for you" [S260]. Cappfinity tests in general are untimed but record response time [S279] (prep-site claim, MEDIUM). Treat speed as scored.

**Stage 2 (Behavioural & Motivational, about 20 min).** A multiple-choice questionnaire on "what drives you, how you work with others, and how your strengths align to the role and our values" [S260, S262]. In Cappfinity products this is usually a strengths-based situational or preference questionnaire. You rank or rate options, sometimes for how effective they are and sometimes for how much you would enjoy them [S280, S279] (prep-site description, MEDIUM). Advice that recurs across sources: answer consistently and authentically, and do not second-guess an "ideal" profile [S279, S280, S281].

**Stage 3 (Work Simulation & Video Interview, about 30 min).** Official wording:
- "A Work Simulation is an assessment that aims to give you a real insight into the role, tasks and team you will be working within ... One unique characteristic of the Work Simulation is that there will be a video interview as part of the assessment." [S262]
- Format: "Online questions ranking responses and submit a short recorded video answer." [S262]
- Rules: video questions are timed, with no pause and no retake, and they auto-advance. Other tasks can be revised until you submit, unless an icon marks them as one attempt only. Adjustments allow up to 25% extra time [S262].
- Cappfinity's helpdesk says you get a practice video question first. Sometimes there is also a non-assessed, timed question in the real format. Assessed answers cannot be re-recorded, "but you have time to prepare your answer ahead of the question" [S277, S278] (SNIPPET).
- The exact prep time and answer length for Schroders' video are **not published**. In previous (HireVue-era) cycles people reported 30 s prep and 2 min answers with 4-6 questions [S289], or "5 questions ... 2 minutes to answer and about a minute to prepare" [S290, SNIPPET]. Plan for **30-60 s prep and 1-3 min answers**, and expect 1-3 video questions, because Schroders calls it a "short recorded video answer" (singular) and "Video Intro". Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM.
- Dress: "please dress as you would for an interview or assessment centre, as your camera will be on" [S260]. The Candidate Zone adds: "We focus on what you say, not where you are or how you look on screen." [S262]
- What they assess, officially: "How you think, communicate and respond to practical workplace scenarios. It is not about being polished." [S260] "Prepare 2-3 examples about yourself ... We'll be looking to understand how your experiences to date have shaped who you are, what you enjoy and what skills you have developed" [S262]. The phrase "what you enjoy" is the strengths-based marker. Expect energy and motivation prompts, not only STAR competency prompts.

**Stage 4 (Assessment Centre, 4-5 h).**
- Official content: "A group activity, short presentation and interview" [S260]. The Candidate Zone says "Group exercises, an individual presentation and a competency-based interview" [S262].
- Group: "How you listen, contribute, collaborate and adapt in group settings - not who speaks the most or takes charge." [S262]
- Interview: "Assessors are looking to understand your underlying motivations and interests for the role alongside previous experiences ... people with potential to succeed; those who possess the strengths or skills needed" and "it may help you to structure your answer using the STAR technique" [S262].
- Scoring: "You are assessed against clear criteria and an assessment skills framework used by our assessors - not against other candidates." [S262]
- Logistics: details arrive about 1-2 weeks before; arrive 10-15 min early; a meal is provided; smart business dress [S262, S260].
- Forum and prep-site colour (LOW-MEDIUM, anecdotal):
  - Group exercise: "you get given a lot of information, and as a team you pick an option which you think is best". Presentation: "given lots of information, then pick an option ... present the findings" [S290/S292 via search snippet, unverified].
  - Interview "with HR and a senior manager", "mostly competency-based" [S288]. A "competency-based interview with either a member of HR, a manager from your designated business unit or a senior executive" [S289].
  - Glassdoor intern questions seen in snippets: "Tell me about a time you had to use a creative method to overcome a challenge"; "Describe a time you collaborated within a team and contributed to its success"; "2 of your biggest flaws, how you got a new interest and developed it further"; "experience dealing with a client problem" [S290, SNIPPET].
  - TSR 2024 Marketing internship thread: a candidate reached the AC for the summer marketing internship (Client Group). No content was verified because the page was blocked [S292].

### 1.3 Cappfinity deep-dive

**Company.** Cappfinity was formerly CAPP (Centre of Applied Positive Psychology). It was founded in 2005 by Dr Alex Linley and Nicky Garcea and rebranded as Cappfinity around 2020 [S279, S280]. GraduatesFirst claims it delivers more than 4 million assessments a year to about 250 clients in 80+ countries [S279] (vendor figure repeated by a prep site; MEDIUM). EY was one of the first UK firms to use strengths-based graduate recruitment systematically, with CAPP's methodology (search snippet, S286 context).

**Core idea.** A strength is something you perform well, find **energising**, and use often. The Strengths Profile scores 60 strengths on "three dimensions of energy, performance and use". It sorts them into four quadrants: Realised Strengths, Unrealised Strengths, Learned Behaviours (good at, but draining) and Weaknesses [S283, S284]. For an interview this means that being competent is not enough. They want evidence that the task **energises** you.

**The 60 Strengths Profile strengths (5 families)** [S283, FETCHED]:
- **Being (14):** Authenticity, Centred, Courage, Curiosity, Gratitude, Humility, Legacy, Mission, Moral Compass, Personal Responsibility, Pride, Self-awareness, Service, Unconditionality
- **Communicating (8):** Counterpoint, Explainer, Feedback, Humour, Listener, Narrator, Scribe, Spotlight
- **Motivating (13):** Action, Adventure, Bounceback, Catalyst, Change Agent, Competitive, Drive, Efficacy, Growth, Improver, Persistence, Resilience, Work Ethic
- **Relating (11):** Compassion, Connector, Emotional Awareness, Empathic Connection, Enabler, Equality, Esteem Builder, Personalisation, Persuasion, Rapport Builder, Relationship Deepener
- **Thinking (14):** Adherence, Creativity, Detail, Incubator, Innovation, Judgement, Optimism, Order, Planful, Prevention, Reconfiguration, Resolver, Strategic Awareness, Time Optimiser

Correction to the brief: "Adaptable", "Relationship Builder", "Organiser" and "Planner" are **not** in the 60-strength Strengths Profile list. The nearest equivalents are Reconfiguration, Relationship Deepener/Rapport Builder, Order and Planful. However, the Schroders Candidate Zone code bundle contains a leftover mapping table from another Cappfinity client (Phaidon). It links client competencies to "Cappfinity 80 Skills", including "Adaptable & Learning Agility", "Commercial Insight & Opportunity Spotter", "Customer Champion & Relationship Deepener", "Persuasion & Centred", "Rapport Builder & Personalisation", "Narrator & Explainer" and "Planful & Time Optimiser" [S262, residual third-party content]. So Cappfinity now appears to run a wider **80-skill library** that includes Adaptable, Commercial Insight, Customer Champion and similar. It is unknown which skills Schroders chose. Confidence: MEDIUM that the library exists; LOW on Schroders' own selection.

**How strengths questions differ from competency questions:**
- Competency questions ask what you *did* (STAR, evidence of past behaviour). Strengths questions ask what you *enjoy*, what comes naturally, and what energises or drains you. Examples: "What energises you?", "Describe a successful day", "Do you most like starting tasks or finishing them?", "Do you prefer the big picture or the small details?", "What tasks are always left on your to-do list?" [S285].
- Scoring leans on authenticity and energy. From Prospects: "If you're genuinely describing something you enjoy, you'll be animated and your enthusiasm and motivation will shine through" [S285]. From the LSE blog: questions "might feel repetitive, but this is to allow the interviewer to calibrate the consistency of your answers" [S286].
- Either answer to a preference question can be fine if you give reasons [S286].
- Schroders mixes both styles. Stage 3 leans strengths/motivation ("what you enjoy"), while the AC interview is officially "competency-based" with STAR [S262]. **Prepare hybrid answers:** open with the energy (why you enjoy it), then give one STAR example, then say what you learned.

**How to tell HR/early-careers assessors from practitioners on the day:**
1. **Introductions.** Early careers staff introduce themselves as "Emerging Talent" or "Early Careers" and usually host, brief and time the exercises. Practitioners give a desk or team (for example "UK Intermediary sales", "Institutional client service", "Product").
2. **Questions they ask.** HR assessors tend to follow the framework strictly: STAR, "what did *you* do", follow-up probes, and watching the clock. Practitioners go off-script into markets, products and clients ("What's a fund you'd pitch right now?", "How would you explain active vs passive to an IFA?").
3. **Note-taking.** Framework assessors write continuously against competency grids. Practitioners take fewer notes and talk more.
4. **Adapt your answers.** For HR, make the structure visible (signpost the STAR steps, say the result). For practitioners, bring a market view, client empathy and commercial "so-what". For both, show energy: this is Cappfinity-style scoring.
5. **Ask a relevant question.** Ask HR about programme structure, buddy and the 2028 graduate pathway. Ask practitioners about client segments, how the UK Client Group reorganisation changed their day, and wholesale vs institutional dynamics.

## 6) Interview angles

### 6.1 Strategy summary
- **Stage 3 video is one shot, timed and auto-advancing.** Rehearse 60-90 s and 2 min versions of each core answer. If prep time is short, use a 3-beat opener: "The honest answer is... / because... / for example...".
- **Lead with energy, then evidence.** Say what you enjoy and why, give one short STAR example, then what it taught you or how it connects to Client Group Sales.
- **Consistency matters.** The Stage 2 questionnaire and the Stage 3 video are both read against the same strengths framework. Keep your story consistent: for example, "I light up when explaining complex things to different audiences and building relationships over time".
- **Bring a market view in one sentence.** The official FAQ invites "a recent market trend or industry news story ... and explaining why it is relevant" [S260]. Have one wholesale/intermediary angle and one institutional angle ready (see WS4).
- **AC group exercise.** The stated criteria are listening, building on others, collaboration and adaptation, not dominance [S262]. Use the "summarise, build, include" moves.
- **Own words only.** Schroders prohibits AI-generated or AI-edited answers [S262]. Use these prompts to rehearse, not to script.

### 6.2 LIKELY Cappfinity video / strengths prompts for this role (30)
None of these are confirmed Schroders questions. They are modelled on the official wording ("what you enjoy", "how your thinking has changed") [S262], the advert's skills [S263], generic strengths banks [S285, S286], and Glassdoor snippets [S290].

Motivational:
1. Why are you interested in a career in asset management?
2. Why Schroders, and why the Client Group rather than an investment team?
3. What do you think a Sales intern in Client Group actually does day to day, and which part of that most appeals to you?
4. Tell us about a recent market or economic event and how it might affect what our clients are asking us.
5. What have you done to learn about the industry, and what surprised you?
6. Where do you see yourself in five years, and how would this internship help?

Strengths and energy:
7. What does a great day look like for you? What made it great?
8. What energises you, and what drains you?
9. Tell us about something you learned recently purely because you were curious.
10. Do you prefer starting things or finishing them? Why?
11. Do you prefer the big picture or the detail?
12. What do you find easy that other people seem to find hard?
13. What tasks tend to stay at the bottom of your to-do list?
14. When have you felt most "in your element"?
15. How would your friends or teammates describe you, and would you agree?
16. What achievement are you most proud of, and why that one?

Client and relationship (Rapport Builder, Relationship Deepener, Listener, Persuasion):
17. Tell us about a time you had to build a relationship with someone very different from you.
18. Describe a time you had to explain something complex to someone with no background in it.
19. Tell us about a time you changed someone's mind. How did you approach it?
20. Tell us about a time you dealt with an unhappy customer, client or member of the public.
21. How do you keep in touch with and maintain a network of people?

Organisation and pressure (Planful, Time Optimiser, Centred, Resilience):
22. Tell us about a time you had several competing deadlines. How did you decide what came first?
23. Describe an event, project or society activity you coordinated. What would you do differently?
24. Tell us about a setback and what you did next.
25. When have you had to stay calm when things were going wrong?

Growth, initiative and teamwork:
26. Tell us about a time you took the initiative without being asked.
27. Tell us about feedback that changed how you work.
28. Describe a time you contributed to a team's success. What was your specific role?
29. Tell us about a time you used a creative approach to overcome a challenge (reported at Schroders, S290).
30. How has your thinking changed over the past year about something that matters to you (official phrasing hint, S262)?

### 6.3 Likely Work Simulation scenarios (Stage 3 "ranking responses") and what each tests
These are modelled on Cappfinity's "day-in-the-life" formats [S276, S280], the Schroders description of Stage 3 [S262] and the Sales advert [S263]. They are not confirmed Schroders content.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Client complaint email.** An IFA or wealth-manager client complains about a fund's underperformance or a late factsheet. | Rank 4 responses; possibly write or record a short reply | Client focus, Emotional Awareness, composure, knowing when to escalate to the relationship manager vs reply yourself, clarity and tone |
| 2 | **Competing requests from sales colleagues.** Three salespeople want a pitch book, CRM update and meeting notes by 2pm; your manager wants an event list. | Prioritise or rank actions; choose what to clarify | Planful, Time Optimiser, judgement, expectation-setting, asking for clarity (usually the most effective option) |

### 6.4 Assessment centre angles
- **Group exercise:** expect a brief with lots of information and a decision among options (forum snippet, S290/S292). Aim to: (a) propose a process and timing, (b) summarise and build on others, (c) invite quieter members, (d) link the choice back to client need.
- **Individual presentation:** "pick an option ... present the findings" (forum snippet). Structure it as recommendation, then 2-3 reasons with numbers, then risks and mitigations, then next steps. Keep to time.
- **Competency interview:** STAR plus the energy line. Have eight stories that map to the advert's skill bullets [S263]: markets interest; self-starter or project launch; composure under pressure; audience-adapted communication; event coordination; client problem-solving; teamwork; a learning moment.
- **Questions to ask:** how the June 2025 Client Group changes under Matt Oomen are changing sales activation in the UK [S267, S268]; how interns rotate across Value Proposition, Sales Activation and Delivery [S263]; what differentiates interns who convert to the 2028 graduate programme [S260, S263].
