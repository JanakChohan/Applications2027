# WS8 - "Why Schroders", built from mechanism

Prepared 2026-09-29 for: Schroders Client Group Internship Programme 2027 (London, Sales within Client Group).
Source IDs: S300-S330 (range S300-S339 reserved). Confidence flags: **[H]** read in primary document, **[M]** read in secondary source / tool summary of a primary page, **[L]** search snippet only.

> **READ THIS FIRST: the ownership story changed this week.** Schroders agreed on 12 Feb 2026 to be bought by Nuveen (the asset manager owned by TIAA) for up to 612p a share (£9.9bn) [S300, S308]. Shareholders approved it in April 2026 with more than 99% in favour. Regulators cleared it on 22 Sep 2026 [S311, S312]. The High Court sanction hearing is **today, 29 Sep 2026**, the last day of trading is 30 Sep, the Scheme is expected to become effective on **1 Oct 2026**, and the listing is expected to be cancelled on 2 Oct [S311 M]. The internship in summer 2027 will therefore take place in a **Schroders that belongs to Nuveen**: it keeps its brand, runs as a standalone business for at least 12 months, and is still led by Richard Oldfield [S308 H, S309 M]. Any "Why Schroders" answer built on "family-controlled, listed, independent" is now **out of date** and would signal that the candidate has not kept up. The rest of this pack reflects that.

## 1) Findings

### 1.1 Why Schroders is structured the way it is

**Three businesses, one client-facing engine.** Schroders reports two segments: Asset Management, which covers Public Markets (£533.1bn AUM) and Schroders Capital, its private markets arm (£72.6bn), and Wealth Management (£123.9bn). JVs and associates add another £94.1bn, taking group AUM to £823.7bn at FY25 [S300 H, S303 H]. It had grown to £867.8bn by 30 Jun 2026 [S302 H]. A separate set of corporate functions sits alongside the businesses: Global Technology, Operations, Finance, Risk and Compliance, People and Culture, Legal and Governance, Internal Audit and Tax [S303 H].

**Where Client Group sits.** On the ExCo page, Matt Oomen, Global Head of Client Group, "leads our client-facing teams across all our regions: Sales, Client Services, Product and Marketing" [S306 M]. The 2026 Singapore internship posting says Client Group "integrates key client-facing functions including Sales, Client Service, Product and Marketing", serving clients from "official institutions and pension schemes to family offices and wealth intermediaries" [S315 M]. The job description gives the three sub-areas (Value Proposition, Sales Activation, Delivery) [S316 L].

**When the current form was set.**
- Client Group existed **before** 2025: Karine Szenberg was already "Global Head of Client Group" [S307 M, S330 L]. I could not find the year it was created (see Gaps).
- **Nov 2024:** Richard Oldfield became CEO, and a transformation office was set up under CFO Meagen Burnett [S301 H].
- **6 Mar 2025 (Strategy Update):** a nine-member Group Executive Committee replaced the 23-person Group Management Committee. The update also set a target of £150m in annualised net cost savings by end-2027 and named the "key focus areas" for change: "simplification of the business to remove duplication and overlapping processes; a more focused product strategy that targets improvement in speed to market; focused sales and marketing; utilisation of shared services" [S301 H].
- **17 Jun 2025:** Matt Oomen was hired from BNY Investments, where he had been "global head of distribution, responsible for product strategy, marketing and sales across 30 countries". He reports to Oldfield and sits on the ExCo. Szenberg became Executive Vice Chair, and Gopi Mirchandani became Head of Client Group, Asia [S307 M]. The annual report puts his start date in **August 2025** [S303 H].
- **During 2025:** the CEO statement says "We also began to reshape Client Group to scale up delivery ... Within Client Group we restructured teams and designed a new sales and marketing model where central support is focused on fewer core propositions. The goal is to anticipate clients' requirements and improve our speed to market with the best solutions" [S303 H].

**The mechanism: why a central Client Group rather than sales teams inside each business or region.** This is my inference from the firm's own wording; the argument is not stated in one place.
1. **Concentrate support on a few propositions.** The nine "leading capabilities" produce more than 85% of Public Markets revenue [S301 H]. More than 16% of the Public Markets fund range is being merged or closed [S300 H]. A central team can decide which propositions get marketing money, product development and sales enablement. Sales teams sitting inside each business line would each push their own long tail of funds. In the JD this is the **Value Proposition** area.
2. **One client, many capabilities.** Schroders' own case is cross-group collaboration: "By sharing insights and expertise across public and private market capabilities globally, we are able to deliver bespoke solutions" [S303 H]. A pension scheme may want core solutions or fiduciary management, private credit and global equities all at once. A single relationship owner (a Client Director) avoids three business lines calling the same Chief Investment Officer. The Mercer long-term asset fund (LTAF) mandate and the Apollo public-private partnership are examples of cross-capability products [S300 H].
3. **Global centre, local execution.** "We engage with clients through specialist teams based in regional hubs around the world ... delivering dedicated support through Client Service Hubs" [S303 H]. The JD's **Sales Activation** area "delivers local sales strategies", so the centre designs and the regions sell.
4. **Scale economics in delivery.** CRM, data and marketing technology are shared infrastructure. The Sales Enablement Platform Analyst posting sits in the "Client Group Data team, collaborating across a virtual network with Distribution & Marketing, Technology, Finance, Product Management" and will "drive consistent adoption of CRM (Salesforce) across Distribution teams" [S317 M]. That is the JD's **Delivery** area.
5. **Cost.** "Focused sales and marketing" and "remove duplication" were explicitly listed as sources of the £150m saving [S301 H]. The adjusted cost:income ratio fell from 75% (FY24) to 71% (FY25) and 68% (H1 2026) [S300 H, S302 H].

**What the model costs (the trade-offs).**
- **Distance from specialist product.** Schroders itself made an exception for private markets. It built a *dedicated* Schroders Capital Business Development team of 40 sales specialists "to take deep, specialist knowledge to clients" [S300 H, S303 H]. That concedes that a generalist central salesforce is not enough for complex products. I have not confirmed whether that team reports into Client Group or into Schroders Capital (see Gaps).
- **Wealth has its own client model.** Cazenove Capital advisers own their clients, and Oomen's ExCo remit reads mainly as Asset Management distribution (inference) [S303 H, S306 M].
- **Job cuts and disruption.** About 200 roles (roughly 3%) were cut in January 2025, mostly in technology [S325 L]. Average headcount fell from 6,385 to 6,110 (-4.3%), and full-time staff from 6,208 to 5,884 (-5.2%) [S305 H]. The firm exited or restructured Australia, Brazil, South Korea, Indonesia and China WFOE funds and sold Benchmark [S300 H, S302 H]. Around £200m of cost was expected to deliver the savings [S301 H].
- **Fewer propositions means some clients lose products** through fund mergers and closures [S300 H].

**Why three businesses, and why combine asset and wealth management (mechanism in numbers).**
- **Margins.** Net operating revenue margins excluding performance fees, FY25 [S300 H]:
  - Schroders Capital: **57bp**
  - Cazenove Capital and other Wealth: **48bp** (52bp exit rate)
  - Equities: 45bp
  - Fixed income: 34bp
  - Multi-asset: 24bp
  - Benchmark: 20bp
  - Core solutions: **6bp**

  Growth in private markets and wealth therefore lifts revenue per £ of AUM, while core solutions brings scale at thin margins.
- **Earnings stability.** Wealth Management adjusted operating profit grew at a 15% compound annual rate over three years to £198.3m [S300 H]. The 2024 strategy update called Wealth "an important source of value creation for the Group" [S301 H]. Wealth AUM is sticky: "we have served one family for over 75 years, across four generations" [S303 H].
- **Distribution.** Schroders Capital's strategy is to "enhance access to the Wealth segment", with evergreen and semi-liquid private funds sold to wealth clients [S301 H, S302 H].
- **Honest caveat.** Wealth NNB of 2.7% in FY25 was below the 5-7% target [S300 H]. In H1 2026 Schroders agreed to sell Benchmark, narrowing Wealth to Cazenove Capital in the UK plus Schroders Wealth Management internationally [S302 H]. The combination therefore works best at the high-net-worth / ultra-high-net-worth end, not in adviser platforms.

### 1.2 How Client Group, investment, product and marketing work together (firm's own words)
- **JD / Bright Network [S316 L]:**
  - Value Proposition: "Develops tailored investment offerings by analysing market trends, reviewing competitor activity, and working alongside investment teams to create innovative solutions that our clients value."
  - Sales Activation: "Delivers local sales strategies and deepens client relationships, focusing on growth opportunities and ensuring high standards of client service across regions."
  - Delivery: "Partners with technology and operations to drive efficient, data-driven processes, leveraging insights and tools to continuously enhance client engagement."
- **Annual report business model [S303 H]:** "We combine our broad investment expertise with client insights and market knowledge to develop client-inspired solutions."
- **CEO statement [S303 H]:** "Within Client Group we restructured teams and designed a new sales and marketing model where central support is focused on fewer core propositions."
- **Oldfield, June 2025 [S307 M]:** "We are taking deliberate steps to embed our teams even more closely with our clients, to collaborate, innovate, co-create and solve for their future prosperity."
- **Wiring to investment teams:** Johanna Kyrklund is both Group CIO and (since 2025) CEO of Public Markets [S306 M, search snippet]. Client Group's product teams work "alongside investment teams" [S316 L]. The "equity solutions team allows us to better customise mandates at scale" [S301 H].
- **New in 2025:** a Strategic Partnership team "to grow our relationships with key clients" [S303 H]. Szenberg now drives "strategic partnerships, including Global Financial Clients" [S306 M].
- **Evidence of client engagement:** "client engagements increasing by more than 30% year on year" in 2025 [S300 H]. Average client duration rose from 4.3 to 4.6 years [S303 H].

### 1.3 The fundamental value to clients

**Who the clients are** (FY25 annual report, AUM basis) [S303 H]:
- Channels: Institutional **64%**, Intermediary **19%**, Wealth Management **17%**.
- Regions: UK **47%**, EMEA 16%, Asia Pacific **25%**, Americas 12%.
- Asset classes: Equities 31%, Core solutions 16%, Multi-asset 14%, Fixed income 12%, Private markets 10%, Wealth 17%.
- Client segments named in the report: wealth intermediaries, family offices, endowments/charities/foundations, insurance, pensions (DB, DC, public), and official institutions.

**What they buy.**
1. **Active returns** across nine leading capabilities. Schroders says it is an "unashamed advocate of the power of active management" [S301 H]. Its 2025 campaign is called "Active Edge" [S300 H].
2. **Solutions.** Core solutions AUM is £118.6bn, with **£8.1bn net inflows in 2025**, "underpinned by continued success in partnering with pension clients on outsourced CIO and fiduciary management solutions" [S300 H]. This business was scaled by the £230m purchase of River and Mercantile's UK Solutions business, completed 31 Jan 2022, which created Schroders Solutions [S322 L].
3. **Private markets access.** Schroders Capital raised £10.9bn gross in 2025 and £5.5bn in H1 2026, with its highest quarter in more than three years in Q2 2026. It focuses on "mid-market high growth themes" [S300 H, S302 H, S303 H].
4. **Sustainability tooling and stewardship.** The proprietary CONTEXT, SustainEx and ThemEx tools; 91% of SustainEx scores beat the benchmark; £42.9bn in sustainability-focused portfolios; votes at 7,042 companies; engagement with 588 companies [S303 H]. The £3.3bn PGGM sustainable equity mandate was won "leveraging our proprietary research platform, including SustainEx" [S303 H].
5. **Research and insight.** "More than 11,000 company research pieces a year" [S303 H]. The Global Investor Insights Survey 2025 covered nearly 1,000 institutions overseeing US$67tn; 80% said they were likely to increase their use of active [S324 L; Schroders' own survey, so treat it with caution].
6. **Brand and trust.** Schroders ranks #5 of 2,770 asset managers in NMG Consulting's brand study and is the top non-US firm, a position it has held for seven years [S303 H].

**Why clients accept the fees (Schroders' evidence).** In FY25, 71% / 70% / 73% of assets beat their comparator over 1 / 3 / 5 years, compared with 70% / **58%** / 76% in FY24 [S300 H]. H1 2026 figures were 72% / 71% / 69% [S302 H]. **Methodology caveat:** the figures are calculated internally, mostly **gross of fees**, and exclude £86.9bn of assets. Only 70% of the assets are measured against a benchmark; the rest are measured against peer rankings (11%), absolute targets (13%) or cash (6%) [S300 H].

**Counter-case (industry-wide).**
- Morningstar European Active/Passive Barometer, 2024 data: active equity managers had a 1-year success rate of 29.1% and a **10-year rate of 14.2%**; bond managers were at 53.5% and 26.0% [S319 M].
- The 2025 edition reportedly puts the 10-year equity rate at 11.4%, with UK large-cap at 10.2% [S320 L].
- SPIVA Europe year-end 2025: **88%** of broad UK equity funds, 89% of UK large/mid-cap funds and 97% of UK small-cap funds underperformed in 2025 [S321 L].
- The implication is that Schroders' case is strongest where active has an edge (credit, EM, small/mid-cap, private markets, solutions) and weakest in developed large-cap equity. This matches the business mix it is steering towards.

### 1.5 Culture, values, people, place
- **Purpose, vision and values [S303 H].**
  - Purpose: "Creating prosperity together" (adopted in the 2025 strategy [S301 H]).
  - Values: "We strive for excellence. We promote innovation and teamwork. We have passion and integrity."
  - 2025 launch of **"Schroders Behaviours"**, which turn the values into expectations.
- **Staff sentiment.** 77% are proud to work at Schroders and 80% agree it is committed to inclusion [S303 H]. More than 95% of highly rated Public Markets staff were retained through the transformation [S300 H].
- **Community.**
  - £5.5m committed to charity in 2025.
  - New Fauna & Flora partnership through the "Better Futures Fund" (£1m a year for three years).
  - 16 hours of paid volunteering; staff logged 8,200+ hours (+24%).
  - Early participant in the UK Dormant Assets Scheme [S303 H].
  - Schroder Charity Trust is a family charity [S304 H].
- **Headquarters.** 1 London Wall Place, EC2Y 5AU [S310 M]. Completed around 2018: 310,000 sq ft, nine roof terraces, a 300-seat restaurant, and a fitness/wellbeing suite [S323 L]. A £20m unexpected charge for "remedial works to our London office building" was booked in 2025 [S300 H].
- **Family ownership and long-term thinking (now historical).**
  - The Principal Shareholder Group held **44.27%** [S304 H]. Trustee companies holding about 41% gave irrevocable undertakings to back Nuveen [S308 H].
  - Family links: the business dates from 1804, and Leonie Schroder, a descendant of the co-founder, sits on the Board [S304 H].
  - Long-term relationships: 13 agreements with 12 institutional clients are 25+ years old [S303 H].
- **Employee ownership.** Deferred Award Plan (DAP) and LTIP awards in Schroders shares; own shares are held in trust for employee share plans [S304 H]. These are cashed out or replaced under the deal, and Nuveen will fund at least **£175m of retention awards** [S302 H, S309 M]. I did not verify a UK all-employee Sharesave/SIP.
- **Interns.**

  - "Coffee Hours with our senior leaders" [S314 M].
  - The Client Group internship is listed as "Client Group (including Sales)" [S314 M].
  - Glassdoor intern rating 4.5/5 from 39 reviews [S328 L].
  - Cazenove Capital "significantly increased its graduate intake for 2026" [S302 H].
  - **No named intern testimonials were found.**

### 1.6 Nuveen: what it means for Client Group specifically
- **Rationale.** "Nuveen and Schroders have an investment-led, client-centric and collaborative culture"; "together ... will design new solutions to meet wealth and institutional clients' increasingly diverse needs"; combined AUM of about **$2.5tn**, making it "a new global leader in public-to-private investment management" [S308 H, S302 H]. Huffman spoke of "bringing our complementary platforms, capabilities, **distribution networks**, and cultures together" [S308 H].
- **Protections.**
  - No "material reductions in the employee base" for two years, "other than reductions arising from the removal of listed company-focused roles".
  - "Continuity within Schroders' existing investment and client facing functions" in each market.
  - London becomes the non-US HQ [S309 M].
- **Why this matters to Client Group.** It is the function that will sell a much larger shelf, including Nuveen/TIAA real estate, US municipal bonds and private credit (via Churchill), to non-US clients. It also faces the most obvious long-run overlap once the 12-month standalone period ends (inference).
- **Near-term friction.** H1 2026 recorded a £6.6bn institutional redemption, and Schroders itself referred to a "temporary lower level of institutional fundraising in longer-dated capabilities" until completion [S302 H]. Clients paused long-dated commitments during the ownership uncertainty.

## 6) Interview angles

### "Why Schroders", 30 seconds
"Three reasons, and they connect. Schroders is one of the few firms that can take a single client across public markets, private markets and solutions, and Client Group is the place where that happens. Since 2025 it has deliberately centralised support around fewer core propositions so that the regions can sell them faster. Second, the proof points are recent: 70% of assets beat their comparator over three years, up from 58%. Third, the next two years are unusual. With Nuveen, Client Group will be taking a much bigger public-to-private shelf to non-US clients under the Schroders brand from London. I'd like to learn sales in a team that is building something rather than just maintaining it."

### "Why Schroders", about 2 minutes (outline)
1. **Structure.** There are three businesses: Public Markets, Schroders Capital and Wealth. Client Group, one central team, owns Sales, Client Service, Product and Marketing across regions. The 2025 strategy called for "focused sales and marketing" and removing duplication, and the CEO says Client Group now concentrates "central support on fewer core propositions".
2. **Mechanism.** That maps onto the three JD areas: Value Proposition chooses and shapes the propositions with investment teams, Sales Activation takes them to clients locally, and Delivery keeps the data and CRM running. Centralising means a pension scheme sees one Schroders, not three. Examples: core solutions took £8.1bn of net inflows from fiduciary and OCIO work, and the Mercer LTAF mixes private assets into DC pensions.
3. **Economics I understand.** Private markets earn about 57bp and core solutions about 6bp, so the mix of what sales brings in matters as much as the volume.
4. **Honesty.** Active management is under pressure. Most European active equity funds lag over ten years, and that is why the firm is leaning into areas where active adds value: credit, EM, small/mid-cap, private and solutions.
5. **Timing.** Completion with Nuveen means Client Group's job gets bigger: roughly $2.5tn combined AUM, London as the non-US HQ, and a two-year employment commitment.
6. **Close:** the one specific thing (below).

### Close: name one specific thing
Pick one and read up on it:
- (a) The **Mercer LTAF / Future Growth Capital** mandate (Dec 2025). It is a Value Proposition plus Sales Activation case study: product was co-designed with the client, and it takes private markets into UK DC pensions.
- (b) The **European active ETF range**: $3.5bn in under 12 months, top-10 in UCITS active ETFs [S302 H]. This is a new wrapper sold through intermediaries.
- (c) The **Schroders Behaviours** launch. Use this only if the answer needs to be about culture.

### Honest counter-case list (have an answer ready)
1. **Share price.** Roughly flat to -2% over five years before the bid and about 40% off its 2021 peak [S326 L]. The Nuveen premium (~34%) was partly a verdict on that.
   - *Answer:* "The market doubted standalone scale. The deal plus the transformation are the response."
2. **Flows.** -£10.8bn in FY24, +£11.2bn in FY25, -£8.3bn in H1 26 including one £6.6bn low-margin redemption [S300, S302 H]. Institutional long-dated fundraising paused during the deal.
3. **Cost cuts and jobs.** About 200 roles cut in January 2025. Average headcount down from 6,385 to 6,110. £150m of savings, partly from "focused sales and marketing" and outsourcing to UST. Several markets exited. Benchmark sold.
4. **Active under pressure.** European active equity 10-year success rate is about 11-14%. 88% of UK equity funds underperformed in 2025 [S319 M, S320/S321 L]. Equity fee margin is falling (46 → 45 → 44bp exit rate) [S300 H].
5. **Wealth below target.** 2.7% NNB against a 5-7% target in FY25 [S300 H]. H1 2026 improved, with Cazenove at 5% annualised.
6. **Nuveen integration risk.** The standalone period is only "at least 12 months". The employment pledge excludes listed-company roles and lasts two years. There is long-run overlap in distribution and product functions (inference). Staff and clients may be unsettled.
7. **Loss of independence.** Strategy will ultimately answer to TIAA/Nuveen in the US. The "long-term family owner" argument no longer applies.
