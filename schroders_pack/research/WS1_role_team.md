### 1.8 Compliance dimension (UK sales/distribution)
- **Financial promotions / communications: COBS 4.** COBS 4.2.1R(1) says: "A firm must ensure that a communication or a financial promotion is fair, clear and not misleading." COBS 4.2.2G says the rule applies proportionately, so professional-client material may differ from retail [S38]. [Confirmed] In practice every factsheet, pitch book or email with performance needs compliance sign-off and the right audience label. The UK intermediary site itself says: "This marketing material is for professional clients or advisers only" [S27]. [Confirmed]
- **Client categorisation: COBS 3.** Per se professional clients (COBS 3.5.2R) include regulated entities such as "pension funds", large undertakings, and "a national or regional government ... a central bank, an international or supranational institution". Elective professional clients (COBS 3.5.3R) need qualitative and quantitative tests and written consent [S39]. [Confirmed] This matters because the JD's client list (central banks, pension schemes) is mostly per se professional, while wealth advisers serve retail end clients. That brings in Consumer Duty through the distribution chain [Inferred].
- **Consumer Duty: Principle 12 / PRIN 2A**, introduced by PS22/9. It applied from **31 July 2023** to new and open products and from **31 July 2024** to closed products [S42]. The cross-cutting obligations are to act in good faith (PRIN 2A.2.1R), avoid foreseeable harm (2A.2.8R) and enable and support customers (2A.2.14R). The four outcomes are products & services (2A.3), price & value (2A.4), consumer understanding (2A.5) and consumer support (2A.6) [S41]. Manufacturers must give distributors adequate information (PRIN 2A.3) [S42]. [Confirmed] For an asset manager as manufacturer this means target-market definitions, fair value (links to Product's value assessments [S4]), and information packs for platforms and wealth managers [Inferred].
- **Inducements: COBS 2.3A** (MiFID business).
  - 2.3A.5R is a general restriction on third-party benefits.
  - 2.3A.15R bans them for independent advice or portfolio management to retail clients.
  - 2.3A.19R lists acceptable minor non-monetary benefits, including "participation in conferences, seminars and other training events on the benefits and features of a specific financial instrument" and "hospitality of a reasonable de minimis value, such as food and drink during a business meeting" [S40]. [Confirmed]
  - Relevance: client events and hospitality run by sales teams must fit these limits (gifts and hospitality registers) [Inferred].
- **Market abuse: UK MAR** (onshored EU Reg 596/2014). Article 7 defines inside information and Article 10 covers unlawful disclosure. FCA guidance is in MAR 1 (MAR 1.4 on unlawful disclosure) [S45]. [Reported via FCA handbook snippets] Relevance: sales staff may hear about client flows, fund events, portfolio trades, or (this year) takeover-sensitive information. Schroders plc was a listed issuer until about 2 Oct 2026 [Inferred].
- **SM&CR.** The FCA published PS26/6 on 22 Apr 2026 (Phase 1 reforms). Most changes applied from 24 Apr 2026, with further tranches on 10 Jul 2026, 30 Jul 2026 (directory changes removing "multiple overlapping certification functions") and 1 Sep 2026 (non-financial misconduct alignment) [S43]. HM Treasury intends to remove the Certification Regime from primary legislation (Phase 2 consultation expected later in 2026) [S44]. [Confirmed/Reported] Conduct Rules apply to almost all staff, interns included in practice. Client-facing salespeople have historically been certified staff (client dealing function) [Inferred; the client dealing function was not verified in-session].
- **Conflicts (SYSC 10) and T&C (TC sourcebook)** were **not fetched in this session**. Treat them as background knowledge, not a verified source.
- **KYD/AML on distributors** is shown by the Luxembourg Distribution Control role [S11]. [Confirmed]

## 3. Worked hour-by-hour day: Sales intern, UK Client Group (London) [Inferred, built from S7, S8, S9, S5, S47]

- **07:45-08:30** Read FT/Bloomberg and Schroders Insights. Note three moves (e.g. gilt yields, US tech, GBP), each with a one-line client implication. Check the team inbox for overnight client requests [S7].
- **08:30-09:00** Sales team huddle: pipeline review (Salesforce), who is seeing whom today, open RFPs and DDQs, any flows to watch.
- **09:00-10:30** Prepare the meeting pack for an RM's afternoon meeting with a DB pension scheme or wealth manager:
  - pull approved factsheet and performance data;
  - update the holdings slide from the latest data;
  - check "professional clients only" labels and disclaimers;
  - send to compliance/marketing for approval if content is new [S38][S27].
- **10:30-11:30** RFP/DDQ workstream: track which sections are outstanding from investment, risk and ops; chase owners; run a quality control pass on the numbers against Consultant Database data [S7][S9].

## 11. Interview angles

1. **"Why Client Group, and why now?"** Tie the answer to three evidenced facts:
   - Oomen's 2025 rebuild around "accountability for sales" [S30].
   - Intermediary gross inflows up more than 40% [S28].
   - The Nuveen combination, which widens the product shelf clients can access (about $2.5tn, public-to-private) [S28][S31].
   - Do not speculate on job impacts.
2. **Show you understand the three pillars as a value chain.** Value Proposition decides what to sell; Sales Activation sells and services it locally; Delivery makes it efficient and measurable in Salesforce. Use the example of a European active ETF: product idea, marketing campaign, RM meetings, flows tracked [S28][S5][S7].
3. **Channel economics.** Be ready to explain why a £6.6bn "low-margin institutional" redemption hurt headline NNB less than it sounds, while "higher-margin intermediary" inflows lifted revenue (net operating revenue +17% in H1 26) [S28]. This shows commercial mindset.
4. **UK institutional thesis.** Mention fewer, larger pension pots (Mansion House / DC consolidation) and Harris's "strategic partnership" model [S23]. It works well as a macro-to-client talking point.
5. **Wealth/intermediary thesis.** Mention model portfolios, discretionary managers, LTAFs, and active ETFs, with Fowler's remit as the hook [S25].
6. **Compliance awareness, framed simply.** Anything you send a client must be "fair, clear and not misleading" (COBS 4.2.1R) and correctly labelled for professional vs retail. Under Consumer Duty, Schroders as manufacturer must give distributors enough information and show fair value; Product's value assessments are part of this [S38][S41][S4]. Hospitality must stay de minimis (COBS 2.3A.19R) [S40]. Mentioning this once shows maturity.
7. **Competencies mapped to evidence.** For "event/diary management", use a real society event with numbers. For "composure", use a deadline crunch as an RFP analogue. For "self-starter", show something you launched. The Stage 3 Work Simulation tests "realistic workplace situations" [S14], so practise triaging an inbox of client requests like the Zurich task list [S7].
8. **Market view drill.** Have a 60-second view on one macro theme (e.g. rates path or US equity concentration). Schroders' own survey found 94% of investors "concerned by market concentration risks" [S20]. Link the view to a product (e.g. active global equity or credit).
9. **Questions to ask interviewers:**
   - "How has the Value Proposition / Sales Activation / Delivery model changed how a UK sales team spends its week?"
   - "What does sales effectiveness look like in Salesforce terms?"
   - "How are clients responding to the Nuveen combination, and what new capabilities are you most excited to bring to UK clients?"
   - "What distinguishes interns who convert to the 2028 graduate programme?" [S1][S14]
