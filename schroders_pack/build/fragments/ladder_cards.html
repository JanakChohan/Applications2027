1. Markets and opinions

JD line: "Shows genuine interest in asset management and current market trends, with the ability to discuss the impact of economic events on investments and form well-reasoned opinions on commercial or financial matters."

What has caught your eye in markets?easy
Setup
The assessor opens with: "What is one market story you have followed recently and why does it matter to our clients?"
Trap
Picking a headline you cannot explain two levels down, or reciting a number with no "so what".
Say
Name one story, one number, one mechanism, one client. "The Bank of England's path on rates matters to a UK DB pension client because it moves gilt yields, which moves the value of their liabilities." Then say what you would watch next.
Why
The JD asks you to "discuss the impact of economic events on investments". A chain from event to client is exactly that skill. {{NEWS_MACRO}}
Twist
"And what would change your mind?"
Hold
Give one data point that would falsify your view. Holding a view and naming its weak point is the frame.
Defend a view on active versus passivemedium
Setup
"Passive is cheaper and usually wins. Why would anyone pay Schroders' fees?"
Trap
Getting defensive, or conceding completely. Both lose.
Say
Concede the fact, then narrow the claim. "On average, after fees, many active funds do lag their index over long periods. {{SPIVA}} The case for a firm like Schroders sits where indices are weak or absent: private markets, solutions, less efficient markets, and clients who need an outcome, not a benchmark."
Why
You show you know the evidence and you still find the commercial logic. That is a "well-reasoned opinion". {{AR_MIX}}
Twist
"So you're saying active is dead in large-cap US equity?"
Hold
"Harder to justify there, yes. That is why the mix matters more than any single fund." Do not retreat further than the evidence.
The client blames the market on youhard
Setup
Role-play: a client says, "Your fund fell 8% this quarter. Explain yourself."
Trap
Guessing at reasons for the fall, promising a rebound, or apologising for the market.
Say
Acknowledge, clarify, commit. "That is a fair question and you should get a proper answer. Can I check which share class and period you're looking at? I'll come back today with the attribution from the investment team, and I'd like to set up a call with the portfolio manager."
Why
An intern should never improvise performance explanations or forecasts. Communications with clients must be fair, clear and not misleading. {{COBS4}} Promises of recovery are exactly what the rules forbid.
Twist
"I don't want the fund manager. I want your view, now."
Hold
"My honest view is that I don't have the full attribution in front of me, and a guess from me wouldn't help you. You'll have it today."

2. Finance knowledge with clients

JD line: "Demonstrates moderate understanding of finance, able to discuss macro-level topics with clients and articulate insights effectively."

Explain a bond to a non-experteasy
Setup
"Explain to me, as if I were a new charity trustee, why bond prices fall when rates rise."
Trap
Jargon. Or a technically perfect answer that takes three minutes.
Say
Use one picture. "A bond pays a fixed amount each year. If new bonds pay more, nobody will pay full price for the old one, so its price drops until its return matches the new rate." Then check understanding: "Does that land?"
Why
The JD asks you to "articulate insights effectively" and to "adapt style to different audiences". Checking understanding is the client-service habit Consumer Duty's consumer understanding outcome points to. {{CD}}
Twist
"So should our charity sell its bonds now?"
Hold
"That depends on what the money is for and when you need it, and it's a decision for you and your adviser. What I can do is send you our latest views and set up a proper conversation."
A macro question you can't answermedium
Setup
In a client lunch the client asks you what the yield curve is saying about recession.
Trap
Bluffing. The follow-up question will expose it.
Say
Say what you know and flag what you don't. "An inverted curve has historically been read as a recession signal, though the signal has been less reliable recently. Our economics team publishes on exactly this; I'll send you their latest piece."
Why
Honest limits build trust faster than a confident guess. Schroders publishes economic views you can point to. {{SCHRODERS_ECON}}
Twist
"You're the Schroders person here. What's the house view?"
Hold
Only quote a house view you have actually read, with its date. Otherwise: "I'd rather send you the house view verbatim than paraphrase it wrongly."
Two clients, opposite fearshard
Setup
A pension trustee fears inflation. A family office fears recession. Both ask you, the same week, "What should we be worried about?"
Trap
Giving each what they want to hear, which means contradicting yourself.
Say
Keep one consistent set of facts and tailor the relevance. The same rate path matters differently to a liability-driven pension and to a growth-seeking family office. Refer both to the house research and the right specialist.
Why
Consistency across clients is part of treating clients fairly. Tailoring relevance, not facts, is the skill. {{CD}}
Twist
"Your colleague told us the opposite last month."
Hold
"Let me check what was said and what has changed since. If our view moved, you deserve to know why."

3. Self-starter

JD line: "Acts as a self-starter who is driven and passionate, willingly takes initiative, and seeks out opportunities, evidenced by leading or launching projects, societies, or volunteering activities."

Week one, nothing to doeasy
Setup
It is day three. Your manager is travelling and you have no task.
Trap
Waiting. Or inventing a big project nobody asked for.
Say
Ask the team what is coming up this week, offer to take the admin off a busy analyst, read the latest fund factsheets and Schroders insights, and send your manager a two-line note: "Here's what I've picked up. Anything you'd rather I prioritised?"
Why
Initiative inside the team's priorities, made visible to the manager. That is what "seeks out opportunities" means in a 10-week internship. {{JD}}
Twist
"Someone on another desk offers you a more interesting task."
Hold
Say yes in principle, then check with your own manager first. Initiative never skips the line manager.
You spot a better waymedium
Setup
You notice the team rebuilds the same client meeting pack by hand every week.
Trap
Rebuilding it your way without asking, or criticising the current process.
Say
Build a small prototype in your own time, show one person, ask what you're missing. "I tried templating the pack; it saves about an hour. Is there a reason it's done by hand I should know about?"
Why
The Delivery pillar exists to "drive efficient, data-driven processes". Asking why before changing respects controls you may not see (approvals, data rules). {{JD}}
Twist
"It's done by hand because Compliance has to approve every version."
Hold
"Then could the template be pre-approved and only the numbers change?" Keep the control, remove the waste.
Your society project that failedhard
Setup
"Tell me about something you launched that didn't work."
Trap
Picking a fake failure, or blaming others.
Say
Real failure, your part in it, what you changed, what happened next time. Keep the lesson specific: "I learned to test demand with ten people before booking a room for a hundred."
Why
Cappfinity-style strengths assessment rewards authentic reflection and learning, not polish. {{CAPP}}
Twist
"Wasn't that just bad luck?"
Hold
"Partly, but the part I controlled was the testing, and I changed that." Own your share, don't take all of it.

4. Composure under pressure

JD line: "Maintains composure and methodical thinking under pressure, balancing commitments and delivering results even in high-pressure environments."

Three deadlines, one afternooneasy
Setup
At 2pm you have a pitch book edit due at 4pm, an event list due at 5pm and a CRM clean-up due end of week. A salesperson asks for a new chart by 3pm.
Trap
Saying yes to everything, then missing one silently.
Say
Sort by deadline and consequence, then renegotiate out loud. "I can do the chart by 3:30 if the event list moves to 10am tomorrow. Which matters more to you?" Confirm in writing.
Why
The JD asks you to manage "time, tasks, and expectations". Expectations are managed by telling people early. {{JD}}
Twist
"Everything is urgent. Just do it all."
Hold
"Then I need you to pick the order, because I can't do all four well by five." Calm, factual, no drama.
The live mistakemedium
Setup
You notice, 20 minutes before a client meeting, that a number in the pack you prepared is wrong.
Trap
Hoping nobody notices, or fixing it silently in a version that skipped approval.
Say
Tell the salesperson now, with the correct number and its source. Let them decide: reprint, correct verbally, or pull the slide. Log it afterwards.
Why
Client materials usually go through approval. A silent edit breaks the record. Correcting before the client sees it is the fair, clear, not misleading standard. {{COBS4}}
Twist
"Just change it, no one will know."
Hold
"I'd rather we correct it verbally in the meeting than hand over a version Compliance hasn't seen."
Assessment centre meltdownhard
Setup
In the group exercise, the brief changes halfway and the group panics.
Trap
Taking over loudly, or going silent.
Say
Steady the group: "Let's take one minute to re-read the new brief and agree the three things that changed." Then assign parts and keep time.
Why
Assessors watch process under pressure, not just the answer. Method is visible. {{AC}}
Twist
An assessor interrupts: "You have five minutes left."
Hold
"Then we present what we have, with one line on what we'd do next." Deliver something, not nothing.

5. Communication

JD line: "Confident, authentic communicator with strong written and verbal skills, able to adapt style to different audiences, present clearly, and build rapport, whether in person, over the phone, or in client-facing situations."

Same fact, three audienceseasy
Setup
"Explain our new fund in one sentence to a pension CIO, an IFA, and your grandmother."
Trap
Three versions that are the same sentence with different words.
Say
Change the frame, not the fact. CIO: risk, return, fit in the portfolio. IFA: suitability, cost, what to tell their client. Grandmother: what it buys and why.
Why
Adapting to audience is written into the JD. Retail audiences also bring stricter promotion rules than professional ones. {{COBS4}}
Twist
"Now do the grandmother version, but she's asking if it's safe."
Hold
"No investment is guaranteed; the value can go down as well as up." Never trade a risk warning for rapport.
The cold callmedium
Setup
Role-play: phone an IFA who has never heard of you to invite them to a Schroders event.
Trap
A script read at speed, or pushing a product.
Say
Name, reason, value, ask, exit. "We're running a session on private markets for advisers on the 14th, with CPD. Would it be useful? If not, no problem." Listen more than you speak.
Why
Events and relationship building sit in Sales Activation. An intern invitation is marketing, not advice. {{JD}}
Twist
"Is this fund right for my client Mrs Jones?"
Hold
"I can't comment on a specific client. I can send you the fund documents and put you in touch with the sales team."
Writing to a senior personhard
Setup
You must email the head of a sales team you've never met to ask for 15 minutes.
Trap
A long email about yourself.
Say
Three lines: who you are, why them specifically, one easy ask with two time options. Subject line that says the ask.
Why
Senior people read the first line. Written clarity is on the JD list. {{JD}}
Twist
No reply after a week.
Hold
One short polite follow-up; then ask your buddy or manager whether there's a better route.

6. Organisation and events

JD line: "Manages time, tasks, and expectations effectively, demonstrating event or project coordination skills, diary management, and successful use of organisational tools."

Diary clasheasy
Setup
Two senior salespeople both want the same client slot for a meeting with the same fund manager.
Trap
Choosing yourself based on who shouts louder.
Say
Lay out both requests with the facts (client, size of opportunity, deadline) and ask the person who owns the fund manager's diary to decide. Offer an alternative slot to the other.
Why
You are the process, not the judge. The escalation route protects you and gives fair service to both. {{JD}}
Twist
"I'm more senior, just book me."
Hold
"I'll put your request first on the list to [owner] with your reasons. It's their call, and I'll tell you within the hour."
The event with 40 guests and a missing speakermedium
Setup
The day before a client seminar, the fund manager speaker drops out.
Trap
Telling guests nothing, or cancelling without asking.
Say
Tell your manager immediately with two options: a named replacement from the same team, or a recorded update plus Q&A later. Draft the guest message for approval.
Why
Coordination is judged on the plan B, not the plan A. Client communication should be approved before it goes. {{JD}}
Twist
"The replacement is a junior analyst. The clients are senior."
Hold
"Then we frame it as a deep dive with the analyst who runs the numbers, plus a follow-up call with the manager."
Tools under pressurehard
Setup
"How do you keep track of 20 small tasks?"
Trap
"I'm very organised." An adjective, not a system.
Say
Name the system: one task list, sorted daily by deadline and owner, a calendar block for deep work, a Friday review. Give one example where it saved a deadline.
Why
The JD asks for "successful use of organisational tools". Show the tool and the result. {{JD}}
Twist
"What happens when your system fails?"
Hold
"I tell the person early and agree a new time. The failure is silence, not lateness."

Sending 40 attachments.
Say
Ask one clarifying question: "Happy to. Is this for a manager review, a new allocation, or a board paper? I'll send the most useful three documents first."
Why
"Quickly understands client issues" and "listens actively" means finding the need behind the request. {{JD}}

Trap

Say
Report both, say which system each came from, and ask the owner of the data which is the record. Note the date and timing differences you suspect.

The Delivery pillar is about data-driven processes. Unresolved data is a finding, not a failure. {{JD}}
Twist
"The MD wants one number in 10 minutes."

Say
Structure it: which clients are at risk, what they value besides price, whether the rival product is truly comparable, what it costs us to respond. Then a recommendation with one number you'd need.

Twist
"You have 30 seconds."
Hold

Defending the process.

Why
Complaints must be handled and recorded properly. Consumer Duty expects firms to act on poor outcomes. {{CD}}
Twist
"I want compensation."
