4.2 The design choices that separate firms

  1. Active or passive. Schroders is fully active, pitched as "Active Edge" [S71]. Its nearest thing to passive economics is core solutions at 6bp. Its European active ETF range reached $4.3bn by September 2026 [S230]. BlackRock, L&G and Amundi run passive at scale [S136, S133, S130].
  2. Public or private. Schroders Capital is about 9% of assets but about 17.5% of revenue [S71] [Inferred]. It is smaller than M&G's (£83bn) or L&G's (£79bn) private markets books [S131, S133]. After the Nuveen deal the combined group has about $414bn in private markets [S125].
  3. Asset manager plus wealth manager. Schroders owns a UK private bank and wealth manager, Cazenove Capital. aberdeen owns platforms; M&G and L&G own insurance balance sheets [S132, S131, S133].
  4. Owner. Family-controlled until this week; now owned by a US pension and insurance group. Fidelity International stays family-controlled; Janus Henderson is now owned by private equity and activist investors [S139, S138].
  5. Global or UK-centric. Schroders operates in more than 40 markets, with 53% of assets outside the UK [S126, S72]. Jupiter and aberdeen are more UK-centric.
  6. How selling is organised. One Client Group integrating product, marketing and sales, against channel-based sales at M&G, Jupiter and Amundi [S306, S131, S134, S130].

4.4 Where Schroders does not lead

  • Organic growth. Net outflows of £8.3bn in H1 2026, against inflows at Amundi (+€56.4bn), BlackRock (about $321bn), M&G (+£2.2bn) and Jupiter (+£0.7bn) [S70, S130, S136, S131, S134].
  • Share price, before the bid. Down about 25% over the five years to February 2026 [S125]. Value was realised through a sale, not a re-rating.
  • Scale. About a third of Amundi and under a tenth of BlackRock [S130, S136]. Smaller than L&G Asset Management's £1.2trn [S133].
  • Passive. No index business at scale [S71].
  • Private markets size. Smaller than M&G's or L&G's [S131, S133]. Schroders Capital's net new business fell to £0.9bn in H1 2026 from £2.3bn [S120].
  • Fee pressure. The equities margin slipped from 46bp in 2024 to 44bp at the 2025 exit rate [S121].
  • Independence. Schroders chose to sell rather than buy. Jupiter bought CCLA; Ninety One took on Sanlam assets [S134, S140].
Say this if asked "What would you change?"

"Flows are the gap. Profit is up because costs came down and markets rose, but net new business has been negative this year. The job now is the one Client Group was rebuilt for: fewer propositions, sold harder, especially in the higher-margin intermediary and private markets channels."

4.5 The consolidation wave

European asset managers face "the stark choice...bulk up or sell out" (Reuters analysis) [S127]. Recent deals:

  • Nuveen buys Schroders for £9.9bn (effective October 2026) [S75].
  • Trian, General Catalyst and QIA take Janus Henderson private for about $7.8bn (completed 30 June 2026) [S138].
  • Jupiter buys CCLA (completed February 2026) [S134].
  • Dai-ichi Life takes about 15% of M&G (May 2025) [S149].
  • Talks between Generali and Natixis, and between Amundi and AllianzGI, fell through or stalled [S127].

Analysts name Jupiter, Liontrust and GAM as possible targets [S127].

BCG's line sums up why this matters to Client Group: "Distribution now defines who wins. Firms that secure access to capital through platforms and partnerships will have a structural advantage" [S144].
