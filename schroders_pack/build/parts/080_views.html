Forming your own view: both sides of every argument

The JD asks you to "form well-reasoned opinions on commercial or financial matters" [S263]. Here are ten live debates. Each has the case for, the case against, a model view and the follow-up question that tests it. Rewrite each model view in your own words, then defend it out loud for 60 seconds.

8.1 How to hold a view under pressure

A good opinion has four parts. A claim ("I think X"). One mechanism ("because Y causes Z"). One number with its source. The best counter-argument, named by you before they name it. If the interviewer pushes, concede facts, not the whole view: "That's fair on the numbers; I still think the conclusion holds because..."
