9B. Cappfinity: how the stages work and what could come up

What Cappfinity is

Schroders' "Emerging Talent Candidate Zone" at schroders.preparationplus.com is "Powered by Cappfinity" [S262]. Cappfinity grew out of CAPP, the Centre of Applied Positive Psychology, founded in 2005 by Alex Linley and Nicky Garcea [S279] [Reported]. The same platform is used by HSBC and EY, among others [S282, S296]. Older prep-site material on Schroders mentions HireVue or Aon; that describes earlier cycles [S289].

Key term: a strength, in Cappfinity's sense

Something you do well, find energising and use often. Cappfinity's Strengths Profile scores 60 strengths on "three dimensions of energy, performance and use" [S283]. Being good at something is not enough. They look for evidence that it energises you.

Strengths versus competency questions. A competency question asks what you did ("Tell me about a time..."). A strengths question asks what you enjoy, what comes naturally and what drains you ("What does a great day look like?") [S285]. Prospects: "If you're genuinely describing something you enjoy, you'll be animated and your enthusiasm and motivation will shine through" [S285]. Questions may feel repetitive "to allow the interviewer to calibrate the consistency of your answers" [S286]. Schroders mixes both: Stage 3 asks about "what you enjoy" [S262], and the assessment centre interview is "competency-based" with STAR suggested [S262].

Stage 1 and Stage 2 in one paragraph each

Stage 1. Numerical (tables and charts: percentages, ratios, growth rates), critical reasoning (does the conclusion follow?) and diagrammatic (patterns and rules). Untimed, but treat speed as scored [S279] [Reported]. Do the three practice tiles in the Candidate Zone first [S262]. Have a calculator and scrap paper ready.

Stage 2. You rank or rate options, sometimes for how effective they are and sometimes for how much you would enjoy them [S280] [Reported]. Answer honestly and consistently. The same strengths framework reads your Stage 3 video, so an energetic video about client work will not fit a Stage 2 profile that says you dislike talking to people.

Stage 3: the work simulation and video

In Schroders' words: "A Work Simulation is an assessment that aims to give you a real insight into the role, tasks and team you will be working within ... One unique characteristic of the Work Simulation is that there will be a video interview as part of the assessment" [S262]. And: "It is not possible to pause the video interview or to retake them after completion. They are timed, and once the timer counts down it will automatically move you to the next question" [S262]. Schroders' advice: "Prepare 2 to 3 examples about yourself ... We'll be looking to understand how your experiences to date have shaped who you are, what you enjoy and what skills you have developed" [S262]. And: "It is not about being polished" [S260].

Set-up checklist (do this the day before)
  • Laptop on a stable surface, camera at eye level, light in front of you, plain background.
  • Dress "as you would for an interview" [S260]. "We focus on what you say, not where you are or how you look" [S262].
  • Close notifications; phone on silent; charger in.
  • Do the Candidate Zone practice for this stage first. The practice video question is not assessed [S277] [Reported].
  • Notes allowed on paper beside you: your 3 stories (one line each), your market view (3 numbers), your "why Schroders" (3 words).
  • If the timer is short: take one breath, say the first line of your answer structure, then speak.
A worked model, to rebuild in your own words and with your own story

Prompt: "Tell us about a time you explained something complex to someone with no background in it."

Energy (15s): "Explaining things is one of the parts of any role I enjoy most. I like the moment someone gets it."
Evidence (60s): "[Your real example: who, what was complex, the analogy you used, how you checked they understood, what happened next, with one number.]"
Learning (15s): "I learned to ask them to explain it back rather than asking 'does that make sense?'"
Link (15s): "That's a big part of Client Group: taking an investment team's view and making it useful to a trustee or an adviser, within what we're allowed to say."

Timing rule. Prep time is not published for Schroders. Older cycles reported 30 seconds of preparation and 2-minute answers [S289, S290] [Reported]. Practise a 60-second and a 2-minute version of each core answer. If the clock runs out mid-sentence you move on automatically, so put the link line early if time is short.

How to rank the workplace scenarios

You will usually see a short workplace situation and four or so responses to rank from most to least effective [S262, S280]. Four rules decide almost every ranking in a client-facing asset management seat [Inferred]:

  1. Rules first. Anything that sends unapproved, out-of-date or inside information goes to the bottom.
  2. The client gets an honest, timely answer. Acknowledge, give a time, keep promises.
  3. The owner decides. Escalate to the relationship manager or diary owner with options, rather than deciding alone or dumping the problem.
  4. Initiative inside the lines. Doing something useful now beats waiting, as long as rules 1 to 3 hold.

9.3 The question bank

Forty-four questions in six groups. Each shows what is really being tested, a structure (a scaffold, not a script), the facts to weave in and the trap. Rehearse out loud.

9.4 Questions to ask them

Pick two. Each can only be asked by someone who has done the research, and each is sourced.

Don't ask

"How many jobs will Nuveen cut?" (Nobody in the room can answer, and it sounds like it's about you.) "What does Schroders do?" "What are the hours?" (Ask your buddy later.)
