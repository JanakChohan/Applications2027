Part 6

The role's own industry: fund distribution and its rules

How asset managers get their products to clients, how that changed, who the gatekeepers are, and the regulations that shape every sales conversation. The regulatory dates here are the highest-leverage technical facts in the pack.

6.1 What "distribution" means

Distribution is everything that gets a fund from the investment team to the client: product design, marketing, sales, client service and the platforms and intermediaries in between [S150]. In most firms it is called "distribution". Schroders calls it Client Group and puts product, marketing and sales under one global head [S306, S91].

6.2 How fund selling changed: five steps

  1. The commission era (before 2013). Fund houses paid advisers commission for recommending their funds. Part of sales was about who paid more.
  2. The Retail Distribution Review (RDR), in force 31 December 2012. It banned product providers from paying commission to advisers and raised adviser qualifications. The FCA found in 2014 that "commission is no longer a driving factor in advisers' recommendations" [S177].
  3. The platform rebate ban, 6 April 2014. Platforms had to be paid by explicit charges; legacy payments ended in April 2016 [S178]. This created "clean" share classes and pushed fees down.
  4. Gatekeepers take over. Investment consultants (Mercer, WTW, Aon, LCP, Hymans Robertson) now shortlist managers for pension schemes using their own ratings [S198]. On the wholesale side, central fund-selection teams and model portfolios decide. Platform model portfolios passed £214bn at end-2025 [S193].
  5. The gatekeepers are regulated too. The Competition and Markets Authority's Order, made 10 June 2019, forced pension trustees to tender fiduciary management covering 20% or more of their assets and to set objectives for their consultants [S179].
Say this in the interview

"Since RDR removed commission in 2012, fund houses win on performance, value and service to professional buyers. That's why Consumer Duty and assessments of value matter so much to sales today."

6.3 How an institutional sale happens

The steps are general industry practice, not a single published Schroders process [Inferred]. A trustee board sets an objective. Its consultant screens the managers it rates. The shortlisted managers answer a request for proposal (RFP) and a due diligence questionnaire (DDQ) covering team, process, risk, fees, operations and sustainability. Three to six managers present in person (the "beauty parade"). One wins, negotiates fees and signs an investment management agreement. Then the real work starts: reporting, meetings and keeping the client for years.

Where an intern helps

Interns rarely pitch. They make pitching possible: updating pitch books with approved figures, drafting DDQ answers from the library, preparing meeting briefs, logging activity in the CRM (Salesforce at Schroders [S317]), organising events and researching prospects. Every one of these touches a rule described below.

6.4 The rules that bind a sales role

A salesperson at a UK asset manager works inside a thick rulebook. You do not need to know it like a compliance officer. You do need to know the six rules below well enough to spot when to stop and ask.

Fair, clear and not misleading
FCA COBS 4.2.1R		"A firm must ensure that a communication or a financial promotion is fair, clear and not misleading." 6.5 What is changing right now

  • Consumer Composite Investments (CCI), PS25/20. Replaces the PRIIPs key information document and the UCITS KIID with a flexible "product summary" showing costs, risk and past performance. Optional from 6 April 2026, mandatory from 8 June 2027 [S168]. That is one week before the internship starts. Every retail fund document changes.
  • Targeted support, PS25/22. Live from 6 April 2026. Authorised firms can suggest products to groups of consumers with similar circumstances, without full advice [S170]. The FCA says around 23 million people are underserved by advice or guidance [S170]. Early take-up is slow [S253].
  • Value for Money for DC pensions. FCA CP26/1 (8 January 2026) and the joint FCA/DWP consultation of July 2026 propose rating default funds on performance, cost and service, with first assessments in 2028 [S172, S173]. Poor-value managers will be switched out.
  • Consumer Duty scope. CP26/23 (29 June 2026) proposes clarifying the Duty and making it lighter for wholesale-focused firms [S256] [Reported].
  • Pension Schemes Act 2026. Royal Assent 29 April 2026: £25bn DC megafunds by 2030, six LGPS pools, a reserve power to mandate private-market allocations capped at 10% (5% UK) [S183, S254].
  • Cash ISA cut. From 6 April 2027 the cash ISA limit is £12,000 for under-65s [S187].
Say this in the interview

"The new CCI product summaries become mandatory on 8 June 2027, a week before the internship starts, and the cash ISA limit falls in April 2027. Both change what retail clients see and where their money goes, so I'd expect the intermediary team to be busy with both."

Don't say this

"PRIIPs KIDs" as if they were the future: they are being replaced. "Consumer Duty applies to everyone": it covers retail customers, and its scope for wholesale firms is under review. "Sustainable fund" loosely: the word is regulated in UK fund names.

6.6 What buyers value today

External clients and fund buyers

  • Clear value for the fee: Consumer Duty and assessments of value make this a rule [S161, S164].
  • Consistent outcomes, not star managers.
  • Access to private markets, with honest liquidity terms [S196].
  • Active fixed income: 81% of fund selectors surveyed by Rathbones expect a bigger role for it [S194] [Reported].
  • Building blocks that fit model portfolios: low charges, platform availability [S193].
  • Sustainability claims that survive the anti-greenwashing rule [S165].
  • Service: data, reporting and education.

Internal clients (fund managers and product teams)

  • Accurate intelligence on what buyers need and what rivals are doing.
  • A fair description of their strategy, never an oversold one.
  • A qualified pipeline, not a long list of maybes.
  • Sticky money rather than "hot money" that leaves after one bad quarter.
  • Feedback for product development, such as demand for an active ETF version or an LTAF wrapper.
  • Communications that pass compliance first time.
