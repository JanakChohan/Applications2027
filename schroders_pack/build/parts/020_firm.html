Part 2

The firm as a business, from the inside

Where Schroders came from, how money moves through it, what each party gives and gets, how risk is controlled, what the strategy is, and what changed this week.

2.1 The founding insight

The history starts in 1804, "when JH Schröder became a partner in J.F. Schröder & Co, a London-based firm founded by his brother JF Schröder" [S72]. J. Henry Schröder & Co was set up in London in 1818 [S81] [Reported]. For most of the next two centuries Schroders was a merchant bank: it financed trade and "became one of London's leading merchant banks, arranging bonds for transformative infrastructure", including an 1870 bond for Japan's first railway [S80].

Asset management grew out of that banking business. Schroders launched its first investment trust in 1924, set up an Investment Department in 1926 and won its first UK pension fund mandate in 1947. It listed on the London Stock Exchange in 1959, "oversubscribed eighteen times" [S80].

The decisive pivot came in 2000. Schroders sold its investment banking arm to Citigroup for about £1.35bn (US$2.2bn), with around 3,000 staff moving to Citi [S81, S82] [Reported]. Schroders' own history page says the sale was to focus on "asset and wealth management" [S80]. It then built out by acquisition:

  • 2005. Joint venture with Bank of Communications in China [S80].
  • 2013. Cazenove Capital, for about £424m [S81] [Reported].
  • 2019. A majority stake in BlueOrchard [S80].
  • 2022. River & Mercantile's UK solutions business, which became Schroders Solutions (enterprise value £230m) [S106] [Reported].
  • Announced December 2021. 75% of Greencoat Capital, for an initial £358m [S105] [Reported].
Why the history matters for a sales role

A merchant bank lives on relationships and reputation over decades. That DNA survives in the client model. The annual report notes 13 agreements with 12 institutional clients that are more than 25 years old, and one wealth family "served for over 75 years, across four generations" [S72]. [Inferred]

In the firm's own words today [S72]:

  • Purpose: "Creating prosperity together."
  • Vision: "We partner with our clients to provide trusted advice and invest in the assets and markets that matter to them, building their future prosperity through delivering excellent investment outcomes."
  • Values: "We strive for excellence. We promote innovation and teamwork. We have passion and integrity."

The brand campaign since 2025 is "Active Edge" [S71]. The JD's older line, "excellent investment performance to clients through active management", is still on the advert [S263].

2.2 How the firm makes money

In its own words: "We generate revenue by charging fees as a percentage of the assets we manage, and for the use of our platform or advisory services, where relevant. We also sometimes earn performance-based revenues, transaction fees and returns on seed and co-investments" [S72]. The next page traces every pound for 2025.

What the money flow shows.

  2. Each business charges a different rate on its assets. The rates range from 6bp for core solutions to 57bp for Schroders Capital [S71].

  5. Adjusted operating profit was £756.6m [S71].
  6. Dividends of £335.8m were paid in 2025, about 44% of them to the Schroder family group [S73, S72] [Inferred].

2.4 The risk and control architecture

Asset managers do not risk their own balance sheet the way banks do. The main risks are to clients' money, to reputation and to revenue. Schroders' structure, from the 2025 annual report [S72]:

  • The Board has "ultimate accountability for identifying, managing and monitoring the firm's principal risks". A combined Audit and Risk Committee reviews a quarterly risk dashboard.
  • The Chief Risk Officer chairs the Group Risk Committee, which meets five times a year. Its sub-committees cover conflicts, financial crime and information security.
  • Three lines of defence. The first line is the business, including Client Group. The second line was narrowed in 2025 to Risk and Compliance only. The third line is Internal Audit.
  • Principal risks for 2025.
    • Business model disruption
    • Financial risk
    • Information security and technology
    • Investment performance
    • Operational process
    • People and conduct
    • Regulatory (including financial crime)
    • Sustainability
    • Third-party risk: new in 2025 "as a result of our increased use of outsourcing"
    • Transformation risk
  • Liquidity rules for private-market funds. The liquidity risk framework was updated for open-ended private-market vehicles such as evergreen funds and LTAFs.
Key term: three lines of defence

A control model. The first line owns and manages risk day to day: the people doing the work, including you. The second line sets rules and checks them: Risk and Compliance. The third line independently audits both: Internal Audit.

Why this produces the firm's characteristic result. An active manager's product is trust over many years. The controls that matter most to Client Group are the ones that protect that trust: approvals for anything a client sees, conflicts management, and the liquidity rules that stop a fund promising more access than its assets allow [Inferred].

2.5 The operating model and the strategy

Richard Oldfield, a former PwC partner who joined as CFO in October 2023, became Group CEO on 8 November 2024 [S306, S90]. His plan, set out on 6 March 2025 as "simplify, scale and deliver", set these targets [S86, S87]:

  • £150m of annualised net cost savings by the end of 2027, with about £200m of costs to achieve them [Reported].
  • An adjusted cost:income ratio below 70%.
  • £20bn of cumulative net new business for Schroders Capital over three years.
  • Wealth Management net new business of 5 to 7% a year.
  • Public Markets focused on nine "leading capabilities" [S71]. The 23-person management committee was replaced by a nine-member executive committee [S301].

Delivery so far. More than 98% of the £150m savings had been delivered by H1 2026, and cost:income was 68% [S70]. The headcount measure fell 10% in 2025 [S72]. Technology and operations were outsourced further to UST [S71].

What was sold or closed.

  • The 49.9% stake in Schroders Personal Wealth was swapped with Lloyds for full ownership of Cazenove Capital (9 October 2025) [S94].
  • Benchmark was sold to Söderberg & Partners for about £215m (agreed 4 July 2026) [S70, S231].
  • The businesses in Brazil and Indonesia were exited [S70].

What was added.

  • European active ETFs, which reached $4.3bn by 21 September 2026 [S230].
  • A bespoke LTAF for Mercer (December 2025) [S71].
  • A partnership with Apollo (9 February 2026) [S96].
  • New Client Group leaders [S91, S93].

2.6 Public or private, and what that means

Schroders plc was a FTSE 100 company until the takeover. FTSE Russell confirmed it leaves the index from the start of trading on 1 October 2026 [S85] [Reported]. The half-year results of 30 July 2026 are very likely its last as a listed company [Inferred]. From here, public information will come from Nuveen and TIAA reporting, Companies House filings for UK subsidiaries such as Schroder Investment Management Limited, and fund documents [Inferred].

Don't say this

"Schroders' share price", "FTSE 100 constituent", "family-controlled" or "the UK's largest listed asset manager", in the present tense, after 1 October 2026.

2.8 What changed this week: the Nuveen deal

  • Terms. 590p cash plus up to 22p of permitted dividends, about £9.9bn and a premium of about 34% to the undisturbed price [S223, S76].
  • Support. The family's trustee companies, holding about 41%, backed it. More than 99% of votes were in favour [S76, S77].
  • Rationale. "one of the world's largest global active asset managers with a scaled public-to-private investment platform and approximately $2.5 trillion of assets under management" [S70].
  • What stays. "The Schroders brand will be retained, and London will serve as the Combined Group's non-US headquarters and largest office" [S71]. There will be "more than 3,100 professionals" in London [S76].
  • People. "For a period of two years following the Effective Date, Nuveen and Bidco do not intend to make any material reductions in the employee base of Schroders, other than reductions arising from the removal of listed company-focused roles" [S309].
  • Governance. Matthew Westerman replaces Dame Elizabeth Corley as chair, and Nuveen executives join the board [S228].
  • Dissent. JO Hambro, a top-25 shareholder, said the price was "10 to 15% below fair value" [S223] [Reported].
Your opinion goes here

Was selling right? For: "In a competitive landscape where scale can help deliver benefits, in Nuveen we see a partner that shares our values" (Oldfield) [S76]. Scale matters when margins fall and technology costs rise [S150, S154]. Against: the premium partly reflected five weak years for the shares [S125], and independence is gone. Model view: "The family chose scale over independence. For Client Group that means a much bigger shelf to sell, provided integration after the first year doesn't distract from clients."

2.10 The skeletons, told straight

  • Hong Kong, 2016. The Securities and Futures Commission fined Schroder Investment Management (Hong Kong) HK$1.8m for failing to disclose notifiable interests between 2005 and 2013. Schroders self-reported and was credited for cooperating [S102] [Reported].
  • Insider dealing by an employee. A former Schroders equities trader, Damian Clarke, was jailed for two years for insider dealing between 2003 and 2012 [S103]. This was individual misconduct, prosecuted by the FCA.
  • 1863. Schroders issued £3m of bonds for the Confederacy in the US Civil War [S81] [Reported].
  • Performance, not misconduct. The shares fell 25% in 2024, a third straight annual fall [S87]. Net outflows were £10.8bn in 2024 and £8.3bn in H1 2026 [S71, S70]. Wealth net new business was 2.7% in 2025 against a 5 to 7% target, "below our expectations" [S71].
  • Job cuts. About 200 roles in January 2025, and headcount down 10% over 2025 [S88, S72].
  • Head office. An unexpected £20m charge in 2025 for remedial building works at 1 London Wall Place [S71].
  • Not found. No SEC settlement and no FCA fine specific to Schroders Personal Wealth turned up in searches. That is absence of evidence, not proof [S71].
If asked about a weakness at Schroders

"Flows have been the weak spot. There were outflows in 2024 and again in the first half of this year. But the H1 outflow was mostly one £6.6bn low-margin mandate, and intermediary gross sales were up more than 40%. So the question for Client Group is keeping institutional clients through the ownership change while growing the higher-margin channels."
