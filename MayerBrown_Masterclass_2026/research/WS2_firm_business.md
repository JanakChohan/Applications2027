## 1. Findings

### A. Founding, mergers and footprint

**1881, Chicago.** "Adolf Kraus and Levy Mayer form a partnership in Chicago" (firm timeline, paraphrased by the fetch tool) [S70] [Confirmed]. The firm's timeline then gives these names:
- **1893:** Moran, Kraus, Mayer & Stein, after Illinois Appellate Judge Thomas A. Moran joined. It became Moran, Kraus & Mayer once Stein was elected a Cook County judge.
- **1909:** Mayer, Meyer, Austrian & Platt.
- **1970:** Mayer, Brown & Platt. A Washington DC office opened the same year. [S70] [Confirmed]

Wikipedia gives an early name of "Kraus, Mayer & Brackett" and lists Sears Roebuck among the early clients [S91] [Reported]. The firm's timeline is the more authoritative source.

**Rowe & Maw (London) was founded in 1895.** John Rowe died in 1899, and Frederick James Maw kept Rowe's name in the firm's title. Rowe & Maw merged with Beddington, Hughes & Hobart in 1975 [S70] [Confirmed]. **London therefore dates from 1895, not 2002.** The firm's September 2026 release says the London office marked "its 130th anniversary in the City of London" in 2025 [S80] [Confirmed].

**Continental Europe:**
- **1996:** Lambert & Lee was founded in Paris.
- **2001:** Mayer, Brown & Platt merged with Lambert & Lee (France) and Gaedertz Rechtsanwälte (Germany). This is the root of the Paris, Frankfurt and Düsseldorf offices. [S70] [Confirmed]
- **1998:** The US firm merged with Blanchfield Cordle & Moore of North Carolina [S70] [Confirmed].

**2002, transatlantic merger.** Mayer, Brown & Platt combined with Rowe & Maw to form Mayer, Brown, Rowe & Maw LLP [S70] [Confirmed]. Secondary sources date it to 1 February 2002 [S91] [Reported]. Mayer Brown International LLP, the English entity, was incorporated on **13 November 2002** [S77] [Confirmed]. **The name was shortened to "Mayer Brown" in 2007** [S70] [Confirmed].

### B. How the money works

**The model in one paragraph** [Inferred, a standard law-firm economics framework applied to the figures below].
- Revenue = lawyers × hours billed × realised rate.
- Net income (distributable profit) = revenue minus salaries (associates, non-equity partners, staff), rent, technology, insurance and other costs.
- Profit per equity partner (PEP) = net income ÷ equity partners.

There are three levers:
- **Leverage:** more fee-earners per equity partner.
- **Rate:** price per hour.
- **Utilisation:** hours worked per lawyer.

Mayer Brown's 2025 numbers show rate driving growth while hours fell.

Arithmetic from these figures [Inferred]:
- $762.5m ÷ 239 ≈ $3.19m, which matches the reported PEP.
- $2.17bn ÷ $1.25m ≈ **1,736 lawyers**, consistent with about 1,800 cited in April 2026 [S82].
- With about 239 equity partners, roughly **86% of lawyers are not equity partners**. That is high leverage.
- Net income is about **35% of revenue**.
- Hours fell about 4% while revenue rose about 10%, so the growth came from price and mix (rates above 10%, fewer and larger clients), not volume.

**Market context.** The 2026 Am Law 100 average PEP was $3.59m (+14.0%) [S118] [Reported]. Mayer Brown's $3.196m is below that average. The firm is a top-30-ish US firm by revenue ("likely keeping it among the 30 largest U.S. law firms", Bloomberg Law, July 2025) [S86] [Reported]. I could not confirm its exact 2026 Am Law 100 rank (see Gaps).

### D. Risk and control architecture

**Regulators.**
- MBI LLP is authorised and regulated by the SRA (no. 369822) [S76] [Confirmed].
- The auditor treated the following as laws with an "indirect impact" on the accounts: the "Solicitors Regulation Authority Code of Conduct, including the Solicitors Accounts Rules". The audit team "inspected compliance documentation, enquired over any risk and breaches in the year and reviewed regulatory returns" [S78] [Confirmed].

**The rules that build the risk function** (SRA Code of Conduct for Firms) [S110] [Confirmed]:
- **Conflicts:** "You do not act in relation to a matter or a particular aspect of it if you have a conflict of interest" (6.2). Exceptions apply only for substantially common interest or competing-for-the-same-objective cases, with informed written consent. This is why every new matter goes through a conflicts search before engagement [Inferred].
- **Confidentiality and information barriers:**
  - 6.3 requires you to keep client affairs confidential.
  - 6.5 lets a firm act against a client whose confidential information it holds only if "effective measures have been taken which result in there being no real risk of disclosure". In practice these measures are information barriers ("ethical walls") [Inferred].
- **Risk management:** "You identify, monitor and manage all material risks to your business" (2.5).
- **Compliance officers:** the COLP (9.1) and COFA (9.2) must take "all reasonable steps" to secure compliance with authorisation terms and the Accounts Rules.
- **Client money:** "You safeguard money and assets entrusted to you by clients and others" (5.2).
- **Anti-money laundering:** AML and counter-terrorist-financing obligations sit outside the Code but are "directly monitor[ed] and enforce[d]" by the SRA. Client due diligence (KYC) at client inception comes from the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 [Inferred; the regulations were not fetched].

**Professional indemnity insurance.** Insurer: **Liberty Mutual Insurance Europe Limited**, with **worldwide** territorial coverage [S76] [Confirmed]. A 2008 Law360 piece said the firm's malpractice cover was "reportedly wearing thin" after "a nearly $100 million settlement in 2005" and three Refco-related suits [S100] [Reported, partly paywalled; the subject of the 2005 settlement was not visible].

**Complaints.** The route is the matter or relationship partner first, then the Legal Ombudsman [S76] [Confirmed].

**SRA enforcement.** I found **no** SRA or SDT sanction against Mayer Brown International LLP in the searches run [Gap: not a register-level check]. In November 2024 the SRA issued scam alerts about fraudsters impersonating "Mayer Brown International LLP". These were warnings about **impersonators**, not about the firm [snippet] [Reported].

**Data security incident (August 2026).**
- Non-Billable (10 Aug 2026) reported that the extortion group Luna Moth claimed a Mayer Brown leak [S105] [Confirmed as read].
- The firm said "one of its personnel mistakenly sent a small number of documents to an unauthorised third party" who "had misrepresented their identity", and that its investigation found "an isolated incident and that the third party did not access our systems" [S105].
- Threat-intel sites attributed a claim dated 7 August 2026 to "SilentRansomGroup", which is reported to be another name for Luna Moth [S106] [Reported, low reliability; unverified group claim].

### F. Private partnership: what that means for research

Mayer Brown has no shareholders, no annual report, no earnings calls and no 10-K [Inferred]. Public financial information comes from three places:
1. **Self-reported figures to the American Lawyer.** These are not audited. The firm reprints them on its own site [S71][S72].
2. **UK LLP statutory accounts at Companies House**, which are audited and public [S78]. This is the single best primary source for the London entity.
3. **Press releases**, which are positive by design [S73][S80].

Partner pay, client concentration, the rate card and the UK revenue mix by practice are not disclosed. The LLP explicitly declined segment analysis as "seriously prejudicial" [S78]. Different definitions also mean US "profit per partner" and UK "profit per member" are not comparable (see Section 2) [Inferred].

### I. Skeletons, told straight

**1. Refco (2005 onwards): the most serious.**
- **What happened.** Refco, a commodities broker, collapsed in October 2005, days after its IPO, when it emerged that CEO Phillip Bennett had hidden a related-party receivable of more than $1bn.
- **Collins's role.** Joseph P. Collins was a partner at Mayer, Brown, Rowe & Maw and Refco's "primary outside counsel" [S94] [Confirmed, DOJ].
- **Prosecutors' case** [S94][S95] [Confirmed, DOJ]:
  - "On at least 17 different occasions from February 2000 through October 2005", lawyers under Collins's direction drafted documents for round-trip loans of more than $5.5bn that hid the debt.
  - Collins "falsely represented to Thomas H. Lee Partners and others" ahead of the $2.4bn leveraged buyout.
  - Investor and lender losses exceeded $2.4bn.
- **Criminal timeline:**
  - **2009:** first conviction. **Jan 2010:** sentenced to 7 years [S96] [Reported].
  - **Jan 2012:** the Second Circuit vacated the conviction because the trial judge had held an ex parte conference with a juror [S96] [Reported].
  - **16 Nov 2012:** convicted at retrial on 7 of 10 counts (conspiracy, securities fraud, false SEC filings, wire fraud) and acquitted on 3 [S94] [Confirmed].
  - **15 Jul 2013:** Chief Judge Preska sentenced him to **one year and one day** plus two years' supervised release [S95] [Confirmed].
  - **2014:** the Second Circuit affirmed, *United States v. Collins*, 581 F. App'x 59 [S97] [Reported, secondary summary].
  - US Attorney Preet Bharara said Collins "gave the legal profession a black eye" [S94] [Confirmed].
- **Civil side:**
  - Thomas H. Lee Partners sued the firm for at least $245m (2007), which the firm called "without merit" [S117] [Reported].
  - In *Pacific Investment Management Co. v. Mayer Brown LLP*, 603 F.3d 144 (2d Cir. 2010), the Second Circuit held that a secondary actor is liable under Rule 10b-5 only if the false statement is **attributed** to it at the time it is made. The investor claims against the firm and Collins failed [S98] [Reported]. This is now a leading case for professional advisers.
  - In the trustee litigation (*Kirschner*), the New York Court of Appeals in 2010 (on certified questions) confirmed a broad *in pari delicto* defence for outside professionals [S101] [Reported].
  - **June 2013:** Mayer Brown agreed to pay **an undisclosed amount** to Refco's bankruptcy trustee to settle claims that it aided the fraud, as disclosed in a Second Circuit filing [S99] [Confirmed as read, lead paragraph only].
  - Other fund and investor claims were reported as resolved around 2013 [snippet] [Reported].
- **Firm's position at indictment (2007):** its review showed it acted in a professional, competent and ethical manner [Reported, snippet].
- **Lesson** [Inferred]: this is why law firms have formal client-inception checks, transaction-structure review and escalation for unusual circular transactions.

**2. GM / JPMorgan UCC-3 termination (2008 error, litigated 2009 to 2017).**
- **The error.** In October 2008, Mayer Brown (acting for GM) prepared closing papers to pay off a roughly $300m 2001 synthetic lease. The papers mistakenly included a UCC-3 termination of the financing statement for a separate **$1.5bn 2006 Term Loan** syndicated through JPMorgan as agent. JPMorgan and its counsel approved the papers, and the filing was made [S102] [Confirmed].
- **Second Circuit, 2015.** After the Delaware Supreme Court answered a certified question (an authorised filing is effective "regardless of whether the secured party subjectively intends or understands the effect"), the court held the security interest **was** terminated: *In re Motors Liquidation Co.*, 777 F.3d 100 (2d Cir. 2015) [S103] [Reported].
- **The lenders' suit and its outcome.** The lenders, including the Oakland Police & Fire Retirement System, then sued Mayer Brown for malpractice and negligent misrepresentation (N.D. Ill., No. 15 C 6742, filed Aug 2015).
  - Judge Gettleman dismissed the case (22 June 2016).
  - The **Seventh Circuit affirmed on 28 June 2017**, *Oakland Police & Fire Ret. Sys. v. Mayer Brown, LLP*, 861 F.3d 644: "We agree with Judge Gettleman that Mayer Brown did not owe a duty to plaintiffs under any of these theories." [S102] [Confirmed].
  - **I found no Mayer Brown settlement.** The firm won on duty. How the term lenders fared in the bankruptcy avoidance action against them is outside Mayer Brown's own liability and was not researched [Gap].
- **Lesson** [Inferred]: this is the textbook case for closing checklists and the duty of supervising lawyers to check what a paralegal-prepared filing actually does. It is directly relevant to trainee work on CPs and filings.

**3. University of Hong Kong "Pillar of Shame" (Oct 2021).**
- Acting for HKU, Mayer Brown wrote to the statue's custodians setting a removal deadline of 13 October 2021.
- After public criticism it said its "legal advice is not intended as commentary on current or historical events". It then stopped acting on the matter, and HKU turned to a local firm [S111][S91] [Reported].
- **Lesson** [Inferred]: reputational and client-acceptance risk in politically sensitive jurisdictions. This was the background to the later Hong Kong restructuring, although no source links the two causally.

**4. Commercial Financial Services (late 1990s/2000s).** The firm was named in suits claiming CFS executives, Mayer Brown and Arthur Andersen misled investors in more than $1bn of securitisations [snippet] [Reported]. The outcome was not established [Gap]. It is probably connected to the "nearly $100 million settlement in 2005" mentioned by Law360 [S100], but this is **unverified**.

## 6. Interview angles

1. **Know the corrections cold.** Rowe & Maw dates from 1895, so London is 130+ years old. The 2002 merger created Mayer, Brown, Rowe & Maw. Brown & Wood went to Sidley. Van Gorp is Chair, Jeremy Clay is Managing Partner, and Chris Harvey has been London MP since 1 Oct 2026. Getting these right signals real research.
2. **The "repositioning" story in one breath:** "Revenue up about 10% to $2.17bn while hours fell. Rates rose more than 10%, the client list was trimmed from 7,600 to 6,316, and 56 lateral partners joined in targeted areas. London grew about 20%." Possible question: *"Is going deeper with fewer clients a risk to junior development, or an opportunity to see bigger matters end-to-end?"*
3. **Show you read the Companies House accounts. Almost no applicant will have done this.** "MBI LLP turnover rose from £163m to £186m in the year to April 2025, and profit before members' remuneration rose by a third. The members' report credits WIP management and lock-up. How does that discipline show up for a trainee: time recording, billing narratives?"
4. **Finance-led London.** Private credit, leveraged finance (the September 2026 Baker McKenzie team), structured finance (Van Gorp's own field) and capital solutions. Possible question: *"How do trainee seats in Banking & Finance interact with the New York-law side of the capital solutions platform?"*
5. **Asia rebuilt.** Ask how the London office works with Tokyo, which sits inside the English LLP, and with Singapore after the Hong Kong decoupling.
6. **AI with guardrails.** There is a mandatory firmwide curriculum using Harvey and Copilot, with human review of all outputs and citations checked. Possible question: *"How has the GenAI curriculum changed what a first-seat trainee does in a due diligence or CP exercise?"*
7. **Risk as a value, not a threat.** Explain conflicts (SRA 6.2), information barriers (6.5) and client inception/AML, then connect them to the lessons from Refco and GM:
   - Refco: client acceptance and scrutiny of unusual transactions.
   - GM: closing-checklist rigour.

   Use them carefully and respectfully. The angle is "what professional discipline looks like", not gotcha questions. GM is the safer example because the firm won on duty in the Seventh Circuit, and it is directly about the detail-checking trainees do.
8. **Private-partnership literacy.** Explain that PEP is net income divided by equity partners, that only about 14% of lawyers are equity partners, and why US PEP and UK "profit per member" are not comparable. This shows commercial awareness without overclaiming.
9. **Pay and culture facts to hold, not to lead with:** NQ £150k (matching the Magic Circle since July 2025), about 14-15 training contracts a year, retention around 80% (Legal Cheek).
10. **Avoid:** speculating on the Trump/EEOC issue (Mayer Brown was not targeted, and Van Gorp is "picking our spots"), naming specific clients as fact beyond those reported, and treating the August 2026 incident as a "hack" (the firm says misdirected documents, with no systems access).
