# WS8 — Why Mayer Brown (mechanism-based, non-transplantable)

Workstream 8 of the Mayer Brown Masterclass research pack. Prepared 2026-10-08 for SEO London "Mayer Brown Masterclass" (2 Nov 2026) and a London training contract application.

Source IDs: S300–S339 (rows in Section 4). Confidence tags: **[HIGH]** = primary source or audited filing read in full; **[MED]** = reputable secondary source read in full, or primary source read through a summariser; **[LOW]** = snippet only, inference, or sources conflict.

Research log: 26 web searches, 33 full fetches (WebFetch and direct download), plus a direct read of the Companies House PDF of Mayer Brown International LLP's audited group accounts. The PDF is a scanned image, so I read it page by page as images.

## Read first: four premises in the brief that the evidence corrects

1. **"Brown & Wood heritage" belongs to Sidley, not Mayer Brown.** Brown & Wood, the New York securitisation and capital markets firm, merged with **Sidley & Austin** in 2001 to form Sidley Austin Brown & Wood [S338][S339] [HIGH]. Mayer Brown's own firm history makes no mention of Brown & Wood [S300] [HIGH]. Mayer Brown built its structured finance strength itself: Chicago, Charlotte (office opened 1998 through the Blanchfield Cordle & Moore merger), New York and London [S300][S324]. **Do not say "Brown & Wood" in interview.** If you do, a Mayer Brown structured finance partner will notice straight away.
2. **"One-firm global partnership" is not how the firm describes itself.** Mayer Brown's own wording is: *"a global legal services provider comprising associated legal practices that are separate entities"* [S307] [HIGH]. In England the practice is **Mayer Brown International LLP** (OC303359), which has its own members, accounts and auditor (RSM) [S308][S309] [HIGH]. Say "integrated transatlantic platform", which is the firm's own phrase [S327]. Avoid "lockstep one-firm partnership".
3. **Asia is no longer a large Mayer Brown platform.** The 2008 combination with Johnson Stokes & Master (JSM) ended in 2024, when JSM became independent again [S300][S305] [HIGH]. Hong Kong is now 21 lawyers. Singapore has 41 and Tokyo 13 [S304] [MED].
4. **No Manila or Belfast shared-services centre was found** in anything I read. The White & Case Manila centre turns up in searches instead, so don't confuse the two. Treat any claim of a Mayer Brown offshore centre as **unverified** (Gaps, Section 5).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Why Mayer Brown is structured the way it is

**F1. Origins: a Chicago firm plus a London firm, joined in 2002.** Chicago founding: "Adolf Kraus and Levy Mayer form a partnership in Chicago" (1881). London: "UK firm Rowe & Maw founded in London" (1895). In 2002, "Mayer, Brown & Platt combines with Rowe & Maw, forming Mayer, Brown, Rowe & Maw LLP", and in 2007 that firm "becomes Mayer Brown" [S300] [HIGH]. Solicitors Journal (May 2026) describes 2025 as the firm's "130th year in the City of London", counting from Rowe & Maw's 1895 founding [S301] [MED]. So London is not a US satellite. It is a 130-year-old English practice that a US firm merged into, and the Companies House record still lists "MAYER, BROWN, ROWE & MAW LLP" as a previous name of OC303359 [S308] [HIGH].

**F2. Where London's strongest rankings sit today.** London's top-tier rankings are concentrated in asset-focused finance and real estate/construction, not in M&A or private equity:
- **Legal 500 UK 2027** (announced 30 Sep 2026), five Tier 1 rankings: Asset Based Lending ("thirteenth consecutive year"), Derivatives and Structured Products ("a new top tier ranking this year"), Trade Finance ("for the second time"), Commercial Property – Corporate Occupiers ("ninth consecutive year"), Construction – Contentious ("fifth consecutive year"). Ranked in 47 practice areas, with 34 Leading Partners and 12 Hall of Fame [S320] [HIGH].
- **Chambers UK 2026** (16 Oct 2025), top tier in Asset-Based Lending ("for the first time"), Construction: Contentious ("fifth consecutive year") and Construction: Professional Negligence ("19th consecutive year"). Ranked in 27 practice areas, with 10 Band One lawyers [S322] [HIGH]. Chambers UK 2027 had not been published as of 2026-10-08.
- Securitisation: Legal 500 Tier 2 [S317]. Pensions (non-contentious): Legal 500 Tier 2 [S315] [HIGH, read from the live Legal 500 page].

**Rowe & Maw heritage (inference) [LOW–MED].** The brief says the Rowe & Maw heritage shows in pensions, real estate and construction. The pattern fits: construction professional negligence has been top tier for 19 consecutive years, and pensions has a 30-lawyer core team. But no source I read explicitly attributes these practices to Rowe & Maw. If you use the point, present it as inference: "London's construction and pensions franchises are long-standing English practices rather than recent US imports."

**F3. What the US side contributes.** It brings structured finance scale and derivatives depth. Mayer Brown claims "more than 150 structured finance lawyers" and "one of the largest structured finance practices in the world" [S324] [HIGH, firm self-description]. It also says it "is one of the only firms with a leading derivatives practice in London, New York and continental Europe (in particular, Germany)" [S323] [HIGH as quote; MED as fact]. The group "frequently represents ISDA and other industry groups" [S323], and the London derivatives team lists ISDA as a key client [S316] [HIGH].

**F4. The 2024–26 repositioning: depth over breadth.** The American Lawyer (25 Mar 2026, reprinted on mayerbrown.com) reports:
- 2025 revenue up "nearly 10%, to $2.17 billion".
- Profit per partner (PEP) up "14.5%, to $3.196 million".
- Equity tier about 239 partners.
- Revenue per lawyer (RPL) $1.25m.
- Net income $762.5m.
- **Clients billed fell from 7,600 (2024) to 6,316 (2025).**
- Chair Jon Van Gorp: *"That's a concerted effort by us to go deeper with fewer clients"* and *"We're sustaining demand, but also repositioning the platform."*
- Stated focus areas: "leveraged finance, private credit, insurance, energy, infrastructure, capital markets, and white collar work" [S304] [MED].

The Hong Kong separation fits the same pattern. Van Gorp said the new set-up lets Mayer Brown "continue its presence in Hong Kong with a practice that aligns with our strategic priorities" (The Standard/Reuters, 3 May 2024) [S305] [HIGH].

**F5. London is the growth engine inside that repositioning.**
- 2025 London revenue was a record **$268.2m, +20.5%**.
- **8 lateral partner hires**.
- **96 transatlantic deals** worked jointly by London and New York lawyers.
- PE deal volumes **+95%**.
- Active structured finance matters **+14.8%** [S301][S302] [MED].
- London has "about 264 lawyers" [S304] [MED].

London managing partner Dominic Griffiths: *"We set out to build something distinctive - a platform where the best lawyers collaborate across borders"* [S301][S302]. And: *"As clients navigate an increasingly complex transatlantic environment, we are able to deliver coordinated, specialist advice from London, New York, and beyond"* [S302] [MED].

Legal Business headlined its interview with him *"We are not an outpost"* (30 Apr 2026). The article is paywalled after the opening; its excerpt paraphrases Griffiths as calling the growth a deliberate plan to deepen relationships with core clients across jurisdictions [S303] [PAYWALLED-NOT-READ beyond excerpt].

**F6. The 2026 lateral push is in leveraged finance and private capital, aimed at the PE gap.**
- **15 Apr 2026:** Sarah Goodwin joined from **Kirkland & Ellis**. London leveraged finance head Philip Butler called her *"a transformational addition to our team"*, adding that she is *"significantly enhancing both our private equity and private credit credentials"* [S327] [HIGH].
- **1 Sep 2026:** Rob Mathews, David Becker and Ben Bierwirth joined from Baker McKenzie. The firm described the hire as supporting *"a fully integrated platform for New York and English law debt and equity solutions"* [S328] [HIGH].
- Companies House confirms Mathews and Bierwirth were appointed LLP members on 1 Sep 2026 [S308] [HIGH].

**F7. What the structure buys.**
1. **Asset-finance depth that pure-M&A firms don't have.** Top tier in ABL, derivatives, trade finance and construction disputes [S320][S322].
2. **Cross-practice products** that need pensions, insurance, derivatives and finance in one firm. See F10, the Pensions Finance Practice.
3. **Credible transatlantic execution:** 96 joint London–New York deals in 2025 [S301].
4. **A small trainee cohort.** About 15 trainees a year, which Chambers Student links to "bespoke" training [S332] [MED].

**F8. What the structure costs.**
1. **Less brand in PE and M&A.** London has no top-tier corporate/M&A ranking in either guide's top-tier list [S320][S322]. The firm is importing PE finance talent from Kirkland [S327] rather than having grown it at home [inference, MED].
2. **A smaller intake:** about 15 trainees a year [S332][S333], so fewer NQ seats in each practice.
3. **A shrinking Asia footprint** since the JSM split [S304][S305].
4. **Lower London profit per member.** The LLP's FY2025 average profit per member was £861,333; the highest-paid member received £3,377,831 [S309] [HIGH]. This sits well below firm-wide US PEP of $3.196m [S304]. The two figures are measured differently (all members versus equity partners), so treat the comparison as directional only.
5. **Weak legal tech in trainees' eyes:** Legal Cheek 2026–27 scorecard grades it "C" [S333] [MED].

### 1.2 How teams work together across practices and offices

**F9. The firm's own collaboration wording** [S307] [HIGH]. Its Client Service Principles include:
- *"Collaborating across offices to deliver the best of our knowledge, wherever in the world it's needed"*
- *"Building diverse teams of lawyers from our market-leading practices to help achieve our clients' goals"*
- *"Leveraging our deep commercial instincts to serve as our clients' strategic partners and trusted advisors"*
- *"Using technology to develop new ways to deliver timely and efficient service"*

The industry groups listed are: Chemicals, Data Centers, Financial Services, Global Energy, Insurance, Funds & Investment Management, Projects & Infrastructure, Technology [S307] [HIGH].

**F10. The clearest example of collaboration: the Pensions Finance Practice and pension risk transfer (PRT).**
- **The Pensions Finance Practice** combines the pensions team with "finance, financial services, insurance, and derivatives lawyers" as a "one-stop shop". The firm says pension scheme advice increasingly involves "other teams in Finance, Employment, Tax, Litigation, Real Estate, Insurance and Corporate". The London UK Pensions Group has "a core team of 30 lawyers (including 7 partners)" [S314] [MED, read through a summariser].
- **Ford buy-in, 27 Oct 2025:** £4.6bn, the largest UK PRT deal announced in 2025. The team was led by **Tom MacAulay (Global Insurance Industry Group partner)**, with **Andrew Block (Pensions Group partner) as relationship partner** [S310] [HIGH]. A pensions relationship opened the door, and the insurance industry group ran the deal.
- **Royal London–Oxford Instruments, 22 Jan 2026:** £213m. The same MacAulay-plus-Block pairing advised the insurer [S312] [HIGH].
- **How the pairing was built:** MacAulay was hired from Legal & General in Sep 2024. Griffiths said he would "bring cross-office and practice expertise across corporate, finance and pensions" [S313] [HIGH].

**F11. Further evidence of cross-practice and cross-office working.**
- The Global Co-Head of Banking & Finance (Alex Dell) talks of offering clients "exceptional depth and breadth of expertise across borders" [S328].
- Goodwin cited the "integrated transatlantic platform … and collaborative culture" as her reason for joining [S327] [HIGH].
- Dominic Griffiths is listed on mayerbrown.com as a partner in both London and New York [S324] [HIGH].

**F12. Trainee seat rotation and working pattern.**
- Four six-month seats, with at least one transactional seat (finance, corporate or real estate) required. Dispute seats are capped at two.
- Finance has "nine subgroups".
- Secondments: Paris (international arbitration) is longstanding, and a New York rotation was "reportedly approved recently". There is a client secondment after a corporate seat.
- Hours: expected 7.2 hours a day, no billable target, roughly 40 hours a week on average.
- Cohort: 34 trainees across two years, 80% retention [S332] [MED].
- TargetJobs (2028/29 intakes) confirms four seats and "a client or international secondment including our New York and Paris offices" [S334] [MED].
- Legal Cheek puts the chance of an overseas secondment at 9% and a client secondment at 9% [S333] [MED].

**F17. The counter-case on clients.**
1. Many named clients (Barclays, BNP, JPM, Deutsche) also instruct the Magic Circle and US rivals, so these are not exclusive relationships.
2. On Haven, Kirkland advised the sponsor side as well [S306].
3. The number of clients billed fell 17% (7,600 to 6,316). That is either deliberate focus or attrition [S304].
4. On PRT, Mayer Brown acts for both trustees (Ford) and insurers (Royal London), so it must manage positional conflicts. That shows market breadth, but it also limits who it can act for on any one deal [inference].

### 1.4 Non-fee-earning side (business services)

**F18. Size, estimated from the audited LLP accounts** [S309] [HIGH data; estimate is mine]. Mayer Brown International LLP, group, year ended 30 April 2025:
- Average employees (excluding members): **429**, of which **227 "Office and Management"** and **202 "Professional"** (2024: 221 + 204 = 425).
- Average members: **92** (2024: 96).
- Turnover: **£185,723,625** (2024: £163,313,187), **+13.7%**.
- Staff costs: £62.8m. Members' remuneration charged: £79.25m.

**My estimate of business services relative to lawyers:**
- Headcount ≈ 429 + 92 = 521.
- Business services ≈ 227 / 521 ≈ **44%** of all people.
- That is ≈ **0.77 business services staff per fee-earner** (227 : 294).

**Cross-check.** 202 professional + 92 members = 294. Subtract about 34 trainees [S332] and you get about 260, close to the "about 264 lawyers" reported by Am Law [S304]. That suggests "Professional" includes trainees and fee-earning staff [MED].

**Caveats.** The accounts' categories are not defined. Some London partners may be members of other Mayer Brown entities. FY ends 30 April, so it doesn't align with the calendar-2025 $268.2m figure.

**F19. How business services are organised.** The careers page lists: *"Accounting & Analysis, Business Development & Marketing, Business Intake & Conflicts, Facilities, Human Resources, Information Technology, Knowledge Management, Paralegal, and Secretarial"* [S331] [HIGH]. No shared-services centre is named on that page. A London "Legal Innovation Analyst" job posting (supporting Harvey and Copilot adoption) appeared in search results, but the posting now returns HTTP 410 [SNIPPET].

**F20. AI and innovation.**
- **14 Apr 2026:** Mayer Brown launched a firmwide GenAI curriculum for "nearly 1,800 lawyers", with **Harvey** and **Microsoft Copilot** as the tools [S329] [HIGH].
- Chair Jon Van Gorp: *"Our approach is focused on amplifying lawyer capabilities and judgement, not replacing them."*
- Global CIO Evette Pastoriza Clift: *"Technology alone doesn't create client value."*
- Safeguards include *"mandatory human review of all GenAI outputs, citation checking requirements, and comprehensive data security measures"*. There is also an AI Champions programme [S329].
- **Legal IT Insider, 9 Jul 2026:** Pastoriza joined from Fried Frank in Sep 2024. Training is compulsory before anyone gets access to Harvey or Copilot. The firm takes a hybrid "buy v build" approach. Quote: *"The AI literacy piece is really fundamental to everything"* [S330] [MED].
- The GenAI initiative was **Highly Commended** for Innovation in Training at the FT Innovative Lawyers Europe 2025 [S325] [HIGH].
- **Tension:** trainees grade legal tech "C" in the Legal Cheek 2026–27 survey [S333] [MED].

## 6. Interview angles

### 6.1 The mechanism: why this answer can't be transplanted to a rival

Mayer Brown London is a 130-year-old English practice (Rowe & Maw) joined to a US firm with one of the largest structured finance groups in the world [S300][S324]. The two together win work that sits *between* practices, not just inside them:

- A **pension scheme's buy-in** is run by an *insurance* industry partner hired from L&G and opened by a *pensions* relationship partner (Ford £4.6bn; Royal London £213m) [S310][S312][S313].
- A **bank's clean-energy CLO** is a structured finance product applied to infrastructure (Standard Chartered/Apollo $3bn, FT "first-of-its-kind") [S325].
- **ISDA netting opinions, SRT credit-linked notes and risk participations** come from a derivatives team now ranked top tier, whose client list includes ISDA itself [S316][S320].

The 2025–26 strategy deliberately goes *deeper with fewer clients*: clients billed fell from 7,600 to 6,316 while revenue rose about 10% and London rose 20.5% [S304][S301]. The firm is now adding the leveraged finance and private capital layer through lateral hires from Kirkland and Baker McKenzie [S327][S328].

### 6.2 "Why Mayer Brown" in 30 seconds (raw material)

> "Mayer Brown London is strongest where finance products cross practice lines.
