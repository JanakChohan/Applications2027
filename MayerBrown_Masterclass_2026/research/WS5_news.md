### Card 2 - FY2025 financials: revenue ~$2.17bn, PEP $3.196m, London up ~20%
- **Headline:** "Mayer Brown Grew Revenue About 10% in Year of 'Repositioning'"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** The American Lawyer, reprinted on mayerbrown.com, 25 Mar 2026 [S220]. https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/news/2026/03/mayer-brown-grew-revenue-about-10-in-year-of-repositioning
- **What happened:** For 2025 (the 2026 Am Law 100 year), revenue rose nearly 10% to $2.17bn; profit per equity partner (PEP) rose 14.5% to $3.196m; net income rose 19.2% to $762.5m; revenue per lawyer up 11.6% to $1.25m. The equity partner count rose 4.1% to about 239 while lawyer headcount dipped slightly. Billable hours fell (about 2.8m vs 2.91m), the number of clients billed fell from 7,600 to 6,316, and billing rates rose by more than 10%. London (about 264 lawyers) grew revenue roughly 20%, with growth in structured finance and private credit. **[HIGH]** (note: these sit slightly before the April-October window but are the latest full-year figures).
- **Beginner explanation:** Revenue is total fees. PEP is the profit shared out per equity partner (the owners), and the market uses it to compare firms. Fewer hours and fewer clients alongside higher revenue means the firm is charging more per hour and focusing on bigger relationships: "go deeper with fewer clients" (Van Gorp).
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** A deliberate "repositioning". The firm is pruning small clients, raising rates and growing through lateral team hires rather than a merger: "Right now, we're pretty focused on executing our strategy".
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** Fewer, bigger clients means more complex, institutional work (banks, insurers, funds), and higher expectations on quality and commerciality from day one. It also explains how the firm can fund £150k NQ pay (Card 9).
- **Industry context:** One summary of the 2026 Am Law 100 says gross revenue rose 13.0% industry-wide and PEP 14.0% [S253-snippet, **LOW**]. On that basis Mayer Brown's revenue growth was slightly below the Am Law 100 average and its PEP growth roughly level with it. Check against The American Lawyer before quoting.
- **Question to raise:** "The firm billed fewer clients and fewer hours in 2025 but grew revenue. How does the London office decide which client relationships to deepen, and how does that change what junior lawyers work on?"

### Card 3 - Leveraged finance and high-yield team hire from Baker McKenzie (Sep 2026)
- **Headline:** "Mayer Brown further strengthens its market-leading Finance team with lateral team hire"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** Mayer Brown, 1 Sep 2026 [S226]. https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/news/2026/09/mayer-brown-further-strengthens-its-market-leading-finance-team-with-lateral-team-hire
- **What happened:** Three partners, Rob Mathews, David Becker and Ben Bierwirth, joined London Banking & Finance from Baker McKenzie. They do high yield bonds, term loan B and leveraged finance, and Bierwirth's practice also covers restructurings, debt exchanges and liability management. They join the Leveraged Finance and Private Capital group and the firm's "Capital Solutions" offering. **[HIGH]**
- **Other 2026 London hires (snippets):** Sarah Goodwin, leveraged finance partner from Kirkland & Ellis (Apr 2026) [S228, MED]; James Clarke, emerging markets/export finance partner from Baker McKenzie (Aug 2026) [S227, MED]; Mark Rajbenbach and Victoria Butcher, real estate/private equity partners (Feb 2026; Global Legal Post reports they came from Taylor Wessing) [S229, MED].
- **Beginner explanation:** Leveraged finance is lending to companies with high debt, typically to fund private equity buyouts. High yield bonds are the bond-market version, usually written under New York law. A "team move" brings a ready-made practice and clients with it.
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** It fills a gap. US-law high yield capability in London links the firm's London private equity and private credit clients to its US finance and restructuring strength. It is also the "lateral team" growth strategy described in Card 2 being carried out.
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** It creates more leveraged finance and capital markets work in London, where finance seats are the core of the training contract. Trainees will see liability management exercises (refinancing or restructuring debt out of court), which are a growth area as rates stay high.
- **Question to raise:** "How are the leveraged finance hires working with the private credit and restructuring teams, for example on liability management exercises where a borrower might be refinanced by a direct lender instead of the bond market?"

### Card 4 - Pension risk transfer: Royal London buy-ins and a £10.2bn H1 market
- **Headline:** "Mayer Brown advises Royal London on £208 million BPA Transaction with the Hickson UK Group Pension Scheme"; market: "Total value of risk transfer deals in H1 2026 hits £10.2bn"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** Mayer Brown, 30 Jul 2026 [S223] https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/news/2026/07/mayer-brown-advises-royal-london-on-208-million-pound-bpa-transaction-with-the-hickson-uk-group-pension-scheme ; Pensions Age, 22 Sep 2026 [S237] https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/Total-risk-transfer-deal-value-hits-10-2bn-during-H1-2026.php
- **What happened:** Mayer Brown (led by Tom MacAulay, Global Insurance Industry Group, with Henry Corrigan) advised insurer Royal London on a £208m bulk purchase annuity (BPA) covering 1,250+ members of the Hickson scheme, which the firm calls "Royal London's third-largest external BPA transaction to date". It follows a £213m Royal London buy-in with the Oxford Instruments scheme (Jan 2026) [S224, MED]. Market-wide, Hymans Robertson data puts H1 2026 PRT volumes at £10.2bn (H1 2025: £9.8bn) across 135+ deals (down from 161). Hymans expects H2 to beat H1, saying: "Several large transactions have already completed since 30 June, and the pipeline is strong." **[HIGH]**
- **Beginner explanation:** A company with a defined benefit (final salary) pension scheme pays a lump-sum premium to an insurer, which then takes on the obligation to pay the pensions. In a "buy-in" the insurance policy is an asset of the scheme, and in a "buy-out" members become direct policyholders. Higher gilt yields have improved scheme funding, so many schemes can now afford to insure.
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** Insurance is "always been a really strong brand for us" (Van Gorp, [S220]). Acting for insurers on BPAs brings in repeat work, and the market is dominated by many small deals (Aon: 82% of deals under £100m, [S237 search snippet]), which suits mid-market insurers like Royal London.
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** It is a distinctive London practice combining pensions, insurance regulation and contract work. Trainees typically help with the policy terms, data and member issues, and the due diligence.
- **Question to raise:** "With deal counts down but values up, and insurers competing hard for smaller schemes, how is the role of insurer counsel changing? Is there more standardisation of buy-in documents, and does that leave room for AI-assisted drafting?"

### Card 5 - Mayer Brown's firm-wide GenAI curriculum (Apr 2026)
- **Headline:** "Mayer Brown launches comprehensive GenAI curriculum to equip all lawyers with advanced AI skills"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** Mayer Brown, 14 Apr 2026 [S230]. https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/news/2026/04/mayer-brown-launches-comprehensive-genai-curriculum-to-equip-all-lawyers-with-advanced-ai-skills . Also reported by Law360 Pulse ("Mayer Brown Focuses On Increasing AI Adoption In 2026") [S251, PAYWALLED-NOT-READ].
- **What happened:** A mandatory curriculum, rolled out across 2026, for nearly 1,800 lawyers plus business services staff. It covers responsible use (human review of every output, citation checking, data security), training on the firm's tools (Harvey and Microsoft Copilot named), practice-specific scenario training, partner training on leading AI-enabled teams, supervised workshops on real matters, a prompt library, and "AI Champions". Van Gorp: "Our approach is focused on amplifying lawyer capabilities and judgement, not replacing them." **[HIGH]**
- **Sector context:** Legora (Harvey's main rival) launched a UK "Legal AI Scholars Programme" with King's College London, City Law School and BPP on 29 Sep 2026 [S246, MED]. A secondary report says Sidley made an AI seat compulsory for London trainees [S246 search, **LOW**, firm source not found]. Harvey was reported at an $11bn valuation and Legora at $5.55bn in March 2026 [MED, snippet].
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** High-volume document work (loan documents, securitisation transaction documents, due diligence) is where AI helps most. The firm's 2025 model of fewer hours at higher rates (Card 2) fits a future where routine hours shrink.
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** Expect AI training from day one, with the expectation that you check everything the tool produces. The traditional "learn by doing the grunt work" model is under pressure, so judgement and verification skills matter more.
- **Question to raise:** "The curriculum requires human review of every AI output. In practice, how do trainees build the judgement to review AI work if AI is doing more of the first drafts they would traditionally have learned from?"

### Card 6 - UK securitisation reform (FCA CP26/6 and PRA CP2/26) and private credit stress
- **Headline:** "New Proposals for reforming the UK Securitisation Framework" (Mayer Brown insight); BoE "Financial Stability Report - July 2026"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** Mayer Brown, 24 Feb 2026 [S232] https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/new-proposals-for-reforming-the-uk-securitisation-framework ; Bank of England FSR July 2026 [S243, SNIPPET]; Alternative Credit Investor, 30 Sep 2026, "BoE warns private credit vulnerable to rising rates" [S243b, SNIPPET].
- **What happened:** On 17 Feb 2026 the FCA and PRA proposed loosening UK securitisation rules. Investors would do proportionate, principles-based due diligence instead of tick-box verification (making it easier to buy non-UK deals such as US CLOs). There would be a new "L-shaped" option for the 5% risk retention, prescribed reporting templates for most asset classes would go, and the public/private distinction would be dropped. Consultations closed 18 May 2026, FCA final rules are expected in H2 2026, and PRA implementation is anticipated around Q2 2027 (per Mayer Brown). The authors include Dominic Griffiths, Neil Hamilton, Chris McGarry and Adam Farrell. **[HIGH]** At the same time, the BoE's Financial Policy Committee has named private credit as one of three main stability risks (with sovereign debt and AI-linked equity valuations). It notes liquidity pressure in some US private credit funds and warns that higher rates raise debt-servicing pressure. **[MED - snippets]**
- **Beginner explanation:** Securitisation pools loans (mortgages, car loans, receivables) and sells bonds backed by them in tranches of different risk. "Risk retention" makes the originator keep 5% "skin in the game". Private credit is lending by non-bank funds. Regulators want to make securitisation easier to support growth, but they are also nervous about opaque leverage outside banks.
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** Securitisation and structured finance are Mayer Brown's signature London practice. The AmLaw piece credits structured finance and private credit with London's growth. Rule changes create advisory work now and new deal structures later. Private credit stress would create restructuring work.
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** Finance seats here will involve this regime. Knowing what risk retention and STS mean, and why the rules are being relaxed, is a strong differentiator at a masterclass.
- **Question to raise:** "Is there a tension between the regulators loosening securitisation rules to boost growth and the Bank of England warning about leverage in private credit? Which of the reforms do clients care about most: due diligence, transparency templates or the L-shaped retention?"

### Card 7 - Bank of England holds at 3.75%, split 6-3 with three votes for a hike (Sep 2026)
- **Headline:** "Bank Rate maintained at 3.75% - September 2026"
- **Outlet / date / URL:** Bank of England, 17 Sep 2026 [S236]. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026
- **What happened:** The MPC voted 6-3 to hold. Greene, Mann and Pill voted to raise to 4%. CPI was 3.1% in August (about 0.7pp of the overshoot from energy) and is forecast at about 3¾% in Q4 2026 and slightly above 4% in Q1 2027. Brent was $106 and UK gas 207p/therm on 14 Sep, up 36% and 78% since July, which the Bank links to "Protracted conflict in the Middle East". Next decision: 5 Nov 2026 (three days after the masterclass). **[HIGH]**
- **Wider context:** The 2026 Iran war and disruption in the Strait of Hormuz caused major energy-price shocks (Brent briefly above $126 on 30 Apr per Oil & Gas Journal) [S250, MED - snippets; volatile, check latest].
- **Beginner explanation:** The base rate drives borrowing costs across the economy. Rates were expected to fall, but the energy shock has pushed inflation back up, so markets are now pricing a possible rise.
- **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** Rates drive almost every practice. Higher-for-longer rates mean more stress on leveraged borrowers (restructuring, liability management) and a stronger PRT market (higher gilt yields improve scheme funding). They slow M&A and IPOs and put pressure on real estate valuations (relevant to the new MP's practice). Energy and Middle East volatility also hit the firm's Middle East growth plans, which Van Gorp flagged in March [S220].
- **Why it matters to a trainee:** This is the single most useful macro fact to have ready. It links neatly to any practice area the firm presents on 2 November.
- **Question to raise:** "If the MPC does hike in November, which of the London practices would feel it first? Is restructuring a counter-cyclical hedge for the firm?"

### Card 12 - London listings revival and capital markets reform
- **Headline:** "London Stock Exchange IPOs surge 215% as UK listings rebound in 2026" (London Loves Business, citing EY) [S244, SNIPPET]; "UK Capital Markets Reform: Where Are We Now?" (Skadden, Sep 2026) [SNIPPET].
- **What happened:** EY counted seven LSE listings raising £577m in H1 2026, up 215% on H1 2025's £183m, but from a very low base. The UK still lost large companies to overseas listings [MED]. - **Why it matters to Mayer Brown:** The firm's London capital markets work is mostly debt, not equity IPOs. The relevance is indirect: issuers, sponsors exiting via IPO, and the high-yield team (Card 3).
- **Question to raise:** "Do you think London's reforms will bring back equity listings, or is the real growth in London debt capital markets and private markets, where Mayer Brown is strongest?"

## 6. Interview angles and reading list

### Six ready-to-use angles (link news to Mayer Brown)
1. **"Deeper with fewer clients" + AI = a different trainee experience.** Fewer hours, higher rates and a mandatory GenAI curriculum (Cards 2 and 5) point to a future where junior value comes from judgement and client understanding. Say what you would do to build that judgement.
2. **Structured finance is the engine of London.** The AmLaw piece credits structured finance and private credit, the former MP is a securitisation partner, and the regulators are rewriting the UK securitisation rules (Cards 1, 2, 6). One sharp observation on risk retention or due diligence reform will stand out.
3. **Independence vs scale.** Cadwalader, a direct structured finance rival, is now part of a ~$3.6bn firm. Mayer Brown is growing through lateral teams (Cards 3, 8). Have a balanced view: scale brings breadth and cross-selling, while independence keeps culture and avoids integration risk and conflicts.
4. **Rates and energy as a single lens.** A 6-3 hold, inflation heading towards 4% and energy shocks from the Middle East (Card 7) link to restructuring/LMEs (Card 3), PRT pricing (Card 4), real estate valuations (Card 1) and IPO caution (Card 12).
5. **Insurance as an underrated strength.** Repeat Royal London mandates in a £10bn+ half-year market (Card 4). Ask about longevity risk, funded reinsurance and PRA scrutiny (topics to research further).
6. **Global one-firm deals.** Nigeria ports (London) and Zankore AI (Singapore with London hedging, DC sanctions advice and Dubai) show cross-office teamwork, and a London trainee is named on the ports deal (Card 10).

### Questions to avoid
- Anything about pay or bonuses.
- Claims about Mayer Brown and the Trump administration.
- Treating the H1 IPO rebound as proof that London is "fixed".
