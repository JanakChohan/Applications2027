# WS3 – Competition: Mayer Brown vs London-relevant peers

Prepared 2026-10-08 for the SEO London "Mayer Brown Masterclass" (2 Nov 2026) and a Mayer Brown London training contract application. Source IDs S120–S149. Confidence tags: **HIGH** (primary source or multiple consistent sources, read in full), **MEDIUM** (one reliable secondary source, or a primary source with an inference), **LOW** (snippet only, conflicting, or unsourced background).

Peer set chosen (9): **Sidley Austin, Latham & Watkins, Kirkland & Ellis, White & Case** (US firms in London that pay at the US rate), **Hogan Lovells Cadwalader** (transatlantic, and since 1 July 2026 includes Cadwalader, the obvious structured-finance comparator), **Clifford Chance, A&O Shearman** (Magic Circle comparators), and **Norton Rose Fulbright, Baker McKenzie** (big full-service global firms with a finance bias). Simmons & Simmons and Dentons appear only through the league tables and the NQ table.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Headline positioning

1. **Mayer Brown is the smallest firm by revenue in this peer set, but its profit per equity partner (PEP) sits in the middle.** 2025 revenue was $2.17bn (up about 10%, its first year above $2bn) and PEP was $3.196m (up 14.5%), with about 239 equity partners [S122][S123]. Every peer in the table reports more revenue: NRF over $2.8bn, Clifford Chance about $3.5bn, Baker McKenzie $3.6bn, White & Case $3.6bn, A&O Shearman $3.7bn, Sidley $3.74bn, Latham $8.3bn and Kirkland $10.6bn [S125][S129–S137]. On PEP, Mayer Brown ($3.196m, or £2.36m per Legal Cheek [S121]) is above Clifford Chance (£2.3m), Hogan Lovells Cadwalader (£2.67m pre-merger HL figure; on a sterling basis this is close to or above Mayer Brown, see the table note), A&O Shearman (£2.2m), Baker McKenzie (£1.8m) and NRF ($2m). It is well below Sidley (£4.46m), White & Case ($4.4m), Latham ($8.7m) and Kirkland ($11.1m) [S129–S137]. **HIGH** (all figures are firm-reported or Legal Cheek; FX conversions differ by outlet).

2. **The firm says itself that it is "repositioning" in 2026.** Chair Jon Van Gorp told The American Lawyer (reprinted by the firm on 25 Mar 2026) that the firm is "sustaining demand, but also repositioning the platform". The plan has three parts: go "deeper with fewer clients" (billed clients fell from 7,600 to 6,316), expand in London and Asia, and put more weight on finance, energy, infrastructure, insurance and white-collar work [S123]. **HIGH.**

3. **London is a growth office.** London revenue was a record $268.2m in 2025 (up 20.5%), with about 264 lawyers, laterals in structured finance and private credit [S121][S123], and a "targeted push into private capital" [S122-adjacent LB coverage; see Gaps]. Sidley's London office, the closest US-firm comparator, made $299m with about 200 lawyers [S129]. White & Case London made $584m [S132]. **HIGH** for the figures, **MEDIUM** for the strategy wording (the Legal Business interview is mostly paywalled).

4. **Pay tier: Magic Circle-level, not US-level.** Mayer Brown raised London NQ pay from £135k to £150k in July 2025, "bringing the Chicago-founded firm in line with the Magic Circle on NQ pay" [S128]. Legal Cheek's 2027 table (September 2026) still has it at £150k, the same as Clifford Chance, A&O Shearman and Baker McKenzie. Sidley, Kirkland and White & Case pay £175k and Latham £173,077 [S120]. Trainee pay is £56k/£61k, identical to the Magic Circle [S121][S133][S149]. **HIGH.**

5. **Intake: one of the smallest cohorts in the set.** Mayer Brown takes about 15 trainees a year [S121][S149]. Only Kirkland ("up to 15") is comparable. Sidley takes 19, Latham 32, Baker McKenzie 40, Hogan Lovells Cadwalader 45, White & Case and NRF 50, A&O Shearman 70 and Clifford Chance 100 [S129–S137]. **HIGH.**

6. **The market changed in July 2026: Cadwalader is now part of Hogan Lovells Cadwalader.** The merger went live on 1 July 2026, creating a firm with more than 3,000 lawyers and over $3.6bn in revenue, described as "the fifth largest legal player by revenue" [S126]. That combines the classic Wall Street structured-finance specialist with a big full-service transatlantic firm. In London rankings, HLC sits in the **same** Legal 500 securitisation tier as Mayer Brown (Tier 2) and **below** it in derivatives (Tier 3 vs Tier 1) [S141][S142]. **HIGH** for the merger facts, **MEDIUM** for the Legal 500 edition (see Gaps).

### 1.2 Where Mayer Brown London leads: Legal 500 UK top tiers

7. Mayer Brown's Legal 500 UK 2027 release (30 Sep 2026) lists five London top-tier rankings [S139]:
   - Finance: Asset Based Lending, 13th consecutive year
   - Finance: Derivatives and Structured Products, "a new top tier ranking this year"
   - Finance: Trade Finance, "for the second time"
   - Real Estate: Commercial Property – Corporate Occupiers, 9th consecutive year
   - Real Estate: Construction – Contentious, 5th consecutive year

   It is ranked in 47 practice areas overall, with 74 individual lawyer rankings [S139]. **HIGH** (primary source).

8. **Chambers UK 2026** (Oct 2025): 27 practice areas ranked and three top bands: Asset-Based Lending (first time), Construction: Contentious (5th year) and Construction: Professional Negligence (19th consecutive year). Ten lawyers are Band 1 [S140]. **HIGH** (primary source; the full PDF was not read).

### 1.3 Where Mayer Brown does NOT lead (stated plainly)

9. **London securitisation is Tier 2, not Tier 1.** Legal 500 London Tier 1 is Clifford Chance, Latham, Linklaters, **Sidley** and A&O Shearman. Mayer Brown is in Tier 2 with HLC, White & Case, NRF, Simmons, Dentons and others [S141]. So "Mayer Brown is the securitisation firm" is true **in the US ABS market** but **not in the London rankings**. **HIGH.**

10. **London pensions is Tier 2, not Tier 1.** Tier 1 is CMS, Eversheds, **Hogan Lovells Cadwalader**, Linklaters, Sackers, Slaughter and May, **A&O Shearman** and Pinsent Masons. Mayer Brown is Tier 2. Pensions disputes is Tier 4 [S145][S146]. Pensions is a genuine London practice, but it is not where the firm leads. **HIGH.**

11. **M&A, private equity and leveraged finance are not strengths in the London rankings.** Legal 500 places Mayer Brown London in Tier 6 for M&A (upper mid-market and premium, £750m+), Tier 3 for mid-market PE, Tier 4 for acquisition finance, Tier 4 for premium commercial litigation, and Tier 7 for EU and competition [S146]. That contrasts sharply with Kirkland and Latham, and with the private-capital push the firm describes [S122][S123]. **HIGH** for the tiers.

12. **It does not pay the US rate, does not have the top PEP, and does not have the top retention rate.** NQ pay is £150k against £175k at Sidley, Kirkland and White & Case [S120]. Its trainee retention was 80%, against 100% at White & Case, 96% at Latham, 95% at NRF and 87% at Kirkland. That is still above Clifford Chance (78%), A&O Shearman (77%), HLC (67%) and Baker McKenzie (67%) [S121][S129–S137]. **HIGH.**

13. **It has shrunk in Hong Kong.** The JSM combination (2008) was unwound in 2024: JSM "re-establishes itself as an independent law firm" [S138]. Press reports say about 125 lawyers went with JSM and Mayer Brown kept a separate Hong Kong branch of about 26 lawyers [S127]. The firm's own 2026 figures give Hong Kong 21 lawyers, with Singapore at 41 and Tokyo at 13. The chair still calls Asia "a very big bright spot" [S123]. Older "Asia footprint" talking points (for example "the biggest international firm in Hong Kong") are **out of date and should not be used.** **HIGH** for the firm timeline and 2026 headcounts, **MEDIUM** for the split numbers (snippet).

14. **It lost the No. 1 US ABS issuer-counsel spot in 2025.** Mayer Brown ranked **2nd** as Top Issuer Counsel for US ABS/MBS in Asset-Backed Alert's 2025 tables (146 deals, about $111bn) [S147]. Asset-Backed Alert's own report says Hunton came first by deal count (159 deals, $60.6bn) and that Mayer Brown "slipped from its usual first-place perch", but Mayer Brown's dollar volume ($111.7bn) was higher [S124]. **HIGH** for the firm's release, **MEDIUM** for the ABA report (snippet of a third-party-hosted PDF).

### 1.4 Things that can be said of Mayer Brown and of no peer in this set

Each claim is limited to the 9-firm peer set and to sources read on 2026-10-08.

- **U1. Only firm in the set that is Legal 500 London Tier 1 in both Derivatives & Structured Products and Asset Based Lending.** The ABL Tier 1 has just two firms, Addleshaw Goddard and Mayer Brown [S144]. None of the nine peers is in the ABL Tier 1; NRF is Tier 2 [S144]. In derivatives, Clifford Chance and A&O Shearman are also Tier 1, but neither appears in ABL [S142][S144]. **HIGH.**

- **U2. Only US-founded firm in the London derivatives Tier 1.** The Tier 1 firms are Ashurst Perkins Coie, Clifford Chance, Linklaters, Mayer Brown and A&O Shearman [S142]. The US peers rank lower: Sidley and White & Case Tier 2, HLC Tier 3, Latham Tier 4, Kirkland "firm to watch" [S142]. (Caveat: A&O Shearman and Ashurst Perkins Coie have US legacy halves from mergers, but both are UK-origin firms.) **HIGH** for the tiers, **MEDIUM** for the "US-founded" framing.

- **U3. Combines a London Tier 1 derivatives ranking with sustained recognition as the leading US ABS law firm.** GlobalCapital named Mayer Brown "ABS Law Firm of the Year" for the sixth consecutive year and "SRT Law Firm of the Year" in May 2026. The firm reports "more than 150 structured finance lawyers" across the Americas, Asia and Europe [S148]. Sidley, the main US ABS rival, was 5th issuer counsel with 58 deals [S124] and is Tier 2 in London derivatives [S142]. Clifford Chance and A&O Shearman are Tier 1 in London but are not in these US issuer-counsel rankings [S124]. **MEDIUM** (the GlobalCapital award is reported by the firm and the GlobalCapital write-up is labelled sponsored content; the ABA top-five is a snippet).

- **U4. Only firm in the set pairing Magic Circle-equal pay (£56k / £61k / £150k) with a cohort of about 15.** The Magic Circle peers pay the same but take 70–100 trainees [S133][S135]. Kirkland has a similar cohort but pays £70k / £75k / £175k [S131]. **HIGH.**

- **U5. Only firm in the set Tier 1 in trade finance and also Tier 1 in derivatives.** Trade Finance Tier 1 includes Mayer Brown, NRF and HLC. White & Case is Tier 2, and Sidley and Clifford Chance are not listed [S143]. NRF and HLC are not Tier 1 in derivatives [S142]. **HIGH** (the trade finance page fetch returned tiers only for the named firms; see Gaps).

- **U6. A US-headquartered finance firm with a long-standing London top-band construction disputes practice.** Chambers UK ranks Construction: Professional Negligence top tier for the 19th consecutive year and Construction: Contentious for the 5th [S140]. Legal 500 also ranks Construction – Contentious top tier [S139]. This comes from the 2002 Rowe & Maw merger, which gave the firm a 1895 London heritage [S138]. **MEDIUM** (peer construction rankings were not checked one by one, so "no peer" is unconfirmed; use the phrasing "unusual for a US firm").

- **Not unique, so do not overclaim:** the Brazil association with Tauil & Chequer (since 2009 [S138]; still active in 2026 per the firm [search snippet, see Gaps]). Other global firms also have Brazilian associations; this was not verified per peer in this pass. Present it as a differentiator of the platform, not as something unique.

**Ready-to-use lines (each tied to evidence):**

1. **vs Sidley ("the other Chicago finance firm"):** "Sidley beats Mayer Brown in London securitisation and pays more. I'm drawn by Mayer Brown's Tier 1 derivatives and structured products practice (Sidley is Tier 2), its Tier 1 ABL and trade finance, and the fact that it has been GlobalCapital's US ABS Law Firm of the Year six years running. I want the broader structured-products side, not just securitisation." [S141][S142][S143][S144][S148]

3. **vs Clifford Chance / A&O Shearman:** Same pay and the same Tier 1 in derivatives, but a cohort of about 15 against 100 and 70. That means a Chambers Student-reported culture of "non-hierarchical", "open-door" teams [S149] and more responsibility per trainee, while the US network still gives access to US ABS and CLO market work [S147].
4. **vs Hogan Lovells Cadwalader:** HLC is now the obvious "transatlantic structured finance" rival after the 1 July 2026 merger [S126]. Mayer Brown is ranked higher in London derivatives (Tier 1 vs 3) [S142], equal in securitisation (Tier 2) [S141], and pays £5k more at NQ (£150k vs £145k) [S120]. HLC leads in pensions (Tier 1 vs 2) [S145]. Asking how the merger has changed the competitive landscape for Mayer Brown's structured finance group is a smart Masterclass question.
5. **vs NRF / Baker McKenzie:** Both are Tier 1 or close in trade finance or ABL, but bigger and more diffuse (40–50 trainees, 59–70 offices) [S136][S137]. Mayer Brown pays £10k more NQ than NRF [S120] and has a higher PEP [S122][S136][S137].

Mayer Brown was overtaken by Hunton as No. 1 US ABS issuer counsel by deal count in 2025, though it still led on dollar volume [S124][S141][S145][S123][S147].

   - "Derivatives and structured products reached Legal 500 top tier this year. What drove it, and how much of the work is transatlantic?" [S139]
