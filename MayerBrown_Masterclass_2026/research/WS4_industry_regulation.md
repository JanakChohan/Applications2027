## 1. Findings

### 4A. The industry from zero

**1.1 What a commercial law firm sells.** A commercial law firm sells legal advice and transaction execution to businesses, banks, funds, insurers, pension schemes and governments. Typical work includes structuring and documenting deals (M&A, financings, securitisations, derivatives), handling disputes (litigation and arbitration), and advising on regulation and compliance. Pricing has traditionally been by the hour. Thomson Reuters' 2026 UK report says that for UK buyers "legal expertise is now the price of entry, not the point of differentiation": clients pick firms for commercial judgement and practical guidance [S167] [H]. Even in late 2026, one opinion piece cites a report that more than 90% of US legal spend is billed by the hour [S185] [L, US figure from secondary opinion].

**1.2 Who the clients are.** In TR's framing, corporate in-house legal departments ("legal buyers") are the core clients. In 2026, 69% of corporate legal respondents named staffing and resource limits as their main barrier to delivering value, and 44% of UK buyers planned to raise international legal spend [S167] [M: figures from search snippet of the TR report; the fetched page confirmed the themes]. Mayer Brown's London practices (finance, structured finance and securitisation, derivatives, pensions, financial regulation) serve banks, asset managers, insurers, pension trustees and corporates. Section 4B maps the regulators that shape that demand.

**1.3 Market size (dated, and the measures differ).**
- **TheCityUK (published 9 Dec 2025, data for 2024):** UK legal services revenue was £52.3bn, up 11% year on year, of which the top 100 firms accounted for £37bn. The sector added a record £38bn of GVA (1.5% of UK GVA), ran a £8.9bn trade surplus and employed 364,000 people, nearly two-thirds of them outside London. The UK is the largest legal market in Europe and second in the world after the US. More than 200 foreign firms from over 40 jurisdictions are in London. Alternative business structures (ABSs) make up 13% of firms in England and Wales [S164] [H].
- **Law Society (as reported by Non-Billable, SNIPPET):** £34bn sector revenue in 2024, with London firms at £23bn (68%). 72 firms have turnover above £100m and together earn 53% of revenue [S165] [M]. This is a different measure from TheCityUK's (probably solicitor firms only). Always say which measure you are quoting.
- **IRN Legal Reports (via Legal Futures, 28 Jan 2026):** forecasts the market rising 6.8% to £59bn in 2026 and reaching £69bn by 2029. Corporate and commercial property together make up 51% of market value [S166] [M: third-party market report].
- **SRA population data (SNIPPET):** about 178,400 practising solicitors (July 2026) and roughly 8,900 regulated firms (April 2026) [S163] [M: cached SRA pages disagree slightly].

**1.4 How firm types differ.** Category labels below are industry convention; numbers are sourced.
- **Magic Circle** (Allen & Overy, now A&O Shearman; Clifford Chance; Freshfields; Linklaters; Slaughter and May). These are London-headquartered elite firms. Clifford Chance revenue rose 9% to £2.6bn in FY to 30 April 2026, and Linklaters' rose just under 7% to £2.5bn in FY26; both cite US growth as a driver. All except Slaughter and May have begun US expansions [S168] [M].
- **US firms in London** (for example Kirkland, Paul Weiss, Davis Polk, Latham, and Mayer Brown, which was founded in Chicago). Among London's top 50, US firms grew revenue 14.7% "last year" against 7.1% for UK firms (Law.com analysis via The Telegraph and Canadian Lawyer, 29 Jun 2026). 14 of the 18 London firms with revenue per lawyer above £1m are US firms. Kirkland partners averaged over £8m, against £2.1–2.2m at Clifford Chance and Linklaters [S168] [M].
- **Transatlantic and global merged firms:** A&O Shearman, HSF Kramer, Ashurst Perkins Coie, Winston Taylor (see 1.7).
- **Mid-tier, national and boutique firms** compete on price or specialism. NQ pay ranges from about £109k to £140k (Pinsent Masons, Eversheds, NRF) [S170, S172] [M].
- **Barristers (the Bar) and chambers.** Barristers are self-employed specialist advocates, grouped in chambers and usually instructed by solicitors. They are regulated by the BSB, not the SRA. This workstream did not research the Bar; see Gaps.
- **In-house lawyers** work inside clients. Clients are adopting AI faster than their law firms: 53% of UK corporate legal respondents report organisation-wide AI use, against about 35% of law firm respondents [S167] [M].
- **Big Four and ALSPs.** On 27 Feb 2025 the Arizona Supreme Court licensed KPMG Law US as an ABS, making KPMG the first Big Four firm licensed to offer legal services in the US [S188] [M]. In the UK, ABSs (non-lawyer ownership under the Legal Services Act 2007) make up 13% of firms [S164] [H].

**1.5 Core economics vocabulary.**
- **Billable hour:** time recorded against a client matter and charged at an hourly rate [S185] [M].
- **Leverage:** the ratio of associates (or all fee earners) to partners. "One partner can supervise multiple people below them", which is why AI-driven efficiency threatens the pyramid [S185, S212] [M].
- **Utilisation:** billed hours as a share of available or target hours. **Realisation:** the share of the value of recorded time that is actually billed and collected. Both are standard definitions; I did not fetch a primary source for them [L, definitional only].
- **PEP (profit per equity partner):** firm profit (revenue minus all operating costs) divided by the number of equity partners. It is not each partner's take-home pay [S212] [M].
- **Lockstep vs "eat what you kill":** lockstep pays by seniority; EWYK pays by the revenue each partner generates. Kirkland is the commonly cited EWYK example, and many firms use a "modified" lockstep [S212, S213] [M].
- **Equity vs non-equity partner:** equity partners own a share of profits (and of risk); non-equity or salaried partners carry the title but are paid mainly by salary [S212] [M].
- **Trainee:** a pre-qualification solicitor doing a two-year training contract or QWE. **NQ:** a newly qualified solicitor. Mayer Brown London trainees earn £56k in year 1 and £61k in year 2 (AllAboutLaw listing, unofficial) and NQs £150k [S169, S173] [M].

**1.6 Structural forces now.**

*(a) Generative AI.*
- On 6 May 2025 the SRA authorised **Garfield.Law Ltd**, the first law firm in England and Wales to provide regulated legal services through AI. It is an AI litigation assistant for recovering unpaid debts of up to £10,000 through the small claims process. Fees start at £2 for a chaser letter. The system cannot propose case law (a hallucination safeguard), clients must approve each step, and named regulated solicitors remain accountable. SRA CEO Paul Philip called it "a landmark moment" [S159, S160] [H].
- **Harvey** raised $200m at an $11bn valuation in March 2026 (Bloomberg Law snippet). Reports of a $15.5bn round in Aug/Sept 2026 are unconfirmed [S180] [M for $11bn, L for $15.5bn].
- **Legora** raised a $550m Series D at $5.55bn (reported March 2026), and reports in 2026 suggested talks at up to $8.5–10bn [S181] [M/L].
- TR's **CoCounsel** is a third major platform. I did not research it in this session [Gap].
- **Mayer Brown itself:** on 14 Apr 2026 it launched a firmwide GenAI curriculum for "nearly 1,800 lawyers worldwide", deploying **Harvey and Microsoft Copilot**. The programme includes mandatory human review of all GenAI outputs, a prompt library, practice-specific tracks and AI Champions [S179] [H].
- Peers: Latham has an enterprise Harvey licence for over 3,600 lawyers, and Ashurst ran a Harvey pilot with over 525 users in 23 offices before rolling it out [S182] [M].
- TheCityUK reports 82% of lawyers using or planning to use AI tools [S164] [H].

*(b) Alternative fee arrangements (AFAs).* Commentary through 2026 points to hybrids rather than an end to hourly billing: caps, blended rates, success fees and portfolio pricing. Partner compensation would need redesigning under fixed fees [S185] [L: opinion and vendor material; no authoritative quantified source found].

*(c) The NQ salary war (London, 2025–26).*
- **Magic Circle:** £150k, unchanged since autumn 2024 [S172, S168] [M].
- **Mayer Brown:** raised NQ pay from £135k to £150k (+11%) in July 2025, matching the Magic Circle. It takes "up to 14 trainees each year" [S169] [H].
- **US firms:** Paul Weiss, Davis Polk and Gibson Dunn £180k; Quinn Emanuel £189k (June 2026); Ropes & Gray £170k from 1 Jan 2026 [S168, S171] [M].
- **Others:** Macfarlanes £150k from 1 Jul 2026 [S172] [M]; NRF £140k from 1 Apr 2026, matching Ashurst, Hogan Lovells and (then) Macfarlanes [S170] [H]; HSF Kramer and Baker McKenzie £145k (July 2025) [S169] [H].

*(e) Private equity and outside capital.*
- **US:** the main route is the MSO (management services organisation) structure. Investors buy into the non-lawyer "business of law" entity because ABA Model Rule 5.4 bars fee-sharing with non-lawyers [S186, S187] [M].
- **Arizona:** abolished its Rule 5.4 (effective 1 Jan 2021) and had approved 136 ABS entities by 30 Apr 2025 [S186, S188] [M].
- **Deals:** reports say McDermott explored a PE stake (Nov 2025) [S187] [L].
- **UK:** ABSs have been possible since the Legal Services Act 2007. 19 of 62 UK law-firm M&A deals in 2025 (31%) involved PE, including Lawfront, Fletchers and Beyond Law [S166] [M].
- **Cautionary UK precedents:** Slater and Gordon's goodwill write-down and Co-operative Legal Services' retrenchment [S187] [L].

*(f) US firms' growth in London and consolidation.* See 1.4 and 1.7(d). Clifford Chance's FY25 statement cited 18% revenue growth in the US [S168] [M].

**1.7 Three or more competing views on the next 3–5 years.**
1. **Radical disruption (Susskind, Legal Futures, 28 Sep 2026).** AI will be "reliable for most practical purposes" by the early 2030s. Human judgement will not protect lawyers ("the judgement objection falls away"). Elite firms see AI as "a way of boosting their profits under the old pyramidic structure". Winners will "build the systems that will replace their old ways of working", and start-ups will beat "the old gatekeepers" [S183] [H for his stated views].
2. **Client-led incremental change (TR Institute, 20 May 2026).** Demand is steady but spend is cautious: net spend anticipation is +5 points, and buyers are "more cautious about external legal spend than they have been at any point in the last five years". Clients are "moving faster than their outside legal advisors" on AI and now expect firms to use it. The value proposition shifts to commercial judgement, not raw expertise [S167] [H].
3. **Scale and consolidation (market structure view).** Transatlantic mergers (1.6d) and the London revenue gap (US firms +14.7% vs UK firms +7.1%) suggest an elite tier of very large, US-linked firms pulling away. PE capital is reshaping the mid-market and consumer end (31% of UK law-firm M&A involved PE) [S168, S166] [M].
4. **Sceptical counterweight (older academic view).** Remus & Levy (reported 5 Jan 2016) argued that "predictions of imminent and widespread displacement of lawyers are premature" because counselling and unstructured work resist automation [S184] [M: dated, pre-GenAI; cite as historical counterpoint only].
5. **Pyramid restructuring (commentary, Jan 2026).** Commentators argue the associate pyramid is being "structurally dismantled". Others argue it will flatten into a "diamond" because AI also reaches partner-level drafting [S185] [L].

### 4B(i). How solicitors qualify in England and Wales

**1.8 SQE route (since 1 Sept 2021).** The SQE was introduced from **1 September 2021** after the Legal Services Board approved it (Oct 2020). The first SQE1 was sat on 8 and 11 November 2021, and the first SQE2 in April 2022 [S152, S153] [H]. The route has these elements:
- a degree, or equivalent qualification or experience (standard SRA requirement; not separately fetched) [M];
- SQE1 (Functioning Legal Knowledge: FLK1 and FLK2, multiple-choice);
- SQE2 (practical legal skills);
- **two years' full-time (or equivalent) qualifying work experience (QWE)**, confirmed by a solicitor with direct experience of the work, real-life work that develops at least two of the competences in the Statement of Solicitor Competence [S151] [H]. Secondary sources add that QWE can be gained in no more than four organisations [S151 search context] [M];
- the character and suitability requirements.

**1.9 Transitional (LPC) route.** Candidates who before 1 Sept 2021 had completed, started, accepted an offer for, or paid a deposit on a QLD, CPE/GDL, LPC or training contract may still qualify the old way. The SRA says: "If you choose to qualify through the LPC route, you will have until 31 December 2032 to apply for admission" and "After 2032, you can only qualify through the SQE route." Anyone who began a QLD in September 2022 or later is not eligible. Most providers are phasing out the LPC in 2025/26, with only a few authorised for 2026/27 (page updated 25 Nov 2025) [S150] [H]. Transitional candidates can use QWE plus an SQE2 pass in place of a period of recognised training [S151] [H].

**1.10 Training contract vs QWE.** A "training contract" is now an employer's branded two-year programme that also counts as QWE. Mayer Brown's direct training contract has an application deadline of 31 Mar 2027, with intakes in March or September 2029 [S173] [M: job-board listing]. QWE is broader: paralegal work, law clinics, placements and in-house roles can all count [S151] [H].

**1.11 SRA rules framework.** The **SRA Standards and Regulations (StaRS)** replaced the SRA Handbook on **25 November 2019**. They include the **Code of Conduct for Solicitors, RELs, RSLs and RFLs**, separate from the Code for Firms (individual code last updated 11 Apr 2025 per the SRA index) [S154] [M: snippet of the SRA index].

**1.12 SQE pass rates and fees (latest).**
- **SQE1 July 2026:** 42% overall pass rate (6,315 candidates sat both parts; first-time sitters 47%). The record low was 41% in July 2025 [S155] [H].
- **SQE1 January 2026:** 53% overall (58% for first attempts), with persistent ethnicity gaps: White 67%, Asian 47%, Black 38% [S156] [M].
- **Fees:** for 2025/26, SQE1 £1,934 and SQE2 £2,974 [S157] [M]. From September 2026 (2026/27), SQE1 is **£2,006** and SQE2 **£3,086**, a total of £5,092 (+3.7%) [S158] [M].

**1.13 Recent SRA reform activity (post-Axiom Ince).** Axiom Ince was closed by SRA intervention in October 2023 after about £60–64.5m of client money was misappropriated. A Legal Services Board review found the SRA had not acted adequately [S161] [M]. Consultations since then:
- an accountants' reports consultation (11 Dec 2025 to 20 Feb 2026) [S161] [M];
- a consultation on mandatory notification of M&A activity and of starting to hold client money (19 Jun to 17 Aug 2026; single news source) [S161] [L/M].
Work on client account interest and Compensation Fund reform was deferred to H2 2026 [S162] [M].

### 4B(ii). Regulation shaping Mayer Brown's core London practices

**1.14 Securitisation.**
- **The Securitisation Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/102)** were made on **29 January 2024** under FSMA 2000 and FSMA 2023 powers. Regulation 2(2) brings the main provisions into force on "the day on which the revocation of the EU Securitisation Regulation 2017 by section 1(1) of, and Schedule 1 to, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 comes into force" [S192] [H].
- That "main commencement day" was **1 November 2024**, when the new regime (SI plus FCA and PRA rules) took effect [S193] [M: law-firm sources; I did not fetch the commencement SI]. SI 2024/102 was amended by SI 2024/705 (made 22 May 2024) [S215] [M].
- **Next reform wave:** on **17 Feb 2026** the FCA (CP26/6) and PRA (CP2/26) consulted on substantial simplification, closing **18 May 2026**. Proposals include principles-based investor due diligence (no longer verifying risk retention or STS compliance for non-UK deals, which would ease UK investment in US CLOs), deleting prescribed reporting templates for most asset classes, a new "L-shaped" 5% risk retention option, and two exemptions from the resecuritisation ban [S194, S195] [H].
- **Timing:** FCA final rules are expected H2 2026; PRA implementation is anticipated Q2 2027. Changes need amendments to legislation and to both rulebooks. The updated SRT supervisory statement SS9/13 is set for 1 Jan 2027 [S194, S195] [M].
- Mayer Brown's own commentary (24 Feb 2026) concluded that the regulators found "the current rules have led to excessive complexity and high compliance costs" [S194] [H].

**1.15 Basel 3.1.** The PRA published **PS1/26 (final rules) on 20 January 2026**. Basel 3.1 rules "will take effect on Friday 1 January 2027". The FRTB internal model approach takes effect on 1 January 2028, with an interim market risk period from 1 Jan 2027 to 31 Dec 2027 [S190] [H]. The history of the date: originally 1 Jul 2025, delayed in Sept 2024 to 1 Jan 2026, then in Jan 2025 by a further year (after consulting HM Treasury) to 1 Jan 2027, citing uncertainty over US implementation. Full phase-in remains 1 Jan 2030 [S191] [M].

**1.16 Derivatives.**
- **UK EMIR Refit** reporting went live on **30 September 2024** (FCA and Bank of England). Firms had until 31 March 2025 to uplift outstanding trades, and 95% were uplifted. The FCA received breach notifications for 267 distinct reporting issues [S196, S198] [M].
- **LIBOR** ended on **30 September 2024** with the final publication of the 1-, 3- and 6-month synthetic USD LIBOR settings. Sterling synthetic 3-month LIBOR had last been published on 28 March 2024 [S197] [M/H: FCA page via snippet].

**1.17 Pensions.**
- **Virgin Media Ltd v NTL Pension Trustees II Ltd [2024] EWCA Civ 843** (Court of Appeal, July 2024) held that amendments to contracted-out ("section 9(2B) rights") benefits made between 6 Apr 1997 and 6 Apr 2016 without the actuarial confirmation required by s37 were void [S214] [M].
- **Pension Schemes Act 2026:** Royal Assent on **29 April 2026**. Clifford Chance's 30 April briefing says "yesterday" [S200] [H].
- **The Virgin Media fix** came into force on Royal Assent. Schemes can obtain retrospective written actuarial confirmation, unless they have taken "positive action" to treat the amendment as void, or the validity question was in issue in legal proceedings begun on or before **5 June 2025** [S199, S200] [H].
- **Surplus release:** a new trustee statutory power, with the threshold possibly moving to full funding on the low dependency basis. Regulations are due by the end of 2027, and direct surplus payments to members are expected from 6 Apr 2027 via a Finance Bill [S200] [H].
- **Superfunds:** a permanent TPR authorisation regime replaces the 2020 interim regime, and "gateway principle two" is dropped. Consultation is expected in 2026, with the regime in force in **2028** [S200] [H].
- **DC scale:** each scheme needs a £25bn "main scale default arrangement" by 2030. A reserve asset-allocation power cannot be used before 1 Jan 2028 [S199, S200] [H].
- **Pension risk transfer:** 2025 saw a record **367 buy-ins totalling £38.2bn**, down from £47.8bn in 2024 and £49.1bn in 2023. H2 2025 had over 200 deals, and longevity swaps reached a record £25.9bn (LCP, 27 Mar 2026) [S201] [H]. Hymans counts 380 risk transfers in 2025 and more than £500bn of liabilities insured in total [S202] [M].

**1.18 FSMA 2023, the Mansion House reforms and growth policy.**
- **FSMA 2023** is the statute that revoked retained EU financial services law, including the EU Securitisation Regulation, and gave the FCA and PRA rule-making powers to replace it [S192, S195] [H]. Royal Assent date: see Gaps.
- **Mansion House Accord (13 May 2025):** 17 pension providers pledged at least 10% of main default funds in private markets by 2030, with at least 5% in the UK [S207] [M].
- **Leeds Reforms and Mansion House speech (15 July 2025):** Rachel Reeves's package covered ringfencing reform, streamlining the Senior Managers regime, limits on the Financial Ombudsman, a Listings Taskforce and a retail investment campaign [S206] [M].

**1.19 Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023.** Royal Assent was on 26 October 2023 [S205] [M]. Home Office guidance was published on 6 Nov 2024, and the "failure to prevent fraud" offence (s199) came into force on **1 September 2025** [S203] [H]. It applies to "large organisations" meeting two of three thresholds: more than 250 employees, more than £36m turnover, more than £18m total assets. The defence is having reasonable fraud prevention procedures [S204] [M].

**1.20 Private credit growth and scrutiny.**
- The **FCA** published a private market valuation review on **5 Mar 2025**. It flagged weak conflicts management, varying independence and inconsistent ad hoc revaluations, and called for granular credit-risk analysis for private credit [S210] [M].
- On **4 Dec 2025** the **Bank of England** launched its second system-wide exploratory scenario (SWES), focused on private markets. Global PE and private credit grew from about $3tn to about $11tn in a decade, and UK PE-sponsored firms account for up to 15% of corporate debt and about 10% of private-sector jobs. The scenario phase began in June 2026, with a final report in early 2027 [S208] [H].
- The **July 2026 Financial Stability Report** says private credit vulnerabilities "persist and in some cases have intensified" [S209] [M].
- One analysis puts UK banks' banking-book exposure to private markets and leveraged sponsor-backed firms at about £173bn [S211] [L/M].

**1.21 Competitive map: who competes for this work.**
- **Securitisation and structured finance in London:** Magic Circle firms (Clifford Chance, A&O Shearman, Linklaters), US firms (Mayer Brown, Dechert, Skadden, Sidley, Latham, Milbank and others), and international firms (HSF Kramer, Hogan Lovells, Ashurst Perkins Coie). Evidence: these firms all published client notes on the same 2026 consultations [S194, S195, S193]. This is an inference from publications, not a ranking [L].
- **Pensions:** Linklaters, Clifford Chance, Sackers (boutique, not researched), Hogan Lovells, Squire Patton Boggs, Womble Bond Dickinson and others published Act briefings [S199, S200] [L as a market map].
- **Non-law-firm competitors:** Big Four and ALSPs (KPMG Law US) [S188]; legal AI vendors (Harvey, Legora) [S180, S181]; AI-native firms (Garfield.Law) [S159] [M].

**1.22 What clients value.**
- Commercial judgement over raw expertise, with more than a third of UK buyers citing business savviness [S167] [H].
- Quality and effectiveness improvements from AI before cost savings [S167] [H].
- **BTI Client Service A-Team 2026:** Mayer Brown is reported as a **newcomer to the BTI Client Service 30**, the top tier, among 258 firms ranked [S189] [M: snippet; position not verified].
