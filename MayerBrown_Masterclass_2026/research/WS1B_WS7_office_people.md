## 6. Interview angles

1. **"Why Mayer Brown London?" Use the hard numbers.**
   - London grew 20.5% to $268.2m in 2025, against about 10% firm-wide [S50][S53].
   - The 2025 statutory accounts show profit per member up about 39% [S43].
   - Then link to the strategy behind it: transatlantic work (96 London and New York deals) and "acquiring talent through teams" [S50][S53].
   - Ask: "How has the 2025-26 leveraged finance build-out (Dechert, Baker McKenzie, Kirkland) changed what trainees see in the finance seats?"
2. **Know the franchises by tier.**
   - Legal 500 Tier 1: ABL (13 years running), Derivatives and Structured Products (new), Trade Finance, Corporate Occupiers (real estate) and Contentious Construction [S54].
   - Chambers Band 1: construction (London), plus ABL (top tier for the first time) [S56][S57].
   - A strong line: *"Mayer Brown is unusual among US firms in London because its top rankings are in asset-based and structured finance and construction disputes, not just PE."*
3. **New London MP.**
   - Chris Harvey took over on 1 October 2026. He is a real estate partner who joined in 2012 and did the Walkie Talkie deal [S48][S64].
   - Griffiths has moved to the global Management Committee.
   - A good question: "With a real estate MP following a finance MP, do you expect any shift in London priorities?" Ask it neutrally.
4. **Training Principal Miriam Bruce.**
   - She joined as a trainee in 2005, qualified in 2007 and made partner in 2020. She is in The Lawyer Hot 100 2026, set up the Business Protection practice, and is Chambers Band 1 for High Court employment litigation [S57][S62][S63].
   - She is a living example of the firm's "grow your own" story. Her modern languages background is relevant if the candidate has languages.
   - Ask her about the Business Protection practice (restrictive covenants and team moves). It ties neatly to the firm's own lateral hiring wave.
5. **Ed Parker and derivatives.**
   - The practice is newly Legal 500 Tier 1. Parker was CLLS President and Master of the City Solicitors' Company in 2024-25 [S67].
   - He literally wrote the book on credit derivatives. For commercial awareness, read up on SRT and synthetic securitisation, since bank capital relief is his current focus.
6. **Size and intake.**
   - London has about 264 lawyers, about 15 trainees a year and about 90-100 partners. The ratio of partners to associates is low, so trainees work closely with partners. The Legal Cheek A* for partner approachability supports this [S60].
   - Use this to argue for responsibility and exposure over a mega-intake firm (White & Case takes about 50).
7. **Diversity and the SEO link.**
   - SEO London is on the firm's list of access schemes [S61]. The firm has a 2013 founding partnership with Aspiring Solicitors and has moved outreach beyond Oxbridge and redbrick universities [S59][S296].
   - The 2025 pay gap widened (mean 21.1%, median 41.8%), and the firm attributes this to the mix of business services roles [S47].
   - Raise this only if invited, and tactfully. For example, ask how term limits for leadership roles [S47] work in practice.
8. **Seat strategy.**
   - At least one transactional seat is mandatory and contentious seats are capped at two. Construction is a popular seat. Secondments to New York and Paris are possible [S58][S59].
   - Prepare a plausible four-seat plan, for example: Banking & Finance (ABL or leveraged finance), Real Estate, Construction disputes, and Derivatives or a client secondment.
9. **Things to avoid.**
   - Do not assert who the firm-wide managing partner is.
   - Do not cite Rare.
   - Do not name a "graduate recruitment partner" other than Bruce, the Training Principal.
   - Do not describe register exits as "defections".
   - Do not quote US 50 rankings, which were not read.
