Part 14

Glossary

Every term used in this pack, in plain English.
TermMeaning
ABS (asset-backed securities)Bonds paid from a pool of loans or receivables, such as car loans or credit cards.
ABS (alternative business structure)A law firm that non-lawyers may own or invest in, allowed in England and Wales since the Legal Services Act 2007.
Acquisition financeLoans used to buy a company.
AFA (alternative fee arrangement)Any pricing other than the hourly rate: fixed fees, caps, success fees.
Am Law 100The American Lawyer's annual ranking of the 100 largest US law firms by revenue.
AML / KYCAnti-money laundering checks; "know your client" identity checks before taking a client on.
Asset-based lending (ABL)Loans secured on a borrower's assets such as receivables, inventory or equipment.
Assessment centreA day of exercises (written, group, interview) used to choose vacation schemers or trainees.
Basel 3.1The final set of global bank capital rules; in the UK from 1 January 2027 (PRA PS1/26).
Billable hourTime recorded to a client matter and charged at an hourly rate.
BPA (bulk purchase annuity)An insurance policy that covers pensions for many members of a scheme; a buy-in or buy-out.
BundleThe indexed set of documents prepared for a court hearing.
Buy-in / buy-outA pension scheme insures its pensions with an insurer; in a buy-out members become the insurer's policyholders.
CLO (collateralised loan obligation)A securitisation of leveraged loans.
CMBSCommercial mortgage-backed securities: bonds backed by loans on commercial property.
COLP / COFAThe firm's Compliance Officer for Legal Practice and Compliance Officer for Finance and Administration, required by the SRA.
Conditions precedent (CPs)Things that must be delivered or done before a loan can be drawn or a deal can close.
Conflict of interestWhere acting for one client would harm another or the firm's own interest; firms must not act (SRA Code 6.2).
CounselA senior lawyer rank between senior associate and partner.
DerivativeA contract whose value depends on something else, such as an interest rate; used to hedge risk.
DisclosureIn litigation, showing the other side relevant documents.
Due diligenceInvestigating a company or asset before a deal to find risks.
Equity partnerAn owner of the firm who shares the profit.
Fact-findAn assessment exercise where you question an assessor to gather facts and then advise.
FCA / PRAFinancial Conduct Authority (conduct regulator) and Prudential Regulation Authority (bank and insurer safety, part of the Bank of England).
FLK1 / FLK2The two multiple-choice "functioning legal knowledge" exams that make up SQE1.
FSMA 2023The Financial Services and Markets Act 2023, which replaced retained EU financial rules with UK rules.
GiltA UK government bond.
High yield bondA bond issued by a riskier company, paying a higher interest rate.
In-house lawyerA lawyer employed by a company rather than a law firm; usually the client.
Information barrierControls that stop confidential information passing between teams in a firm.
Insider dealingTrading on price-sensitive information that isn't public; a crime under the Criminal Justice Act 1993.
ISDAThe International Swaps and Derivatives Association, which publishes the standard derivatives contracts.
Lateral hireA partner or lawyer who joins from another firm.
Leverage(Law firm) lawyers per partner. (Finance) borrowing relative to equity.
Leveraged financeLending to companies with high debt, often for private equity buyouts.
Liability management exerciseReshaping a company's debt outside court.
LIBORThe old benchmark interest rate, ended 30 September 2024.
LLPLimited liability partnership: the legal form of most UK law firms; partners are "members".
Lock-upTime between doing work and being paid for it.
LockstepPartner pay by seniority rather than by individual performance.
Magic CircleFive London-headquartered elite firms: A&O Shearman, Clifford Chance, Freshfields, Linklaters, Slaughter and May.
Mansfield certificationA diversity programme measuring whether firms consider diverse candidates for leadership roles.
NQNewly qualified solicitor.
PartnerA senior lawyer who owns (equity) or holds the title of owner (non-equity) in the firm.
PEPProfit per equity partner: net income divided by the number of equity partners.
PGDLPostgraduate Diploma in Law: the law conversion course for non-law graduates.
PIIProfessional indemnity insurance: covers claims by clients for negligent advice.
Private creditLending by investment funds rather than banks.
Private equity (PE)Funds that buy companies, usually with borrowed money, to sell later.
PrivilegeThe right to keep legal advice confidential from the other side and the court.
Pro bonoFree legal work for people or charities who can't pay.
PRT (pension risk transfer)Moving pension obligations to an insurer: buy-ins, buy-outs, longevity swaps.
QWEQualifying work experience: two years needed to qualify via the SQE.
RealisationThe share of recorded time that is actually billed and paid.
RetentionThe share of trainees kept on as NQs.
Risk retentionThe rule that a securitisation's originator keeps at least 5% of the risk.
SeatA six-month placement in one practice group during a training contract.
SecuritisationPooling loans and selling bonds backed by them.
Silver CircleInformal label for elite UK firms just below the Magic Circle.
SPVSpecial purpose vehicle: a company set up only to hold assets for a deal.
SQESolicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE1 and SQE2), required since 1 September 2021.
SRASolicitors Regulation Authority: regulates solicitors and law firms in England and Wales.
SRT (significant risk transfer)A bank deal that moves credit risk to investors to reduce capital needs.
STS"Simple, transparent and standardised" securitisation with lighter capital treatment.
SuperfundA consolidator that takes over pension schemes without a full insurance buy-out.
Syndicated loanA loan made by a group of banks, run by an agent bank.
Trade financeFinancing for buying and selling goods across borders.
Training contractA firm's two-year trainee programme; counts as QWE.
True saleA sale of assets that would survive the seller's insolvency.
UCC-3A US filing that amends or terminates a public record of a lender's security.
UK MARThe UK Market Abuse Regulation: bans insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation.
UKEFUK Export Finance: the government agency that guarantees loans supporting UK exports.
UtilisationThe share of available time that is billed.
Vacation schemeA paid placement of one to two weeks that serves as an extended interview.
VereinA Swiss association structure some global firms use; Mayer Brown uses "associated practices" instead.
WIPWork in progress: time recorded but not yet billed.
