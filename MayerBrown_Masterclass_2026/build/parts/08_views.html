Part 8

Forming your own view

Eight live arguments about the firm and the industry, each with the case for, the case against, the facts on both sides, and a defensible position you could hold under pressure.

Partners don't mark you on agreeing with them. They mark you on having a view, backing it with a fact, and changing it only when given a better fact. For each debate below, pick a side and practise defending it for 60 seconds.

1. Should Mayer Brown merge to keep up?

ForRivals are combining: Hogan Lovells Cadwalader went live on 1 July 2026 with about 3,200 lawyers [S240]. Mayer Brown is the smallest firm in the peer set by revenue ($2.17bn) [S220]. Scale funds AI and pay.
AgainstMayer Brown grew revenue about 10% and PEP 14.5% in 2025 without merging [S220]. Mergers bring integration risk, conflicts and culture clashes. Lateral teams target gaps precisely [S84].
Your opinion goes here"I think focus is working for now: London grew 20% in 2025. The test is whether lateral hiring can build private equity strength fast enough. If not, a targeted combination might make sense."

2. "Deeper with fewer clients": smart or risky?

SmartHigher rates, bigger matters, better margins: net income up 19.2% [S220]. Matches what buyers say they value: judgement, not volume [S167].
RiskyConcentration: losing one big client hurts more. Fewer small matters may mean fewer chances for juniors to run something end to end Inferred.
Your opinion goes here"It's smart for a firm whose strengths are repeat institutional products. For trainees, it raises the bar: you're more likely on big matters, so the skill is learning fast inside a large team."

3. Will AI shrink the trainee intake?

YesSusskind: AI "reliable for most practical purposes" by the early 2030s [S183]. First drafts and document review are the work trainees used to learn from.
NoFirms must grow future partners; clients want quality first [S167]; Mayer Brown requires human review of every output [S230]. Mayer Brown's intake is already small (about 15).
Your opinion goes here"AI changes what trainees do faster than how many are hired. The risk is a training gap: if AI drafts, who learns to draft? I'd want to know how the firm trains judgement."

4. Should UK securitisation rules be loosened?

ForRegulators say current rules "have led to excessive complexity and high compliance costs" [S194]; UK investors struggle to buy US CLOs; growth agenda.
Against2008 showed opaque securitisation can amplify a crisis; the Bank of England says private credit vulnerabilities "persist and in some cases have intensified" [S209].
Your opinion goes here"Loosening due diligence for well-understood assets makes sense; I'd keep risk retention strict, because skin in the game is the lesson of 2008."

5. Is matching the Magic Circle on pay, rather than US firms, a weakness?

WeaknessSidley, Kirkland and White & Case pay £175k NQ [S120]; talent may go there.
StrengthLower cost base; hours average 9:26am to 8:20pm [S15]; no billable target for trainees [S16]; 80% retention, above Clifford Chance and A&O Shearman [S121].
Your opinion goes hereHold privately; never argue pay in an interview. If pressed: "It's a positioning choice, and it fits a firm that trains a small cohort."

6. The AI push versus a "C" for legal tech

The firm launched a mandatory curriculum for about 1,800 lawyers in April 2026 [S230]; trainees graded legal tech C in Legal Cheek's 2026-27 survey [S13]. Both can be true: a curriculum is about people; a survey grade may reflect tools trainees use day to day. Good question: "How has the curriculum changed what a first-seat trainee does?"

7. Is London still the place for international finance law?

YesUK legal services £52.3bn and growing; more than 200 foreign firms in London [S164]; English law dominates cross-border contracts; Mayer Brown London +20.5%.
Not so fastEquity listings still weak despite a first-half 2026 rebound from a low base [S244]; inflation and energy shocks [S236].

8. Bank-side strength versus the private capital push

The firm's top-tier rankings are bank-side and product-led; its hiring is sponsor-side (leveraged finance, PE) [S139][S327]. The opportunity: private credit funds need the same documentation skills banks do. The risk: acting for both lenders and sponsors creates conflicts and competes with Kirkland and Latham on their home ground. Good question: "How do the new leveraged finance teams fit with the bank-side practices?"
