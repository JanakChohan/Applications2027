A. Mayer Brown itself

1 · New London managing partner: Chris Harvey, from 1 October 2026Mayer Brown, 28 Sep 2026 [S222]

What happened. Chris Harvey, a real estate partner who joined in 2012 and co-leads the firm's global Real Estate group, became London Managing Partner on 1 October 2026. He succeeds Dominic Griffiths, a structured finance partner who ran London from 2022 and now joins the firm's global Management Committee. The firm says London had "record revenue growth of 20.5%" in 2025 and marked 130 years in the City [S222] Confirmed (firm source).

Why it matters to the firm. The office head moves from finance to real estate as the firm hires real estate and private equity partners in London (story 3). Chair Jon Van Gorp: "London is central to our international platform and to the strategic growth priorities" [S222].

Why it matters to you. The managing partner shapes intake, seat options and investment. He took office one month before the masterclass; he may even speak.

Question to ask. "With a real estate partner now leading London after a finance-led period of record growth, how do you see the balance between finance and real estate and private capital developing?"

2 · FY2025 results: about $2.17bn revenue, profit per equity partner $3.196mAmerican Lawyer via Mayer Brown, 25 Mar 2026 [S220]

What happened. Revenue rose nearly 10% to $2.17bn; profit per equity partner (PEP) rose 14.5% to $3.196m; revenue per lawyer rose 11.6% to $1.25m. Billable hours fell (about 2.8m from 2.91m) and clients billed fell from 7,600 to 6,316, while billing rates rose by more than 10%. London, with about 264 lawyers, grew revenue roughly 20%, led by structured finance and private credit [S220] Confirmed.

Plain English. Revenue is total fees. PEP is profit divided among the owning partners; the market uses it to compare firms. Higher revenue from fewer hours and fewer clients means higher prices and bigger relationships. Van Gorp called it "a concerted effort by us to go deeper with fewer clients" [S220].

Why it matters to you. Fewer, bigger, institutional clients means complex work and high expectations from day one.

Question to ask. "The firm billed fewer clients in 2025 but grew revenue. How does London decide which relationships to deepen, and does that change what juniors work on?"

3 · Lateral team hires: leveraged finance and high yield from Baker McKenzieMayer Brown, 1 Sep 2026 [S226]

What happened. Three partners (Rob Mathews, David Becker, Ben Bierwirth) joined London Banking & Finance from Baker McKenzie, covering high yield bonds, term loan B and leveraged finance, with restructuring and liability management experience [S226] Confirmed. Earlier in 2026: a leveraged finance partner from Kirkland & Ellis (April) [S228], an export finance partner from Baker McKenzie (August) [S227], and two real estate and private equity partners (February) [S229] Reported.

Plain English. Leveraged finance is lending to companies that already carry a lot of debt, often to fund private equity buyouts. High yield bonds are the bond-market version, usually under New York law. A "liability management exercise" is refinancing or reshaping debt without going to court.

Why it matters. It is the firm's chosen way to grow: hire teams, don't merge (story 8).

Question to ask. "How do the new leveraged finance hires work with the private credit and restructuring teams?"

4 · Pension risk transfer: Royal London buy-ins in a £10.2bn half-year marketMayer Brown, 30 Jul 2026 [S223]; Pensions Age, 22 Sep 2026 [S237]

What happened. Mayer Brown advised insurer Royal London on a £208m bulk purchase annuity with the Hickson UK Group Pension Scheme (1,250+ members), after a £213m Royal London deal in January [S223][S224] Confirmed (firm source). Hymans Robertson put first-half 2026 UK risk transfer volumes at £10.2bn across 135+ deals, against £9.8bn and 161 deals a year earlier [S237] Confirmed.

Plain English. A company with a final salary pension scheme pays an insurer a lump sum to take over paying the pensions. In a "buy-in" the policy sits inside the scheme; in a "buy-out" members become the insurer's policyholders. Higher gilt yields since 2022 made schemes better funded, so many can now afford to insure.

Why it matters. Van Gorp calls insurance "always been a really strong brand for us" [S220]. London's pensions and insurance teams together are a distinctive Mayer Brown offer (Part 4).

Question to ask. "With deal counts down but values up, is buy-in documentation becoming standardised, and where does that leave the lawyer?"

5 · A mandatory GenAI curriculum for about 1,800 lawyersMayer Brown, 14 Apr 2026 [S230]

What happened. A compulsory 2026 programme for lawyers and business services staff covering responsible use (human review of every output, citation checks, data security), the firm's tools (Harvey and Microsoft Copilot are named), practice scenarios, partner training and "AI Champions". Van Gorp: "Our approach is focused on amplifying lawyer capabilities and judgement, not replacing them" [S230] Confirmed.

Why it matters. Document-heavy work (loan documents, securitisation documents, diligence) is where AI helps most. Fewer hours at higher rates (story 2) fits that future.

Why it matters to you. Expect AI training from day one and the duty to check everything it produces.

Question to ask. "If AI does more first drafts, how do trainees build the judgement to review them?"

6 · Deals across offices: Nigeria ports (£746m) and an AI infrastructure loan (up to $3.1bn)Mayer Brown, 25 Mar and 10 Sep 2026 [S225][S231]

What happened. London advised Citibank and UK Export Finance on a £746m UKEF-guaranteed financing for Lagos's Apapa and TinCan Island ports, with a trainee named on the team [S225]. Singapore led a senior loan of up to $3.1bn for Zankore's AI cloud infrastructure, with a London partner on hedging and Washington and Dubai lawyers on sanctions and export controls [S231] Confirmed (firm source).

Plain English. Export credit agencies like UKEF guarantee loans so foreign buyers can buy UK goods. GPUs and data centres are now financed like infrastructure.

Question to ask. "How do lenders handle the risk that GPUs become obsolete before the loan is repaid?"

B. The legal sector

7 · NQ pay: £150k is now the City benchmark, and Mayer Brown is thereLegal Cheek, 21 Jul 2026 [S235]; Jul 2025 [S234]

What happened. Baker McKenzie moved its newly qualified (NQ) salary to £150k in July 2026, matching the Magic Circle and Macfarlanes; Ashurst Perkins Coie and Norton Rose Fulbright sat at £140k; Quinn Emanuel led at £189k [S235] Confirmed. Mayer Brown moved London NQ pay from £135k to £150k from 1 July 2025 [S234] Reported.

Why it matters. Mayer Brown pays at Magic Circle level, below the top US payers. That is a positioning choice (Part 4).

Don't say thisNever give pay as a reason for applying. If asked, talk about it as market positioning.

What happened. Hogan Lovells and Cadwalader launched as Hogan Lovells Cadwalader on 1 July 2026 with about 3,200 lawyers; reported combined revenue varies between about $3.6bn and $3.9bn [S240] Confirmed launch Reported revenue. Ashurst and Perkins Coie partners approved a merger to form Ashurst Perkins Coie (about 3,000 lawyers) [S239]. Question to ask. "Several rivals chose transatlantic mergers for scale, including Cadwalader. What does a firm of Mayer Brown's size gain by staying independent?"

9 · SQE1 pass rate falls to 42%Legal Cheek, 6 Oct 2026 [S238]

What happened. 42% of 6,315 candidates passed SQE1 in July 2026, down from 53% in January; first-time takers 47% [S238] Confirmed. Separately, the SRA has proposed that firms notify it before merging [S247] Reported.

Why it matters to you. Firms sponsor SQE preparation. The pass rate makes the quality of that support a fair question.

C. Markets that drive Mayer Brown's work

10 · UK securitisation rules are being loosened, while the Bank of England worries about private creditMayer Brown, 24 Feb 2026 [S232]; BoE, Jul 2026 [S243]

What happened. On 17 February 2026 the FCA and PRA consulted on loosening UK securitisation rules: more proportionate investor due diligence, a new "L-shaped" option for the 5% risk retention, dropping prescribed reporting templates for most asset classes. Consultations closed 18 May 2026; FCA final rules were expected in the second half of 2026 and PRA implementation around the second quarter of 2027, according to Mayer Brown's own note, co-written by Dominic Griffiths [S232] Confirmed. The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee named private credit as a key stability risk [S243] Reported.

Plain English. Securitisation pools loans and sells bonds backed by them. "Risk retention" makes the originator keep 5% of the risk. Private credit is lending by funds rather than banks.

Source uncertainWhether the FCA has published final securitisation rules by 8 October 2026 was not confirmed. Check fca.org.uk before the event.

Question to ask. "Is there a tension between loosening securitisation rules for growth and the Bank's warnings on private credit?"

11 · Bank Rate held at 3.75% on a 6-3 vote; inflation heading towards 4%Bank of England, 17 Sep 2026 [S236]

What happened. Three members voted to raise rates to 4%. CPI inflation was 3.1% in August and is forecast at about 3¾% in Q4 2026 and slightly above 4% in Q1 2027. The Bank links higher energy prices to "Protracted conflict in the Middle East". The next decision is due on 5 November 2026, three days after the masterclass [S236] Confirmed.

Why it matters. Rates touch almost every practice: higher for longer means more stress for leveraged borrowers (restructuring work), better pension scheme funding (more buy-ins), slower IPOs and pressure on property values.

Question to ask. "If the Bank hikes in November, which London practices feel it first?"

12 · Basel 3.1 arrives on 1 January 2027; London listings recover from a low base[S242] [S244]

What happened. The PRA confirmed that Basel 3.1 bank capital rules take effect on 1 January 2027, with internal models for market risk deferred to 1 January 2028 [S242] Reported. EY counted seven London listings raising £577m in the first half of 2026, up 215% on a weak 2025 [S244] Reported.

Why it matters. Higher capital requirements push banks to transfer credit risk to investors through "significant risk transfer" deals and synthetic securitisations, which is structured finance work. Mayer Brown's London capital markets work is mostly debt, so the IPO story matters indirectly.

Question to ask. "Will Basel 3.1 create a lasting pipeline of risk transfer deals for banks in London?"
