The role's own industry and its regulation

Two layers. First, the rules for becoming and being a solicitor, which bind you from your first day. Second, the regulation that creates the work in Mayer Brown London's core practices. Dates and instrument names are exact; this is where precision earns most.

Layer 1: how you become a solicitor

{{FIG:sqe_route}}

Since 1 September 2021, the route to qualifying as a solicitor in England and Wales is the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) [S152] Confirmed. It has four parts: a degree or equivalent; SQE1, two multiple-choice exams on "functioning legal knowledge" (FLK1 and FLK2); SQE2, practical skills such as drafting, interviewing and advocacy; and two years of qualifying work experience (QWE), confirmed by a solicitor [S151]. You must also pass character and suitability checks. People who started on the old Legal Practice Course (LPC) route before September 2021 have "until 31 December 2032 to apply for admission" [S150] Confirmed.

A "training contract" is now a firm's two-year programme that also counts as QWE. Mayer Brown's future trainees take SQE1 and SQE2 with BPP University before starting; non-law graduates do the Postgraduate Diploma in Law (PGDL) first. The firm gives a £20,000 grant for the SQE year and £15,000 for the PGDL, and trainees can ask for an extra £5,000 interest-free loan [S2] Confirmed.

The exams are hard. In July 2026, 42% of the 6,315 candidates who sat both parts of SQE1 passed; first-time takers did better at 47% [S155]. In January 2026 the pass rate was 53%, with large gaps between ethnic groups (White 67%, Asian 47%, Black 38%) [S156] Reported. From September 2026 the fees are £2,006 for SQE1 and £3,086 for SQE2 [S158].

Say this in the interview"I know the SQE pass rate fell to 42% in July. I'd treat the firm's preparation year like a job, because failing would delay the training contract for everyone, not just me."

The rules you live by from day one

The SRA's Standards and Regulations replaced the old SRA Handbook on 25 November 2019 [S154]. Two documents matter most:

  • The SRA Principles (current version in force from 11 April 2025). You act in a way that upholds the rule of law and public trust, with independence, honesty and integrity, encouraging equality, diversity and inclusion, and in the best interests of each client. Where Principles conflict, those protecting the wider public interest "take precedence over an individual client's interests" [S341] Confirmed.
  • The Code of Conduct for Solicitors. Paragraph 1.4: "You do not mislead or attempt to mislead your clients, the court or others". 6.2: no acting where there is a conflict of interest. 6.3: "You keep the affairs of current and former clients confidential unless disclosure is required or permitted". 7.11: you are honest and open with clients if things go wrong [S342][S12] Confirmed.

Outside the SRA rules, the criminal law also applies to you. Insider dealing is a crime under Part V of the Criminal Justice Act 1993, and UK MAR prohibits passing on inside information [S343][S344]. Finance trainees regularly hold price-sensitive information about listed companies. That is why deals have codenames, and why you never discuss work on the train.

Why the SRA is changing thingsAfter the Axiom Ince collapse in October 2023, when about £60m of client money went missing, the Legal Services Board found the SRA had not acted adequately. Since then the SRA has consulted on accountants' reports (Dec 2025 to Feb 2026) and on firms notifying it before mergers or before starting to hold client money (summer 2026) [S161][S247] Reported.

Layer 2: the rules that create Mayer Brown London's work

Securitisation and structured finance

{{FIG:securitisation_how}}

The UK left the EU's securitisation rules behind in stages. The Securitisation Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/102) were made on 29 January 2024 under powers in the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 [S192] Confirmed. The new regime, made up of that statutory instrument plus FCA and PRA rules, took effect on 1 November 2024 [S193] Reported.

Fifteen months later the regulators proposed loosening it. On 17 February 2026 the FCA (CP26/6) and the PRA (CP2/26) consulted on principles-based investor due diligence, dropping prescribed reporting templates for most asset classes, a new "L-shaped" option for the 5% risk retention, and two exemptions from the ban on resecuritisation. Consultation closed on 18 May 2026. FCA final rules were expected in the second half of 2026, with PRA changes around the second quarter of 2027 [S194][S195] Confirmed. Mayer Brown's own note says the regulators concluded "the current rules have led to excessive complexity and high compliance costs" [S194].

Key termsRisk retention: the originator must keep at least 5% of the risk, so it has "skin in the game". STS: "simple, transparent and standardised" securitisations that get lighter capital treatment. SRT: "significant risk transfer", where a bank uses a securitisation to move credit risk and free up capital. CLO: a securitisation of leveraged loans.

Bank capital: Basel 3.1

The PRA's policy statement PS1/26 (20 January 2026) confirms that the Basel 3.1 rules "will take effect on Friday 1 January 2027", with internal models for market risk following on 1 January 2028 [S190] Confirmed. The date had already slipped twice; the January 2025 delay cited uncertainty over US implementation [S191].

Derivatives

A derivative is a contract whose value depends on something else, such as an interest rate. Banks and companies use them to hedge risk, usually on standard ISDA documents. Two recent milestones: UK EMIR Refit reporting went live on 30 September 2024, and LIBOR, the old benchmark interest rate, ended the same day with the final synthetic US dollar settings [S196][S197] Reported. Mayer Brown London is now in the Legal 500's top tier for derivatives and structured products [S139].

Pensions

{{FIG:buyin_how}}

In Virgin Media v NTL Pension Trustees II [2024] EWCA Civ 843, the Court of Appeal held that some changes to contracted-out pension benefits made between 1997 and 2016 were void because the required actuarial confirmation was missing [S214]. It threatened the validity of many schemes' past amendments. The Pension Schemes Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026. The Virgin Media fix took effect immediately, allowing retrospective actuarial confirmation unless the point was already in court proceedings by 5 June 2025 [S200] Confirmed. The Act also creates a power to release scheme surplus and a permanent regime for "superfunds" (expected 2028) [S199][S200].

Economic crime

The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 created a "failure to prevent fraud" offence, in force from 1 September 2025. It applies to large organisations (two of: over 250 staff, over £36m turnover, over £18m assets), whose only defence is reasonable fraud prevention procedures [S203][S204] Confirmed.

Private credit

Private credit is lending by funds rather than banks. The Bank of England says global private equity and private credit grew from about $3tn to about $11tn in a decade, and launched a system-wide stress exercise on private markets on 4 December 2025, with a final report due in early 2027 [S208] Confirmed. The FCA reviewed private market valuations in March 2025 [S210]. Mayer Brown says private credit drove part of London's 2025 growth [S220].
