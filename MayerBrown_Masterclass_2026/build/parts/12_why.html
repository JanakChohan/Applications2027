"Why Mayer Brown?" in three lengths

Built from mechanism, not adjectives. Every sentence would need changing to work for any other firm. Fill the bracketed parts from your own experience.
{{FIG:why_onepage}}

Thirty seconds

Say this"Three things. First, the work: Mayer Brown London is top tier for derivatives and asset-based lending, which are the finance products I find most interesting, because [one line from your experience]. Second, the shape: about fifteen trainees a year, so I'd be doing real work on those deals rather than being one of a hundred. Third, the moment: London grew 20% last year by going deeper with fewer clients, and Basel 3.1 in January will push banks into exactly the risk transfer work the firm is known for."

Sources: [S139][S16][S302][S220][S190][S148].

Two minutes

Say this

"I want to train somewhere where the finance work is the core of the firm, not a side-line, and where a trainee is close enough to the deal to understand why each document exists.

Mayer Brown fits on both. On the work: the London office is top tier in the Legal 500 for derivatives and structured products, asset-based lending and trade finance. Behind that is one of the largest structured finance practices in the world; GlobalCapital has named it US ABS law firm of the year six years running. I like that combination because the regulation is moving right now. The FCA and PRA consulted in February on loosening the UK securitisation rules, and Basel 3.1 starts on 1 January, which makes banks look for ways to transfer risk. That is work this firm already does.

On the structure: I was struck that the Ford pension buy-in last year, the biggest of 2025, was run by an insurance partner with a pensions partner as relationship lead. A product like that needs four teams to work as one, and that's the kind of collaboration I'd want to learn from.

On the shape: about fifteen trainees a year at the same pay as firms that take seventy or a hundred. At the masterclass, [trainee or partner name] told me [one specific thing], which made that concrete.

And it's a firm making deliberate choices. It grew London by 20% last year without merging, while competitors like Cadwalader chose to combine. I'd rather join a firm with a clear strategy than one in the middle of integration."

Sources: [S139][S148][S194][S190][S310][S16][S302][S240].

The close: one thing you want to work on

Pick one and make it yours Derivatives: "The thing I most want to work on is derivatives: the team acts for ISDA itself, so a trainee sees how the market's standard documents are made, not just used." [S316]

Risk transfer: "I'd most like to see a significant risk transfer deal from the inside. With Basel 3.1 arriving, it's where bank capital rules turn into legal work." [S190][S148]

Pensions buy-ins: "I'd like a seat in the pensions and insurance work. Buy-ins combine contract law, regulation and very human stakes: 35,000 Ford retirees' pensions." [S310]

The honest counter-case

Know these so a partner can't surprise you, and so you don't overclaim:

  • It is the smallest firm by revenue in its peer set, and its London PEP is not top [S220][S78].
  • London securitisation and pensions are tier 2, and M&A is tier 6 [S141][S145][S146].
  • Hunton overtook it as the leading US ABS issuer counsel by deal count in 2025 [S124].
  • Pay is below the top US firms [S120].
  • Asia is smaller since 2024 [S70].
  • Trainees grade legal tech C [S13].
  • A small intake means fewer NQ roles in each practice; retention is 80% [S16].
