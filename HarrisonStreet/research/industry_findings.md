## 1. FINDINGS

### 1.1 What IR / "investor solutions" / capital formation means at a private RE manager
- **Definition.** At a private fund manager (the GP), IR finds and convinces new Limited Partners (LPs) to invest in the firm's funds. It also manages existing LP relationships and handles LP secondary sales [I42]. Alternative names include "capital formation", "client solutions", "client relations", "capital raising", "business development", "LP services" and "product specialist" [I42]. M&I calls it "mission-critical" because a firm cannot invest without committed capital [I42].
- **Two halves.** (a) **Fundraising/capital raising** means prospecting, meetings, materials and closing. (b) **Client service/servicing** means reporting, capital calls, distributions, data requests and the AGM. Industry commentary says re-up rates have fallen, so good servicing is itself a fundraising lever, and LP expectations on "pace and granularity" have shifted [I43, I44 snippets: Atominvest/Hg and Apax; Praxis Rock]. Praxis Rock (vendor) describes IR as maintaining relationships between a GP and roughly 20–40 LPs across a 10-year fund life [I43].
- **Fundraising lifecycle (synthesis).** Source support is given at each step.
  1. **Target list / prospecting.** Narrow LPs to those whose strategy and geography fit [I42]. Use databases (PERE's own database advertises "key contact information and investment preferences for more than 15,000 institutions globally" plus placement agent and investment consultant profiles [I1]), consultant databases (Mercer's MercerInsight, Nasdaq eVestment [I24, snippet I27]) and a CRM. Salesforce is the usual CRM [UNSOURCED-GK]; one job posting cites "STAR" as a CRM example (snippet).
  2. **Pre-marketing** (EU-regulated, see §1.6) and first meetings. These involve multiple meetings with senior deal professionals, and the overall process usually takes 1–2 years [I42]. PERE data shows average time on the road of **25 months** for funds closed in 2025, an all-time high [I1].
  3. **Materials.** These are the Private Placement Memorandum (PPM), track record and fund cash-flow information [I42], pitch decks, a virtual **data room** and the **DDQ**. INREV publishes a standard DDQ that is "used for information exchange" and helps "an investor or consultant judge, in principle, whether a proposal fits their investment objectives". The DDQ is a tool that complements the INREV Guidelines rather than part of their formal structure [I17 snippet]. INREV is consulting on streamlining the DDQ and adding topics such as operational real estate and AI [I17 snippet]. IR job postings list "coordinate and manage due diligence requests (DDQs, RFIs, data rooms)" [snippet, job posts].
  4. **Consultant due diligence and rating.** The consultant longlists, shortlists and then runs in-depth due diligence. UN PRI describes a funnel from roughly 50–300 managers down to one appointment [I26 snippet]. Mercer rates strategies **A, B, C or R** on four factors: idea generation, portfolio construction, implementation and business management. It also considers operational risk, ESG and track record. Mercer has 11,400+ ratings and "more than 250 dedicated due diligence professionals" (figures as of 31 Oct 2025) [I24].
  5. **LP investment committee approval.** [UNSOURCED-GK; standard practice]
  6. **Legal.** The Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) is negotiated, along with tailored terms in **side letters**, such as reduced fees or enhanced rights [I42].
  7. **First close → subsequent closes → final close.** [UNSOURCED-GK for the mechanics. PERE tracks "final closes" [I1].] 52% of real estate funds closed in 2025 met or exceeded their target, up from 40% in 2024 [I1].
- **Ongoing reporting and servicing.**
  - The INREV Reporting module requires an **annual report** (a yearly review plus GAAP financial statements) and **interim reports**. Interim frequency "should be defined in vehicle documentation" [I16].
  - Report content covers six areas: vehicle information and governance; performance and investor position; the manager's report; the property report; risk management; and ESG. KPIs go in the **SDDS** (Standard Data Delivery Sheet) [I16].
  - The current INREV reporting guidelines apply to reporting periods beginning on or after **1 January 2024** [I16].
  - The other INREV modules are corporate governance, property valuation, performance measurement, INREV NAV, fee and expense metrics, liquidity, data delivery and tax conduct [I17 snippet].
  - The ILPA Reporting Template v2.0 was released in **January 2025** (22 Jan 2025 per secondary sources). It applies to funds still in their investment period during Q1 2026 or "commencing operations on or after January 1, 2026", and covers "fees, expenses and carried interest" [I20]. A separate **Performance Template** applies to funds starting on or after 1 Jan 2026, with first delivery after Q1 2027 [I21 snippet].
  - Servicing tasks include capital calls, distributions, investor onboarding, NAVs, factsheets, quarterly letters, the AGM and LP meetings, and LP-led secondaries [I42; job post snippets]. The LPAC (LP Advisory Committee) is a governance body for conflicts and valuations [UNSOURCED-GK].
- **Placement agents.** These are third-party fundraisers paid mostly by success fees on capital raised, sometimes with a retainer [I53 snippet, Wikipedia]. A 2011 PERE feature put fees at 1.5–3% of committed capital (dated). Placement costs are usually borne by the vehicle and are often offset against management fees [snippet; INREV definitions]. Preqin found that 61% of established managers using an agent surpassed target, against 47% without one (date unclear) [snippet]. M&I describes bank private funds groups as "outsourced capital raising teams" [I42].
- **Conferences (2026 dates).**
  - **MIPIM:** Cannes, 9–13 March 2026 [I56 snippet].
  - **INREV Annual Conference:** Barcelona, 7–9 April 2026 (500+ attendees). The next one is 12–14 April 2027 [I55 snippet].
  - **SuperReturn International:** Berlin, 8–12 June 2026 [I59 snippet].
  - **PERE Europe Forum:** London, 15–16 Sept 2026, co-located with Infrastructure Investor's Investor Forum [I58 snippet].
  - **Expo Real:** Munich, **5–7 October 2026**, which ended the day before this research [I57 snippet].
  - INREV also ran a Cannes seminar during MIPIM on 11 March 2026 [snippet].

### 1.2 Who the clients are (LPs and gatekeepers)
- **LP types:** DB pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, endowments and foundations, family offices, wealth managers and RIAs. IPE Real Assets' Top 150 says pension funds, SWFs, insurers and others hold **US$2.18trn (€1.86trn)** of property assets (2026 edition, headline only) [I64 PAYWALLED-NOT-READ; headline snippet].
- **UK LGPS pooling ("Fit for the Future").**
  - The government response on **4 June 2025** backed six of the eight pools. Brunel and ACCESS member funds must join other pools, with a deadline of **31 March 2026** for the pooling requirements [I22].
  - Pools must be FCA-authorised investment management companies. Funds must delegate implementation and take "principal investment advice" from their pool [I22].
  - The **Pension Schemes Act 2026** received Royal Assent on **29 April 2026**; LGPS pooling sits in ss.1–8 [I23 snippet]. Supporting LGPS (Pooling, Management and Investment of Funds) Regulations 2026 were expected to come into force on 30 June 2026. Pools must be UK AIFMs by 30 Sept 2027 (Squire Patton Boggs, snippet) [I23].
  - **Why it matters for IR:** fewer, bigger buyers, with decisions taken by the pool rather than the individual council fund.
- **Dutch pensions (APG, PGGM).**
  - Under the Wtp (Future Pensions Act), roughly €1.8–1.9trn must transition by January 2028 [I40 snippet, Nuveen].
  - DNB counted 34 converted funds at 30 June 2026 [I40 snippet]. PGGM's clients transitioned on 1 Jan 2026 [snippet].
  - APG's private markets share is about 26%, could rise to just over 30%, and real estate is about 10% of its assets (Reuters via secondary sources) [I41 snippet].
- **Middle East sovereign wealth funds.**
  - Global SWF's 2026 Annual Report says seven Gulf SWFs (PIF, ADIA, ICD, Mubadala, ADQ, QIA, KIA) invested a record **$119bn in 2025**, up 43% [I39 snippet].
  - Gulf SWFs deployed $53.9bn across 108 deals in H1 2026 [I39 snippet].
  - Real estate was 5% of volume and 11% of value of MENA sovereign dealmaking (9M 2026 Playbook) [I39 snippet].
  - PERE: capital targeted by MENA-focused real estate funds rose from $1.35bn (2020) to $3.5bn (2024) and $2.98bn (2025) [I1].
  - No fund-specific real estate allocation for ADIA, QIA or PIF was found [GAP].
- **Allocation data.**
  - **Hodes Weill & Cornell Baker 2025 Allocations Monitor (13th edition, Oct 2025):**
    - Average target allocation to real estate is **10.7%**, down 10 bp. This is the first decline since the survey began in 2013, after a 10.8% plateau in 2022–24 [I2, I3].
    - The under-allocation gap is **90 bp**, the widest since 2021 and up from 60 bp [I3]. The implied actual allocation of about 9.8% is the MBA article's arithmetic, not an official figure.
    - Institutions expect a +10 bp increase in 2026, led by EMEA [I2, I3]. Competition comes especially from infrastructure and private credit [I4 snippet].
    - By region (same-store): APAC −100 bp, Americas −40 bp, EMEA unchanged [I4 snippet].
    - No 2026 edition had been found as of 8 Oct 2026. It usually publishes in October or November.
  - **INREV/ANREV/PREA Investment Intentions Survey 2026 (14 Jan 2026):**
    - Global current allocation is 12.4% against a 12.5% target. European investors are about 20 bp over their **13.9%** target [I15].
    - Real estate debt funds are the most favoured vehicle. Net interest in non-listed funds rose from 8% to 17% [I15].
    - The UK and Germany are the top destinations [I15].
    - Note: Hodes Weill and INREV use different samples and weightings, so their numbers are not directly comparable.
- **Investment consultants as gatekeepers.**
  - Mercer has over 680 "highly rated" real estate managers and about 3,000 strategies according to its own materials [snippet].
  - One European pension investor said real estate manager selection is more expensive in consultant fees than for equities or bonds [I27 snippet, IPE].
  - Investors and consultants value "authenticity, patience and a sincere interest in the investor's needs" [I25].
- **How this differs from REIT or bank customers** [UNSOURCED-GK synthesis]:
  - A REIT's "customers" are tenants. Its investors are dispersed public shareholders who trade on an exchange, and IR is governed by MAR.
  - A bank's lending customers are borrowers.
  - A private fund's clients are a few dozen professional investors who make 10+ year commitments and negotiate terms.

### 1.3 How private RE/real-assets IR differs from other roles
- **Listed-company IR.**
  - Audience: shareholders and sell-side analysts. Work centres on earnings calls, results and press releases [I43 snippet].
  - Binding law: the EU/UK **Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) Article 17**, which requires public disclosure of inside information. Art 17(8) covers selective disclosure to analysts and major shareholders [I50 snippet].
  - US equivalent: **Regulation FD**, which bans selective disclosure of material nonpublic information [I50 snippet].
  - M&I notes that moving from corporate IR to PE IR is "uncommon" because the skill sets differ [I42].
- **Long-only asset manager sales.** The product is liquid funds sold through wholesale and institutional channels with daily dealing. Private RE IR sells illiquid, closed-end commitments with long diligence [UNSOURCED-GK; supported indirectly by I42 on the 1–2 year process].
- **Hedge fund IR.** This involves monthly NAVs and factsheets, open-ended subscriptions and redemptions, and liquidity terms [one job post lists "monthly NAVs, factsheets"; otherwise UNSOURCED-GK].
- **Private equity IR.** This is the closest analogue: same GP/LP model, same templates (ILPA). Real estate adds property-level KPIs (INREV SDDS, valuations, GRESB) and more open-end core funds [I16, I20].
- **Brokerage/agency (CBRE, JLL, Colliers).**
  - Agents act for clients on leasing and investment sales and earn one-off transaction commissions, and their capital markets teams arrange debt and equity for deals. A fund manager earns recurring asset-based fees plus carry [I52 snippet].
  - CBRE itself reports "transactional" lines (sales, leasing, carry) separately from "resilient" lines (property management, asset management fees) [I52 snippet].
  - **Key point:** agency capital markets raises money for a building or deal, while fund IR raises money for a blind-pool or semi-blind fund.
- **Property management.** This means operational management of buildings and tenants on a contract fee [I52 snippet]. It is not investor-facing.

### 1.4 Fund structures and economics
- **Open-ended vs closed-ended.**
  - Open-end core funds: evergreen, with periodic subscriptions and redemptions at NAV (INREV NAV module).
  - Closed-end value-add and opportunistic funds: a fixed term (typically about 10 years), commitments called over an investment period, and capital returned on realisation [UNSOURCED-GK for the term; ~10-year life also in I43 snippet].
- **Style (INREV classification, 2012 revision, via glossary summary)** [I51 snippet]:
  - **Core:** 15% or less of GAV non-income-producing; 5% or less in (re)development; at least 60% of return from income. LTV is shown as a label (40% or less, or above 40%).
  - **Value added:** more than 15% and up to 40% non-income-producing; more than 5% and up to 25% development; max LTV more than 40% and up to 60%.
  - **Opportunity:** above those limits (LTV above 60%).
  - Core-plus is a marketing term that INREV maps to core or value added.
- **Fees and terms.**
  - PE-style "2 and 20" over an 8% preferred return is often quoted but poorly sourced [I32/I33 snippets].
  - A Wharton paper describes opportunity funds charging 150 bp on committed equity plus 20% carry. Core managers are about 70 bp on GAV [I32 snippet].
  - INREV 2012 found hurdles of about 9–10% [I33 snippet, dated].
  - Preqin reported a median real estate fund management fee of **1.00%** in 2025, a "near-record low" [I35 SNIPPET, low confidence].
  - The INREV Management Fees and Terms Study (TGER/REER) publishes in Sept/Oct, and the 2026 edition covers 2025 data [snippet].
- **Commitment vs called capital.**
  - An LP commits an amount. The GP draws it down via capital calls as deals are made. Unfunded commitment is "dry powder" [UNSOURCED-GK].
  - Fees are charged on commitments or on invested capital depending on the fund. Towers Watson criticised charging on commitments in value-add and opportunistic vehicles [snippet].
- **GP/LP, separate accounts, co-investment, JVs and clubs.** INREV IIS 2026 says JVs and club deals rank second as a vehicle after debt funds [I15]. Co-investment and secondaries appear as strategy categories in PERE data [I1].
- **Semi-liquid and evergreen wealth vehicles.**
  - **ELTIF 2.0 (Regulation (EU) 2023/606):** in force 9 April 2023 and applicable from **10 January 2024**. The RTS are Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2759 (OJ 25 Oct 2024) [I65 snippet].
  - Marketed ELTIFs numbered 21 (2023), 49 (2024) and 87 (2025) [I36].
  - **UK LTAF:** PS21/14 published 25 Oct 2021; rules in force **15 November 2021** [I66 snippet].
  - Preqin counted a record **123 evergreen launches in 2025** [I36].

### 1.5 State of the market 2024–2026
- **Fundraising slump and rebound (PERE)** [I1]:
  - Global private real estate fundraising by year: 2020 $213.85bn; 2021 $304.45bn; 2022 $266.57bn; 2023 $204.25bn; **2024 $172.42bn** (worst since 2020); **2025 $222.16bn** (+29%).
  - 2025 was the first year-on-year increase since 2021. Q1 2025 alone raised $81.4bn.
  - **Concentration:** Blackstone and Brookfield made up 16% of 2025 capital. The largest closes were Brookfield Strategic Real Estate Partners V ($16bn) and BREP Europe VII ($11.05bn).
  - **By strategy:** opportunistic 33% (from 18%), debt 24% (from 31%), value-add 22% (from 30%).
  - **By sector** (sector-specific funds): data centres 37% (from 2%), residential 32% (from 49%), industrial 16% (from 26%).
  - **By region:** North America $89.2bn, multi-region $69.9bn, **Europe $40.6bn**, APAC $21.3bn.
  - **Funds in market** (1 Jan 2026): Europe-focused $90.42bn, now second behind North America ($205.07bn).
- **Other providers:**
  - Preqin put 2025 real estate fundraising at **$155bn** [I34]. The difference from PERE comes from methodology.
  - **2026 so far:** PERE's preliminary Q1 2026 figure is just under $44bn (snippet of PERE podcast) [I61]. Colliers reports US private RE fundraising of $92.6bn in H1 2026, down 38% YoY, with 70% of funds meeting target and time on the road of 18.1 months [I60].
  - PERE 100 (June 2026): top-100 five-year fundraising rose $52bn to $698bn [I62 snippet].
- **Denominator effect.** When public markets fell in 2022, private allocations rose as a share of portfolios, and over-allocated LPs paused new commitments. A CBRE example: a 50/50 portfolio where equities drop 10% leaves real estate at 52.6% [I67 snippet]. CalPERS real estate was 3.7 pp over its 13.4% target at 30 Sept 2022 [I68 snippet]. Slow distributions prolonged the effect [snippet].
- **Rate cycle** [UNSOURCED-GK]: rapid rate rises from 2022 hit valuations and created price-discovery problems. Hodes Weill calls 2025 a "tactical pause" rather than a strategic shift [I3].
- **Competing real assets.**
  - **Infrastructure 2025:** $217.2bn (IREI), $224bn (With Intelligence) or about $300bn (Infrastructure Investor teaser). GIP V closed at $25.1bn [I30 snippet].
  - **Private credit 2025:** $165bn (McKinsey, closed-end) to $224–240bn (With Intelligence) [I31 snippet].
  - IPE Real Assets survey 2026: "Infrastructure identified as the biggest competitor in terms of relative value" [I63 snippet].
- **Alternatives and living sectors.** Data centres led 2025 sector fundraising [I1]. Residential is 27% of INREV single-sector vehicles by number and 32% by GAV, and residential is Europe's most preferred sector [I18, I15]. The INREV Vehicles Universe stands at **609 vehicles** (Q2 2026 release, 8 Sep 2026) [I18]. The Q4 2025 release showed 593 vehicles, 133 managers and €482.4bn GAV [I19 snippet].
- **Private wealth.**
  - Blackstone's private wealth fundraising was $43bn in 2025 (+53%) [I37 snippet].
  - Morningstar (June 2026): the semi-liquid fund market is near $600bn, but credit demand has cooled and redemptions are rising [I38 snippet].
  - Preqin: 37% of European private wealth investors have invested in an evergreen fund [I36].
- **AI in IR** (vendor surveys, directional only):
  - Juniper Square: nearly 80% of GPs are at least piloting AI [I45 snippet].
  - DiligenceVault 2025-26 (780+ managers): 14% say allocators are starting to ask about managers' AI use [I46 snippet].
  - Dynamo 2026 GP survey: AI tools are the top strategic priority, and 78% expect to raise tech spend [I47 snippet].
  - INREV is adding AI to the DDQ [I17 snippet].
- **ESG data.** GRESB's 2025 Real Estate Assessment had 1,002 fund managers and 2,382 assessments [I54 snippet]. LPs and consultants expect it in DDQs.

### 1.6 Regulation binding marketing (UK/EU)
- **EU AIFMD (Directive 2011/61/EU) marketing passport.** EU AIFMs can market EU AIFs to professional investors across the EEA after notification [UNSOURCED-GK for the mechanics].
- **AIFMD II = Directive (EU) 2024/927.**
  - Published in the OJ on **26 March 2024**, in force **15 April 2024**, with Member State transposition and application by **16 April 2026** [I8, I10 snippet].
  - Some reporting provisions (Art 1(12), and Art 2(7) re UCITS Art 20a) apply from **16 April 2027** [I10 snippet].
  - Areas covered: delegation, liquidity risk management (LMTs), supervisory reporting, depositary/custody, and loan origination by AIFs [I8].
  - Luxembourg's transposition law is dated 3 March 2026 (published 9 March 2026) [I9 snippet].
  - Grandfathering for loan-origination funds runs from 15 April 2024, with a transitional period to 16 April 2029 [I9 snippet].
- **Cross-Border Distribution of Funds (CBDF) package.** This is Directive (EU) 2019/1160 plus Regulation (EU) 2019/1156, applying from **2 August 2021** [I14 snippet].
  - It introduced an EU **pre-marketing** definition: testing interest among professional investors in an AIF not yet established or not yet notified.
  - Any subscription within **18 months** of pre-marketing is treated as marketing.
  - National regulators require notification of pre-marketing within 2 weeks [I14 snippet].
- **UK NPPR** (post-Brexit, the route for non-UK funds into the UK).
  - Under the UK AIFM Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/1773): reg 57 covers full-scope UK AIFMs marketing third-country AIFs, reg 58 small third-country AIFMs, and reg 59 above-threshold third-country AIFMs [I28].
  - Notification goes via FCA Connect. The FCA says "Once we have received the notification, the AIFM can start marketing the AIF" [I28]. Rules are in FUND 10.5 [I29 snippet].
  - Material changes must be notified, and transparency reporting is required [I28].
- **UK AIFM regime reform.**
  - FCA Call for Input and HMT consultation: **7 April 2025**, comments by 9 June 2025 [I5].
  - On **14 July 2026**, the FCA published **CP26/28** (UK AIFM regime), CP26/26 (FRAME reporting) and CP26/27 (remuneration), and HMT published draft AIFM Regulations 2026 [I6].
  - Proposed size tiers by NAV: small (below £750m), medium (£750m–£5bn) and large (above £5bn) [I6].
  - Main consultation closes **14 October 2026**. Rules are to be finalised in 2027 and the regime is intended to take effect in **2028** [I6].
  - **NPPR is retained with limited changes.** UK AIFMs would notify rather than seek permission to market UK AIFs, and the 20-working-day pre-notification is removed [I6].
- **UK financial promotions.**
  - **s.21 FSMA 2000** restricts communicating an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity unless it is made or approved by an authorised person or exempt (Financial Promotion Order 2005) [I49 snippet].
  - Since **7 Feb 2024**, approving promotions for unauthorised firms requires the FCA s21 approver gateway permission [I49 snippet].
  - **s.238 FSMA** restricts authorised persons from promoting unregulated collective investment schemes, which covers most private RE funds. It is lifted only via the PCIS Exemptions Order 2001 or COBS 4.12B.7R [I48].
  - COBS 4 requires promotions to be "fair, clear and not misleading" [I49 snippet].
- **UK SDR and the anti-greenwashing rule.**
  - PS23/16 was published 28 Nov 2023 [I13 snippet].
  - The **anti-greenwashing rule (ESG 4.3.1R) applied from 31 May 2024** to all FCA-authorised firms. Sustainability references must be "consistent with the sustainability characteristics" and "fair, clear and not misleading" [I13 snippet].
  - Guidance FG24/3 was published 23 April 2024 [I13 snippet].
  - There are four labels: Sustainability Focus, Improvers, Impact and Mixed Goals [I13 snippet; label names UNSOURCED-GK].
- **EU SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088) and "SFDR 2.0".**
  - The Commission proposal of **20 Nov 2025** would replace Articles 8 and 9 with three voluntary categories: **Sustainable, Transition and ESG Basics**. Each requires a minimum 70% alignment plus exclusions [I11].
  - Only categorised products could use sustainability terms in names or marketing [I11].
  - It would apply 18 months after entry into force, so about 2028. It is not yet law [I11].
- **MiFID II, GDPR and compliance sign-off** [UNSOURCED-GK; no primary source fetched in this pass]:
  - MiFID II governs investor categorisation (professional vs retail) and placement by investment firms.
  - UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 govern personal data held in CRMs (lawful basis, retention, data subject rights).
  - All marketing materials (decks, DDQs, factsheets, LinkedIn posts) go through compliance sign-off because of s.21/s.238, COBS 4, the anti-greenwashing rule, and pre-marketing/NPPR limits.
