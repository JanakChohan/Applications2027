Part 0. The answer on one pageRead this if you only have ten minutes

Real estate investor relations is the job of finding money for property funds and then looking after the people who gave it.

A real estate investment manager like Harrison Street does not mostly use its own money. It runs funds. Big savers, such as pension funds that pay teachers' and firefighters' pensions, put money into those funds. The manager's deal teams then buy, build and run buildings with it. The manager is paid fees for doing this, plus a share of profits if returns are good.

Investor relations, which Harrison Street calls the Investor Solutions Group (ISG), sits between the investors and the funds. It does two things:

  1. Raise: find investors who might want a fund, pitch it, answer their due diligence questions, and help get them to sign a commitment.
  2. Retain: report to existing investors every quarter, answer whatever they ask, and keep them happy enough to invest in the next fund.
QuestionShort answer
Who is the client?The investor in the fund: pensions, sovereign wealth funds, insurers, endowments, and wealth advisers who invest for rich individuals. Plus the investment consultants who advise them. Not the tenant. Not a buyer of a building.

Part 1. Real estate investing from zeroThe building, the fund, the manager, the investor

Four players

Every real estate fund has four players. Learn them and the rest of this pack falls into place.

PlayerWhat they areAt Harrison Street
Investor (LP)The source of the money. "LP" means limited partner: a part-owner of the fund whose liability is limited to what it put in, and who has no say in day-to-day decisions.Over 1,100 institutions and over 300 wealth advisers [F8]Confirmed

FundThe legal pot that holds the money and the buildings. Often a limited partnership, sometimes a Luxembourg vehicle in Europe.e.g. Core Property Fund, Real Estate Partners IX, European Property Partners III [F11, F6, F26]

KEY TERM: AUMAssets under management: the total value of money and assets a manager runs for clients. It includes borrowed money in some counts, so "fee-paying AUM" (the part that earns fees) is often much lower. Harrison Street: $108.2bn AUM but $54.2bn fee-paying at 31 Dec 2025 [F3].

How the money flows

  1. Commitment and capital calls. An investor promises an amount, say $100m. The manager does not take it all at once. It "calls" capital when it has a deal. Money promised but not yet called is called dry powder or unfunded commitment.
  2. Equity in, plus debt. The fund adds bank loans to the investors' money. LTV (loan-to-value) is loan divided by property value. More debt means higher potential return and higher risk.
  3. Income. Occupiers pay rent. For senior housing or student housing, the building is also a business with staff and services.
  4. Cash and value. Income and any sale proceeds flow back to the fund.
  5. Distributions. The fund pays investors back. In a closed-end fund this comes mostly as buildings are sold.
  6. Fees and carry. The manager takes a management fee (a percentage of money committed or invested) and, if returns pass a minimum level called the hurdle or preferred return, a share of profits called carried interest. Older industry studies quote about 150 basis points (1.5%) on committed equity and 20% carry for opportunity funds, and about 70 basis points for core funds [I32]Reported. These are dated, general figures, not Harrison Street's terms.
SOURCE UNCERTAINHarrison Street's actual fees are private and set fund by fund in legal documents. No source in this pack shows them. Never quote a number for HS fees in the interview.

Why investors use a manager at all

A pension fund with £20bn could, in theory, buy student housing itself. Most do not, for four reasons. [Inferred]

  • Access: the manager finds and wins deals the pension never sees.
  • Operations: running a senior housing community is closer to running a hotel and care business than owning an office. Specialists do it better.
  • Diversification: one fund cheque buys exposure to 70 assets. Fund IX was about 70% allocated across 70 assets at final close [F6]Confirmed.
  • Staff: a pension's real estate team may be three people. They outsource the work and keep the oversight.

That last point is why IR exists. The pension's three people need answers, data and confidence. ISG provides them.

Part 2. Who is the client?The question you asked, answered in full

Two different customers, do not mix them up

A real estate firm has two kinds of customer, and people outside the industry often confuse them.

The occupier

Students, residents, patients, lab tenants. They pay rent. They are the customer of the property and asset management teams and the operators who run the buildings. ISG never sells to them.

The investor (your client)

The institutions and advisers who put money into the funds. They pay fees. They are the customer of ISG. The JD's "current clients, consultants and prospective investors" all sit here.

The two are linked. Happy occupiers mean full buildings, steady income and good returns. Good returns are what ISG reports and sells. So ISG cares about occupancy figures, but it does not manage occupiers. [Inferred]

The gatekeepers: consultants and pools

Investment consultants such as Mercer advise pensions on which managers to hire. They research managers and give ratings. Mercer grades strategies A, B, C or R and says it has over 11,400 ratings and more than 250 due diligence staff [I24]Confirmed. If a consultant does not rate your fund, many pension clients will not even take the meeting. That is why the JD lists consultants separately and why HSAM's global co-head of ISG leads "consultant engagement" [F19].

UK LGPS pools. The UK government is forcing local council pension funds into fewer, bigger pools. Its 4 June 2025 response backed six pools, down from eight, with a pooling deadline of 31 March 2026, and the pools must be FCA-authorised [I22]Confirmed. The Pension Schemes Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026 [I23]Reported. For an IR team this means fewer, larger buyers with professional teams.

Dutch pensions are moving about €1.8 to €1.9 trillion into a new system by January 2028 [I40]Reported, which shifts how they buy illiquid assets. APG is reported to be lifting private markets from about 26% to just over 30% [I41]Reported.

KEY TERM: Placement agentA third-party firm a manager hires to help raise money, paid mainly a success fee. It works for the manager, not the investor [I53]. So it is a partner of ISG, not a client.

How much do investors want real estate right now?

The Hodes Weill and Cornell Baker 2025 survey found the average institutional target allocation to real estate fell to 10.7%, the first fall since the survey began in 2013. Investors sat 90 basis points below target, and expected a 10 basis point rise in 2026 led by Europe, Middle East and Africa [I2, I3]Confirmed. Douglas Weill called it "a tactical pause... rather than a strategic shift away from real estate" [I3].

The INREV/ANREV/PREA 2026 intentions survey put the global allocation at 12.4% against a 12.5% target, with European investors about 20 basis points over a 13.9% target, and debt funds the most favoured vehicle [I15]Confirmed. The two surveys use different samples, so do not compare the numbers directly.

Retaining investors: the quiet half

Once an investor is in, ISG and its Investor Services colleagues owe them a steady stream of information. Industry standards set the shape:

  • INREV Reporting Guidelines (Europe): an annual report plus interim reports, covering governance, performance, the property portfolio, risk and ESG. The current version applies to reporting periods from 1 January 2024 [I16]Confirmed.
  • ILPA Reporting Template v2.0 (mainly private equity, US-led): released January 2025, applies to funds starting from 1 January 2026, and standardises how fees, expenses and carry are shown [I20]Confirmed.
  • Ad hoc requests: "what is your exposure to UK student housing by city?", "send us your carbon data for our own report". The JD calls these "ad-hoc client requests for information and investment analysis".

Part 4. How real estate IR differs from everything near itSo you never confuse it in the interview

Versus listed company investor relations

A REIT (real estate investment trust, a company that owns property and trades on a stock exchange) has an investor relations team too, but the job is different. Its rules are about disclosure: under the Market Abuse Regulation, Article 17, inside information must go to everyone at once, not to favoured analysts [I50]Reported. Fund IR rules are about marketing: who you may approach, with what, and when (Part 7). Mergers & Inquisitions notes a move from corporate IR into private fund IR is "uncommon" because the skills differ [I42].

Part 6. The firm: Harrison Street Asset ManagementWhat it is, where it came from, how it is built

The founding idea

Christopher Merrill founded Harrison Street in Chicago in 2005 [F2, F6]Confirmed. He had spent his career at Heitman, a Chicago real estate manager, where he launched its first foreign fund and in 1997 "moved to London with the idea of building a business around Central European real estate" [F5]Confirmed. He started Harrison Street as a 50-50 partnership with Christopher Galvin, a former Motorola CEO, and his brother Michael [F5].

His design idea, in his own words: "Let's not necessarily be a macro-investor, but a demand investor." He wanted to be "the first firm that was a pure-play investor around alternative real estate" [F4, F5]Confirmed. Instead of betting on offices and shops, which rise and fall with the economy, the firm backs buildings people need whatever the cycle: student beds, care for older people, medical space, storage, and later data centres and infrastructure. The firm's boilerplate calls them "demographic-driven, needs-based assets" [F6].

Part 9. The market right nowWhat the people interviewing you are living through

  • Fundraising is slower. Funds that closed in 2025 took 25 months on average, a record. Only 52% hit their target, though that was up from 40% in 2024 [I1]Confirmed.
  • Money concentrates in the biggest names. Blackstone and Brookfield took 16% of 2025's total [I1]. Mid-sized specialists must differentiate.
  • Alternatives are winning share. Data centres took 37% of sector-specific capital in 2025, up from 2% [I1]. That is Harrison Street's home turf: Merrill says the firm has $7-8bn in data centres [F4]Reported.
  • Europe has a backlog. Europe-focused funds raised $40.6bn in 2025, with $90.4bn still in market at 1 January 2026 [I1]. A London ISG team competes for that capital.
  • 2026 is mixed. Colliers reported US real estate fundraising of $92.6bn in H1 2026, down 38% [I60]Reported.
  • Wealth is the new channel. Blackstone raised $43bn from private wealth in 2025 [I37]Reported. HSAM's Versus purchase and its interval funds and ETF are its route in [F22, F37].
  • The denominator effect is fading. When public markets fell in 2022, private assets became too large a share of portfolios and investors paused new commitments [I67].

Harrison Street began as the specialist and is now becoming the platform. Pick a side and be ready to defend it.

QuestionPoints to hit
What is real estate investor relations?Raise + retain. Client = investor. Sell a fund, not a building. Good service drives re-ups (Fund IX ~60% from existing LPs [F6]).
Who are Harrison Street's clients?Pensions, Taft-Hartley, SWFs, insurers, endowments [F6]; 1,100+ institutions, 300+ RIAs [F8]; consultants as gatekeepers.
Why Harrison Street?Demand, not macro [F5]. 20-year alternatives specialist now with infra, credit and wealth under one brand [F1]. London ISG growing, Middle East office [F14]. Name a sector you care about.
How is this different from working at an agency like CBRE?Deal-level vs fund-level; one-off fees vs recurring fees [I52]. Agents sell buildings; ISG sells a team.
What is the difference between open-end and closed-end?Core Property Fund vs Real Estate Partners. Continuous vs episodic fundraising.
What is happening in fundraising?$172bn in 2024, $222bn in 2025, 25 months on road [I1]. Alternatives and data centres up.
A consultant asks for a number tomorrow and finance has not replied. What do you do?Tell your senior early, chase with a clear deadline, never estimate a number, keep a record. Accuracy over speed.
How would you use AI in this role?Monitor news and fund closes, summarise pension minutes; verify every output; never put confidential client data into unapproved tools.
