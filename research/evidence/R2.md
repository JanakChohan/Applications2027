## 1. Segments and revenue: what "asset management is about 30% of revenue" really refers to

GS reports three segments: Global Banking & Markets, Asset & Wealth Management (AWM) and Platform Solutions (R2-C01). Asset Management is not a segment. It is one of two businesses inside AWM, and the 10-K shows the split.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Firm net revenues | 58,283 | 100% | 56,244 | 100% | 18,679 | 100% |
| AWM segment | 16,679 | 28.6% | 13,726 | 24.4% | 5,051 | 27.0% |
| of which Asset management | 6,784 | 11.6% | 5,852 | 10.4% | 2,194 | 11.7% |
| of which Wealth management | 9,895 | 17.0% | 7,874 | 14.0% | 2,857 | 15.3% |

Sources: 10-K pp. 80, 81, 221; 10-Q p. 118; earnings release p. 1. Percentages are our arithmetic (R2-C02, C03, C08, C09, C10).

**Verdict on the former intern's claim.** "About 30% of revenue" matches the AWM segment in 2025 (28.6%). It does not describe Asset Management alone (11.6%), and it says nothing about the Client Solutions Group, for which GS publishes no revenue at all (R2-C44). AWM also contains private banking and lending ($3,347m in 2025) and principal Investments ($1,305m), neither of which is fee income from managing client assets. Status of the claim as he phrased it: supported for AWM, wrong if applied to Asset Management.

AWM revenue lines, FY2025 (10-K p. 80, R2-C04, C05):

- The current presentation has a single "Investments" line. Equity and debt investments are no longer shown as separate lines.
- "Stable fee income": management and other fees were 69% of AWM net revenues and 19.8% of firm net revenues in 2025. GS's own wording is that the fees "are affected by investment performance, as well as asset inflows and redemptions", so recurring is not the same as guaranteed.
- Profitability: AWM pre-tax earnings $4,127m, pre-tax margin 25%, ROE 12.5% in 2025 (10-K p. 80); nine months 2026 pre-tax earnings $3.36bn, margin 24% (10-Q p. 118). Targets announced in 2026: pre-tax margin about 30% and ROE 17% to 19% within three to five years of year-end 2025 (R2-C06, C07).

Q3 2026 AWM (earnings release and 10-Q p. 118): net revenues $5,051m, up 14% on Q3 2025. Management and other fees $3,587m, incentive fees $587m, private banking and lending $827m, Investments $50m. GS attributes the fee increase to "higher average assets under supervision" and the incentive fee increase to "fund distributions".

## 2. Assets under supervision

**Definition in GS's words** (10-K p. 82, R2-C11): "AUS includes our institutional clients' assets, assets sourced through third-party distributors and high-net-worth clients' assets where we earn a fee for managing assets on a discretionary basis... AUS also includes client assets invested with third-party managers, private bank deposits and advisory relationships where we earn a fee for advisory and other services, but do not have investment discretion. AUS does not include the self-directed brokerage assets of our clients."

AUS is wider than AUM. Use "AUS" when quoting GS.

By region (R2-C15): Americas $3,021bn, EMEA $799bn, Asia $277bn at 30 Sep 2026. EMEA was $820bn at 31 Dec 2025 and $794bn at 30 Sep 2025. EMEA is the smallest geographic unit disclosed. There is no UK number and no channel-by-region table.

Points that matter for interpretation:

- The nine-month 2026 inflow figure includes $31bn of acquired Innovator ETF assets and $5bn from Industry Ventures, less $15bn from the disposal of Goldman Sachs TFI, the Polish fund company (R2-C16, C36). Acquired assets count as "inflows", so the third-party distributed figure is not all organic selling.
- "Liquidity products includes money market funds and private bank deposits" (10-K p. 82). It is not a clean number for the Liquidity Solutions fund business. Q3 2026 liquidity flows were a net outflow of $14bn.
- Q3 2026 saw net market depreciation of $27bn, mostly in fixed income. AUS rose in the quarter only because of inflows.
- Total wealth management client assets, a different and wider measure, were about $2.1 trillion at September 2026.
- The GSAM UK institutional website shows "$3.7+ Trillion" AUS "as of September 30, 2026", which does not match $4,097bn. The page does not explain the difference (R2-C29, Conflicting evidence). Quote the filing.

These are firm-wide blends across every client, region and vehicle. They are not a price list and must not be shown as "what GS charges" a UK pension scheme. Their value for teaching is the mix effect: the same pound earns roughly three times as much in equity as in fixed income. The 10-K risk factors say that if clients keep moving to lower-fee products "our average effective management fee will decline further" (R2-C24).

**Alternatives and OCIO** (10-K pp. 83 to 84; R2-C18 to C21):

- Alternative AUS $420bn at end-2025 ($491bn at 30 Sep 2026). Total alternative assets including non-fee-earning were $627bn at end-2025.
- 2025 average alternatives AUS and fees: funds and discretionary accounts $264bn at 77 bps; **advisory and OCIO accounts $112bn at 17 bps**; total $376bn at 58 bps. Q3 2026: advisory and OCIO accounts $144bn at 17 bps.
- OCIO is defined as "accounts where we are the outsourced chief investment officer of our clients". Since Q4 2025 OCIO assets are "reported in aggregate with advisory accounts". **No standalone OCIO AUS, revenue or mandate is disclosed** in either filing. The only number is the alternatives slice above, mixed with advisory.
- Fundraising: $115bn of third-party commitments in 2025; $122bn in the first nine months of 2026; management expects about $150bn for 2026. Of $438bn raised from 2020 to 2025, $333bn was in AUS and $105bn was non-fee-earning, of which about $81bn "will begin to earn fees (and become AUS) if and when the commitments are drawn and assets are invested". This is primary-source support for keeping commitments and fee-paying assets apart.
- Management and other fees from alternatives: $2.37bn in 2025; $2.18bn in nine months 2026.

## 4. The Claude Code claim and GS's AI tools

**What the former intern said:** Goldman recently restricted staff use of Claude Code.

**What is established:**

- Bloomberg News published "Goldman Restricts Traders and Bankers From Using Some AI Tools" on 2 Nov 2026 (R2-C38, Verified as to headline and date only; the article body returned HTTP 403).
- Two fetched secondary write-ups of that article (PYMNTS and The Decoder, both 2 Nov 2026) agree that internal guidance issued in October 2026 limited Anthropic's Claude Code and Microsoft's GitHub AI coding tools to engineers and removed them from bankers, traders and most other staff. Both quote Goldman spokesperson Nick Carcaterra citing "a variety of factors including productivity and risk management" and saying tools go to people "best suited to leverage them for the long term" (R2-C39, Supported inference).
- Earlier and separate: Reuters reported on 28 Apr 2026, following the Financial Times, that Goldman removed Hong Kong bankers' access to Claude on its internal platform while other models stayed available; Goldman declined to comment (R2-C40).

**Verdict:** the claim is substantially supported, with these limits.

1. It rests on one Bloomberg report relayed by others. That is one source, not several.
2. It is about AI coding tools for non-engineers. It is not a ban on Claude, on Anthropic or on AI across the firm. Engineers keep access.
3. Nothing found says how Asset Management client-facing staff are treated. The reports name "traders and bankers" and "most employees".
4. A statement that "Strats" are exempt appears only in an unsourced TokenPost item. Unconfirmed.
5. Whether it is right to say this in an interview is a judgement call for later chapters; factually, the safe wording is "Bloomberg reported in November 2026 that Goldman limited AI coding tools such as Claude Code to its engineers".

**Other public facts about GS and AI:**

- GS AI Assistant was launched firmwide in June 2025 after a rollout to about 10,000 staff, with versions for developers, investment bankers, research analysts and asset and wealth management staff, for summarising, drafting, data analysis and translation into client-preferred languages (FOX Business reporting an Argenti memo; R2-C41). That is a June 2025 picture; later changes not established.
- CNBC reported on 6 Feb 2026 that Goldman had worked for about six months with embedded Anthropic engineers on agents for trade accounting and client due diligence and onboarding (R2-C42, snippet-level). Client onboarding is a function adjacent to client service, but nothing ties this to Asset Management in EMEA.
- Governance: a Firmwide Artificial Intelligence Risk Committee co-chaired by the CIO and the chief risk officer (10-Q; R2-C43). The 10-K risk factors mention generative AI output risk and AI-enabled fraud.
- The reported pattern over 2025 to 2026 is broad rollout of a general assistant, then narrowing of specialised coding tools on productivity and risk grounds. That sequence is our reading (Layer B), not a GS statement.

## 5. Fee terminology for worked examples

From the 10-K revenue-recognition note (p. 145) and MD&A (R2-C22, C23):

- **Management fees.** Mutual funds: percentage of daily NAV, received monthly. Hedge funds: percentage of month-end NAV, generally received quarterly. Separately managed accounts: percentage of daily or monthly NAV, received quarterly. Private equity funds: percentage of "monthly invested capital or committed capital", received quarterly, semi-annually or annually.
- So a segregated fixed-income mandate example can use NAV as the fee base with quarterly billing. A private-markets example must state whether the base is committed or invested capital; NAV is not the default.
- **Incentive fees.** A percentage of return or of excess return over a benchmark or target, measured over twelve months or over the life of a fund, recognised only when a significant reversal is not probable. Life-of-fund fees can be clawed back. GS calls its enhanced profit share on private equity and credit funds "overrides".
- **Distribution fees.** Payments GS makes "to brokers and advisors related to the placement of the firm's investment funds", calculated on the management fee, NAV or committed capital, booked as transaction based expenses. This is the disclosed cost side of third-party distribution; no amount is broken out.
- **Fee-bearing versus not.** GS distinguishes AUS from "non-fee-earning alternative assets" ($207bn at end-2025), which include unfunded client commitments "where we do not charge fees on commitments".
- **Sanity check** (Layer B, R2-C45): 31 bps on average AUS of $3,328bn is about $10.3bn, below reported management and other fees of $11.5bn because that line also holds non-asset-based fees.
