## 2. GSAM's UK OCIO / fiduciary management business

### Names in public use
- "UK fiduciary management business" (2021–2022) [R3-S16][R3-S15].
- "International OCIO and UK Fiduciary Management Team" (2023, 2024) [R3-S01][R3-S08].
- "UK Fiduciary Management OCIO business" (2024 awards page) [R3-S12].
- "Outsourced Chief Investment Officer business" with a "Global Co-Head" (2026) [R3-S10]; "EMEA OCIO Institutional Business" (2026) [R3-S11].

GSAM's own article says OCIO and fiduciary management "can mean different things in different geographies" and are used interchangeably [R3-S08]. Its working distinction:

| | OCIO (GSAM's usage) | Fiduciary management (GSAM's usage) |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets strategy and strategic asset allocation | Trustees, usually with a third-party adviser | Manager advises, trustees approve |
| What is delegated | Execution of the policy | "All remaining functions", including asset allocation, reporting, analytics |
| Typical user (GSAM's view) | Larger schemes with experienced in-house teams | Smaller schemes with limited resources |
| Oversight | Scheme staff or a third party | GSAM suggests a separate strategic adviser to avoid over-reliance on one firm |

Supporting passage: the fiduciary manager "assumes full fiduciary responsibility and is active at all levels of the investment process" [R3-S08].

### How it works (public evidence)
- Portfolios combine "strategies run by both internal and external managers" with bespoke hedging [R3-S15].
- Models range "from fully delegated fiduciary models to hybrid structures involving consultants and/or independent oversight providers" [R3-S07].
- The trustees' consultant can stay: Redington continued "to oversee implementation with GSAM" at BAE [R3-S02].
- Every UK appointment in the evidence followed a competitive tender with a third-party adviser: PwC (Short Brothers), Redington (BAE), Isio (Shell), Barnett Waddingham (Calor, snippet). This follows from the tender rule below.
- Fee structure example (not GS): "a fiduciary fee based on the asset value of the return seeking and CDI portfolios", with the fiduciary manager having "discretion to negotiate fee agreements with underlying managers" and the LDI manager invoicing separately [R3-S17].

### What the awards establish
Both 2026 awards are trade-publication awards decided by a judging panel on nominations and supporting statements, weighted to the previous 12–18 months, with "No compensation was paid for this recognition" [R3-S10][R3-S11]. They show a panel preferred GSAM's entry in that category. They are not a performance ranking, client survey or market-share measure.

## 4. UK client types and how they buy

Verified regulatory and market facts are cited. General descriptions of how each client type is governed are standard industry knowledge and are flagged as Layer B where they are interpretation.

- **Corporate DB schemes.** Trustees hold the assets and owe duties to members; the sponsoring employer funds deficits. Trustees take regulated advice from an investment consultant and delegate to managers or a fiduciary manager. Most are closed and maturing; the aggregate PPF 7800 funding ratio was 133.7% at end October 2026 [R3-S29]. Endgame choices are buyout with an insurer or run-on. Buy-in activity in 2025: 370 deals, about £38bn [R3-S27].
- **Fiduciary manager selection.** Trustees must run a qualifying tender, inviting at least three unconnected providers, before fiduciary managers run 20% or more of assets; the duty moved from the CMA's 2019 Order into pensions regulations on 1 October 2022 [R3-S24]. TPR recommends a third-party evaluator, scorecards and standardised performance data [R3-S24].
- **DC master trusts and GPPs.** Scale requirement of £25bn by 2030 (or on track by 2035), in force no earlier than 1 January 2030 [R3-S20]. Mansion House Accord targets for private markets [R3-S23]. Buyers are few, large and fee-constrained (Layer B).
- **LGPS.** Since 30 June 2026 funds must delegate implementation to one of six pools and take principal advice from it [R3-S21][R3-S22]. The pool, not the council fund, increasingly selects managers (Layer B).
- **Endowments, foundations, charities.** Not researched to source in this stream (search budget ran out). The Charity Commission's investment guidance (CC14) is the relevant rulebook and should be cited from GOV.UK before use.
- **Sovereign and official institutions.** Not researched in this stream.
- **Investment consultants.** WTW, Mercer, Aon, LCP, Hymans Robertson, Isio, XPS, Redington and Barnett Waddingham (now Howden) advise trustees, rate managers and run tenders. The sector is consolidating [R3-S34][R3-S35][R3-S36] (snippet-level for most deals).
