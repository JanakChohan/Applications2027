## 2. The intermediary chain and how selection works

Evidence gathered is regulatory. Market-practice statements below are **Layer B reasoning** and need an industry source before being presented as fact; that search was not run (budget).

Chain (generic, UK): asset manager (manufacturer) → gatekeeper / fund selection team at a private bank, wealth manager, discretionary fund manager (DFM) or fund-of-funds → investment committee and buy list / model portfolio → platform (custody and dealing) → adviser or portfolio manager → end investor.

Verified regulatory anchors:

- **Consumer Duty**: four outcomes — products and services; price and value; consumer understanding; consumer support (R4-S31).
- **Assessment of Value**: "require AFMs to carry out a Value Assessment at least annually"; in effect since September 2019 (R4-S32).
- **Platform rules (PS13/1)**: "The rules will come into effect on 6 April 2014"; legacy payments from 6 April 2016 (R4-S33). The landing page does not itself spell out the ban on provider payments and cash rebates; cite the PDF or Handbook for that.
- **Overseas Funds Regime**: "The Government has granted equivalence in respect of EEA UCITS (except for money-market funds)" (R4-S30).
- **CCI disclosure regime**: optional from 6 April 2026, in force 8 June 2027 (R4-S28).
- **Targeted support**: "We expect the rules to take effect from 6 April 2026" (R4-S29).

Not yet investigated (no search run): the RDR adviser-charging rules text; MiFID II inducement rules for independent advice and portfolio management; the EU Retail Investment Strategy's 2026 status; fund selector survey evidence; platform market shares (Hargreaves Lansdown, AJ Bell, Quilter, Aberdeen, Transact, FNZ).

Why approved-list status does not guarantee flows (Layer B, `R4-C40`): the gatekeeper permits a fund; separate people (advisers, model managers) decide whether to allocate; the platform must carry the share class; and the end client must have money to invest. Each step can stall. Needs an external source.
