## How to read this register

- Every amount is labelled with what it measures. Scheme assets handed to an OCIO, a fund's capital raised, a target under a memorandum of understanding (MoU) and fee-paying assets are four different things. No source in this register discloses fees or fee-paying assets by strategy.
- "Verified" means the supporting page was fetched and read. Where a page blocked automated access (goldmansachs.com, ipe.com, pif.gov.sa, democracy.kent.gov.uk, etf.com), the source is recorded with `fetched: false` and the entry is capped at "Supported inference" unless another fetched source carries the point.
- No source attributes a win to a named London sales or relationship team. People named in releases are business heads speaking for the firm. Do not infer origination.
- One extension to the vocabulary: `kind: "Regulatory action"` is used once (M-R7-31) so the register is not only wins.

## 1. The two companies the former intern called confidential

### BAE Systems: public and verified

The relationship is public. Goldman issued a press release on 6 September 2023 headed "BAE Systems Pension Schemes Trustees Appoint Goldman Sachs Asset Management for Approximately £23 Billion UK Pension Schemes Mandate" [R7-S01].

- What the £23bn measures: UK defined benefit scheme assets of the BAE Systems Pension Scheme and the BAE Systems Executive Pension Scheme. It is not a fee figure.
- Process: a "highly competitive tender process" [R7-S01]. Redington advised the trustees (their strategic investment adviser since 2021), Travers Smith acted as trustees' lawyers, PwC advised the sponsor [R7-S02].
- Team: "BAE Systems' in-house investment management team will join Goldman Sachs Asset Management" [R7-S01]. Pensions & Investments names the entity as BAE Systems Pension Funds Investment Management [R7-S07]. Headcount is not public.
- Stated rationale: sponsor finance director Brad Greve said "reducing the cost and improving the efficiency of asset management are essential" [R7-S01].
- Goldman people quoted: David Solomon, Marc Nachmann, and Ed Francis as Head of International OCIO and UK Fiduciary Management [R7-S01].
- Still current: the scheme's implementation statement for 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 says "the day-to-day management of the Scheme's investments is carried out by GSAM, the Scheme's OCIO", that the SIPs were updated on 11 July 2024 for the appointment, and that Gallagher is the trustee's investment adviser [R7-S06].

Conclusion: the BAE mandate can be named in interview material and shareable outputs, citing R7-S01 and R7-S06. Anything about fees, internal economics or what the intern saw inside the firm stays out.

### Jaguar Land Rover: not publicly established, and contradicted by the latest public documents read

- The Jaguar Pension Plan annual report for the year ended 31 March 2025 lists "FIDUCIARY INVESTMENT MANAGER: Willis Towers Watson Limited" and "INVESTMENT CONSULTANTS: Hymans Robertson LLP", and states "The return seeking and CDI portfolios are managed by Willis Towers Watson on a delegated basis" [R7-S22].
- The Land Rover Pension Scheme report for the year ended 31 March 2024 also lists Willis Towers Watson Limited as fiduciary investment manager [R7-S23].
- Both PDFs were converted to text and searched for "Goldman" and "GSAM": zero occurrences.
- Two searches for a 2025 or 2026 change of fiduciary manager found nothing.

Conclusion: status "Not publicly established". A change after 31 March 2025 cannot be ruled out, but there is no public evidence for it. Keep JLR out of interview material and out of any shareable file as a Goldman client. The intern's statement stays an "Unverified account".

Observations (Layer B, interpretation):

- Every appointment with a described process went through a tender run or supported by a third-party adviser. Four cases is not a market statistic.
- Sun Chemical and Calor were taken from an incumbent fiduciary manager (Mercer, Cardano). Fiduciary mandates are contestable; an incumbent can lose.
- Team transfers appear only in the very large deals (BAE; "some staff" at Shell [R7-S11]). The smaller appointments report none. So the intern's description of OCIO as bringing the client's team in-house fits some deals, not the model as a whole.
- The Shell group's Dutch fund chose BlackRock [R7-S09]. Selection is by each trustee board.
- BAE's trustees kept a separate investment adviser after appointing an OCIO [R7-S06].
- Professional Pensions (28 Nov 2025) quotes Chloe Kipling as co-head of the EMEA institutional business and reports a September 2025 hire of Carolyn Schuster-Woldan as managing director in the UK fiduciary management/OCIO team [R7-S24]. People leads for R6.

## 3. Large non-EMEA OCIO deals that show the model

- UPS (May 2024): $43.4bn of US and Canadian DB assets at 31 March 2024; the 22-person in-house team joined GSAM on 1 September 2024 [R7-S21][R7-S07].
- Verizon and Lockheed Martin (9 July 2026): "over $70 billion", split by the release itself into $30bn DB assets under management and $40bn DC assets under advisement [R7-S13]. OCIO assets under supervision $480bn as of 31 March 2026.
- Bombardier "and others" (2022): $5.4bn pension and insurance mandate with team members joining; single trade source [R7-S04].
- Earlier scale came partly from acquisitions: Pacific Global Advisors (2015), Verus's OCIO business (2017), Aptitude (2018) [R7-S04].

OCIO asset figures quoted over time, each on its own basis: more than £200bn (Sept 2023, no as-of date) [R7-S01]; $311.8bn OCIO AUM at 31 Dec 2024 [R7-S07]; nearly $450bn (Sept 2025, no as-of date) [R7-S08]; $480bn AUS as of 31 March 2026 [R7-S13].
