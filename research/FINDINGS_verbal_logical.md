# Phase 1 Research — Aon scales **verbal** + the **logical / inductive** family (+ scoring & integrity)

Companion to `research/FINDINGS.md` (numerical). Same method: parallel web passes reading primary
sources (Aon's own practice PDFs) and established vendors, every claim rated for confidence, conflicts

**Confidence key:** **High** = Aon primary source or unanimous vendors · **Medium** = several vendors

## A. scales verbal (worded True / False / Cannot Say — no maths)

**Primary source:** Aon's official **verbal-reasoning practice PDF** (read in full — 5 worked examples
about a fictional Milan fashion house). It overrides vendor descriptions on conflicts.

### Format
- **~49 tasks / 12 minutes** (~14–15 s/item). 49 is the dominant figure (JobTestPrep, GraduatesFirst,
  AssessmentDay, psychometric-success, psychometrictests.org, fintest — **High**). **Conflict:**
  practiceaptitudetests says **47**; several sources say "up to 49" and stress **you are not expected
  to finish** — Aon PDF: *"you are not expected to answer all the questions — you just need to work
  quickly and accurately."* Use **~49**, completion not required.
- **~5–6 topic tabs, each holding SHORT paragraphs, all about ONE fictional company.** The official PDF
  shows **5** tabs (About the Company, Values, Corporate Strategy, Locations, Marketing), each ~3 short
  blurbs. Vendors commonly say **6** (Structure, Strategy, Principles, Products & Services, Locations,
  Executive Board). Count varies by version (**High** on "short paragraphs, one company"; **Medium** on
  exact tab count). One outlier (psychometric-success) claims "large extracts" — contradicted by the
  official PDF; weight to **short**.
- **Each statement relates to exactly ONE tab, and you must find it yourself.** Aon PDF (verbatim):
  *"each statement relates to only one data sheet. You need to find and refer to that data sheet."*
  Tabs are labelled by topic; you match the statement's subject to the label. There's a learning curve —
  *"after 10–15 questions you should be able to tell what each tab is about"* (**High**).
- **Navigation:** one tab visible at a time, free movement between tabs. Across statements, Aon PDF says
  navigation is *possible but* *"it is recommended to work on the tasks in the predefined order."*
  Whether you can freely change submitted answers at the end is a **vendor claim (Medium)**, not Aon-stated.

### The decision rule (encode exactly — confirmed against the 5 official worked examples)
- **TRUE = fully corroborated/entailed by the relevant tab**, *including via synonym/paraphrase that
  genuinely matches.* Ex1: "imported" ⇒ "sourced outside Italy" → TRUE. Ex2: "dare to think differently"
  ≈ "leave known paths" → TRUE.
- **FALSE = at least partly contradicted.** Ex4: statement says courses are on an "online platform" but
  the passage says "on-premises" → **FALSE** even though the rest of the statement was right. **Encode:
  any contradicted element ⇒ FALSE.**
- **CANNOT SAY = the tab neither confirms nor refutes it** (information not given, or requires an
  unstated inference). Ex3: attributes a plan to a specific person the passage never links → CANNOT SAY.
  Ex5 (explicit outside-inference trap): website-traffic claim rests on a social-media demographic that
  belongs to the *social-media* presence, not the *website* → CANNOT SAY.
- **Confidence: High** (Aon primary).

### Sector variants
| Variant | What it is | Differs by |
|---|---|---|
| verbal (admin) | general verbal reasoning | topic/vocabulary |
| verbal (consumer) | retail topics | topic/vocabulary |
| verbal (finance) | "**complex** data, lists, articles" | topic + modestly denser |
| verbal (industry) | "**complex** …" (industrial) | topic + modestly denser |
| verbal (instruct) | judge an action **correct/incorrect** vs a set of instructions | **different task type** — a sibling, not a T/F/CS reskin |

admin/consumer/finance/industry differ mainly by **topic + vocabulary** (finance/industry flagged
"complex" — **Medium** that they're harder). **instruct is a different format** — I'll treat it as
out of scope for the T/F/CS verbal module. **Confidence: High** on the variant list.

### Traps (each with the fix)
1. **Absolute / quantifier words** ("all / always / never / only"): one exception makes the statement
   False; an absolute rarely matches a hedged passage → lean **False/Cannot Say**. *(Medium — general
   T/F/CS strategy, consistent with Aon Ex4.)*
2. **Outside knowledge** — the biggest error. Aon Ex5 is built as this trap; GraduatesFirst: *"Only use
   the information provided… Do not consider any external knowledge."* Answer from the tab only. *(High.)*
3. **Paraphrase that flips a detail ⇒ False**; **near-synonym that genuinely matches ⇒ True.** Verify
   each key word of the statement against a specific sentence. *(High — Aon Ex1/Ex2/Ex4.)*
4. **Quantifier swaps** (some↔all, may↔will): the scope word must match. *(Medium.)*
5. **Wrong tab:** answering with the wrong passage open reportedly loses points even if correct.
   *(Medium-High vendor claim; the underlying "one statement → one data sheet" is High/Aon.)*
6. **Time:** ~14–15 s/item; build a mental tab-map in the first ~10 items; accuracy over completion. *(High.)*

### Verbal scoring note
Aon's verbal PDF is **silent on negative marking**; fintest & psychometric-success say it applies. The
scoring research (§C) confirms negative marking applies to cut-e **ability tests generally**. We apply
consistent negative marking across all modules and flag the verbal-specific silence. *(Medium for verbal
specifically; High for the family.)*

## B. The logical / inductive "shapes" family

> **Correction to the brief:** sources agree **clx is NOT a colour-sort variant of cls.** **cls** is the
> colour-sort (letters/numbers into two groups); **clx** is a distinct "which 2 of 4 shape-grids match a
> reference rule" test. We build the **cls colour-sort** you described, and note clx as a sibling.

### scales ix — "discovering rules" (inductive odd-one-out) · **High**
- **Mechanic:** a series of **9 objects**; all but one share a hidden rule — **pick the ONE that breaks
  it.** Exactly one correct answer.
- **20 tasks / 5 minutes** (~15 s), ordered by rising difficulty, not expected to finish. **Not adaptive.**
- **Varying attributes:** side/edge count, rotation/orientation, count/repetition cycles, containment/
  nesting, fill/colour, position/symbol-to-position mapping.
- **Skip/return: CONFLICT** — JobTestPrep says you can revisit; GraduatesFirst says you cannot. *(Medium.)*

### scales cls — inductive-logical (colour-sort grids) · **High**
- **Mechanic:** **ten 3×3 grids** of letters/numbers; **6 pre-classified into two colour groups**
  (orange vs blue); discover the hidden rule and **assign the 4 unmarked grids** to a group.
- **12 tasks / 12 minutes** (~1 min/task; = 48 grid assignments). **Not adaptive.**
- **Hidden-rule types:** count of a specific symbol (e.g. three O's), number of *distinct* elements
  (4 vs 3), positional (a value fixed in a cell).

### scales clx — inductive-logical (grid-matching) · **High (mechanic)**
- **Mechanic:** 2 reference 3×3 shape-grids share a rule; from 4 answer grids **pick exactly the 2** that
  follow the same rule. **~6 minutes**; item count not consistently published. *(Sibling of cls; noted,
  not the primary build.)*

### scales lst / gapChallenge — deductive "shape sudoku" (Latin square) · **High**
- **Mechanic:** a grid where **each shape appears once per row AND once per column**; one cell is **"?"** —
  **choose the shape** that belongs there. **This is the "given shapes, click the correct shape" test —
  the most likely match for the candidate's description (Medium-High).**
- **4×4 (4 options) growing to 5×5 (5 options)** as difficulty rises. **~6 minutes**, unfixed item count.
  **Adaptive** (harder after correct answers). **You can pre-fill blank cells to reason** (**Medium-High**).
- Skip/return not clearly documented.

### also in the family (context, not built)
- **scales sx / switchChallenge** — deductive (pick the operator/"funnel" that reordered symbols), ~5–6 min,
  adaptive. **gridChallenge** — spatial working memory (recall dot positions), ~9 rounds/9 min.

**Most likely "click the shape" test:** **scales lst** (you literally select the one correct shape),
with **scales ix** the secondary bet (if the memory is "the one that doesn't fit"). We build both.

## C. Scoring & integrity (encode into behaviour + GUIDE)

- **Negative marking — High.** Aon (aon.com): incorrect answers "have a negative effect." psychometric-
  success: *"one point… deducted if a question is answered incorrectly"* → **flat −1**; **blank = 0**;
  *"better to leave the answer blank than risk a point deduction."* Confirmed for **scales ix** explicitly
  (*"deducted for incorrect ones… you better move on and not randomly choose"*) and framed as applying to
  cut-e ability tests generally → **covers verbal, ix, cls, lst.** **⇒ blind guessing is negative-EV.**
  *(Conflict: a fractional −⅓/−¼ appears only in low-authority sources — unsupported; use flat −1.)*
- **Norm-referenced — High.** No universal pass mark; raw score → **Stanine (1–9) + percentile** vs a
  country/role norm group; **each employer sets its own cut-off** (some cited ~Stanine 6 / ~75th pct —
  Medium). Stanine 5 = average.
- **Unique item bank per candidate — High.** Each test is drawn from a large bank at run-time, so every
  candidate's items differ (anti-cheat). Classic **scales tests are fixed-length** (parallel forms, not
  item-adaptive); **smartPredict challenges are genuinely adaptive.**
- **Integrity — the key point (High).** Proctoring (webcam/audio) is available but often the first stage
  is unsupervised. Crucially, Aon/employers **re-test a module under supervision at the interview stage**:
  *"If you are invited to an interview after the online assessment, you will be expected to complete a
  repeat module… If you cheated in the online assessment, it will come to light during the retest, at the
  very latest"* → dishonest candidates are *"immediately excluded."*
- **Why skill is the only thing that transfers (for the honest GUIDE note):** the unsupervised score is
  provisional and re-verified under supervision; the item bank is randomised so there's no answer key to
  memorise; scoring is norm-referenced + negatively marked, so the only durable way to raise your
  percentile is faster, more accurate underlying reasoning. Legitimate practice raises **both** the
  unsupervised and supervised score identically; anything else collapses at the retest.

1. Verbal item count **49 vs 47**; tab count **~6 vs 5** (official PDF).
2. Verbal passage length: short (official + most) vs "large extracts" (one outlier).
3. Verbal back-navigation / changing answers: Aon says "possible but work in order"; some vendors say free.
4. Verbal negative marking: Aon PDF silent; vendors + family-level evidence say it applies.
