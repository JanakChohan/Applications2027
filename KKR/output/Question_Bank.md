## KKR's culture is the marking scheme

KKR's founders have written down what they look for. Read this section as the hidden rubric behind every question, including the technical ones.

Henry Kravis, co-founder (kkr.com, Oct 2024)

"When we are interviewing candidates wishing to join KKR, we look beyond financial acumen, strategy and leadership skills. We look for like and trust and we look to understand who the person is." [S385] [Confirmed]

"Those who are arrogant fail this test." [S385] [Confirmed]

On trust and arrogance

"People don't want to work with you, let alone entrust you with their capital, if they can't trust you will do what you say." [S384] [Confirmed]

"There is something that eats away at like and trust. It is arrogance." Kravis keeps a framed "Arrogance kills" sign behind his desk [S384] [Confirmed].

Kravis also says KKR watches how candidates treat people they are not trying to impress, such as waiters or office staff [S385] [Confirmed]. The KCS leadership uses the same words. Jim Burns, Head of Institutional Client Solutions in the Americas: "We subscribe to the view of Henry Kravis and George Roberts that people do business with those they like and trust. Partnership and alignment shape our relationships at every turn" [S386] [Confirmed]. A 2026 KKR Client Solutions intern advert in Singapore says KKR operates "with one P&L where most places have silos. It's not for everyone" [S389] [Confirmed].

Say this in the interview (one story worth knowing)

Kravis calls Walter Industries his "best deal", even though the company went bankrupt after KKR bought it in 1987. KKR spent almost 17 years and settled for over \$300 million, against \$117 million of KKR equity, to get its fund investors their money back plus a small profit [S384] [Confirmed]. For a KCS candidate this is gold: it is a story about keeping promises to investors, which is what Client Solutions does every day. Use it once, briefly, and only if it fits the question.

Don't say this

"I really embody like and trust." Repeating KKR's slogans back is not evidence. Give your own example of earning someone's trust (a job, a society, a volunteering role) and let the interviewer make the link [Inferred].

Traps in the written application

- "KKR is the largest alternative manager." False: Blackstone (\$1.35tn) and Apollo (\$1.05tn) are larger than KKR (\$796bn) [S213] [S220] [S200] [Confirmed].
- "KKR manages over \$800bn." The last reported figure is \$796bn at 30 June 2026. Q3 numbers come out on 9 November [S330] [S333].
- Number dumping. Three figures in a paragraph read as a fact sheet. One figure, explained, reads as understanding [Inferred].
- Naming KKR staff you have never spoken to. It reads as name-dropping, which clashes with "arrogance kills" [S384] [Inferred].
- Implying KCS is your route into investing. KCS is a career in its own right; senior KCS leaders came from PIMCO, Goldman Sachs and UBS [S79] [S120] [S124] [Confirmed].

## KCS question bank: 59 questions with scaffolds

How to use this bank. Each block gives the question, what it tests, an answer structure, facts you can weave in, and the trap. Tags show where the question comes from: a source ID means it is reported in that source; [Inferred] means it is strongly implied by KKR's adverts or culture pages but not seen as a real question. The CV walk-through and live scenario role-plays are covered in other parts of this pack.

Key term

**Scaffold:** the skeleton of an answer: the order of points and the facts to hang on it. Rehearse the scaffold, not a word-for-word script. Scripts sound recited on HireVue and collapse when an interviewer interrupts [Inferred].

### A. Motivational (8)

1\. Tell me about yourself. [S394] [S419]

Tests: can you tell a clear story in 60 to 90 seconds that ends at KCS.

**Structure:** now (degree, one hook), then two past chapters that built the skills KCS needs (explaining, serving people, accuracy), then why you are in this room. **Weave:** nothing numeric; keep facts for later. **Trap:** reading your CV top to bottom. Full CV prep is in the CV part of this pack.

2\. Why KKR? [S394] [S395] [S402]

Tests: specific interest in KKR, not alternatives in general (Fortune lists real interest in KKR specifically as a recruiter test) [S393].

**Structure:** one structural fact, one recent milestone, one cultural point, each with "which matters to me because". **Weave:** one firm, one compensation pool [S385]; GII V close [S334]; private wealth 18% of AUM [S71]. **Trap:** praise that fits Blackstone too [S414].

3\. Why Global Client Solutions rather than an investment team? [S397]

Tests: whether KCS is a real choice or a fallback.

**Structure:** what you enjoy (explaining, relationships, breadth) then what KCS gives that a deal team does not (seeing the whole platform, every asset class) then evidence you have done that kind of work. **Weave:** "connect the dots" across PE, infrastructure, real estate, credit, capital markets and insurance [S390]. **Trap:** "I want to learn about investing first and maybe move later."

4\. Why private markets, rather than a bank or a traditional asset manager? [S399] [S398]

Tests: do you know what is different about alternatives.

**Structure:** long-term ownership, relationships that last a decade or more, and products that need real explanation. **Weave:** a closed-end fund runs about ten years, so the client relationship does too [Inferred]; KKR's investors span 65 countries [S70]. **Trap:** "the pay is better" or "banking hours are worse".

5\. Why did you rank KCS first, and would you be happy in your second choice? [Inferred]

Tests: consistency and maturity.

**Structure:** use the one-line ranking logic from the ranking section, then a real reason for choice two. **Weave:** KCS partners with Capital Markets on distribution (team text); Danzey's dual role [S156]. **Trap:** sounding as if you would take anything.

6\. Why London, and why apply through SEO London? [Inferred]

Tests: commitment and honesty.

**Structure:** London covers clients across EMEA; SEO trained you and is a long-standing KKR partner. **Weave:** KKR was a founding steering-group firm of SEO London's Alternative Investments Programme in 2021 [S381]; Kravis chairs SEO's US board [S383]. **Trap:** treating SEO as a shortcut. Show what you learnt from its training.

7\. Where do you see yourself in five years? [S394] [S419]

Tests: realism and long-term motivation.

**Structure:** skills, not titles: deep knowledge of two or three products, trusted by a set of clients, able to run a meeting. **Weave:** KCS career paths run Analyst, Associate, Principal, Director, MD, Partner [S80]; people move between product and coverage roles inside KCS [S132] [S129]. **Trap:** "running my own fund".

8\. What would you add to the team that other candidates would not? [S393]

Tests: self-awareness. Fortune says KKR asks how your background "complements the team".

**Structure:** one distinct asset (a language, a community you understand, a skill like clear writing), proof, and how it helps a client. **Weave:** London KCS adverts call German or French "a plus" [S388]; KCS covers family offices, private banks and institutions [S150]. **Trap:** generic strengths ("hard-working").

### B. KKR as a firm (7)

9\. What do you know about KKR? Give me 60 seconds. [Inferred]

Tests: can you compress a firm into a clear picture.

**Structure:** founded 1976 with \$120,000 [S13]; \$796bn AUM at 30 June 2026 [S74]; three segments (asset management, Global Atlantic insurance, strategic holdings) [S1]; less than 25% now traditional PE [S332]; one-firm culture. **Trap:** overclaiming size (KKR is fourth by AUM among big peers) [S213] [S220].

10\. Talk me through a recent KKR deal or fund close you find interesting. [S393] [S394]

Tests: real curiosity and the ability to say why something matters.

**Structure:** what happened, why KKR did it, why a client would care. For KCS, a fund close often works better than a deal. **Weave:** GII V \$19.2bn with private wealth platforms among investors [S334]; North America Fund XIV about \$23bn [S335]. **Trap:** picking something you cannot explain past the headline.

11\. What makes KKR different from Blackstone or Apollo? [Inferred]

Tests: balanced judgement.

**Structure:** where KKR is not first (size, wealth scale, insurance scale), then what is distinctive (one compensation approach, capital markets tied to client work, Capital Group partnership, Family Capital segment). **Weave:** Blackstone private wealth about \$324bn vs K-Series \$42bn [S216] [S200]; Lewin's "1 firm" line [S3]. **Trap:** "only KKR has in-house capital markets" (Blackstone and Apollo do too) [S214] [S220].

12\. What does "people do business with people they like and trust" mean to you? [Inferred]

Tests: whether you understand the culture beyond the slogan.

**Structure:** trust is built by doing what you said (Kravis: "Our word is our bond") [S384], then your own example. **Weave:** Walter Industries [S384]. **Trap:** a speech with no example of your own.

13\. Why does it matter to a client that KKR runs one compensation pool? [Inferred]

Tests: linking internal structure to client value.

**Structure:** teams are paid on the whole firm, so sharing ideas and clients costs nobody anything; a client gets introduced to the right expert. **Weave:** 10-K "single expense pool" [S70]; Kravis "we all succeed and fail together" [S385]. **Trap:** claiming you know how rivals pay (not verified) [Inferred].

14\. What do you think of KKR's push into private wealth? [Inferred]

Tests: forming a view with both sides.

**Structure:** opportunity (growth, stickier capital), risk (redemptions, reputation), what good looks like (honest liquidity explanation). **Weave:** K-Series \$42bn [S73]; K-FIT paid about 80% of requests in Q1 2026 and 100% in Q2 [S338] [Reported]; Nuttall "up 20% net year-to-date" [S77]. **Trap:** one-sided cheerleading.

15\. Have you read any KKR Insights? Do you agree with it? [S394]

Tests: whether you follow KKR's house view, which clients hear from KCS.

**Structure:** name the piece, its core idea, one part you agree with and one question you have. **Weave:** Mid-Year 2026 "The Divergence Conundrum": "the cycle continues, but the rules of the game are changing" [S360]; Outlook 2026 "High Grading" [S361]. **Trap:** agreeing with everything.

### C. KCS and the role (9)

16\. What does KCS do, in your own words? [S390] [Inferred]

Tests: whether you understand the job.

**Structure:** find investors, explain products, handle due diligence, onboard, report for ten years, raise the next fund. Two halves: developing and servicing. **Weave:** two focus areas, "Sales/Relationship Management and Product/Strategy Management" [S390]. **Trap:** describing only the selling half.

17\. What is the difference between a relationship manager and a product specialist? [Inferred]

Tests: org awareness.

**Structure:** RM owns the client and territory; product specialist owns the fund's story, materials and diligence; juniors support both. **Weave:** product teams "own marketing materials, developing thought leadership and leading investor diligence processes" [S106]. **Trap:** ranking one above the other.

18\. What would you expect to do as a KCS summer intern? [Inferred]

Tests: realistic expectations and "support mindset".

**Structure:** briefing notes before meetings, updating decks, DDQ and RFP coordination, CRM notes, events, research. **Weave:** Frankfurt KCS intern duties: client requests, due diligence and RFPs, pitch decks, investor events [S390]; London analyst: reporting, KYC, investment updates [S388]. **Trap:** expecting to pitch clients yourself.

19\. How is selling to a pension fund different from selling through a private bank? [Inferred]

Tests: audience thinking.

**Structure:** who decides, ticket size, the product (drawdown fund vs evergreen), regulation, what the end client needs explained. **Weave:** institutions mostly use drawdown funds, SMAs and co-investment [S70]; wealth uses wirehouses, private banks, feeders [S70]; EEA retail sales bring suitability rules [S70]. **Trap:** "same pitch, smaller numbers".

20\. What do LPs care about most right now? [S398]

Tests: market awareness from the client side.

**Structure:** one main point (cash back, measured by DPI), two supporting points (fewer, bigger manager relationships; co-investment and fee terms). **Weave:** distributions about 14% of NAV in 2025 per Bain [S262]; consolidation of LP relationships [S260]. **Trap:** answering with IRR only.

21\. Walk me through a typical fundraising process. [S398]

Tests: process thinking.

**Structure:** plan and target list, pre-marketing, materials and data room, meetings, DDQ and operational diligence, legal terms and side letters, first close, final close, then reporting [S70] [Inferred]. **Weave:** ILPA DDQ format is widely used [S320]. **Trap:** forgetting that service after close is half the job.

22\. What would you put in a quarterly investor report? [S398]

Tests: client empathy and accuracy.

**Structure:** headline performance (net, with DPI and TVPI), cash flows (calls and distributions), portfolio changes, valuation approach, outlook and risks, contact points. **Weave:** ILPA's Reporting Template v2.0 applies from Q1 2026 for funds in their investment period [S318]. **Trap:** marketing spin in a report.

23\. How would you tailor one message for a German pension and a UK family office? [S388] [Inferred]

Tests: the London advert's "tailor messages to suit different audiences" [S388].

**Structure:** same facts, different emphasis: governance, regulation and long-term liabilities for the pension; flexibility, co-investment and direct access for the family. **Weave:** KKR offers families "Co-Investment Opportunities" and "Bespoke Separately Managed Accounts" [S84]. **Trap:** changing the facts, not the framing.

24\. How does KCS work with KKR Capital Markets? [Inferred]

Tests: whether you read the team description closely.

**Structure:** KCM commits to more of a deal than the fund needs, then syndicates the excess; KCS knows which clients want co-investment and brings them in. **Weave:** 10-K on syndication of co-investments [S70]; Danzey leads both in Europe [S156]. **Trap:** mentioning confidential deal details: information barriers apply [Inferred].

### D. Industry and markets (6)

25\. How is the macro environment affecting investors, and what does it mean for KKR? [S393]

Tests: Koo's exact criterion.

**Structure:** one macro force, its effect on investors, the KKR product it points to. **Weave:** McVey: inflation's higher "resting heart rate", stocks and bonds diversifying each other less, "Security of Everything" [S360]; infrastructure fits that theme. **Trap:** a macro lecture with no "so what" for a client.

## Conduct on the day, whichever team you face

KKR's founders say they judge "who the person is", not only skills [S385] [Confirmed]. Most of that judgement happens between the questions. The checklist below applies to every team and every stage [Inferred].

#### Before

- Re-read your own application. Interviewers often start from what you wrote [Inferred].
- Know three current numbers: AUM \$796bn at 30 June 2026, \$34bn raised in Q2, K-Series \$42bn [S330]. After 9 November, update them from the Q3 release [S333].
- One-line answers ready for "why KKR", "why this team", "why your ranking".
- For video: camera at eye level, quiet room, notes out of shot. HireVue gives one attempt per answer [S394].

#### During

- Answer first, then explain. Interviewers have 20 to 30 minutes [S402] [S403].
- For a technical question you cannot answer: say so, then reason aloud from what you do know. Guessing a number is worse than a clear "I don't know, here is how I would work it out" [Inferred].
- Ask to write things down in a maths or case question. It is normal and shows care [Inferred].
- Use "we" for team results and "I" for your own actions.

#### Everyone counts

- Kravis: KKR watches how candidates treat people they are not trying to impress; "Those who are arrogant fail this test" [S385] [Confirmed]. That includes reception, SEO staff, IT support on a video call and other candidates.
- Be on time and do what you say. "Our word is our bond" [S384] [Confirmed].

#### After

- Note every question you were asked within an hour. Patterns help in the next round [Inferred].
- If you send thanks, keep it short and specific to one point from the conversation. Send it only through the channel the recruiters used [Inferred].
- Do not post interview questions online; treat the process as confidential [Inferred].
