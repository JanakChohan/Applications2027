## 1. Findings

### A. Founding insight and early history

**A1. The three founders and Bear Stearns.**
- Jerome Kohlberg Jr. ran corporate finance at Bear, Stearns & Co. His two protégés were first cousins Henry Kravis and George Roberts. [S27][S28] [Confirmed]
- At Bear Stearns, from the mid 1960s, they did what they called "bootstrap" acquisitions. These were purchases of mostly family-owned companies using a small slice of equity and a lot of borrowed money. This is the model later called the leveraged buyout (LBO). [S27][S28] [Confirmed]
- The encyclopedia history says Kohlberg devised or first used the technique in 1965 at Bear Stearns. Roberts and Kravis then completed 14 buyouts between 1969 and 1975, with "generally mediocre results". [S27] [Reported]
- Named Bear Stearns era deals: Stern Metals (1965), Incom (1971), Cobblers Industries (1971, bought for about $27m, later bankrupt), Vapor Corp. (1972, about $37m; investors got 12 times their stake on sale in 1978), Boren Clay (1973), Industrial Components Group, a Rockwell division (1975, 22 times the investment in five years). [S27][S28] [Reported]
- Why they left: Bear Stearns leadership (Wikipedia names executive Cy Lewis) rejected the idea of an in-house buyout fund. The trio then set up their own firm. [S28] [Reported]
- Beginner's note: a "leveraged buyout" means buying a company mainly with borrowed money, secured on the company's own cash flows. The buyer improves the company, pays down the debt, then sells. Because the buyer only put in a small amount of equity, a modest rise in the company's value can multiply that equity many times. [Inferred]

**A2. Founding in 1976.**
- KKR's own history page: "On May 1, 1976, Henry Kravis and George Roberts co-founded KKR alongside their mentor Jerome Kohlberg with $120,000, launching what we now know as the alternatives industry." [S13] [Confirmed: primary]
- KKR describes the early firm as "3 guys and a broom". [S14] [Confirmed: primary]
- KKR held its 50th anniversary on 1 May 2026. [S5][S13] [Confirmed]
- First buyout as KKR: A.J. Industries, 1976. [S28] [Reported]
- KKR's FY2025 10-K still lists the "1976 Fund": $31m committed, $537m realised, 39.5% gross IRR, 35.5% net IRR, 17.1 times invested capital. [S2] [Confirmed: primary]
- Early landmarks from the company history: Houdaille Industries (1979, about $355m, the largest LBO to that time and KKR's first buyout of a major public company); Beatrice (1986, about $6.2bn, KKR equity $402m, Drexel junk bond financing $2.5bn; KKR said LPs earned a 43% annual return). [S27] [Reported]

**A3. Kohlberg leaves (1987).**
- Kohlberg resigned in 1987, aged 61, and founded Kohlberg & Co. He was uneasy that KKR's deal hunting "disturbingly echoed the tactics of corporate raiders" and became known internally as "Doctor No". He sued his former partners in 1990 over reduced ownership stakes. The suit was settled on undisclosed terms. [S27][S28] [Reported]
- Kravis and Roberts were co-CEOs from 1987 until October 2021. [S10][S28] [Confirmed]

**A4. RJR Nabisco (1988 to 1989).**
- The bidding started in October 1988 when CEO F. Ross Johnson proposed a management buyout at about $75 per share. KKR won at $109 per share against management's $112, because the board judged KKR's bid to be more certain. [S28] [Reported]
- Size: about $25bn of equity value, roughly $31bn including assumed debt. Sources differ ($25bn, $30.6bn, $31.1bn, $31.4bn) depending on what is counted. Completed in early 1989 (sources give February or April 1989). [S27][S28] [Confirmed for "about $25bn equity, about $31bn with debt"; dates Reported]
- It became the subject of the bestselling book "Barbarians at the Gate". [S28] [Confirmed: widely known]
- Outcome: the encyclopedia history says "Kravis won the battle but clearly overpaid for his prize". KKR made no new LBOs for about three years after February 1989. KKR traded its remaining RJR stake for Borden in 1995. [S27] [Reported]
- Hard number from KKR's own 10-K: the "1987 Fund" (the vehicle behind RJR) had $6,130m committed, 12.1% gross IRR, 8.9% net IRR, 2.4 times invested capital. Compare the 1986 Fund: 34.4% gross IRR and 13.5 times. [S2] [Confirmed: primary]
- So RJR was a reputational landmark but a weak financial result relative to KKR's other early funds. [Inferred]

### B. How KKR makes money now

**B1. The three segments (since 2024 reporting).** [S1][S2] [Confirmed]
1. **Asset Management.** Five business lines: Private Equity, Real Assets (infrastructure and real estate), Credit and Liquid Strategies, Capital Markets (KCM), and Principal Activities (KKR's own balance sheet invested alongside clients). Earns management fees, transaction and monitoring fees, fee related performance revenues, carried interest, and investment income.
2. **Insurance.** Global Atlantic, a retirement and life insurer (annuities, reinsurance). Earns the spread between investment income and the cost of policies.
3. **Strategic Holdings.** Long-term stakes in operating companies owned by KKR itself, today mainly KKR's own participation in its Core Private Equity strategy. Earns dividends now, and gains on sale later. 19 companies at 31 Dec 2025. [S2]

KKR calls these the "Three Engines of Growth". [S12] [Confirmed]

**B2. KKR's earnings "ladder" (plain English).** [S1][S5] [Confirmed]
- **Fee Related Earnings (FRE)** = management fees + transaction and monitoring fees (net) + fee related performance revenues, minus fee related compensation and other operating expenses. This is the steady, recurring part of the asset manager.
- **Total Operating Earnings (TOE)** = FRE + Insurance Operating Earnings + Strategic Holdings Operating Earnings. KKR calls this "the more durable and recurring portion" of earnings. Introduced in November 2023. [S1][S11]
- **Total Investing Earnings** = net realised performance income (carried interest after staff share) + net realised investment income (gains on KKR's own money).
- **Adjusted Net Income (ANI)** = TOE + Total Investing Earnings, minus interest expense and taxes. This is KKR's headline "bottom line".
- 84% of last-twelve-month (LTM) segment earnings to Q2 2026 came from the recurring pieces (TOE). [S1][S3] [Confirmed]

**B4. Each revenue stream explained.**
- **Management fees.** A yearly percentage charged on client money, usually paid quarterly. Ranges by product (from the 10-K): drawdown PE funds 1.0% to 2.0% of committed capital during the investment period, then 0.75% to 1.50% of invested capital; drawdown infrastructure and real estate 0.75% to 1.50%; drawdown credit 0.85% to 1.50% of invested capital; CLOs 0.4% to 0.5%; other leveraged credit 0.4% to 0.8%; open-ended vehicles (core infrastructure, core+ real estate, evergreen direct lending, K-Series) 0.50% to 1.25%. [S2] [Confirmed]
- **Transaction and monitoring fees.** Fees from portfolio companies for advice on deals and ongoing services. Since 2014, KKR's fund agreements typically share 100% of monitoring, transaction and break-up fees allocable to a fund with fund investors (after broken deal expenses). So these fees now mainly come from co-investment money and capital markets work. [S2] [Confirmed]
- **Capital markets fees (KCM).** KKR Capital Markets arranges and underwrites debt and equity for KKR deals, Global Atlantic and third parties. Capital markets transaction fees were $930m in 2025. In Q2'26 they were $178m; about 85% were debt focused and about 70% originated in North America. [S1][S2][S5] [Confirmed]
- **Fee related performance revenues.** Performance fees from open-ended or evergreen vehicles, paid against a preferred return and a high-water mark. From Q2'26, realised performance fees from the K-Series private equity vehicle moved here (compensation rate 15% to 20%) from carried interest (compensation rate 70% to 80%). CFO Rob Lewin said this "structurally increases" forward earnings per share. [S1][S3] [Confirmed]
- **Carried interest ("carry").** KKR typically gets 10% to 20% of fund profits, depending on asset class, usually after a 6% to 8% preferred return (hurdle) with a catch-up to the general partner. Realised performance income was $1,879.5m in 2025 and $847.5m in Q2'26 alone, KKR's "largest monetization quarter in its history". Gross unrealised carried interest was $10.2bn at 30 June 2026. [S1][S2][S3] [Confirmed]
- **Investment income.** Gains and dividends on KKR's own balance sheet investments. $2.6bn of embedded unrealised gains on the balance sheet at Q2'26. Total embedded gains (gross carry plus balance sheet gains incl. Strategic Holdings) were $18.2bn. [S1] [Confirmed]
- **Insurance operating earnings.** Net investment income ($2.0bn in Q2'26) minus net cost of insurance ($1.5bn) minus overheads. KKR also shows "Total Insurance Economics" (insurance earnings plus fees Global Atlantic pays to KKR's asset manager, plus Ivy sidecar fees and GA-related capital markets fees): $2.0bn net of compensation over the LTM to Q2'26, up 13%. [S1][S3] [Confirmed]

### C. The contracts: what LPs give and get, and how KKR aligns itself

**C1. The LP/GP deal in plain English.**
- Limited partners (LPs: pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, insurers, endowments, family offices, and now individuals via K-Series) commit money to a fund. KKR is the general partner (GP) and manages it.
- LPs pay a management fee (see B4) and give KKR a share of profits (carry, 10% to 20%) after a hurdle (6% to 8% typically). [S2] [Confirmed]
- Typical time frames from the 10-K: infrastructure and real estate drawdown funds have investment periods of up to six years and fund terms of 10 to 13 years; drawdown credit funds last 8 to 10 years (up to 12), investing for four to five years; core private equity vehicles can last up to 25 years from first investment; open-ended vehicles have no end date. [S2] [Confirmed]
- Example from the Q2'26 vehicle table: North America Fund XIV, $21.9bn committed, investment period April 2025 to April 2031. Global Infrastructure Investors V, $18.6bn, July 2024 to July 2030. European Fund VI, $7.5bn, June 2022 to June 2028. [S1] [Confirmed]
- Clawback: if KKR takes carry early and the fund later underperforms, KKR must pay it back. This happened in Q4 2025 (see I6). [S2][S4] [Confirmed]

**C2. "We invest alongside our clients."**
- As of 31 Dec 2025, KKR and its employees had about $30bn invested in or committed to KKR funds and portfolio companies: about $15bn funded from KKR's balance sheet, $10bn more committed from the balance sheet, $4bn from personal investments and $1bn more personally committed. [S12] [Confirmed]
- GP commitments are usually a contractual requirement, but KKR says they also show "conviction". KKR says the size of its commitments has declined as funds scaled and it redirected retained earnings to other uses. [S2] [Confirmed]
- Employees and others contributed $611.9m (2025), $863.8m (2024), $629.0m (2023) in cash to invest in KKR vehicles, mostly without paying management fees or carry. [S2] [Confirmed]

**C3. Employee ownership and the carry pool.**
- KKR employees own about 30% of KKR common stock (assuming exchange of vested equity), versus about 2% insider ownership on average in the S&P 500 (Lewin, 30 July 2026). [S2][S3] [Confirmed]
- Carried interest is allocated to KKR Associates Holdings L.P., "the carry pool". Up to 80% of carried interest earned from funds can be allocated to employees and other persons. [S2] [Confirmed]
- Compensation ratios (2026 proxy deck): 15% to 20% of fee related revenues; 70% to 80% of realised carried interest; 10% to 20% of realised investment income. KKR claims "the lowest compensation ratio in the industry and the most success-based compensation approach": fee related comp ratio 17.5% in 2025 vs peers at 26% to 34%. [S5] [Confirmed as KKR's own claim]
- The 17.5% fee related compensation figure was "right at the midpoint" of guidance in Q2'26. [S3] [Confirmed]
- Pay mix at KKR: base salary, possible discretionary cash bonus, discretionary equity, and for select employees a carry pool allocation. [S2] [Confirmed]

**C4. Compensation changes.**
- 29 Nov 2023: KKR changed its framework to be "more success based". It now draws a greater share of compensation from carried interest and less from fee related earnings. [S11] [Confirmed]
- Bloomberg reported the numbers: carry share to employees up from 60% to 70% to 70% to 80%; fee related share down from 20% to 25% to 15% to 20%. [S30] [Reported; the new ranges match KKR's own deck S5, so Confirmed for the new ranges]
- On the "2021 compensation/carry changes" in the brief: the October 2021 reorganisation changed who controls the carry pool (KKR takes control at the Sunset Date). I found no separate 2021 change to carry percentages. See Gaps. [S10]

**C5. The 2021 Reorganization Agreement and the 2026 "Sunset Date". This is happening now.**
- Signed 8 Oct 2021, announced 11 Oct 2021 with the CEO succession. [S2][S10] [Confirmed]
- Step 1 (done 31 May 2022): KKR acquired KKR Holdings L.P. and its 258.3m KKR Group Partnership Units, issuing 266.8m shares of common stock to KKR Holdings' limited partners. This collapsed the old two-tier "Up-C" structure. [S2] [Confirmed]
- Step 2, the **Sunset Date**: "no later than December 31, 2026". KKR will cancel the Series I preferred stock (held by KKR Management LLP, owned by senior employees including Kravis and Roberts), give all common stock one vote per share including the vote for directors, and take control of the carry pool. It can come earlier: the earlier of 31 Dec 2026 and six months after the death or permanent disability of both founders, unless KKR Management consents to an earlier date. [S2][S10] [Confirmed]
- Today, common stockholders cannot elect or remove directors. The Series I holder appoints them. KKR uses the NYSE "controlled company" exemptions. [S2] [Confirmed]
- Preparing for this, KKR held a special meeting on 21 April 2026. Proposals 2, 3 and 4 passed (meetings only, no written consent: 59.03% of outstanding for; board fills vacancies: 71.82%; board fixes its size and charter clean-up: 70.09%). [S6] [Confirmed]
- Proposal 1 (remove the legacy 90% supermajority to amend parts of the charter) needed 90% of all outstanding shares. It got 85.97% on 21 April. The meeting was reconvened on 21 May 2026: 97.83% of votes cast were in favour, but that was only 86.60% of outstanding shares, and a 90% quorum was not present. Proposal 1 was not voted on and the meeting was not adjourned again. [S6][S7] [Confirmed]
- So the 90% supermajority provision survives for now, even though the one share, one vote switch will still happen by 31 Dec 2026. [Inferred from S2, S5, S7]
- KKR's governance timeline (from the 2026 proxy deck): NYSE listing July 2010; Nuttall and Bae named Co-Presidents and Co-COOs July 2017; conversion from partnership to corporation July 2018; CEO transition October 2021; S&P 500 June 2024; 50th anniversary 1 May 2026; one share, one vote by 31 Dec 2026. [S5] [Confirmed]

**C6. Partnership to corporation (2018) and S&P 500 (2024).**
- KKR & Co. L.P. converted into a corporation on 1 July 2018 ("the Conversion"). [S2] [Confirmed]
- KKR says it has raised its annualised dividend every year since the C-corp conversion. The regular dividend went from $0.74 to $0.78 per year from Q1 2026 ($0.195 per quarter). [S1][S4] [Confirmed]
- S&P Dow Jones Indices announced on 7 June 2024 that KKR would join the S&P 500 before the open on 24 June 2024, replacing Robert Half. [S31] [Reported; also in KKR's own timeline S5, so Confirmed]
- Why it matters: as a partnership, KKR could not be in most indices and many funds could not easily own it. The C-corp conversion opened the door to index inclusion and a wider shareholder base. [Inferred]

### D. Risk, governance and the "one firm" model

**D1. Investment committees and portfolio management committees.**
- The 10-K: "Our investment committees, or similar committees, review and evaluate investment opportunities using frameworks that are designed to include qualitative and quantitative assessments of the key investment risks." [S2] [Confirmed]
- KKR has committees per strategy and region. Examples from KKR bios: Tara Davies sits on the Infrastructure Investment Committee and Infrastructure Portfolio Management Committee. Mattia Caprioli sits on the European Private Equity Investment Committee and European Portfolio Management Committee. [S16][S17] [Confirmed]
- Plain English: the investment committee decides whether to make a deal. The portfolio management committee then oversees each company KKR owns, tracking performance and value creation plans. [Inferred]
- Scott Nuttall sat on KKR's Balance Sheet Committee (2021 release). Joe Bae had led or served on all private markets investment committees. [S10] [Confirmed]
- Board level: the 2026 proxy lists a majority independent board, 100% independent audit and risk committees, and annual director self-evaluations. Independent directors include Evan Spiegel, Patricia Russo, Kimberly Ross and Robert Scully (from the 10-K director list). [S2][S5] [Confirmed]

**D2. "One firm" is real and written down.**
- 10-K: "One of our most important values for our employees is our 'one firm' approach with shared responsibility and success... our compensation program is based on the performance of the firm as a whole as well as on an individual's contributions to the firm. We generally do not compensate our employees based solely on an individual's accomplishments in relation to the profits and losses of..." [S2] [Confirmed]
- 10-K: operating expenses in Asset Management "are shared across a single expense pool given the collaborative nature of our five business lines". [S2] [Confirmed]
- Investor Day and proxy decks: "One P&L and compensation pool". [S5][S9] [Confirmed]
- Lewin, 30 July 2026: "We run KKR as 1 firm with 1 compensation approach. Relationships travel, ideas travel, lessons learned travel." [S3] [Confirmed]
- So the brief's "firm-wide carry pool" is verified: carry from all funds goes into one vehicle (Associates Holdings) and comp is set on whole-firm performance. [Confirmed from S2, S5]

**D3. Global Atlantic.**
- Founded at Goldman Sachs in 2004 and separated as an independent company in 2013. [S5] [Confirmed]
- KKR announced the deal in July 2020 and took a majority controlling interest on 1 Feb 2021. [S2][S5] [Confirmed]
- 29 Nov 2023: agreed to buy the remaining 37% at 1.0 times book value, about $2.7bn in cash, from KKR's balance sheet. Completed 2 Jan 2024 ("the 2024 GA Acquisition"). [S2][S11] [Confirmed]
- GA AUM grew from $72bn (2020) to $158bn (Nov 2023) to $219bn (Dec 2025) to $220bn (Jun 2026). [S1][S2][S11] [Confirmed]
- KKR says it has "Strong conviction we can double Global Atlantic AUM from here". [S5] [Confirmed: target]
- July 2026 caution: Lewin said KKR will "allocate a little bit less capital to insurance" because of heightened competition and structurally low returns. [S3] [Confirmed]

**D4. Strategic Holdings.**
- Created as a segment in the Nov 2023 strategic update and reported from Q1 2024. Holds long-term, lower-risk stakes mainly via Core Private Equity. Third-party fees and carry from core funds still sit in Asset Management. [S2][S11] [Confirmed]
- Dec 2023: Core PE had $35bn AUM including $6.5bn on KKR's balance sheet. [S11] [Confirmed]
- KKR's share of LTM adjusted revenue $4.5bn and adjusted EBITDA $1.1bn (to Q1'26). EBITDA by geography: Americas 66%, Europe 28%, Asia Pacific 6%. [S1] [Confirmed]

### I. Skeletons and how KKR responded

**I1. RJR Nabisco (1989).** See A4. Overpaid by many accounts; the 1987 Fund returned 8.9% net IRR. Became the archetype of 1980s LBO excess. [S2][S27]

**I2. SEC broken deal expenses (29 June 2015).** The SEC found KKR misallocated more than $17m of "broken deal" costs (costs of deals that did not happen) to its flagship PE funds instead of sharing them with co-investors, including KKR executives. Over six years to 2011, KKR incurred $338m of such costs. KKR paid nearly $30m: over $14m disgorgement, over $4.5m interest, $10m penalty, without admitting or denying. The SEC called it "the first SEC case to charge a private equity adviser with misallocating broken deal expenses". KKR said the matter related to "historical expense allocation disclosures and policies and not to any current practices". [S20] [Confirmed: SEC primary]; KKR quote [Reported via search snippet of CFO.com/Fortune coverage, S45]

**I3. SEC off-channel communications (January 2025).** KKR paid $11m in a sweep of 12 firms totalling $63.1m for staff using unapproved messaging apps for business communications. [S29] [Reported]
Note: the brief said "2024". The settlement I found is January 2025; KKR disclosed the probe in 2024. [S29]

**I4. DOJ Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) case (2025 to 2026).** The DOJ alleged KKR broke US pre-merger filing rules on at least 16 deals in 2021 and 2022: altered documents for 8, omitted documents for 10, and failed to file for 2. KKR sued the DOJ and FTC, arguing the penalties were unconstitutional. On 26 Aug 2026 the DOJ announced a proposed $250m civil penalty, the largest ever for HSR. KKR said it believed it "acted in good faith … consistent with industry practice" but wanted to settle. KKR said outside law firms would fully reimburse it for the penalty. [S2][S21] [Confirmed]
- The 10-K also notes a European Commission investigation linked to KKR's acquisition of Telecom Italia/FiberCop infrastructure assets. [S2] [Confirmed]

**I5. Kentucky pension lawsuits (since 2017).** Claims about separately managed accounts run by Prisma Capital Partners (a former KKR subsidiary) for Kentucky Retirement Systems. Kravis and Roberts were named personally. The 2017 action was dismissed (final 16 Feb 2024) for lack of standing. The Kentucky Attorney General's 2020 action (plus a 2024 breach of contract claim) continues; motions to dismiss were denied on 1 May 2024. Separate "Tier 3" member suits (including a federal RICO class claim, stayed) continue. The Kentucky Supreme Court granted review of jurisdiction and indemnification issues on 14 Aug 2024; opening briefs were filed 22 Sep 2025. [S2] [Confirmed] Final outcome: unknown as of this research (see Gaps).

**I6. Asian Fund II clawback (Q4 2025).** Asian Fund II (2013 vintage, $5.8bn) had paid carry that it later had to give back. KKR repaid $344m in Q4 2025; net of amounts recouped from current and former employees, it cut Q4 net realised performance income by $207m. ANI per share for 2025 was $4.87, or $5.05 excluding it. [S1][S2][S4] [Confirmed]

**I7. TXU / Energy Future Holdings.** In 2007 KKR, TPG and Goldman Sachs' PE arm bought TXU for about $45bn (reports range from $44bn to $48bn), the largest LBO ever. It was a bet that natural gas prices would rise; they fell. Energy Future Holdings filed for Chapter 11 on 29 April 2014 with about $49.7bn of debt. Equity was largely wiped out. [S28][S32] [Confirmed: widely reported]

**I8. Toys "R" Us.** KKR, Bain Capital and Vornado bought it in 2005 for about $6.6bn. It filed for bankruptcy in September 2017 and was liquidated in 2018. KKR and Bain each put $10m into a $20m hardship fund for laid-off workers (Nov 2018), though not legally required. Advocates said about $75m was needed. [S28][S33] [Reported]

**I9. Other reputational items.** Wikipedia cites controversies such as the 2020 Coastal GasLink pipeline protests and a 2024 boycott of Superstruct festivals. [S28] [Reported, not checked further]

**Pattern in KKR's responses** [Inferred]: settle without admission (SEC 2015, SEC 2025, DOJ 2026), stress that issues are historical and that practices have changed, fight hard first where it sees a legal principle (DOJ countersuit), and occasionally make voluntary gestures (Toys "R" Us fund).

## 5. Gaps

1. **"Industrialist vision" text.** Not found in any KKR document I read. The candidate should not quote it unless they find it themselves.
2. **Kravis Nikkei "My Personal History" chapters (Oct 2024).** Exists on kkr.com but the 30 chapters sit in an embedded viewer I could not read. No founder quotes from it are included.
3. **Kohlberg's later life and death date.** Not verified, so omitted.
4. **"2021 compensation/carry changes".** I found the Oct 2021 reorganisation (carry pool control moves to KKR at the Sunset Date) and the Nov 2023 ratio changes. I found no separate 2021 change to carry percentages.
5. **Investment professional headcount.** KKR's 10-K gives about 4,200 employees but no investment professional split.
6. **Total London headcount.** Only the Partners LLP is quantified (182 employees, 88 members). KKR Credit Advisors (EMEA) LLP and KKR Capital Markets Ltd accounts were not read. The "550 across 11 EMEA offices" figure is a Dec 2024 trade press snippet.
7. **Live FCA Register.** The register API refused access without headers. FRNs come from the FCA's own warning page (last updated Nov 2020). Current permissions and approved persons not checked.
8. **Sunset Date exact day.** Filings say "no later than December 31, 2026". I did not find an announcement of an earlier date. The supermajority amendment failed on quorum on 21 May 2026; I did not find any later attempt.
9. **DOJ HSR settlement court approval.** Proposed 26 Aug 2026; court entry not confirmed.
10. **Kentucky litigation outcome.** Status known to Sep 2025 briefs; any 2026 Kentucky Supreme Court ruling not found (search budget ran out).
11. **Off-channel fine date.** Found January 2025 ($11m). No separate 2024 fine on KKR was found; the brief's "2024" may refer to KKR's 2024 disclosure of the probe.
12. **FS KKR Capital (FSK) and private credit stress in 2025 to 2026.** Not researched (search budget exhausted). Worth checking separately as a possible skeleton.
13. **Leadership changes 2025 to 2026.** A secondary site claims COO Ryan Stork stepped down effective 8 Jan 2026. Not verified, so excluded. Date of the Stavros/Taylor global co-head promotion not established.
14. **Spectris completion date and final price** (£4.7bn vs £4.8bn) not pinned down from a primary source.

16. **AUM 2010 to 2020 year-by-year** values are read from chart labels in KKR's proxy deck. Endpoints are firm; the middle years are my mapping.
17. **Wikipedia infobox** lists 4,834 employees (2024) and "20 offices in 16 countries"; this conflicts with KKR's own 10-K (about 4,200 plus about 800; 36 offices). I rely on the 10-K.

## 6. Interview angles

**How to use the facts (KCS lens first).**
- KCS is the team that raised the $305bn since 2024. Say you know the number and that it beat the 3-year $300bn target in 2.5 years. Then link it to the job: relationship managers, product specialists and juniors who "facilitate client access to products and services across KKR's platform". [S1][S3]
- Show you understand why fundraising is the engine: fee paying AUM drives management fees, and management fees drive FRE, the most valued part of KKR's earnings. $72bn of committed capital is not yet paying fees at about 90bps; that is roughly $650m of annual fees waiting to switch on (my estimate). [S1] [Inferred]
- Know the product shelf a client might buy from KCS: flagship drawdown funds (e.g. North America XIV $21.9bn, Global Infrastructure V $18.6bn), Asia funds, credit and asset-based finance, core PE and infrastructure, K-Series for wealth clients, Capital Group public-private interval funds, Arctos sports and GP solutions, Helix AI infrastructure. [S1][S3][S22]
- Mention the CEO of Global Client Solutions, Eric Mogelof (ex PIMCO), and his line "We are laser focused on serving the needs of investors". [S18][S22]
- KCS "works in partnership with Capital Markets in syndication/distribution". KCM fees were $930m in 2025, about 85% debt focused in Q2'26. A good line: KCS brings the clients, KCM structures and places the deal, and co-investment fees flow back because fund fees are 100% shared with LPs. [S1][S2][S5]

**Opinions a candidate could form (and defend).**
- "KKR's real advantage is the one-firm comp model." Evidence: one P&L, one comp pool, firm-wide carry pool, 30% employee ownership. Risk: harder to keep star individuals who could earn more at a pure-eat-what-you-kill shop. [S2][S3][S5]
- "Private wealth is the growth frontier, and it is a client solutions job." K-Series went from $16bn to $42bn in 18 months, and KKR runs "KKR Academies" for 100 advisers at a time. [S1][S3][S5]
- "KKR is becoming less of a buyout firm and more of a capital solutions platform." PE was over 70% of AUM in 2010, under 25% traditional PE now; credit is the largest line; insurance is a whole segment. [S2]
- "The $7 ANI miss is a timing story, not a business story." Fundraising and FRE targets were hit; the shortfall is in exits, which depend on markets. A balanced view would also note insurance competition and the Asian Fund II clawback. [S1][S3][S4]
- "Governance is about to change." From 2027, ordinary shareholders will elect directors. This is a big moment in KKR's 50-year history. [S2][S7]

**Smart questions to ask (pick 2 or 3).**
1. "With wealth now a big share of inflows, how does KCS in London split its time between institutional LPs and wealth platforms, and how does the KKR Academies model work in Europe?"
2. "How does KCS work with KCM day to day on syndication, for example on a large co-investment?"
3. "How does the one-firm compensation model change how a junior in KCS is evaluated compared with a deal team member?"
4. "After the Sunset Date, does anything change inside the firm, or is it mainly a shareholder matter?"
5. "How are London-based clients reacting to the public-private products, like the Capital Group funds, and are there European versions coming?"
6. "Helix and Arctos are both new types of product. How does KCS learn to sell something that has no track record yet?"

**Things to avoid.**
- Do not call RJR Nabisco a triumph. KKR's own 10-K shows the 1987 Fund made 8.9% net IRR. Call it "the deal that made KKR famous".
- Do not quote "industrialist vision". It was not verified.
- Do not over-state the DOJ matter. It is a proposed settlement over filing rules, not fraud, and KKR disputed it.
- Do not say "KKR is a private equity firm". Say "a global investment firm with asset management, insurance and strategic holdings". That is KKR's own framing. [S1][S12]
