## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The big picture in plain English

- "Alternative asset managers" raise money from big investors (pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, insurers) and, more and more, from wealthy individuals. They invest it in things that are not ordinary listed shares and bonds: private companies (private equity), loans to companies (private credit), buildings (real estate) and infrastructure (power, data centres, roads).
- They earn two kinds of income. **Management fees** are a steady percentage of the money they manage. **Performance fees** ("carried interest" or "carry") are a share of the profits when investments do well. Analysts value steady fees most. So **fee-paying AUM** (the money that actually pays fees) matters more than headline AUM.
- The people who raise and look after the money are the "client solutions" teams. At KKR this is Global Client Solutions (KCS). At Blackstone it is Institutional Client Solutions plus Private Wealth. At Apollo it is Client & Product Solutions. The names differ. The job is the same: find investors, match them to products, keep them happy, and bring them back for the next fund.
- Since about 2021 the industry has split three ways in how it gets "permanent" money that never has to be handed back: (1) **owning an insurer** (Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic), (2) **managing insurers' money under contract without owning them** (Blackstone's main approach; Brookfield via a sister company), and (3) **selling evergreen funds to wealthy individuals** (everyone, but Blackstone is far ahead). [Inferred from S201, S214, S221, S236]

### 1.5 Design axes: where KKR sits on each spectrum

**(a) "One firm" economics vs siloed strategies**
- KKR CFO Robert Lewin on the Q2 2026 call: "We run KKR as 1 firm with 1 compensation approach. Relationships travel, ideas travel, lessons learned travel." [Confirmed, S207]
- 10-K: operating expenses "are shared across a single expense pool given the collaborative nature of our five business lines". Pay is "based on the performance of KKR as a whole, in addition to an individual's contributions". [Confirmed, S201]
- The KCS mandate (from the brief) says "Apply integrated approach by leveraging all KKR executives and capabilities globally". [Confirmed, brief]
- Contrast: Blackstone runs its segments with their own headcounts (Real Estate "operates as one globally integrated business with approximately 785 employees", etc.). [Confirmed, S214] Many rivals also say "one team" (Apollo job ads: "we work as one team"; Blue Owl value "One Team"). [Confirmed, S223, S233] So the slogan is not unique. The claim of **one compensation approach across the whole firm** is KKR's sharper version. I did not verify how rivals' compensation works. [Inferred]

**(b) Balance sheet investing vs capital-light**
- KKR: "we and our employees and other personnel have approximately $30 billion invested in or committed to our own funds and portfolio companies", including about $15bn funded from the balance sheet and $10bn more committed. [Confirmed, S201]
- KKR reports a dedicated **Strategic Holdings** segment (own stakes in 19 companies). [Confirmed, S201] No peer in this set reports an equivalent segment. Peer segments are: Blackstone (Real Estate, Private Equity, Credit & Insurance, Multi-Asset Investing); Apollo (Asset Management, Retirement Services, Principal Investing); Carlyle (Global Private Equity, Global Credit, AlpInvest). [Confirmed, S213, S220, S226] [Inferred: "no equivalent" is my reading of their segment lists]
- Blackstone and Blue Owl are closer to "capital-light". TPG uses its balance sheet mainly for credit support and GP commitments per its 10-K. [Confirmed, S230; overall positioning Inferred]

**(c) Owning an insurer vs partnering**
- Owners: Apollo (Athene, plus affiliate Athora), KKR (Global Atlantic), Ares (Aspida, as controller), Carlyle (Fortitude, through a fund). [Confirmed, S201, S220, S221, S225, S227]
- Partners / contract managers: Blackstone (insurance SMAs, sold Resolution Life stake), Brookfield (manages BWS, which is a sister of Brookfield Corporation), CVC ($3.5bn AIG partnership), Blue Owl (Kuvare asset management). [Confirmed, S214, S235, S236, S238, S233]
- KKR is the second largest owner-model after Apollo: Global Atlantic $220bn vs Athene-related perpetual AUM $416bn. [Confirmed, S200, S220]
- KKR is now pulling back at the margin. Lewin: "We have seen heightened competition. We've made the determination that we're going to allocate a little bit less capital to insurance based on what we're seeing in the market today." [Confirmed, S207]
- KKR reports a "Total Insurance Economics" figure of about $2bn net of compensation (LTM), showing how insurance feeds fees elsewhere in the firm. [Confirmed, S200, S207]
- KKR also sells **Ivy** sidecars (third-party reinsurance vehicles): $62bn of Global Atlantic's $220bn sits in Ivy and similar vehicles. That is a product KCS can offer institutions. [Confirmed, S200; KCS link Inferred]

**(d) In-house capital markets vs relying on banks**
- KKR began "establishing a capital markets business in the United States, Europe and Asia" during 2007, "with the objective of increasing the amount of third-party capital that we are able to commit to individual transactions". [Confirmed, S208]
- Today KCM "is integrated with KKR's asset management business, and serves our firm, including our portfolio companies, our insurance business, and third-party customers". Q2 2026 capital markets transaction fees $178m. [Confirmed, S201, S200]
- KKR's UK capital markets entity is "KKR Capital Markets Partners LLP", with FCA permissions for dealing and arranging. [Confirmed, S201]
- But Blackstone (BXCM) and Apollo (Apollo Capital Solutions, $277m fees in Q2 2026) also have in-house capital markets. Ares has a broker-dealer (AMCM) used for wealth distribution. [Confirmed, S214, S220, S225] So the honest claim is about **integration with KCS** (the KCS mandate explicitly includes partnering with Capital Markets on syndication), not about being the only one. [Inferred]

- Products: K-Series evergreen vehicles in private equity, infrastructure, credit and real estate ($42bn). [Confirmed, S200, S201] About 85% of K-Series is in private equity and infrastructure. [Reported, S207 coverage]

[Confirmed, S201]
- On 21 April 2026 shareholders approved three charter proposals. The proposal to remove a 90% supermajority requirement got 97.83% of votes cast (86.60% of shares outstanding) but **failed for lack of a 90% quorum** on 21 May 2026. [Confirmed, S202, S203]
- On 29 May 2026 KKR Management LLP re-elected the board, including Kravis and Roberts. [Confirmed, S204]
- Employees own about 30% of KKR's shares. Lewin: "For context, the other companies in the S&P 500 have an average of approximately 2% insider ownership." [Confirmed, S201, S207]
- So in late 2026 KKR is mid-way through a governance change that most listed peers made earlier or never needed. [Inferred]

### 1.6 Five to seven things that can be said about KKR's client solutions model that (as far as this research found) cannot be said of any rival

Each is worded carefully so the candidate does not overclaim.

1. **One client team selling four "engines" that KKR itself owns or runs: funds, an owned insurer, a balance-sheet holdings segment and an in-house capital markets business.** Apollo has funds, an insurer and capital solutions but no Strategic Holdings-type segment. Blackstone has funds and BXCM but no owned insurer. [Inferred, from S200, S201, S214, S220]
2. **An exclusive strategic partnership with Capital Group**, one of the largest traditional fund managers, including KKR sub-advising Capital Group-branded interval funds and a Capital Group equity fund investing in a K-Series vehicle. Other alts firms have partnered with traditional managers, but this pairing is KKR's alone. [Confirmed partnership S210, S201; uniqueness of the pairing Inferred]
3. **A "Family Capital" client segment** shown alongside Institutional Investors and Global Wealth on kkr.com. I did not see a peer present family offices as a separate top-level client category in the sources reviewed. [Confirmed for KKR S209; peer comparison Inferred and limited]
4. **The KCS mandate explicitly includes working with Capital Markets on syndication and distribution.** KCM can bring third parties into deals and KCS supplies the clients. Peers have capital markets units, but KKR's published client-team description names this link. [Confirmed for KKR (brief, S201); peer comparison Inferred]
5. **A sports-ecosystem product shelf through Arctos (closed May 2026), with an explicit thesis to feed Global Atlantic.** Lewin said the opportunity to "create flow for Global Atlantic" was a critical part of the investment case. [Confirmed, S200, S207; uniqueness Inferred. Note: Blue Owl's GP Strategic Capital also owns sports stakes, so "only firm in sports" would be wrong.]
6. **A client team operating through a founder-control sunset in real time.** The Series I sunset date is no later than 31 Dec 2026. [Confirmed, S201, S202, S203]
7. **Asia depth that KKR claims leadership in:** "We are the largest private equity player. We're the largest infrastructure player" in Asia, with "nearly 1,000 people on the ground", over 200 in Tokyo. This is KKR's own claim and not independently verified. [Reported (company claim), S207]

### 1.7 Where KKR does NOT lead (so the candidate never overclaims)

- **Total size:** Blackstone $1.35tn and Apollo $1.05tn vs KKR $796bn. Brookfield also says "over $1 trillion". KKR is fourth by headline AUM in this group. [Confirmed, S213, S220, S235, S200]
- **Private wealth:** Blackstone about $324bn private wealth AUM and 357 dedicated wealth professionals vs KKR K-Series $42bn. [Reported S215, S216; Confirmed S200]
- **Insurance scale:** Apollo's Athene-related perpetual AUM $416bn vs Global Atlantic $220bn. [Confirmed, S220, S200]
- **Recent fundraising pace:** Q2 2026 Blackstone $68.3bn, Brookfield $77bn, Apollo $60bn, Ares $36.4bn, KKR $34bn. [Confirmed, S213, S235, S220, S224, S200] (Different firms define "inflows" differently; Apollo and Brookfield include insurance flows.)
- **Credit specialism:** Ares and Blue Owl are more credit-focused and Ares has a larger disclosed relationship-management team (200+). [Confirmed, S225]
- **AUM per employee:** KKR is below Blackstone, Blue Owl and Carlyle on this crude measure (see 1.8), partly because its headcount includes Global Atlantic's insurance staff. [Inferred]
- **Returns:** KKR traditional PE appreciated 10% over the LTM to Q2 2026 (per call coverage). Blackstone's corporate PE gained 14.4% LTM. These are not like-for-like and must not be used to "rank" firms. [Reported S207; Confirmed S213]
- **In-house capital markets is not unique:** Blackstone (BXCM) and Apollo (Apollo Capital Solutions) both have one. [Confirmed, S214, S220]
- **Wealth redemption risk is industry-wide:** Partners Group saw $3.8bn redemptions against $4.2bn evergreen demand in H1 2026; Blue Owl's private wealth inflows fell sharply. KKR's wealth flows were still positive (net up 20% YTD per Nuttall). [Confirmed S239; Reported S234, S207]
- **Regulatory:** KKR is paying $250m to settle a DOJ HSR complaint. [Confirmed, S205]

## 6. Interview angles

**How to use the facts**
- Lead with structure, not size. KKR is not the biggest (Blackstone $1.35tn, Apollo $1.05tn vs KKR $796bn). Its pitch is breadth under one roof: funds, Global Atlantic, Strategic Holdings, KCM and Capstone, all sold by one client team. Say this, and say plainly that Blackstone is far bigger in wealth.
- Use the KCS text. It says KCS works "in partnership with Capital Markets in the syndication/distribution of product". Link it to KCM's 2007 founding rationale: to commit more third-party capital to deals. That shows you understand why KCS and KCM sit side by side.
- Talk about the three client segments KKR shows: Institutional Investors, Global Wealth, Family Capital. Ask which one the London KCS intern supports.
- Know the wealth numbers cold: K-Series $25bn to $42bn in a year; Blackstone about $324bn. Opinion you could hold: "KKR is a fast follower in wealth, and its edge is product breadth (infrastructure and PE K-Series) plus the Capital Group distribution route, rather than scale."
- Insurance nuance: KKR owns Global Atlantic but said in July 2026 it will allocate "a little bit less capital to insurance" because of competition. A good candidate can explain the trade-off: owning an insurer gives permanent capital but ties up equity; partnering (Blackstone, Brookfield) is lighter.
- Governance: the founders' Series I control ends by 31 Dec 2026. Mention it briefly and neutrally as a sign of an institution maturing.

**Opinions the candidate could form (label them as opinions)**
- "The industry is converging: everyone now has wealth, insurance and credit. The difference is how tightly those pieces are joined. KKR's bet is that one compensation pool makes the joins tighter."
- "Wealth flows are lumpy. Partners Group and Blue Owl show redemption and slowdown risk. KKR's emphasis on adviser education (KKR Academies) looks like the right defence."
- "AUM per head flatters capital-light firms. KKR's asset management arm alone is about $194m per head, close to Carlyle and below Blackstone."

**Smart questions to ask**
1. "How does KCS split coverage between institutional, Global Wealth and Family Capital in EMEA, and where would a summer analyst sit?"
2. "When KCM syndicates a deal, how does KCS decide which clients get the co-investment?"
3. "How has the Capital Group partnership changed what KCS does day to day, or is it run separately?"
4. "With KKR allocating less capital to insurance, do Ivy-style sidecars become a bigger KCS product for institutions?"
5. "Lewin said KKR wants to stay small. How does KCS scale wealth distribution without the headcount Blackstone has?"
6. "How are Arctos's sports and GP-solutions clients being brought onto the KCS platform?"

**Things never to say**
- "KKR is the largest alternative manager" (false).
- "KKR is the only firm with in-house capital markets" (false: BXCM, Apollo Capital Solutions).
- "KKR leads private wealth" (false: Blackstone leads by a wide margin).
- "KKR has the biggest insurer" (false: Apollo/Athene is larger).
