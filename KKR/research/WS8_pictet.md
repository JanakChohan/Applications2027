## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The Pictet Group: who they are

**Founding and history**
- Pictet traces itself to 1805, when the de Candolle and Mallet families formed "De Candolle, Mallet & Cie" in Geneva. This was "the partnership that was to become Pictet". Edouard Pictet-Prevost joined in 1836 and became a Partner in 1841. Between 1848 and 1926 the firm was always headed by a Pictet. [Confirmed] [S491]
- 2025 was the firm's 220th anniversary. That year it began moving into a new Geneva headquarters, the "Campus Pictet de Rochemont". [Confirmed] [S490][S491]
- The ninth generation of the Pictet family is still active in the firm. [Confirmed] [S491]
- The firm describes itself as "an independent investment partnership known for our long-term mindset, responsible approach to business, and entrepreneurial spirit. These principles have defined us since 1805." [Confirmed] [S514]

**Ownership and legal form**
- Pictet is structured as a "société en commandite par actions, a partnership limited by shares". [Confirmed] [S491]
- The top holding entity is Pictet & Cie Group SCA. The "significant equity owners are the Managing Partners". [Confirmed] [S490]
- Change of legal form: announced 6 February 2013 (the same day as Lombard Odier), effective 1 January 2014. Pictet ended its unlimited-liability general partnership. Partners no longer carry unlimited personal liability. The Swiss bank (Banque Pictet & Cie SA) became a limited company, and the group sits under an SCA. The new structure required a consolidated annual report and an independent supervisory board. [Confirmed] [S497] (the 2014 date and SCA form also appear in Pictet's own governance text [S490])
- Pictet "does not engage in investment banking, nor does it extend commercial loans". [Confirmed] [S490][S492] This is a useful contrast: no conflicts from deal advisory.

### 1.4 Technical content of client servicing and onboarding at a UCITS manager

These are the concepts a client-group intern meets. Each is tied to an authoritative source.

1. **Client categorisation (UK COBS 3 / MiFID II).** Clients are retail, professional (per se or elective) or eligible counterparty. Per se professionals include credit institutions, investment firms, insurers, collective investment schemes, pension funds and large undertakings. An elective professional must pass a qualitative test and, for MiFID business, two of three tests: about 10 significant trades per quarter over four quarters; portfolio over EUR 500,000; or at least one year working in a relevant financial sector role. [Confirmed] [S543] Why it matters: it decides which share classes and documents a client can get. [Inferred]
2. **Share classes and eligibility.** Pictet share-class letters seen on UK platforms: "P" (retail-type) and "I" (institutional), with "dy" meaning distributing and currency suffixes like GBP. [Confirmed] [S524][S526] (The exact minimums and eligibility rules sit in the prospectus, which I did not read. [Gap])
3. **KYC / AML at onboarding.** Risk-rate the client (PEP, high/medium/low risk), verify identity and beneficial owners, check tax status (FATCA, CRS), and document approval. [Reported] [S523] The 2025 Swiss OAG fine shows what happens when accounts are not classified as high risk and transfers are not monitored. [Confirmed] [S498]
4. **Transfer agent and dealing.** Orders go to the fund's transfer agent before a cut-off. Example from a Pictet SICAV notice (July 2018): many compartments moved to a 1:00 pm Luxembourg-time cut-off on the Valuation Day; Pictet - Global Megatrend Selection moved to 11:00 am; settlement for some compartments moved to within 2 week days after the Valuation Day. [Confirmed, dated 2018] [S527] This is "forward pricing": you deal at the next NAV, not a known price. [Inferred] Hong Kong distributors had a separate 5:00 pm HK-time deadline through Bank Pictet & Cie (Asia). [Confirmed] [S527]
5. **Suspensions.** In February 2022 the board of Pictet - Russian Equities suspended NAV calculation and dealing. [Reported] [snippet within S527 search; see S527 note] This is the daily-dealing world's version of a gate. [Inferred]
6. **MiFID II product governance and target market.** Manufacturers define a target market on five categories: client type, knowledge and experience, financial situation (ability to bear losses), risk tolerance, and objectives and needs (including sustainability preferences). [Reported] [S548]
7. **EMT / EPT / EET data templates (FinDatEx).** Manufacturers send distributors standard data files. EMT (European MiFID Template) v4.3 dated 15 January 2026 covers target market and costs; EPT (European PRIIPs Template) v2.1 includes a UK section; EET (European ESG Template) v1.1.3 dated 10 April 2026. [Confirmed] [S547]
   - Real example: AJ Bell shows the EMT target market for Pictet-Water I dy GBP: retail, professional and eligible counterparty all "Yes"; knowledge basic, experienced and advanced all "Yes"; SRRI 5; primary objective growth; recommended holding period 5 years; available for execution-only, non-advised, portfolio management and advice; fair value assessment completed with review date 30 April 2026; statement "Product expected to provide fair value for reasonably foreseeable period". [Confirmed] [S526]
8. **UK Consumer Duty.** Consumer principle: "A firm must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers." Three cross-cutting rules (good faith, avoid foreseeable harm, enable customers) and four outcomes (products and services, price and value, consumer understanding, consumer support). [Confirmed] [S542] Manufacturers pass fair-value information down to distributors, which is why the "fair value" field appears on platforms. [Inferred from S526]
9. **Assessment of Value (UK authorised funds).** UK authorised fund managers publish an annual value assessment against seven criteria: quality of service, performance, AFM costs, economies of scale, comparable market rates, comparable services, classes of units. [Reported] [S549] The FCA (August 2023) found reports "significantly improved" but said some independent directors "did not provide sufficient challenge". [Confirmed] [S541] Pictet's UK-sold funds are mostly Luxembourg UCITS, which are not UK authorised funds, so the Consumer Duty fair-value assessment is the more relevant test for them. [Inferred; supported by S555]
10. **Overseas Funds Regime (OFR).** In January 2024 the UK found EEA UCITS (excluding money market funds) equivalent. EEA UCITS move from the Temporary Marketing Permissions Regime into the OFR through FCA "landing slots"; the TMPR was extended to the end of 2026. [Confirmed] [S546] This is how a Luxembourg Pictet fund can be sold to UK retail. [Inferred]
11. **UK retail disclosure is changing now (CCI regime).** FCA PS25/20 (published 8 December 2025) replaces the UCITS KIID and PRIIPs KID with a "Product Summary Document". Rules start 6 April 2026; UK UCITS exemption expires 31 December 2026; firms must move by 8 June 2027. Risk indicator moves to a 1 to 10 scale; ongoing costs shown as a headline figure; core data supplied in machine-readable form such as EMT/EPT. [Reported] [S544][S545] Being able to say "the KIID is being replaced by the CCI product summary" shows current knowledge. [Inferred]
12. **Factsheets and reporting.** Platforms show manager names, launch dates, prices and charges (e.g. Pictet-Water P dy GBP price GBP 415.37 on 9 October 2026). [Confirmed] [S524] Client reporting teams at Pictet produce "investment, marketing, product and regulatory reports". [Reported] [S518]
