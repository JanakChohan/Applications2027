## 1. Findings

### 1.0 The one-paragraph summary

KKR is having a record 2026. It raised $34bn of new money in Q2 alone and $133bn over the 12 months to 30 June 2026, and manages $796bn [S330, Confirmed]. Its management expects "a record fundraising year for the firm" [S362, Reported]. Big flagship funds closed: North America Fund XIV at about $23bn (April) and Global Infrastructure Investors V at $19.2bn (August) [S335, S334, Confirmed]. Its private wealth funds (the "K-Series") reached $42bn [S330, Confirmed]. But the wider industry is under strain. Wealthy individuals tried to pull more than $20bn out of private credit funds in Q1 2026, and several managers limited withdrawals, KKR's own retail credit fund among them [S337, S338, Reported]. Fundraising is concentrating in the biggest managers [S336, Reported]. Regulators on both sides of the Atlantic are opening private markets to ordinary savers (US 401(k) rule, SEC proposals, UK Pension Schemes Act 2026). They are also rewriting fund-manager rules (AIFMD II in the EU, a new UK AIFM regime) [S342, S344, S345, S346]. Inside KKR, the founders' special control rights end by 31 December 2026 at the latest [S332, Confirmed]. KKR also paid a $250m settlement to the US Department of Justice in August [S349, Confirmed].

### Story 1. KKR posts record Q2 2026 results and says it expects a record fundraising year

- **Headline:** "KKR & Co. Inc. Reports Second Quarter 2026 Financial Results"
- **Outlet / date:** KKR earnings release, filed with the SEC as an 8-K exhibit, 30 July 2026. Call transcript via The Motley Fool.
- **URLs:** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1404912/000140491226000022/q226earningsrelease_vf.htm and https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/07/30/kkr-kkr-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/ (both free)

**What happened, in plain English.** Every quarter, KKR (a listed company on the New York Stock Exchange) reports how much money it manages and how much it earns. In Q2 2026:
- Assets under management (AUM, all the money KKR looks after for clients) were $796bn, up 16% on a year earlier [S330, Confirmed].
- Fee-paying AUM (the part that actually pays KKR a management fee) was $638bn, up 15% [S330, Confirmed].
- New capital raised was $34bn in the quarter and $133bn over the last 12 months [S330, Confirmed].
- Fee Related Earnings (FRE, the steady profit from fees) were $1.2bn, or $1.32 per adjusted share, up 37% year on year [S330, Confirmed].
- Total Operating Earnings were $1.5bn ($1.68 per adjusted share), up 29%. Adjusted Net Income was $1.5bn ($1.63 per adjusted share), up 40% [S330, Confirmed].
- GAAP net income for common shareholders (the official accounting profit) was about $660m [S330, Confirmed].
- Capital invested was $24bn in the quarter and $104bn over 12 months. Uncalled commitments ("dry powder", money promised by clients but not yet invested) were $142.7bn [S330, Confirmed].
- Global Atlantic, KKR's insurance company, had $220bn of AUM [S330, Confirmed]. Perpetual capital (money that does not have to be handed back on a fixed date) was $334bn, up 16% [S330, Confirmed].
- The co-CEOs' statement said the quarter was KKR's "strongest monetization quarter ever" (monetisation = selling or partly selling investments and returning cash to clients) [S330, Confirmed].
- Secondary coverage reports management said KKR had raised $305bn since the start of 2024. That beats its three-year $300bn target in two and a half years [Reported via search summary of the call; not checked against the transcript, so treat with care].

On the call, co-CEO Scott Nuttall said: "We expect a record fundraising year for the firm." He added "we expect a record third-party credit fundraising year." He also said: "I don't recall a period of time where the external perception is so disconnected from the operating fundamentals" [S362, Reported].

The 10-Q (KKR's detailed quarterly filing) shows how KKR has changed. On 31 December 2010, traditional private equity was over 70% of AUM. On 30 June 2026 it was "less than 25%" [S332, Confirmed]. AUM splits into Credit and Liquid Strategies $331bn, Real Assets $211bn and Private Equity $255bn [S332, Confirmed]. Alternative credit includes $91bn of asset-based finance and $48bn of corporate private credit, of which $39bn is direct lending [S332, Confirmed].

**Next date:** KKR will release Q3 2026 results on Monday 9 November 2026, before the NYSE opens, with a call at 10:00 a.m. ET [S333, Reported: press release seen only as a search snippet, because Business Wire blocked the fetch].

**Why it matters to KKR.** Fundraising is the fuel for future fees. A record year shows clients still trust KKR even though the industry is under pressure. KKR is now mostly a credit, infrastructure and insurance business, not just a buyout shop [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS.** KCS is the team that raises this money. "$34bn in a quarter" is the scoreboard KCS is measured on. The mix (credit, infrastructure, wealth) tells you what products a KCS junior will help explain to clients [Inferred].

**Question you could raise:** "Scott Nuttall said he expects a record fundraising year even while the industry fights redemption headlines. From a client coverage side, is the growth coming more from re-ups by existing institutional clients or from new channels like wealth and insurance?"

### Story 4. K-Series private wealth reaches $42bn, but KKR's retail credit fund had to limit withdrawals in Q1

**Plain English.** "K-Series" is KKR's family of funds for wealthy individuals, sold through banks and wealth advisers. Most are "evergreen" funds: they stay open, take new money monthly, and let investors ask for money back each quarter, usually capped at about 5% of the fund per quarter.
- K-Series AUM was $42bn on 30 June 2026, versus $25bn a year earlier [S330, S332, Confirmed].
- It was $38bn on 31 March 2026 [S331, Confirmed].
- From Q2 2026, KKR reports K-Series private equity performance fees (about $160m in Q2) inside Fee Related Earnings. This makes wealth profits look more like steady fees [S332, Confirmed].
- KKR FS Income Trust (K-FIT), a KKR retail private credit fund: in Q1 2026 investors "tried to redeem 6.3% of shares", so the fund paid out about 80% of requests (pro-rated). In Q2, requests fell to 1.65% and were paid in full [S338, Reported].
- On the Q2 call, Nuttall said: "What you don't read about in the articles is the inflows." On whether the turbulence changes KKR's wealth plans he said: "it has not changed our perspective on investing in growth" [S362, Reported].
- He also said software is "about 6% of our AUM" [S362, Reported]. This answers a market worry that AI will hurt software companies that private credit funds have lent to.

**Why it matters to KKR.** Wealth is KKR's fastest-growing channel. It brings stickier, fee-rich capital, but also redemption risk and reputational risk [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS.** KCS covers both institutions and the wealth channel. Juniors will prepare investor letters, FAQs and adviser education material when redemptions spike. Understanding "gates", pro-rating and quarterly tender offers is now basic knowledge for the role [Inferred].

**Opinion you could raise:** "I think the Q1 redemption episode was a stress test that the semi-liquid model mostly passed. K-FIT paid about 80% of requests in Q1 and 100% in Q2. The real lesson for client teams is to set liquidity expectations at the point of sale, not when the queue forms."

### Story 5. Industry-wide redemption wave in private credit and evergreen funds

- **Headlines:** "Private credit fund redemption requests topped $20bn in Q1" (Private Equity Wire, citing the FT, 9 April 2026). "Partners Group Ready to Cap More Funds as Withdrawals Mount" (Bloomberg via swissinfo, 4 June 2026). "Blackstone private credit fund maintains 5% cap as redemption requests remain high" (BNN Bloomberg, 3 September 2026)
- **URLs:** https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/private-credit-fund-redemption-requests-topped-20bn-in-q1/ (free) ; https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/partners-group-ready-to-cap-more-funds-as-withdrawals-mount/91525795 (free) ; https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/09/03/blackstone-private-credit-fund-maintains-5-cap-as-redemption-requests-remain-high (snippet only). The FT original is PAYWALLED-NOT-READ.

**Plain English.** In Q1 2026, wealthy investors asked to withdraw $20.8bn from private credit funds run by Apollo, Ares, Blackstone, Blue Owl, KKR and others. Managers with about $300bn of assets paid out "just over half" of requests [S337, Reported]. Blackstone and Oaktree paid above the 5% cap. Apollo, Ares, Blue Owl, HPS and Morgan Stanley kept their limits [S337, Reported]. The driver was "heightened investor concern over private credit exposure to software companies backed by private equity" [S337, Reported]. The same article says "underlying loan performance remains largely stable despite the outflows" [S337, Reported].

The anxiety spread to private equity evergreen funds. Partners Group (Swiss, about $185bn AUM) said requests at its Delaware PE evergreen fund would be "slightly above the 5%" limit. Requests at its $8.6bn Global Value fund reached an estimated 9.8% in Q2, and its shares fell as much as 18.2% in a day [S339, Reported].

In Q3, Blackstone's $77.2bn BCRED reportedly kept its 5% cap with requests near 10% [S340, Reported, snippet only]. Blue Owl's requests at two main funds eased to $4.2bn from $4.7bn [S341, Reported, snippet only].

**Why it matters to KKR.** KKR named itself among the affected managers. It competes for the same adviser shelf space as Blackstone, Apollo and Blue Owl. How a manager behaves in a redemption queue shapes its brand with advisers for years [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS.** This is the client-service side of the job. Clients ask "can I get my money out?". KCS must answer consistently across institutions, private banks and advisers. It is the best live example of why the KCS description says "relationship-focused, solutions-oriented" [Inferred].

**Question:** "When a semi-liquid fund pro-rates redemptions, how does KCS decide what to tell distribution partners first: the numbers, the portfolio health, or the structural explanation of why gates exist?"

### Story 8. Founders' control ends by 31 December 2026, a failed charter vote, and a $250m DOJ settlement

- **Sources:** KKR 10-Q for Q2 2026; 8-Ks dated 21 April, 28 May, 29 May and 26 August 2026 (all SEC EDGAR, free, fetched)
- **URLs:** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001404912/000140491226000027/kkr-20260630.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1404912/000114036126034520/ef20081051_8k.htm

**Plain English: the "Sunset Date".** Today a partnership of senior KKR people (KKR Management LLP, which includes founders Henry Kravis and George Roberts) controls KKR & Co. Inc. through one special "Series I preferred" share. On the "Sunset Date", which "will occur no later than December 31, 2026", KKR "will cancel the Series I preferred stock, establish voting rights for all common stock on a one vote per share basis, and acquire control of the carry pool" [S332, Confirmed]. The carry pool is the share of investment profits paid to employees. In plain terms, ordinary shareholders get a normal vote from 2027 [Inferred].

**The 2026 charter votes.** On 21 April 2026 shareholders approved three charter changes: meetings as the only way shareholders act, the Board alone filling board vacancies, and a fixed board size [S350, Confirmed]. A fourth proposal, to remove the "supermajority" (90%) requirement to change parts of the charter, got support from 86.60% of outstanding shares. A quorum of 90% was not reached at the reconvened meeting on 21 May, so it was not adopted [S351, Confirmed]. On 29 May 2026 the directors were elected by KKR Management LLP, as the current bylaws allow [S352, Confirmed].

**The DOJ settlement.** On 26 August 2026 KKR settled a civil antitrust case. The US Department of Justice filed it in January 2025 over HSR (pre-merger notification) filings for 2021 and 2022 deals. A KKR subsidiary will pay $250m. KKR said the penalty "will have no financial impact on the firm, our funds, or any of our investors and will be fully reimbursed by outside law firms." It also said: "We strongly disagree with the Antitrust Division's characterization of this matter" [S349, Confirmed]. The DOJ also ended all related investigations [S349, Confirmed]. The judgment still needs court approval [S349, Confirmed].

**Why it matters to KKR.** Governance moves from founder control to a normal public company. Index providers and some investors care about this. The DOJ settlement removes a legal overhang [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS.** LPs' due diligence questionnaires ask about governance, litigation and regulatory matters. KCS staff must be able to point clients to the accurate, approved public statement. They should never improvise [Inferred].

**Question:** "As KKR moves to one share, one vote at the Sunset Date, do institutional LPs who are also KKR shareholders bring it up in fund due diligence, or do they treat the listed company and the funds as separate conversations?"

### Story 11. US retail access: DOL 401(k) rule proposed, SEC proposes wider retail access

- **Headlines:** "Department of Labor Proposes Rule to Reduce Risks Associated with Opening 401(k) Plans to Private Market Assets" (Morrison Foerster, 3 April 2026). "SEC Chair Atkins Speaks on Retailization of Private Markets" (Columbia CLS Blue Sky Blog, 2 October 2026)
- **URLs:** https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260403-dol-proposed-rule-401-k-alternative-assets (free, fetched) ; https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2026/10/02/sec-chair-atkins-speaks-on-retailization-of-private-markets/ (snippet)

**Plain English.** A 401(k) is the main US workplace pension. The trillions in these plans have mostly been kept out of private equity and private credit, partly because employers fear being sued.
- On 30 March 2026 the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a "process-based safe harbor" for choosing investment options. If employers follow a process covering six factors (performance, fees, liquidity, valuation, performance benchmarks and complexity), they get legal protection [S342, Confirmed with S343].
- It follows President Trump's August 2025 executive order "Democratizing Access to Alternative Assets for 401(k) Investors" [S342, Confirmed].
- Comments closed on 1 June 2026. I found no evidence the rule is final as of 11 October 2026 [S342, S343, Reported].

### Story 12. EU and UK fund-manager rules: AIFMD II goes live, FCA proposes a new UK AIFM regime

- **Headlines:** "AIFMD II takes effect at last" (Linklaters, 16 April 2026). "A new regime for UK AIFMs: a consultation on the FCA's latest proposals in CP26/28" (Macfarlanes, 22 July 2026)
- **URLs:** https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102mq84/aifmd-ii-takes-effect-at-last (free) ; https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102ndli/a-new-regime-for-uk-aifms-a-consultation-on-the-fcas-latest-proposals-in-cp26-2/ (free)

**Plain English: AIFMD II (EU).** The AIFMD is the EU law for managers of "alternative investment funds" (private equity, credit, real estate and hedge funds). The update, AIFMD II, had to be in national law by 16 April 2026 [S344, Confirmed]. Main changes [S344, Confirmed]:
- A common EU regime for funds that lend money ("loan origination"), with leverage caps of 300% for closed-ended and 175% for open-ended funds.
- A 5% "skin in the game" retention on loans sold.
- A ban on lending only to sell the loans on.
- Open-ended funds must choose at least two liquidity management tools.
- Tighter delegation rules, including at least two EU-resident full-time people.
- New reporting starts 16 April 2027. Non-essential Level 2 detail is delayed until after 1 October 2027.

**Plain English: UK AIFM regime.** After Brexit the UK can set its own rules. The FCA published CP26/28 on 14 July 2026 with HM Treasury's draft regulations [S345, Confirmed]. Key points [S345, Confirmed]:
- Managers are split by net assets: small (under £750m), medium (£750m to £5bn) and large (over £5bn).
- Investor disclosures become less prescriptive. Firms choose how to disclose leverage.
- Consultation closes 14 October 2026. Final rules are expected in 2027, with implementation expected in 2028.

**Why it matters to KKR.** KKR markets funds to EU and UK investors and runs European credit funds. EU loan-origination rules affect how European direct-lending funds are built. A lighter UK regime could make London more attractive [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS.** Fundraising in Europe depends on marketing rules (passports, national private placement) and on product structure. A London KCS junior needs to know why a fund is offered as a Luxembourg vehicle to one client and through a different route to another [Inferred].

**Question:** "With AIFMD II tightening EU loan-origination rules and the UK consulting on a lighter regime, do you see any change in where KKR builds European credit products for wealth clients?"

### Story 13. UK pensions: Pension Schemes Act 2026, Mansion House Accord, LGPS megafunds

- **Headlines:** "Landmark Pension Schemes Bill receives Royal Assent" (Gateley, 2 June 2026). "£400bn LGPS: What pooling means for private credit investments" (Alternative Credit Investor, 30 April 2026)
- **URLs:** https://gateleyplc.com/insight/article/landmark-pension-schemes-bill-receives-royal-assent/ (free) ; https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2026/04/30/400bn-lgps-what-pooling-means-for-private-credit-investments/ (free)

**Plain English.**
- **The Act.** The Pension Schemes Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026 [S346, Confirmed].
- **Megafunds.** Large multi-employer defined contribution (DC) schemes must have at least one default fund of £25bn or more by 2030. Schemes with at least £10bn can use a transition route if they can reach £25bn by 2035 [S346, Confirmed].
- **Reserve power to direct investment.** The government can, once and not before 1 January 2028, require megafunds to hold "qualifying assets". These are private equity, venture capital, private credit, land, infrastructure and unlisted shares. The cap is 10% of the default fund, of which no more than 5% can be UK-specific. The power lapses at end-2032 if unused and is repealed by end-2035. Trustees can opt out if compliance is "likely not to be in the best interests of members" [S346, Confirmed].
- **Mansion House Accord.** This is the voluntary May 2025 pledge by 17 workplace pension providers to put 10% of default funds in private markets by 2030, with 5% in the UK. The Act's cap mirrors it [S348, Reported; S346 confirms the 10%/5% caps].
- **Early evidence.** A May 2026 Isio study of 13 master trusts found they are "rapidly increasing" illiquid allocations [S348, Reported].
- **LGPS.** The Local Government Pension Scheme holds over £400bn across 86 funds in England and Wales. It is consolidating from eight pools into six "megafunds" after ministers rejected the ACCESS and Brunel pools' plans. The 31 March 2026 pooling deadline was missed. Investment decisions move to the pools [S347, Reported].

**Why it matters to KKR.** UK DC money and LGPS pools are becoming large, sophisticated buyers of private markets. They prefer big managers with infrastructure and private credit platforms that can offer low fees and UK assets [Inferred].

**Why it matters to KCS (London).** This is the most "local" story for a London KCS team. The client universe is shrinking into fewer, larger buyers (six LGPS pools, a handful of £25bn+ master trusts), each wanting customised, fee-efficient access. That is exactly the "customized products" line in the KCS description [Inferred].

**Opinion:** "The UK reforms create fewer but bigger clients. For KKR the opportunity is not just selling funds but building separate accounts or co-investment programmes for pools, especially in UK infrastructure like offshore wind, where KKR already invests."

### 1.15 KKR's house view: Henry McVey's "Outlook for 2026" and the Mid-Year 2026 update

Henry McVey is Head of Global Macro and Asset Allocation and CIO of KKR's Balance Sheet [S360, Confirmed]. His team's outlooks are KKR's public "house view". Clients read them, and KCS teams use them in client meetings [Inferred].

**A. "Outlook for 2026: High Grading" (published 17 December 2025)** [S361, Confirmed; date from Business Wire snippet]
Authors: Henry McVey, David McNellis, Aidan Corcoran (Dublin) and Changchun Hua (Hong Kong) [S361, Confirmed].
Core idea in plain English: the economy is fine but the cycle is late. Do not run away from risk. Instead "high grade": upgrade the quality of what you own, how it is financed, and who you deal with. KKR argued this upgrade is cheap to do now [S361, Confirmed].
Key calls [S361, Confirmed]:
1. Create your own "alpha" (extra return) in private markets through operational improvement, for example carve-outs that move companies from capital-heavy to capital-light.
2. Favour businesses that can grow margins using technology, automation and AI ("winner takes most").
3. Look for relative value, because expected returns are compressing.
4. Overweight Asia: "We want to own more Asia at this point in the cycle."
5. Own more "collateral-based cash flows" linked to nominal GDP, because inflation has a higher "resting heart rate".
6. Avoid "cheap" assets with weak competitive positions or capital structures.
7. Expect international earnings to accelerate (Europe, Japan, emerging markets).
8. Expect credit losses to keep normalising, especially in 2021 vintages.
9. Expect the government bond "Regime Change" (bonds no longer a reliable hedge) to spread to Japan and the UK.
10. GDP growth above consensus in the US, Japan and China.
Mega-themes: Corporate Reform; Security of Everything; Productivity and Worker Retraining; Intra-Asia Trade; Consumption Upgrades in emerging markets; Global Services [S361, Confirmed].
Memorable lines: "Using an education analogy, we think a B is an A in this environment, but a C could be an F." and "The productivity boom is gaining momentum, but more of this improvement is now in the price." [S361, Confirmed]

**B. "Mid-Year Outlook for 2026: The Divergence Conundrum" (June 2026; press release 10 June 2026)** [S360, Confirmed]
Authors of the In Brief: Henry McVey and Lauren Goodwin (Chief Investment Strategist for Global Wealth) [S360, Confirmed].
Core idea: "the cycle continues, but the rules of the game are changing." The economy moves from "a low-cost, efficiency-first model" to one where "redundancy, reliability, resilience, and control carry a higher premium". Some parts of the economy (AI, productivity, high-end services, national security) attract capital. Others (housing, lower-income consumers, old-economy capex, rate-sensitive sectors) are under pressure. "For investors, the implication is not to avoid risk, but to be more disciplined in how that risk is owned." [S360, Confirmed]
Key calls in plain English [S360, Confirmed]:
1. **Divergence is the defining feature.** Stay in quality, focus on durable cash flows, and back managers who create value through operations.
2. **Productivity extends the cycle, but unevenly.** Since ChatGPT launched, S&P 500 real revenue per worker is up about 8% after 20 years of no growth. Small caps show the opposite.
3. **"Security of Everything" is now a capital allocation decision.** It covers defence, cyber, data, shipping lanes, food, water, energy and critical minerals. KKR favours infrastructure and real assets tied to this.
4. **Higher "resting heart rate" for inflation.** KKR expects goods inflation to settle about 100 basis points above pre-pandemic levels. Portfolios need more links to nominal GDP.
5. **Stocks and bonds diversify each other less.** Be careful relying on long-duration Treasuries as a safe haven. Add "differentiated return streams".
6. **Expected returns are fine but harder to capture.** Manager selection matters more.
7. **In private markets, tilt to operational improvement, not financial engineering.** KKR is constructive on carve-outs, public-to-private deals and situations where investors control outcomes. Its exhibit shows leverage contributing less to buyout returns over time.
8. **Europe and Asia remain compelling.** In Europe KKR favours domestic durability, infrastructure, defence, grids, energy systems, logistics and the periphery (Spain, Italy, Poland), not just a weak Germany. In Asia: corporate reform in Japan and Korea, and consumption upgrades in India and Southeast Asia.
9. **Bond volatility is broadening.** Yields are rising for different reasons in different countries.
10. **Less positive on equity supply and demand than a year ago** because IPOs are reopening. Credit technicals are more supportive.
11. **"High Grading" still matters.** The cost to upgrade is low (AAA vs BBB credit spreads near lows).
12. **Collateral-based cash flows** (asset-based finance, real estate credit, structured solutions) matter more.
13. **Manager selection** is a bigger part of returns in a lower-return, higher-dispersion world.

Why a KCS candidate should know this: KCS sells KKR's view as well as its funds. Almost every 2026 product launch lines up with the house view: infrastructure (Security of Everything), asset-based finance (collateral-based cash flows), Asia Fund V (overweight Asia) and wealth products for portfolio diversification [Inferred].

## 6. Interview angles

**A. Three facts to drop naturally (all verifiable)**
1. "KKR raised $34bn in Q2 and $133bn over 12 months to June, and now manages about $796bn." [S330]
2. "Global Infrastructure Investors V closed at $19.2bn in August, KKR's largest infrastructure fund, with private wealth platforms alongside sovereigns and pensions." [S334]
3. "K-Series reached $42bn, up from $25bn a year earlier, even though the industry had a redemption scare in Q1." [S330, S337]

**B. Opinions a candidate could form (with reasoning)**
- **Scale wins, but service decides.** Capital is concentrating in $1bn+ funds [S336]. KKR benefits, but bigger multi-product relationships raise the cost of poor service. That is the case for a strong KCS function.
- **The Q1 redemption scare was a test, not a failure.** K-FIT pro-rated in Q1 and paid in full in Q2 [S338]. The lesson is honest liquidity communication at the point of sale.
- **The UK is turning into a market of a few giant buyers.** These are £25bn+ DC megafunds and six LGPS pools [S346, S347]. KKR's UK assets (DCC, Norfolk Vanguard) and infrastructure platform give London KCS a story that matches the "UK productive investment" agenda.
- **KKR's house view and product shelf line up.** "Security of Everything" maps to infrastructure. "Collateral-based cash flows" maps to asset-based finance. "Own more Asia" maps to Asia Fund V. A KCS junior who can link a macro theme to a product is useful from day one.

**C. Smart questions (pick two or three)**
1. "Scott Nuttall expects a record fundraising year. Which channel do you expect to surprise most in the next two years: insurance, private wealth, or the 401(k) route via Capital Group?"
2. "How does the London KCS team split coverage between UK pensions, European institutions and Middle East sovereigns, and has the UK pension consolidation changed that?"
3. "After the Q1 redemption wave, did KCS change how it educates private bank partners about semi-liquid funds?"
4. "With Arctos now part of KKR Solutions, how do you see secondaries fitting into conversations with LPs who are short of distributions?"
5. "Henry McVey's mid-year note says manager selection matters more in a high-dispersion world. How do KCS teams prove that to a sceptical consultant?"

**D. Traps to avoid**
- Do not say KKR "manages $800bn+". The last reported figure is $796bn at 30 June 2026. Q3 is out on 9 November.
- Do not confuse FS KKR Capital Corp (FSK, a listed BDC) with K-FIT (KKR's non-traded retail credit fund).
- Do not call the Mansion House Accord law. It is voluntary. The Pension Schemes Act 2026 holds a reserve power capped at the Accord's 10%/5% levels.
- On the DOJ matter, stick to KKR's public statement: a civil settlement, $250m, reimbursed by outside law firms, and KKR disagrees with the characterisation [S349].
