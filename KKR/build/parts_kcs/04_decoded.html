The life of a fundraise

A flagship fund is a multi-year project for KCS. The diagram shows the stages from design to the next fund, with what KCS does at each. The sequence is the standard private markets pattern [Inferred]; the examples are KKR's.

Key terms

Close: a date on which investors sign up and become bound. Side letter: a private agreement giving one investor extra terms (reporting, fee or legal points). Capital call: KKR asking investors to send part of their commitment. Distribution: cash paid back to investors. Re-up: an existing investor committing to the successor fund. Donnenfeld on GII V: "strong support from both longstanding clients who have partnered with us across multiple fund vintages and new investors" [S334] [Confirmed].

The life of an RFP or DDQ

A request for proposal (RFP, called a "tender" in the UK and EU) asks managers to bid for a mandate. A due diligence questionnaire (DDQ) is a long list of questions an investor asks before committing: strategy, team, track record, risk, legal, operations, ESG, fees. KKR's Dublin posting: "Work with the internal CAPS team to complete tenders/Due Diligence Questionnaires for potential new clients across credit while leading investor diligence processes" [S106] [Confirmed]. Frankfurt interns "Coordinate responses to client due diligence requests/RFPs, including underlying portfolio statistics, marketing materials and presentation decks" [S141] [Confirmed].

The life of a co-investment syndication with KCM

KKR's 10-K describes the mechanism. KKR Capital Markets "provides syndication services for co-investments in transactions participated in by KKR, our investment vehicles, Global Atlantic, and third-party clients, which may entitle the firm to receive transaction fees, management fees, and a carried interest" [S70] [Confirmed]. KKR may commit to more of a deal than it keeps and "syndicate a portion of our capital commitment to third parties. However, if we are unable to syndicate all or part of such commitment, or if such co-investors fail to fund their commitments, we may be required to fund the remaining commitment" [S70] [Confirmed].

Key terms

Syndication: selling slices of a deal to other investors. Underwrite: commit to the whole amount first, taking the risk of not selling it. Wall-crossing: formally giving someone confidential (possibly price-sensitive) information, after which they are restricted from trading on it. MNPI: material non-public information.

The London analyst role covers institutions and family capital [S101]; wealth sits in GWS [S157]. But a summer intern may see both, and the work differs in shape. Don't say this

"KCS is basically sales." It is partly sales, but the postings put reporting, KYC, client requests and investor diligence first [S101, S106]. Also avoid "KCS is the back office": the team "leads fundraising activities" [S106] and sits on KKR's main growth line.

The compliance dimension: bright lines

Client work at an asset manager is regulated work. KKR's 10-K names the frameworks: in the UK and EU it markets under "private placement exemptions and marketing registrations, for example under the AIFMD in Europe, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 ... and the Alternative Investment Fund Managers Regulations 2013" [S70] [Confirmed]. Sales to EEA individuals are governed by MiFID II, AIFMD and the ELTIF Regulation, which "contain requirements for, among other things, marketing, investor suitability assessments, and conflicts of interest management" [S70] [Confirmed]. KKR's London LLPs are FCA-authorised [S171, S172] [Confirmed].

Why this matters

KKR's culture line is "our word is our bond", and Kravis tells of apologising to a bank's CEO after an employee retraded a deal in KKR's favour [S384] [Confirmed]. In KCS that culture shows up as accuracy and honesty with clients. Raising compliance yourself, briefly and calmly, signals you understand the seat.
