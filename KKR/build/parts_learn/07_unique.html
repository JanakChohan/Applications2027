Don't say this

"I could only learn this at KKR." Say instead "KKR is where I would see this most clearly, because..." and name the mechanism. The difference is one clause and it removes the overclaim.

The follow-up questions your answer will trigger

A specific answer invites specific questions. That is the point: it moves the interview onto ground you have prepared. For each phrase in Version A, here is the likely follow-up and a scaffold for the reply.

"You mentioned K-INFRA. What is it, in one breath?"
Tests: whether the name is decoration.

"KKR's evergreen infrastructure vehicle for individual investors. It owns stakes in KKR infrastructure assets through joint ventures with funds like GII V and the core infrastructure fund, strikes a NAV each month, charges 1.25% plus 12.5% of returns over a 5% hurdle, and lets investors redeem up to 5% of NAV a quarter." [S421]

"Why not just put private bank clients into GII V?"
Tests: whether you understand why wrappers exist.

Minimums, capital calls over years and no liquidity suit a pension, not an individual. The evergreen wrapper invests money at once, offers limited liquidity and accepts small cheques, in exchange for a liquidity sleeve, a gate and a different fee structure [S421] [Inferred]. Then add the cost: the 5% gate means a client may not get out when they want, as K-FIT's Q1 2026 queue showed [S338].

"What is the Total Portfolio Approach?"
Tests: whether you read KKR's own research.

Instead of fixed buckets ("10% private equity"), the client sets one return and risk target for the whole portfolio and lets every asset compete for a place against a simple reference portfolio. KKR's 2026 papers describe ideas such as "zero-based budgeting" and a "minimum viable TPA" [S435, S436]. For KCS it turns a fund sale into a portfolio conversation [Inferred].

"Why does co-investment matter to KKR, not just to clients?"
Tests: commercial understanding.

Clients get extra exposure at low or no fee. KKR gets bigger deals done, and capital markets fees earned by KCM are not offset against fund fees, unlike fund-level transaction fees: about $930m of KCM fees in 2025 [S425]. Then add the safeguard: allocation has to be fair and disclosed, which is why ILPA's DDQ asks about it [S444].

"What do you mean by information walls?"
Tests: maturity about rules.

Capital markets work can involve material non-public information about listed companies. People who hold it are separated from people who do not, and anyone brought over the wall is logged and restricted. In a client seat that means never passing on deal information outside a controlled process [Inferred; rules in Part 5].

"Isn't 'trust is the product' a cliché?"
Tests: whether you can back a value with evidence.

"It would be without the evidence. Kravis describes spending almost 17 years on Walter Industries to get fund investors their money back [S384]. In a client team the same idea is mechanical: if one number in a DDQ is wrong, every other number becomes doubtful."

The one-paragraph version for an interview

Say it in one line (or four)

"I want to learn the technical core of a client seat at a firm built as one P&L. That means three things: the plumbing of how one strategy is packaged for different clients, like infrastructure through GII V or K-INFRA; how clients build portfolios, from pacing to KKR's Total Portfolio Approach; and how co-investment flows from Capital Markets to clients behind proper walls. KKR is where I would see all three in one seat."
