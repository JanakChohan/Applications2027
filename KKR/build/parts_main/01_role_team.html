Part 1

The internship decoded, the six teams mapped, and how they link

Every line of the advert explained, each team made concrete, and one map that shows how money, deals and stories move between them.

You are ranking six teams you have never sat in. This part gives you a working picture of each one: what it does on a Tuesday, who it talks to, what it hands to the others, and what it would want from you. Then it puts all six on one map. If you understand the map, you can answer "why this team?" for any of them, and you can explain why Client Solutions (KCS) sits at the centre of it.

How to read the tags

[Confirmed] means a primary source (KKR filings, kkr.com, KKR job posts, Companies House) or two good outlets. [Reported] means one credible source. [Inferred] is reasoning from the facts, mostly mine. [Advert] is the internship text you were given. Source IDs like [S175] point to the source list at the back of the pack.

Block 1: "A leading global investment firm that offers alternative asset management as well as capital markets and insurance solutions"

This is KKR's standard boilerplate. It maps to KKR's three reporting segments: Asset Management (five business lines: Private Equity, Real Assets, Credit and Liquid Strategies, Capital Markets, Principal Activities), Insurance (Global Atlantic) and Strategic Holdings [S1, S2] [Confirmed]. Notice what comes first: asset management, not "private equity". Traditional private equity was over 70% of AUM in 2010 and under 25% at the end of 2025 [S2] [Confirmed]. The phrase "patient and disciplined investment approach, employing world-class people, and supporting growth in its portfolio companies and communities" is word for word the sentence in every KKR earnings release [S1] [Confirmed].

Don't say this

"KKR is a private equity firm." Say "a global investment firm with asset management, insurance and strategic holdings". That is KKR's own framing [S1, S12] [Confirmed].

Blocks 3 and 4: the teams and the common duty line

The advert names seven teams: Capital Markets, Capstone, KKR Client Solutions, Global Impact, Infrastructure, Private Equity and Real Estate. You rank six, because Global Impact sits inside the Private Equity choice. Then comes one sentence that applies to everybody: interns will be "involved in helping identify attractive investment themes and opportunities, synthesizing industry and market data and analysing potential investments" [Advert].

Read that carefully. Even the client-facing team (KCS) and the operations team (Capstone) are told they will work on investment themes. In KCS, a "theme" is what you pitch: why infrastructure, why now, why Europe [Inferred]. In Capstone it is where the operating upside is. So every candidate, whatever the first choice, needs one investment idea they can explain and defend. KKR's head of talent acquisition says KKR wants candidates who "understand how the macro environment is affecting investors and what that might mean for KKR's businesses" [S393] [Reported].

Block 5: the AI line

"Interns are encouraged to leverage KKR's approved AI tools to accelerate their work, deepen their learning, and contribute fresh perspectives on how emerging technology can enhance the way we operate" [Advert]. Three words matter. Approved: only tools the firm has cleared, because client and deal data are confidential. Accelerate: first drafts of research, summaries of long documents, formatting. Fresh perspectives: they want ideas from you on how the team could work better. KKR's London KCS analyst ad already asks for "Strong working knowledge of AI-enabled productivity tools, with the ability to use generative AI effectively and responsibly" [S140] [Confirmed].

Say this in the interview

"I use AI for the first 70% of a task, like summarising a long report or drafting a structure, and then I check every number against the source. In a client team I would expect the approved tools to help most with first drafts of due diligence answers, which then need a human to verify." [Inferred]

A smart observation to make

The Infrastructure paragraph says the team invests "out of a global $17bn fund" [Advert]. That matches the predecessor fund, Global Infrastructure Investors IV, which raised $17bn in March 2022 [S334] [Reported]. The successor, Global Infrastructure Investors V, closed at $19.2bn on 3 August 2026, KKR's largest infrastructure fund ever [S176] [Confirmed]. So the advert text is older than the latest fund. Saying "I noticed the advert mentions the $17bn fund, but Fund V has since closed at $19.2bn" shows you read primary sources, not just the job page. Say it as a helpful note, not a correction.

The Real Estate paragraph has a similar wrinkle. It says "As of Q4 2025, our team consists of 130+ dedicated investment and asset management professionals across 10 countries, with more than $85 billion" [Advert]. KKR's London Real Estate off-cycle post, updated October 2026, says "120+ ... across 11 countries" and about $84bn as of March 2026 [S142] [Confirmed]. Both are KKR's words; the numbers move with each update. Quote whichever you cite with its date.

2. The six teams, one by one

The six choices fall into two groups. Three teams invest: they decide what to buy (Private Equity with Global Impact, Real Estate, Infrastructure). Three teams make the investing possible: one raises and serves the money (KCS), one finances the deals and sells slices to co-investors (KCM), and one improves the companies after purchase (Capstone). KKR runs all of them as "one firm" with one profit and loss account and one compensation pool [S2, S5] [Confirmed]. That is why the links between them are real.

How big each team is in London

KKR's public people directory lists staff at Principal level and above (not analysts or associates). Counting London entries by team gives a fair picture of relative size [S149] [Confirmed]. KCS is the largest of the six intern teams in London at senior level.

2.1 Private Equity (including Global Impact)

Private Equity buys large or controlling stakes in private companies with money from a fund plus borrowed money, works with management for roughly five years to make each company bigger and better, then sells. KKR's Private Equity business line managed $254.7bn at 30 June 2026 [S175] [Confirmed], across 230+ portfolio companies [S191] [Confirmed]. In Europe, KKR said in 2023 it had been investing for "nearly 25 years" [S185] [Reported]. Global Impact is a separate private equity strategy inside this choice that backs companies solving big social and environmental problems while aiming for commercial returns [Advert].

KKR's own description (from the advert)

"A Private Equity Intern in our London office will be involved in various aspects of the private equity investment process, including sourcing and evaluating potential investments and presenting opportunities to the broader team, performing due diligence, and implementing improvements at portfolio companies. As we generally operate in small teams, interns will play an integral role and obtain significant responsibility from Day 1." [Advert]
