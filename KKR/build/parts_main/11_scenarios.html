Part 11

The scenario playbook: frame method, ladder, composure set, CV grill

One method for any "what would you do?" question, a fixed priority order, 30 practice scenarios across all six teams, five composure tests and a CV drill.

Every KKR team asks some version of "what would you do if...?". The question tests judgement, honesty and calm more than knowledge. This part gives you a short method, then drills it on the general intern duties and on each qualification the internship asks for, with scenarios set in Private Equity, Infrastructure, Real Estate, Capital Markets (KCM), Capstone and Client Solutions (KCS). The KCS deep dive has a fuller version for client roles.

How to read this part

The protocol, priority order, counters and drills are method: our advice, not KKR facts. Scenarios are invented for practice. Facts inside them carry source IDs and confidence tags. KKR does not publish its scenario questions. What is known: candidates "typically" meet 6 to 8 executives [S393] [Reported], and Henry Kravis says KKR looks "beyond financial acumen" for "like and trust" [S385] [Confirmed].

The method: protocol, priority order, one sentence

The four-step protocol (45 to 75 seconds)

The priority order when good things collide

Method. Hard scenarios set good against good: helping your MD against fairness, speed against sign-off. Decide the order now, so you never invent it under pressure. For any KKR intern role:

The one sentence that holds every frame

"I would get it right, keep it fair, write it down, and then make it fast." Say it to yourself whenever pressure scrambles your thinking. Method.

Why this order. Kravis: "Our business really runs on trust. People don't want to work with you, let alone entrust you with their capital, if they can't trust you will do what you say" [S384] [Confirmed]. Fairness between investors is policed: in 2015 the SEC found KKR had misallocated more than $17m of broken-deal costs to its flagship funds rather than sharing them with co-investors, and KKR paid nearly $30m [S20] [Confirmed]. Records matter: in January 2025 KKR paid $11m in an SEC sweep over business messages sent on unapproved apps [S29] [Reported]. Investment committees "review and evaluate investment opportunities using frameworks that are designed to include qualitative and quantitative assessments of the key investment risks" [S2] [Confirmed], so a hidden risk is a process failure as well as an accuracy one [Inferred].

Eight frame breakers and the counters

Method. Interviewers push on purpose to see whether you fold. The rule for all eight: acknowledge the pressure, keep the order, offer a better option.

Staying calm physically

  • Rehearse out loud, timed, with a phone stopwatch. Rehearsed answers feel short in the room.
  • One slow breath out before Step 1; feet flat, hands still.
  • Write two words on your pad when the question lands ("speed / accuracy"). It anchors Sort.
  • Slow your pace, not your volume. Pauses read as thought.
  • If you blank: "Can I take five seconds to order my thoughts?" Nobody marks that down.
  • Be kind to everyone in the building. Kravis says KKR watches how candidates treat people they are not trying to impress [S385] [Confirmed].

The ladder: three scenarios for each duty and qualification

Ten lines, three rungs each: easy (do you understand it?), medium (two good things collide) and hard (pressure plus a twist). Lines 1 to 4 are the general intern duties; lines 5 to 10 are the testable qualifications. Each scenario is set in a named team so you practise across the whole programme.

Where these lines come from

Every duty and qualification line here is quoted from KKR's own 2027 London internship description, as published on the SEO London listing and the attached team descriptions [Advert]. The core sentence reads: "summer interns will be involved in helping identify attractive investment themes and opportunities, synthesizing industry and market data and analysing potential investments. Interns are encouraged to leverage KKR's approved AI tools to accelerate their work, deepen their learning, and contribute fresh perspectives on how emerging technology can enhance the way we operate." The SEO application form itself sits behind a login and was not read [S392].

The CV grill, compact

CV walk-throughs feature in reported KKR first rounds [S394] [Reported]. The interviewer picks one line and goes three levels deep: the claim (what did you do?), the method (how exactly, what number, why that way?) and the judgement (what went wrong, what would you change?). Your frame is consistency: the same facts, the same number and the same lesson at every level. Method.

Eight attacks and the holding answer

AttackHold the frame
"What did you personally do?"Split "we" and "I" cleanly. Never borrow credit.
"How did you get that number?"Base, end, period, source in one breath. Say if it's an estimate.
"Why did you leave so soon?"Fact, reason, what you took away. No blame.
"That's not relevant here."Bridge to one duty line in one sentence.
"This grade is weak."Own it, no excuses, show the fix and the result.
"You list Excel. Show me."List only what you can demonstrate live.
"What happened in markets this week?"One dated item, one view. KKR wants macro awareness [S393] [Reported].
"What's your real weakness?"A real flaw with a real fix, not a disguised strength.

The four-line drill (every bullet)

Template

1 Claim: "I ..." in one sentence.

2 Number: from ___ to ___, over ___, source ___.

3 Choice: "I chose ___ over ___ because ___."

4 Lesson: "What went wrong was ___; since then I ___."

Example only (invented bullet)

"Treasurer, university investment society: grew sponsorship 40%." Claim: "I rebuilt how we pitched sponsors, one tailored page per firm." Number: "£5,000 to £7,000 over one year, from the bank statements." Choice: "Eight targeted firms over a mass email to 40." Lesson: "I nearly lost a sponsor over a missed invoice, so I set up a shared tracker." Replace with your own true details.

Say this when you feel the grill tighten

"That's the honest limit of what I did. My part was X; the number came from Y; what I'd do differently is Z." Saying where your knowledge stops is a strength. Bluffing is what fails, and arrogance fails fastest [S385] [Confirmed].
