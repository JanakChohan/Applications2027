KKR against nine rivals at firm level: size, staffing, permanence of capital, the competitive map, six claims no peer can match, and the areas where KKR is behind.

Why this matters

Three firms are bigger than KKR. Two of them (Blackstone, Apollo) also raise far more money in a year. So "Why KKR?" cannot be answered with size. It has to be answered with structure: what KKR owns, how it pays its people, and how those pieces connect. [Inferred]

The competitive map

Since about 2021 the industry has split three ways in how it gets "permanent" money that never has to be handed back: owning an insurer (Apollo, KKR), managing insurers' money under contract without owning them (Blackstone, Brookfield), and selling evergreen funds to wealthy individuals, where Blackstone is far ahead [S201, S214, S221, S236] [Inferred]. The map below adds a second question: how many different engines does each firm run under one roof?

Size, fundraising and staffing

Three readings. First, KKR is the third-largest fundraiser in 2025 but its $129bn was its own record, and it beat its $300bn three-year target in two and a half years ($305bn since January 2024) [S207] [Confirmed]. Second, its AUM per person looks low only because Global Atlantic's 1,491 insurance staff are in the count. Third, KKR's stated aim is to keep it that way: Lewin says KKR can grow "without requiring us to significantly increase our headcount or sacrifice our culture" [S207] [Confirmed].

Claims that belong to KKR, not to you

Two strong-sounding lines are KKR's own claims, not independent facts. "The lowest compensation ratio in the industry" (17.5% of fee related revenues in 2025 vs peers at 26% to 34%) comes from KKR's shareholder deck [S5]. "We are the largest private equity player. We're the largest infrastructure player" in Asia comes from the Q2 2026 call [S207] [Reported]. Use them only as "KKR says...".

Don't say this
  • "KKR is the biggest private markets firm." It is fourth by AUM in this group.
  • "Only KKR has its own capital markets business." Blackstone (BXCM) and Apollo (Apollo Capital Solutions) have one too [S214, S220].
  • "KKR has the most permanent capital." Apollo has more, in dollars and as a share [S220].
  • "KKR is a private equity firm." Traditional PE was under 25% of AUM at end 2025 [S2]. Say "a global investment firm with asset management, insurance and strategic holdings".
  • "KKR's returns beat Blackstone's." The research found no like-for-like comparison; LTM figures are not comparable [S207, S213].
Say this in the interview: "Why KKR and not Blackstone or Apollo?"

"Blackstone is bigger and far ahead in wealth. Apollo has the bigger insurer. KKR's difference is how the pieces fit: funds, an insurer it owns, its own stakes in companies, and an in-house capital markets arm, all in one P&L with one compensation pool and staff owning about 30% of the firm. For a client that means one relationship can reach every part of KKR. For me it means the client team is the place where you see all of it."
