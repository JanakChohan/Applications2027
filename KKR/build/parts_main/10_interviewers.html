Reading the interviewer in the first two minutes

You are not trying to "work" the interviewer. You are trying to pitch the depth and length of your answers at the right level, the way a good client professional would [Inferred]. Three honest signals tell you most of what you need:

  1. How they introduce themselves. A title and team ("I'm an associate in Infrastructure") tells you seniority and practitioner status. A recruiter usually introduces the process first [Inferred].
  2. Their first question. "Walk me through your CV" or a values question suggests a fit screen. A technical or market question suggests a practitioner testing depth [Inferred].
  3. How they react to your first answer. Follow-ups on detail mean "go deeper"; moving quickly on means "shorter answers" [Inferred].
Don't do this

Mirroring body language on purpose, flattering the interviewer, guessing at their politics or private life, or changing your story to suit them. These are manipulation tactics, they are easy to spot, and they clash with KKR's stated test of "like and trust" [S384] [Inferred]. Adapt depth and length; never adapt the truth.

When names arrive: a 20-minute research routine

Recruiters sometimes share interviewer names a day or two before. Spend no more than 20 minutes per person. The aim is one relevant, professional point of connection and the right pitch level, not a dossier [Inferred].

MinutesDoWhy
0 to 4Find the person's bio on kkr.com ("Our People"). Note title, team, city and how long they have been at KKR.Primary source; confirms identity and seniority, which tells you which box of the 2x2 they sit in.
4 to 8Search KKR's press releases and media centre for their name: deals, fund closes, appointments, promotions.Gives you one recent professional fact, for example a fund they helped raise [S184] [S165].
8 to 12Check whether they have authored or spoken in public: KKR Insights, conference panels, interviews.Public views you can respond to thoughtfully if the topic comes up.
12 to 15Look at their professional profile only (role history). Do not send connection requests or messages before the interview.Career path context. Contact before the interview can look like lobbying [Inferred].
15 to 18Write one question for them that links their team's public work to your interest. Use the question lists in Part 9.Turns research into a genuine question, not a recital.
18 to 20Write down the pitch level (2x2 box) and one identity check (is this definitely the same person?).Avoids the embarrassment of confusing two people with the same name.
Why this matters

The point of the routine is relevance and respect. One light reference ("I saw your team's infrastructure fund closed in August") shows homework. A list of facts about the person reads as surveillance and breaks the "like and trust" test [S384] [Inferred].

Fair game
  • Job title, team, city, years at KKR.
  • Deals, funds and initiatives they are publicly linked to.
  • Their published articles, talks and quoted statements.
  • Prior employers and roles listed on their KKR bio.
Off limits
  • Family, home, health, religion, politics, age.
  • Personal social media, photos, hobbies not shared professionally.
  • Anything found by digging beyond professional sources.
  • Contacting them, their colleagues or their assistants to gain an edge.
