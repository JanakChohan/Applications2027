The interview: process, question bank, questions to ask

How KKR London is likely to interview across all six teams, what each team tests, 75 questions as scaffolds, and ten questions to ask back.

You rank six teams on one application. Each team is likely to test you differently. This part gives the whole-programme view: the shared funnel, then each team's style and its own questions, then a general bank that every interviewer can draw from. The Client Solutions deep dive (in the separate KCS pack) goes further on that one team.

Every candidate likely goes through the same early stages. The split comes at the final round, where each team sets its own task. Why this matters

Fortune's report lists what KKR recruiters look for: real interest in KKR specifically, how your background adds to the team, familiarity with recent deals and milestones, and an understanding of how the macro environment affects investors [S393] [Reported]. Those four apply to every team. The team tasks only decide how the technical part is tested.

1. Private Equity (including Global Impact)

What is reported. LBO or modelling tests at the final round; paper LBOs; deal walk-throughs; "what makes a good LBO candidate" [S394] [Reported]. Extern claims a 60 to 90 minute blank-sheet three-statement LBO, pass or fail [S395], unsourced. Leland says PE in the Americas uses a 2 to 3 hour in-person LBO case with no internet [S394] [Reported]. Glassdoor posters describe "basic live cases" comparing two hypothetical businesses [S402] [Reported], anecdote. London intern format: unknown.

What it tests. Accounting fluency, LBO logic, investment judgement, and whether you can talk about a deal. London context. KKR's European Fund VI had $7,513m of commitments in the June 2026 10-Q, with a reported $8bn close in April 2023 [S175] [S185]. Recent European growth deals include Fresha in London ($80m at a $1bn valuation, May 2026) [S183] [Confirmed]. Global Impact Fund II had $2,709m of commitments [S175] [Confirmed].

Key term

Paper LBO: a leveraged buyout done in your head or on paper in a few minutes. Example with illustrative numbers: buy a company with EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation) of 100 at 10x, so the price is 1,000. Borrow 500, put in 500 of equity. Five years later EBITDA is 200, sold at 10x for 2,000, and half the debt is repaid, so 250 is left. Equity is now 1,750, which is 3.5x the money (MOIC), roughly a 28% a year return (IRR). This question pattern is reported by a low-reliability site [S396]; the numbers are invented for teaching [Inferred].

PE questionScaffold
Walk me through an LBO. What makes a good LBO candidate? [S394]Price, debt and equity, cash flow pays debt, exit. Good candidate: steady cash flow, low capex, room to improve, sensible price.
Here are two businesses. Which is the better PE investment? [S402]Compare growth, margins, cash conversion, competition, cyclicality, price. Pick one and say what would change your mind.
Here is a company description: what are the three biggest risks? [S396]One market risk, one operating risk, one financing risk. Say how you would test each.
Depreciation rises by $10 and the tax rate is 25%: what happens to net income? [S395]Net income falls by $7.50; cash rises by $2.50 via lower tax. Walk through each statement.
Why private equity rather than investment banking? [S403]Ownership and long-term judgement, not "better hours". Tie to a KKR example.
Pitch a deal you would invest in. [S396]Business, why now, how value is created (operations, not only leverage), risks, exit. KKR's mid-year outlook tilts to "operational improvement, not financial engineering" [S360].

2. Real Estate

What is reported. A London RE analyst (full-time, interviewed 2022) described headhunter vetting, technical questions, a modelling exercise with debrief, then a three-interview superday over about two months [S401] [Reported], anecdote. KKR's 2027 London RE off-cycle advert stresses "Strong financial modelling skills" and "Demonstrable interest in Real Estate" [S391] [Confirmed].

What it tests. Property cash flows and valuation, modelling, and real passion for the asset class. London context. KKR's European logistics platform Mirastar bought UK logistics assets from PLP (about £170m, July 2026) and from Ares funds (September 2026) [S184] [Confirmed]. KKR formed a joint venture of up to £500m with Puma Property Finance for UK living-sector lending (March 2026) [S184] [Confirmed]. Global real estate: 120+ professionals and about $84bn of AUM (March 2026) [S142] [Confirmed].

RE questionScaffold
How would you value a property? [Inferred]Net operating income divided by a cap rate; check with comparables and a discounted cash flow. Explain each term.
Why logistics, and what could go wrong? [Inferred]E-commerce and supply chains drive demand; risks are oversupply and tenant quality. Use the Mirastar deals [S184].
What does a rise in interest rates do to property values? [Inferred]Higher financing cost and higher required yields push values down unless rents rise. Mention inflation-linked leases.
Equity or debt: where is the better risk-reward in European real estate now? [Inferred]Define both, give a view, cite KKR doing both (equity platforms and the Puma lending JV) [S184].
Tell me about your interest in real estate. [S391]A specific building, street or sector you have studied, not "I like buildings". This is the advert's own ask.
Walk me through your model. [S401]If a modelling test is set, the debrief matters: assumptions first, then outputs, then what you would check with more time.

London context. All [Confirmed].

Infrastructure questionScaffold
What makes an asset "infrastructure"? [Inferred]Essential service, long life, high barriers to entry, often contracted or regulated cash flows.
Why do investors want infrastructure now? [Inferred]Inflation links, steady income, and KKR's "Security of Everything" theme (energy, data, supply chains) [S360].
What is Core+ and how does it differ from a buyout? [S334]Lower risk, steady income plus some growth, lower target return than buyouts. GII V is a Core+ fund [S334].
Offshore wind: what are the main risks? [Inferred]Construction cost, power prices, grid connection, policy. Mention the RWE JV and that capex is over $15bn [S181].
Why are data centres an infrastructure asset? [Inferred]Long contracts with large tenants, power-hungry, essential to AI demand. Helix Digital Infrastructure launched June 2026 with $10bn+ [S3].
How would higher rates affect an infrastructure valuation? [Inferred]Higher discount rate lowers value; offset if revenues are inflation-linked. Show both sides.

4. Capital Markets (KCM)

What is reported. A prep site describes about four rounds, with debt financing and LBO capital structures, market views or a pitch idea, and a case on capital structure or debt capacity [S407] [Reported], low reliability. An older leveraged-credit anecdote: "how would you value a company" [S407].

What it tests. Debt basics, capital structure, and market awareness. London context. Mark Danzey is European Head of KCM, Global Co-Head of Debt Capital Markets and European Head of Institutional Client Solutions [S156] [Confirmed]. KKR's UK capital markets LLP had 10 employees and 10 members in 2025 [S172] [Confirmed]. KCM earned $930m of transaction fees in 2025 [S70] [Confirmed]. KCM began in 2007 to raise the amount of third-party capital KKR could commit to deals [S208] [Confirmed].

KCM questionScaffold
Why would KKR have its own capital markets team rather than use banks? [S208]Control over financing, speed, fees kept in the firm, ability to syndicate to clients. Note Blackstone and Apollo also have one [S214] [S220].
Walk me through a typical LBO capital structure. [S407]Senior secured loans, high-yield bonds or private credit, equity. Explain seniority and cost.
How much debt can this company carry? [S407]Start from EBITDA and a sensible leverage multiple; check interest cover; consider cash-flow stability.
Bank loan, bond, or private credit: when would you use each? [Inferred]Speed and certainty (private credit), cost and size (bonds), flexibility (loans).
What is syndication? [S70]Committing to a large financing or equity cheque then selling parts to other investors. KCM syndicates co-investments to clients [S70].
Give me a market view. [S407]One trend in credit markets, with data. KKR's outlook calls credit technicals "more supportive" [S360].

KCS questionScaffold
Why Client Solutions rather than an investment team? [S397]What you enjoy (explaining, relationships, seeing the whole firm), plus evidence. KCS "seeks to 'connect the dots' across all businesses at KKR" [S390].

6. Capstone

What is reported. Glassdoor: three 30-minute interviews, a CV and behavioural one first, then a "high-level case with simple mental math" on improving a company's metrics [S405] [Reported], anecdote. Fishbowl: fit plus cases plus an analytical test "cutting raw data rather than building a financial model" [S406] [Reported], anecdote, older.

What it tests. Structured problem solving, quick numbers, and practical business sense. Context. KKR's 10-K describes "operational guidance from our operating professionals at KKR Capstone" [S174] [Confirmed]. Capstone fees were $113.6m in 2025 [S201] [Confirmed]. London has 15 Capstone bios at Principal level and above, the same as London PE [S149] [Confirmed]; Juan de Ochoa, Partner, is co-head of Capstone EMEA [S161] [Confirmed].

Capstone questionScaffold
A company's margins are falling. How would you find out why? [S405]Split revenue (price, volume, mix) from costs (materials, labour, overheads); find the biggest mover first.
How would you improve this company's EBITDA by 10%? [S405]Revenue levers and cost levers; size each one with quick maths; pick the top two.
Here is a table of raw data: what does it tell you? [S406]Sort, group, look for outliers; state one insight and one question. Do not over-model.
Quick maths: 15% of 340? Cost falls 8% on a 60% cost base: margin change? [Inferred]51. Costs fall 4.8 points of revenue, so margin rises by 4.8 points. Say your steps aloud.
Why operations rather than deal-making? [Inferred]You like fixing things you can see. Tie to "operational improvement, not financial engineering" [S360].
Tell me about a process you improved. [Inferred]Before, what you changed, measured result. Credit the team.

Conduct on the day, whichever team you face

KKR's founders say they judge "who the person is", not only skills [S385] [Confirmed]. Most of that judgement happens between the questions. The checklist below applies to every team and every stage [Inferred].

Before

  • Re-read your own application. Interviewers often start from what you wrote [Inferred].
  • Know three current numbers: AUM $796bn at 30 June 2026, $34bn raised in Q2, K-Series $42bn [S330]. After 9 November, update them from the Q3 release [S333].
  • One-line answers ready for "why KKR", "why this team", "why your ranking".
  • For video: camera at eye level, quiet room, notes out of shot. HireVue gives one attempt per answer [S394].

During

  • Answer first, then explain. Interviewers have 20 to 30 minutes [S402] [S403].
  • For a technical question you cannot answer: say so, then reason aloud from what you do know. Guessing a number is worse than a clear "I don't know, here is how I would work it out" [Inferred].
  • Ask to write things down in a maths or case question. It is normal and shows care [Inferred].
  • Use "we" for team results and "I" for your own actions.

Everyone counts

  • Kravis: KKR watches how candidates treat people they are not trying to impress; "Those who are arrogant fail this test" [S385] [Confirmed]. That includes reception, SEO staff, IT support on a video call and other candidates.
  • Be on time and do what you say. "Our word is our bond" [S384] [Confirmed].

After

  • Note every question you were asked within an hour. Patterns help in the next round [Inferred].
  • If you send thanks, keep it short and specific to one point from the conversation. Send it only through the channel the recruiters used [Inferred].
  • Do not post interview questions online; treat the process as confidential [Inferred].
