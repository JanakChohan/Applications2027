The six months in one paragraph

KKR is having a record year while parts of its industry wobble. That tension is the story an interviewer will want you to hold in your head.

KKR raised $34bn of new money in Q2 2026 and $133bn over the 12 months to 30 June, and manages $796bn [S330] [Confirmed]. Co-CEO Scott Nuttall said: "We expect a record fundraising year for the firm" [S362] [Reported]. Two flagship funds closed: North America Fund XIV at about $23bn in April and Global Infrastructure Investors V at $19.2bn in August [S335, S334] [Confirmed]. The private wealth funds (K-Series) reached $42bn [S330] [Confirmed]. Meanwhile wealthy individuals asked to pull more than $20bn out of private credit funds in Q1, and several managers, KKR among them, limited withdrawals [S337, S338] [Reported]. Regulators kept opening private markets to ordinary savers (US 401(k) proposal, SEC accredited investor proposal, UK Pension Schemes Act 2026) while rewriting fund-manager rules (AIFMD II, the new UK AIFM regime) [S342, S275, S346, S344, S345]. Inside KKR, the founders' special control rights end by 31 December 2026 at the latest, and KKR settled a $250m civil case with the US Department of Justice in August [S332, S349] [Confirmed].

KKR's house view in plain English

Henry McVey is KKR's Head of Global Macro and Asset Allocation and CIO of its Balance Sheet [S360] [Confirmed]. His team's outlooks are KKR's public "house view": what the firm thinks about the economy. Clients read them, and client teams use them in meetings [Inferred]. KKR's head of talent acquisition told Fortune that KKR wants candidates who "understand how the macro environment is affecting investors" [S393] [Reported]. These two documents are how you show that.

Dec 2025: "Outlook for 2026: High Grading"

The economy is fine but the cycle is late. Do not run from risk; upgrade what you own, how it is financed and who you deal with, because upgrading is cheap today. "Using an education analogy, we think a B is an A in this environment, but a C could be an F." Calls include: create your own returns through operational improvement; favour firms that use AI to grow margins; "We want to own more Asia at this point in the cycle"; own "collateral-based cash flows" because inflation has a higher "resting heart rate" [S361] [Confirmed].

Jun 2026: "The Divergence Conundrum"

"The cycle continues, but the rules of the game are changing." The economy moves from "a low-cost, efficiency-first model" to one where "redundancy, reliability, resilience, and control carry a higher premium". Some sectors attract capital (AI, productivity, national security); others struggle (housing, lower-income consumers, rate-sensitive sectors). "For investors, the implication is not to avoid risk, but to be more disciplined in how that risk is owned" [S360] [Confirmed].

Other calls worth knowing from the mid-year update [S360] [Confirmed]: since ChatGPT launched, S&P 500 real revenue per worker is up about 8% after 20 years of no growth; goods inflation should settle about 100 basis points (1 percentage point) above pre-pandemic levels; stocks and bonds diversify each other less; in Europe KKR favours infrastructure, defence, grids, logistics and the periphery (Spain, Italy, Poland), not just a weak Germany; and manager selection "is a bigger part of returns" when results vary more between managers.

Say this in the interview

"KKR's mid-year outlook says the cycle continues but the rules are changing, and that manager selection matters more when returns diverge. That is a fundraising argument as much as a macro view: it explains why capital is concentrating in large platforms like KKR."
