Why the client function has its own industry

Private markets firms do two jobs. They invest money, and they raise it. This part is about the second job: the people, steps and rules that sit between an investor's savings and a fund like KKR's. It is the ground a Global Client Solutions (KCS) intern stands on every day.

The basics section explained the fund itself: a general partner (GP, the manager) and limited partners (LPs, the investors) signing a limited partnership agreement (LPA). Here we look at how an LP is found, convinced and kept, and at the walls of regulation that decide what may be said, to whom, and where.

Read those three numbers together. The pot of money is not growing. More funds than at any time in five years are competing for it. And the biggest managers take a rising share. In a market like that, the quality of a firm's client team decides who wins [Inferred].

A short history of raising money

1980s to 1990s: the address book. GPs raised money mostly through their partners' personal networks. A fund was a handful of relationships with pension funds and wealthy families [Inferred].

2000s to 2010s: the professional IR team. As funds grew to tens of billions and LPs multiplied, the large GPs built in-house investor relations (IR) or "client solutions" teams. These have relationship managers per region or client type, product specialists per strategy, and junior generalists [Inferred]. KKR's own team, KCS, was "first formed in 2009" and now has "over 300 executives located in over 20 [cities] across four continents" [S390] [Confirmed]. It is organised around "Sales/Relationship Management and Product/Strategy Management" [S390] [Confirmed].

2010s: the LP pushes back. LPs organised. The Institutional Limited Partners Association (ILPA) published its first Principles in September 2009 and its first due diligence questionnaire in 2013 [S317, S320] [Confirmed]. Fund terms, reporting and diligence became more standard.

2020s: the individual arrives. Bain estimates individuals hold about 50% of global assets ($275tn to $295tn) but only 16% of the assets held in alternative funds [S293] [Reported]. Closing that gap is the "democratisation" story. Platforms such as iCapital and CAIS, plus wirehouses and private banks, now carry private funds to wealthy individuals. PitchBook says "growing inflows from retail investors are becoming the main source of new capital" for private equity [S260] [Confirmed].

Why this matters

KKR's 10-Q says traditional private equity was over 70% of its assets in 2010 and is "less than 25%" today [S332] [Confirmed]. The client function grew up alongside that shift: more products, more client types, more rules. A KCS junior now explains credit, infrastructure, insurance-backed strategies and wealth funds, not one buyout fund.

Key terms

Gatekeeper: anyone who controls access to an LP's money, usually a consultant. Feeder fund: a small fund whose only job is to pool many investors and invest in one bigger fund. Re-up: an existing LP committing to the manager's next fund. Re-ups are the cheapest money a client team ever raises [Inferred].

How an institutional LP picks a manager

A pension fund does not wire $200m after one good meeting. Selection takes months and passes through a funnel of checks (see the funnel figure). Each stage creates work for the GP's client team. The KKR EMEA client relations analyst advert lists exactly this work: "investor reporting", "supporting relationship managers in fundraising", "KYC and onboarding", and answering requests with product, investment, operations, finance, legal, compliance and risk teams [S388] [Confirmed].

The stages in words

  1. Screening. The LP or its consultant filters by track record, strategy fit and team stability [Inferred].
  2. Investment due diligence. Deal-by-deal attribution (which deals made the money), DPI (cash actually returned divided by cash paid in), PME (a comparison with a stock index), and reference calls [Inferred].
  3. Due diligence questionnaire (DDQ). A long standard form, often the ILPA DDQ. The first ILPA DDQ came out in 2013, was updated in 2016 and 2018, and DDQ 2.0 arrived in November 2021 with new sections on succession planning, co-investment, GP-led secondaries, credit facilities and cybersecurity [S320] [Reported].
  4. Operational due diligence (ODD). A separate team checks the back office: valuation policy, cyber, compliance, administrator, auditor. The FCA's March 2025 review of private market valuations found weaknesses in governance records, conflicts, independence and ad hoc revaluations [S310] [Reported]. Expect ODD teams to ask about exactly those.
  5. Investment committee. The LP's own board or committee approves [Inferred].
  6. Legal. LPA terms and a side letter: a private contract giving one LP extra terms such as fee breaks, reporting rights, co-investment rights or the right to skip certain deals. A most-favoured-nation (MFN) clause lets an LP claim terms given to similar or smaller LPs [Inferred].
  7. Commitment and monitoring. Quarterly reports, annual meetings, a seat on the LP advisory committee (LPAC) for big LPs, and in time the next re-up [Inferred].

ILPA: the rule book written by the clients

ILPA's three guiding principles are alignment of interest, governance and transparency, which it says "form the essence of an effective private equity partnership" [S317] [Confirmed]. ILPA has no legal power. Its weight comes from the fact that its members are the LPs.

Regulation in one picture: who may be marketed what

All fundraising rules answer one question: who is this investor, and where are they? Professional investors are assumed to look after themselves. Ordinary savers are protected, so they can only buy private assets through special, regulated products. The map below is the one to carry into an interview.

The European Union

AIFMD: the base law

The Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive (AIFMD), Directive 2011/61/EU, regulates managers of "alternative" funds: private equity, credit, real estate and hedge funds. It was signed on 8 June 2011, entered into force on 21 July 2011 (some sources say 22 July), and had to be in national law by 22 July 2013 [S300] [Confirmed].

  • Marketing passport. From 22 July 2013 an EU manager could sell an EU fund to professional investors across the EU after a notification [S300] [Confirmed].
  • NPPR (national private placement regime). Non-EU managers have no passport. They use each country's own private placement rules under Article 42, country by country [S300] [Confirmed].

Pre-marketing and reverse solicitation

The Cross-Border Distribution package (Directive (EU) 2019/1160 and Regulation (EU) 2019/1156) was published on 12 July 2019, came into force on 1 August 2019 and applied from 2 August 2021 [S299] [Confirmed]. It defined pre-marketing: testing investor interest before a fund is set up or notified, without an offer. An EU manager must tell its home regulator within two weeks of starting. AIMA's summary: "Within two weeks of starting to pre-market, an EU AIFM must send an informal letter or email to its home regulator" [S299] [Confirmed].

Reverse solicitation means the investor approached the manager on its own initiative, so no "marketing" happened. The package closed the loophole: any subscription by a professional investor within 18 months of pre-marketing counts as marketing [S299] [Confirmed]. For a client team, this means every early conversation in Europe is logged and timed.

AIFMD II

Directive (EU) 2024/927 was published in the Official Journal on 26 March 2024, entered into force on 15 April 2024, and had to apply from 16 April 2026 [S296, S294] [Confirmed]. Linklaters marked the day: "AIFMD II takes effect at last" [S344] [Confirmed].

ELTIF 2.0 and MiFID II

The European Long-Term Investment Fund regulation, revised as Regulation (EU) 2023/606 ("ELTIF 2.0"), applies from 10 January 2024. It removed the €10,000 minimum for retail investors and widened eligible assets. Its technical standards became Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2759, published on 25 October 2024 [S301, S302] [Confirmed]. Morningstar counts 189 new ELTIFs since the start of 2024; Scope counts a record 113 launches in 2025 and about €34bn of assets at end-2025, up 55% [S302] [Reported].

MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU) has applied since 3 January 2018. Its product governance rules make manufacturers and distributors define a "target market" for each product, so a fund is only sold to the kind of client it was designed for [Inferred] (dates not re-verified).

The United Kingdom

The UK AIFM regime and its rewrite

The UK put AIFMD into law in 2013 and kept it after Brexit. Overseas funds still reach UK investors through the UK NPPR [S304] [Reported]. HM Treasury and the FCA opened a reform consultation on 7 April 2025 [S304] [Confirmed]. Then came FCA CP26/28 "The UK AIFM Regime", published in July 2026 (14 July per Macfarlanes; Debevoise's note is dated 23 July) [S345, S303] [Confirmed].

  • A new "ALTS" sourcebook. Investor disclosures become less prescriptive; firms choose how to disclose leverage [S303, S345] [Confirmed].
  • UK managers marketing UK funds move from a 20-day pre-approval to a simple notification. NPPR kept with limited changes [S303] [Confirmed].
  • Companion papers: CP26/27 on pay (the FCA "does not propose to make malus and clawback mandatory") and CP26/26 on reporting [S303] [Confirmed].
  • Comments close on 14 October 2026, three days after this pack was written. Policy statement in 2027; rules in force 2028 [S303] [Confirmed].

Retail and wealth routes in the UK

  • LTAF (Long-Term Asset Fund). Rules in force 15 November 2021. PS23/7 (29 June 2023) made LTAF units "Restricted Mass Market Investments" from 3 July 2023, opening them to retail investors with protections, and to self-select DC pensions and SIPPs [S305, S306] [Confirmed].
  • Financial promotions exemptions. On 31 January 2024 the high-net-worth thresholds rose to £170,000 income or £430,000 net assets. After an industry backlash a new order from 27 March 2024 restored £100,000 and £250,000 and brought back the "two or more unlisted investments" test for self-certified sophisticated investors [S307] [Confirmed]. A good example of rules moving twice in two months.
  • Consumer Duty. Applied to open products from 31 July 2023 and closed products from 31 July 2024. It requires firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers [S308] [Confirmed].
  • SMCR (Senior Managers and Certification Regime). Extended to FCA solo-regulated firms, which include most UK private markets managers, on 9 December 2019 (PS19/20) [S309] [Confirmed]. It makes named senior people personally accountable.

UK pensions: the Mansion House Accord and the Pension Schemes Act 2026

The Mansion House Accord (13 May 2025) is voluntary. 17 workplace pension providers, covering about 90% of active defined contribution (DC) savers, pledged to put at least 10% of DC default funds into private markets by 2030, with at least 5% in the UK. It could unlock up to £50bn, about £25bn in the UK [S315] [Confirmed]. HM Treasury: "At least 5 percent of these portfolios will be ringfenced for the UK" [S315] [Confirmed].

The Pension Schemes Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026 after the Bill was introduced on 5 June 2025 [S316, S346] [Confirmed]. Main points:

  • Large multi-employer DC schemes need a default fund of at least £25bn by 2030 ("megafunds"); a transition route exists for schemes of £10bn or more that can reach £25bn by 2035 [S346] [Confirmed].
  • A reserve power lets the government, once and not before 1 January 2028, require megafunds to hold "qualifying assets" (private equity, venture capital, private credit, land, infrastructure, unlisted shares). Cap: 10% of the default fund, of which no more than 5% UK-specific. Trustees can opt out if compliance is "likely not to be in the best interests of members". The power lapses at end-2032 if unused [S346] [Confirmed].
  • Separately, the Local Government Pension Scheme (over £400bn across 86 funds in England and Wales) is consolidating from eight pools into six [S347] [Reported].

The United States

  • Regulation D. Rule 506(b) allows private offerings with no general solicitation (advertising). Rule 506(c), effective 23 September 2013 under the JOBS Act, allows general solicitation if every buyer is a verified accredited investor [S314] [Confirmed].
  • Accredited investor. An individual with income over $200,000 ($300,000 joint) in each of the last two years, or net worth over $1m excluding the home [S314] [Confirmed]. August 2020 amendments added professional licence routes (Series 7, 65, 82) [S275] [Reported]. Around 30 September 2026 the SEC proposed a FINRA-developed accredited investor exam (pass valid ten years) plus Series 79, 86 and 87, CPA, CFA and CFP routes, with a 60-day comment period [S275] [Reported]. Chairman Paul Atkins: "accredited investor access to private offerings should not be limited solely to individuals satisfying financial thresholds" [S275] [Reported].
  • Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1). Adopted 22 December 2020, effective 4 May 2021, compliance from 4 November 2022. It replaced the old advertising and cash solicitation rules [S313] [Confirmed]. It governs how performance is shown in any pitch book.
  • Private Fund Adviser Rules. Adopted August 2023 (quarterly statements, audits, restricted activities, preferential treatment, adviser-led secondaries). Vacated in full by the Fifth Circuit on 5 June 2024 in National Association of Private Fund Managers v. SEC [S311, S312] [Confirmed]. Much of what they required now travels through ILPA templates and side letters instead [Inferred].
  • 401(k) access. Executive Order 14330, "Democratizing Access to Alternative Assets for 401(k) Investors", signed 7 August 2025 [S273] [Confirmed]. The Department of Labor proposed a process-based safe harbour on 30 March 2026, with six factors: performance, fees, liquidity, valuation, benchmarks and complexity. Comments closed 1 June 2026 [S272, S274, S342] [Confirmed]. No final rule found as of 11 October 2026 [S343] [Reported].
  • SEC retail agenda. A roundtable on retail access to private investments on 4 March 2026 [S277] [Reported]; in early October 2026 the SEC reportedly voted to propose wider retail access through regulated funds [S368] [Reported].

The walls around a client function

A client team talks to outsiders all day, and it sits inside a firm that holds secrets. Four kinds of wall keep the two apart (see the figure below). The detail differs by firm and is set by each firm's compliance team; what follows is the standard industry pattern, not KKR's internal policy [Inferred].

  • Information barrier (the "Chinese wall"). Separates people with MNPI from people who trade listed securities. Breaching it risks insider dealing [Inferred].
  • Need-to-know wall. A client team learns about live deals only when compliance approves it ("wall-crossing"). Example: before DCC confirmed an approach from Energy Capital Partners and KKR on 29 April 2026, any knowledge of the bid would have been MNPI about a London-listed company [S356] [Reported]. The application to KCS is [Inferred].
  • Marketing gate. Every pitch book, DDQ answer and performance figure is approved before it leaves. It must match the investor type and country (the "who may be marketed what" map), meet EU pre-marketing timing and the Marketing Rule in the US [S299, S313] [Confirmed]; how firms apply them is [Inferred].
  • Gifts and entertainment. Hospitality given or received is logged, kept within limits and pre-approved where policy requires; extra care applies to public pension officials [Inferred].
Why this matters

On governance, litigation and regulation, LP questionnaires want the approved answer. On the August 2026 DOJ settlement, KKR's public line is that the $250m civil penalty "will have no financial impact on the firm, our funds, or any of our investors and will be fully reimbursed by outside law firms" [S349] [Confirmed]. A junior repeats that line and nothing more.

What to say about all this

What a candidate should be able to say unprompted

  1. "Fundraising is concentrating: funds above $1bn took 78.2% of capital in H1 2026, against 59.1% in 2021." [S260]
  2. "LPs choose through a funnel: consultant screen, due diligence, the ILPA DDQ, operational due diligence, investment committee, side letter, then commitment."
  3. "In Europe a non-EU manager markets through national private placement; AIFMD II applied from 16 April 2026." [S300, S294]
  4. "In the UK, CP26/28 proposes three tiers of manager by size, with new rules in 2028; the Pension Schemes Act 2026 caps any mandation at 10% private, 5% UK." [S303, S346]
  5. "In the US the direction is wider access: EO 14330 in August 2025, the DOL safe harbour proposal in March 2026, and the SEC's accredited investor proposal this autumn." [S273, S272, S275]
Say this in the interview

"Regulation is moving in two directions at once. Access is widening for individuals, through ELTIF 2.0, the LTAF and the US 401(k) work. But the rules on credit funds and liquidity are getting tighter, through AIFMD II. A client team has to sell the first while explaining the second."

Don't say this

"The Mansion House Accord forces UK pensions to invest 10% in private markets." It is voluntary. The legal backstop is a reserve power in the Pension Schemes Act 2026, capped at the same 10% / 5% levels and not usable before 1 January 2028 [S315, S346].

Say this in the interview

"The SEC's private fund adviser rules were vacated in June 2024, so a lot of LP protection now comes from ILPA's templates and from side letters. That puts more of the transparency burden on the client team."

Don't say this

"Reverse solicitation is an easy way round EU marketing rules." Since August 2021, a subscription within 18 months of pre-marketing counts as marketing [S299]. Calling it a loophole signals you would be a compliance risk.
