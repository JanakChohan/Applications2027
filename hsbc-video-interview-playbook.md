## 2. Exact mechanics — how the recording works

**[OFFICIAL unless noted][8][9][13][20]:**

- **Number of video questions:** the simulation's final exercise contains ~6 questions, a subset recorded on video (the rest SJT/free-text) [ANECDOTAL][20]. Across sources, plan for **roughly 3–6 recorded video answers**.
- **Prep and record time:** **~2 min to prepare, up to ~2 min to record** per question is the HSBC-configured figure most sources report [CORROBORATED][13]; Cappfinity's generic default elsewhere is ~30 s prep / up to 3 min [ANECDOTAL][8]. Cappfinity's own line: **"the exact time will be specified as you attempt the question"** [OFFICIAL][8]. **Read the on-screen timer — it's authoritative.**
- **A countdown is shown**, for both the prep window and the record window; recording auto-starts when prep ends and auto-stops at the limit [CORROBORATED/INFERRED][8].
- **One take, no re-record (assessed questions):** *"Video responses for assessed questions cannot be re-recorded, but you have time to prepare your answer ahead of the question"* [OFFICIAL][8]. Do **not** count on a retake.
- **Warm-up / set-up first:** you *"verify that your camera and microphone are working… by recording a short video,"* replay it, and can *"record this video several times until you can see and hear yourself well,"* then sometimes answer a *"non-assessed, timed"* practice question in the real format [OFFICIAL][9]. **Fix framing, lighting and audio here.** (Conflict: one prep source says HSBC's set-up question *is* scored — so **treat it as if it counts** [ANECDOTAL][13].)
- **Question format:** the question is typically **shown on screen as text** (sometimes a short clip); you answer by video [OFFICIAL/CORROBORATED][8]. Expect to *read* the prompt, then prep, then record.
- **Pause:** no source indicates you can pause a live recording; the one-take timed design implies you cannot [INFERRED][8].
- **The container is "untimed," the video answers are not.** HSBC says *"our online assessments are not timed, but you can ask for additional time during any of the interview stages"* — i.e. you control when you sit it within your ~10-day window, but each video answer is individually timed; **extra time is an available adjustment if you need it** [OFFICIAL][4].

## 3. How a single video answer is scored

- **AI-assisted or human — HSBC's answer is human.** Cappfinity's product offers **"AI-assisted scoring to accelerate reviews, or human-led evaluation when nuance matters most,"** with **"structured questions and scoring criteria to reduce bias"** [OFFICIAL][7]. But the governing HSBC statement is: **"The AI-based platform does not make any automated decisions… all decisions will be made by our hiring team"** [OFFICIAL][3]. Net: a **human reviews your video**; any AI only assists triage.
- **What a marker is actually weighing** (from Cappfinity's strengths methodology + HSBC's stated criteria) [CORROBORATED/INFERRED][7][3]:
  1. **Content / evidence (dominant):** a real, specific example that demonstrates the target strength/competency and answers the actual question.
  2. **Structure:** a clear, logical answer (STAR / point-first).
  3. **Communication / delivery:** clarity, steady pace, articulation, minimal filler.
  4. **Values & motivation:** genuine fit with HSBC's four values and real reasons for the role/bank.
  5. **Authenticity & consistency:** natural, first-person delivery consistent with the rest of your assessment (strengths tests reward what you genuinely do well and flag "gamed" answers).
- **Myth to ignore.** A prep site claims the video *"uses an AI feature to track your facial expressions"* — that **misattributes an old HireVue-style feature**; HSBC's simulation is Cappfinity and there's no official evidence it scores facial emotion [ANECDOTAL, flagged][16]. **Don't perform expressions — communicate content clearly.** (Being calm and personable still helps; being *theatrical* doesn't.)
- **No public rubric with weightings exists** — the five factors above are inferred from official methodology, not a leaked scoresheet [INFERRED].

## 4. What sinks people on video (the screen-out patterns)

Synthesised from candidate reports + prep guidance [CORROBORATED unless noted][15][16][3]:

- **Rambling / no structure** — the number-one killer. Without a skeleton you wander and get cut off before the result.
- **Not answering the actual question** — drifting into a rehearsed story that doesn't fit the prompt.
- **Running out of prep or record time** — over-scripting in prep, then reading slowly and getting cut off; or finishing in 30 seconds with nothing of substance.
- **Generic answers** — "HSBC is a big respected bank" / textbook values with no personal specifics.
- **Reciting a script** — reads as robotic and inauthentic; the strengths/consistency checks and a human reviewer both notice.
- **Weak commercial awareness** — for CIB, being unable to discuss a recent market development is a visible gap.
- **Delivery basics** — poor lighting/audio, looking at your own image instead of the lens, low energy/monotone, heavy filler ("um, like").
- **Technical failure** — untested webcam/mic, weak Wi-Fi, notification pop-ups, device dying mid-answer (HSBC literally says *"fully charge your… device"*) [OFFICIAL][1].

## 5. Integrity on the video — don't trip it by accident

The video runs under Cappfinity's integrity layer (Camera-On recording, Time-Recording, and an Integrity Dashboard that flags likely AI use) [OFFICIAL][12]. For an honest candidate the risks are *accidental*:

- **Reading a script off-screen / eyes darting** → looks like reading external help. **Look at the lens; glance at your prep notes only briefly.**
- **Another person or voice in frame** → looks like assistance. **Be alone in the room.**
- **AI-generated answers.** HSBC explicitly prohibits using AI to *"generate responses during live or recorded interviews"* and requires you can *"personally explain, validate, or replicate"* what you submit [OFFICIAL][3]. **Prep with AI; never read AI text to camera.**
- **Timing extremes** — answers that are implausibly polished/fast can look pre-read; work at a natural pace.
- **Read the on-screen declaration/instructions** before you start — those are the binding rules for your sitting.

(Per your ask, nothing here is about defeating detection — it's about not looking dishonest while being honest.)

## 6. Delivery technique — set-up and performance

### Set-up (fix this during the warm-up)
- **Camera at eye level**, laptop raised if needed; you should look slightly *up or level*, not down. Face and shoulders in frame, ~an arm's length away [CORROBORATED][15].
- **Light in front of you** (a window or lamp facing you), never behind — backlight turns you into a silhouette [CORROBORATED][15].
- **Look at the lens, not your self-view** — HSBC's explicit instruction; put the question/notes near the webcam so your eyeline stays up [OFFICIAL][1].
- **Audio:** quiet room, no echo; test in the warm-up and replay it [OFFICIAL][9].
- **Background:** tidy, neutral, no one walking through. **Close notification apps** [OFFICIAL][1].
- **Dress** as for a face-to-face interview — smart; it also puts you "in the zone" [OFFICIAL][1].
- **Device charged / on power; stable connection** [OFFICIAL][1].

### Performance (while recording)
- **Front-load:** first sentence states your answer/point; then evidence; then result. Assume you might be cut at the limit [CORROBORATED][15].
- **Pace:** steady and clear; slightly slower than feels natural; pause instead of "um" [CORROBORATED][16].
- **Energy & body language:** sit upright, natural hand gestures, a genuine (not forced) smile, warmth and calm confidence [CORROBORATED][16].
- **Length:** aim to fill most of the ~2 minutes with substance; land the result by ~90 s. Don't pad to hit the limit.
- **Voice:** first person — "**I** did", not "we"; specific numbers and outcomes beat adjectives.

### Using the ~2-minute prep window
Jot a **skeleton, not a script** [CORROBORATED][15]:
- Behavioural → one keyword per **S-T-A-R(-R)** letter + the number you'll land on.
- "Why" / motivational → your 2–3 points + opening line.
- Pick **one** specific example; note how it evidences a value.

### If you blank or fumble (remember: no re-record)
- **Pause, breathe, continue** — a brief natural recovery reads fine; don't spiral [INFERRED][8].
- Fall back to the **STAR spine**: "The situation was… my task was… so I… and the result was…" to get moving.
- **Misspoke? Correct and carry on**: "—to be clear, the result was…". Reviewers expect natural speech.
- Watch the countdown and give a **clean one-sentence close** before it ends rather than trailing off.

## 8. Answer frameworks — skeletons to rehearse (not scripts)

HSBC recommends **STAR** for behavioural answers and a strengths/values-based approach; deliver in your own words, since authenticity is scored [OFFICIAL][3].

### Tell me about yourself (~90–120 s) — Present → Path → Future
- **Now** (1 line): who you are + a hook relevant to CIB.
- **Path** (2–3 lines): two experiences showing the skills the role needs (analysis, stakeholder/client work, teamwork under pressure) — evidence, not your whole CV.
- **Why here/now** (1–2 lines): banking → HSBC → this division, pointing forward.
- Slip in one value implicitly (e.g. an international detail → *We value difference*).

### Why HSBC? (~90 s) — three layers, then make it personal
- **HSBC's distinctive edge:** the leading *international* bank — Asia–Europe–Middle East corridors, trade-finance leadership, a global network few peers match (not "big and respected").
- **Division fit:** tie that edge to CIB specifically (cross-border client coverage, trade & payments, markets franchise).
- **You:** one concrete personal reason that proves real research (a language, an international background, a specific HSBC deal/report/strategy you followed).
- Weave *We succeed together* + *We value difference*. Keep to 2–3 points, concrete.

### Why CIB / this division? (~90 s)
- **What CIB does**, in one accurate sentence (shows you know it's the wholesale, markets-led + financing bank — not retail).
- **Why it suits you:** the work that genuinely energises you (long-horizon client relationships + analytical rigour; or market/deal tempo) tied to evidence.
- **Why HSBC's version** beats peers (network, sector coverage, East–West clients).
- Forward look: what you want to learn. Map *We get it done* + *We take responsibility*.

### Joining a new senior team — introduce yourself & build relationships (~90 s) — forward-looking, no STAR
- **Posture:** brief, confident, humble intro — who you are, what you bring, genuine eagerness to learn; don't over-claim to seniors.
- **Listen-first actions:** ask their priorities, observe how the team works, offer help on small things early, find a buddy/mentor, adapt your communication to each person.
- **Over time:** deliver reliably on your first tasks (credibility), follow up, show you value their expertise.
- **Close on** *We value difference* (learn from varied perspectives) + *We succeed together*.

### Behavioural (teamwork / leadership / failure / conflict) (~2 min) — STAR(+R)
- **S** (1–2 sentences, brief context) · **T** (your specific responsibility) · **A** (the bulk, ~50–60% of airtime; **"I"**, concrete steps and *why*) · **R** (quantified outcome + what you learned) · **+R** one-line reflection.
- Choose a story that also demonstrates a value; end failure/conflict on **resolution + learning**, never blame.

### Strengths / weakness (~75–90 s)
- **Strength:** name one (max two) relevant to the role → a crisp proof example → why it matters here. Pick something you're genuinely energised by (authenticity is scored).
- **Weakness:** a real, non-fatal one (not a disguised humble-brag) → the concrete steps you're taking → evidence of progress. Avoid anything core to the role.

### Recent macro event & market impact (~90–120 s) — analyst mini-structure
- **Event:** name one, precise and recent (a rates decision, an inflation print, a geopolitical/trade shock, a big FX/commodity move).
- **Transmission:** *how* it moves markets — rates → bonds/FX → equities/credit → corporate financing costs.
- **So-what for HSBC/CIB:** impact on clients, deal flow, trade finance, or the markets desks (this connect-back is what separates strong answers).
- **Balanced read:** one risk, one opportunity; show you follow markets habitually. Map *We take responsibility*.

### A recent deal / industry you find interesting (~90 s)
- **Name it** (recent, ideally an HSBC/CIB angle) → **facts** (parties, rationale, rough size/structure, 1–2 lines) → **your analysis** (strategic logic, financing, risks, trend) → **HSBC relevance** (adviser/lender/markets) → **personal hook** (why it grabs you).

### Weaving the four values (every answer)
Don't announce them — **evidence** them. *We value difference* = cross-cultural examples, seeking other views · *We succeed together* = collaboration, credit to the team · *We take responsibility* = owning outcomes/mistakes, flagging risk, the long view · *We get it done* = pace, decisiveness with limited info, delivering under pressure. Aim for **1–2 values per answer**, never a checklist of all four [OFFICIAL values][5].

## 9. A focused video rehearsal plan (inside your ~10-day window)

- **Day 1 — Material bank.** Write 6–8 STAR(+R) stories that between them cover teamwork, leadership, failure, conflict, ownership, a decision with limited info, and adapting to change. Tag each to a value.
- **Day 2 — Motivation.** Draft your *Tell me about yourself*, *Why HSBC*, *Why CIB* skeletons (bullets, not scripts). Nail one specific HSBC/CIB fact per answer.
- **Day 3 — Commercial awareness.** Prepare 2 recent market stories using the §8 event→transmission→so-what structure. Skim FT/Bloomberg on HSBC and CIB daily from here.
- **Day 4 — Record & review.** Record yourself (phone is fine) answering 5 prompts in ~2 min each, from bullets. Watch back for: eyeline on lens, structure, filler, pace, energy. Redo the worst two.
- **Day 5 — The flagged prompts.** Rehearse the *new senior team* intro (#4) and a *recent macro event* (#5) until they're tight and natural.
- **Day 6 — Delivery polish.** Fix your set-up (camera height, front light, tidy background, quiet room). Practise front-loading and a clean one-sentence close under the timer.
- **Day 7 — Mock under pressure.** Do 6 questions back-to-back, one take each, no restarts — simulate the real constraint.
- **Days 8–9 — Platform dry run.** Register on the Preparation Plus portal, run its practice, confirm webcam/mic/browser, and repeat any weak answers.
- **Day 10 — Sit it** fresh, quiet room, front light, charged device, home connection, on the lens. Do the warm-up properly (record/replay until audio and framing are right), then go.
