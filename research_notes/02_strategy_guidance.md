## The number the firm is managed to
> "we continue to feel that **17% is a reasonable expectation of our through-the-cycle
> returns**... the 17% through-the-cycle target is not aspirational; rather, it serves as a
> helpful backstop measure to think about the trade-offs between investing in every single
> SVA positive business and focusing on maximizing returns."

Key nuance, direct quote: **"ROTCE is an output, not an input."**
> "we do not make decisions in order to achieve a particular outcome on ROTCE. Our focus is
> on growing long-term shareholder value, which we believe is best approximated by our
> ability to deploy capital at returns in excess of our cost of equity"

They explicitly REFUSED to raise the 17% target despite a 20% "launch point" (FY25 ROTCE).
Reason given: much capital deployment is deliberately into sub-17% return businesses where
returns still exceed cost of equity → adds shareholder value added (SVA) even if dilutive to
the headline ratio. This is a genuinely important point for anyone interviewing: JPM
optimises SVA (absolute economic profit), not the ROTCE percentage.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION FOR THE REPORT: the $19.8bn technology spend is NOT the same as the
$11.0bn "Technology, communications and equipment expense" line in the FY25 income
statement. The $19.8bn is a management-defined total cost of technology including engineer
compensation and outsourced services. Do not present them as the same thing.
Tech spend trajectory per press: ~$17bn (2024) → ~$18bn (2025) → ~$19.8bn (2026e).
The 2024/2025 points are SECONDARY (trade press); the 2026 figure is PRIMARY.

## Expense philosophy (quote)
> "we are not managing the Firm for short-term operating leverage... long-term PPNR growth is
> a much better lens through which to assess our investments. ... our PPNR CAGR continues to
> outpace both revenue and expense growth"
(PPNR = pre-provision net revenue = revenue minus expense, before credit losses.)

## Technology commentary worth quoting
> "we are probably past the point of peak modernization... we will always continue to
> modernize our technology and have shifted focus from infrastructure modernization to
> modernizing the underlying application code and data."
Cost pressure named explicitly: "higher hardware expense, as AI-related shortages are pushing
up memory prices" and public-cloud/software costs rising with volume.

## Macro framing
> "We remain cautiously optimistic... the macro backdrop remains supportive and the consumer
> remains resilient, but the labor market is the key driver there. Business volumes, activity
> and pipelines all remain very [strong]"
They also flag the reverse scenario: "in the event of a sustained equity market sell-off,
revenue in a number of our capital markets-sensitive businesses would be challenged."

## Loan/deposit growth drivers named for 2026
"Healthy acquisition finance activity, strong infrastructure and AI-related spending, as well
as ongoing strength in securities-based lending and subscription finance."
=> NOTE for Part 3: "subscription finance" (lending against private-equity fund investor
commitments) and infrastructure/AI-datacentre finance are the named growth engines in
wholesale lending. Good, current, specific detail for interviews.
