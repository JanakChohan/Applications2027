# HireVue one-way video interviews: how answers are actually scored and reviewed (state as of November 2026)

Confidence tags used on every finding:
- **[OFFICIAL]** = HireVue's own documentation (Explainability Statement, help centre, press release, white paper) or a HireVue-commissioned audit. Official does not mean independently verified.
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** = journal article (note: the key paper is authored by HireVue staff).
- **[REPORTING]** = reputable press / law-firm / regulator publication.
- **[THIRD-PARTY]** = prep sites, forums, anecdote. Treat as unverified.
- **[HISTORICAL]** = describes pre-2021/2022 behaviour that HireVue says is no longer in use.

Two things a reader must know before anything else:
1. HireVue is two things at once: (a) a video-recording and human-review tool, and (b) an optional, separately licensed AI assessment layer. Many on-demand interviews are only ever watched by people.
2. HireVue currently runs two product lineages with different AI documentation: "HireVue Assessments" on the classic platform (HV4 / "HV Classic") and "AI scored" on the "Talent to Opportunity" (T2O) / "Hiring Experience Platform" lineage. Thresholds and recruiter screens differ between them.

## 1. What does HireVue's AI assessment analyse today? (transcript vs audio vs face)

### Takeaway
As officially documented since 2022 and restated in the 2025 Explainability Statement, the AI scores only the transcript of what the candidate says; it does not use video, facial expressions, body language, background, or tone of voice. Facial analysis (dropped from new models early 2020, announced January 2021) and speech/tone features (removal announced September 2021) are historical, although at least one stale HireVue help article still describes them.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Current statement of inputs: "our AI relies only on what is said by the candidate and does not use any video analysis or other audio characteristics (meaning that we do not assess a candidate's facial expressions, body language, their background and surroundings, or tone-of-voice)." — [HireVue 2025 AI Explainability Statement (PDF created May 2025)](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The pipeline has three stages: "(1) transcribing spoken words to text, (2) understanding the meaning of the text, and (3) assessing/scoring the candidate's answers based on expert human rater evaluations of answers to similar competency-based interview questions." — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Speech-to-text is done by a third party, Rev.ai, HireVue's "only supplier of AI components". HireVue reports English word error rates under 10% on average for Rev.ai in the US (7-10% across White, Black, Hispanic and Asian applicants) versus 15-30% for alternatives it tested; human transcription is put at roughly 5-10%. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The same "only what is said ... no video analysis or other audio characteristics ... or tone-of-voice" sentence already appears in the original (copyright 2022) statement, which HireVue re-uploaded in January 2026 as the "short form". — [HireVue Short-Form Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue's legal documentation site lists a "2025 version (version 2.0, effective June 16, 2025)" of the statement (taken from the site listing; page not opened). — [legal.hirevue.com HV4 Explainability Statement](https://legal.hirevue.com/product-documentation/hv-4-explainability-statement)
- **[OFFICIAL]** For the T2O "AI scored" product: question sets "may use AI to evaluate the words contained in the responses"; "Candidate responses are transcribed and scored by AI models". — [AI scored question sets](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490662475156-AI-scored-question-sets); [AI scored](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490688121748-AI-scored)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue's NYC Local Law 144 FAQ: "Interview-based assessments use the text transcription of the words candidates say in response to interview questions in order to rate candidates." — [NYC Law FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43071241107348-NYC-Law-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Candidate FAQ: "Hirevue does not use facial recognition to score candidates"; scoring "considers the whole context of a response, not just certain words here or there"; on eye contact, "do what feels most natural to you". — [HireVue candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** HireVue's own validation paper: "The current AVI-CAs were built using word-based features only (i.e., words and NLP features)... We explicitly excluded paraverbal (e.g., pitch, cadence, tone), and nonverbal (e.g., facial expressions) variables." — [Liff, Mondragon, Gardner, Hartwell & Bradshaw (2024), Journal of Applied Psychology 109(6)](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)

### Inferences
- For AI scoring, the transcript is the whole input. Anything the speech-to-text engine does not capture (smile, eye line, lighting, outfit, vocal warmth) cannot move the AI score; anything that degrades the transcript (mumbling, low volume, background hum, very fast speech) can.
- Human reviewers still watch the video, so delivery and appearance remain visible to people even though the algorithm ignores them. "Not analysed by AI" is not the same as "not judged".
- The contradictory classic-platform FAQ is the likely origin of many current "HireVue tracks your eyes/tone" claims on prep sites; it describes the pre-2021 product.
- An EU-law prohibition on emotion inference makes a return of facial/tone analysis for EU candidates legally implausible.

### Gaps
- No dated HireVue statement confirms that every legacy/custom model containing speech or visual features was retired; the 2021 blog says removal happens "as they come up for review". Whether any old custom model survived past 2022 is not publicly documented.
- The "25,000 data points" figure is unexplained in current documentation.
- I could not open the original Washington Post article directly (reprint used).

## 2. How are the AI models built, what competencies are scored, and what does the recruiter get?

### Takeaway
Each competency has its own language model (HireVue names 19) trained to reproduce the 1-5 ratings that trained human raters gave to real interview answers using behaviourally anchored rating scales (BARS, "Novice" to "Expert"). The recruiter receives competency levels plus an overall result that is relative to other candidates: a percentile "HireVue Score" shown as top/middle/bottom tiers on the classic platform, or a suggested rating / rank-ordered list on T2O.

### Cited Findings
**Competencies**
- **[OFFICIAL]** "There are 19 interview-based competencies which we can cover"; a sample named: "adaptability, problem solving, communication and willingness to learn". One CUSTARD model is fine-tuned per competency. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** The 15 competencies in the published validation: Adaptability, Communication, Compassion, Composure, Coordination of People and Resources, Dependability, Developing Others, Drive for Results/Initiative, Negotiation & Persuasion, Problem Solving, Relationship Building, Safety & Compliance Orientation, Service Orientation, Team Orientation, Willingness to Learn. — [Liff et al. 2024, Table 2](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL, audit]** The early-career/campus pre-built assessment audited by ORCAA generated scores in eight areas: "Communication, Team Orientation, Problem Solving, Willingness to Learn, Adaptability, Dependability, Drive for Results & Initiative, and Cognitive Ability" (the last from games). — [ORCAA audit description, 15 Dec 2020](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** T2O "AI scored question sets" use differently named competencies, e.g. for "Banking/Financial Services - Entry Level": Produces quality work; Works efficiently; Provides exceptional service; Communicates effectively; Works well with others. The experienced set includes "Adapts to change". Questions are explicitly STAR-shaped, e.g. "Describe a situation in which you had to adapt your style, approach, or ideas to meet the needs of others. What was the situation? What did you do in the situation, and what was the outcome?" — [AI scored question sets](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490662475156-AI-scored-question-sets)
- **[OFFICIAL]** December 2025 release notes refer to "all 19 areas of Hirevue's Capabilities + Skills Framework" for on-demand and live question libraries. — [December 2025 Quarterly Product Release Notes](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/44177928321556-December-2025-Quarterly-Product-Release-Notes)

**Training**
- **[OFFICIAL]** BARS "have five rating levels, from 'novice' to 'expert'". HireVue "trained diverse teams of around 60 expert raters"; "2-3 separate evaluators scoring each candidate's answer"; regular calibration; unreliable data filtered out "(for example where there were audio issues or insufficient words in a response)"; rater studies hold "99,411 expert ratings on video interviews across 19 competencies"; bias analyses sample from "over 900,000 applicant records". The rater-study sample is 75% North America, 9% Europe; 85% under 40; 77% non-manager. — [2025 Explainability Statement, incl. Appendix C](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Model: "CUSTARD", HireVue's fine-tune of RoBERTa, turns each transcribed answer into a 768-dimension embedding, which is fed to a "multipenalty optimized" regression trained to match human ratings while "reducing subgroup differences". Reported average correlation with expert ratings r = 0.69. Models are static per hiring cohort and updated "about once a year". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** Published detail: transcripts produced "binary bag-of-words predictors" plus "768 RoBERTa predictors", and "Both bag-of-words and RoBERTa predictors were used in all AVI-CA models"; ridge, lasso and elastic-net regression compared with 10-fold cross-validation. Raters received frame-of-reference training and "focused solely on the content of interview responses"; training questions had to follow "the Situation-Task-Action-Result interview question structure". Convergence with human ratings averaged r = .66 (range .55-.74); test-retest r = .72 (mean 43.5 days apart); criterion validity against job performance, uncorrected r = .24 across five organisations; subgroup differences small (Cohen's d no worse than -.14). — [Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf); summary stats also at [APA PsycNet DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001173)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** Communication is rated on every question, not via a dedicated question: "a rating for Communication occurred at the same time a rating for Adaptability was made". Communication scores correlated r̄ = .74 with all other competency scores, and the authors acknowledge "there is likely a high common method effect". — [Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** T2O "AI scored": built from "tens of thousands of candidate interview responses" rated with BARS by HireVue I-O psychologists; claims machine-human correlation averaging 0.77, trained on "tens of thousands of expert ratings and 1.5 million interview responses". — [The science behind AI scored](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490688965268-The-science-behind-AI-scored)
- **[OFFICIAL, audits]** ORCAA: "The assessments work as advertised with regard to fairness and bias issues", but "This was not a comprehensive audit" and did not cover custom assessments; ORCAA also found "Some candidates don't understand what the assessment is 'looking for'". Landers Workforce Science: "HireVue reaches or exceeds industry standards for the creation of high-stakes assessments". — [ORCAA](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf); [HireVue press release on Landers audit](https://www.hirevue.com/press-release/independent-audit-affirms-the-scientific-foundation-of-hirevue-assessments)

**What the recruiter sees (classic platform / "HireVue Assessments")**
- **[OFFICIAL]** "The HireVue score is a percentile ranking of each candidate's interview responses as compared to all other candidates that have been scored by this same assessment in this account." Scores are not recalculated later: "once a candidate has taken an interview and/or a game-based assessment, their score does not change." — [HireVue Score and Percentile Ranking FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169134508436-HireVue-Score-and-Percentile-Ranking-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Colour bands: "default Red = 0% to 33%, Yellow = 34% - 66%, and Green = 67% to 99%. The size, number, and name of these bands are customizable." And: "Often customers will use automated actions to advance or reject candidates based on their HireVue score and correlating with these color bands." — [HireVue Assessments FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070724859796-HireVue-Assessments-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The Candidate Report for Managers shows the score "as a percentile ranking: bottom tier, middle tier, or top tier" plus each measured competency on a Novice-to-Expert scale. Competency labels map to 1-5 but raw decimals (e.g. 4.1 vs 4.9, both "Advanced") drive the percentile. — [Understanding the Candidate Report for Managers](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070599963796-Understanding-the-Candidate-Report-for-Managers); [Score and Percentile Ranking FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169134508436-HireVue-Score-and-Percentile-Ranking-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue's recommended configuration: "Candidate Assessment Result Tiers (On/Off): Recommended On with Result Tier Labels Reflecting Client Use Case (e.g., Top/Middle/Bottom labels)" and "Competency and Assessment Numeric Score (On/Off): Recommended Off". Customers can weight competencies. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The 2022 workflow diagram: Top Tier (67-100%) "Recruiter to Advance candidate"; Middle Tier "Recruiter to view interview and Advance..., put on Hold, or Reject"; Low Tier (0-33%) "Recruiter to view interview (if volumes are insufficient) and put on Hold, or Reject accordingly"; Insufficient Data "Issue a retake". — [Short-Form (2022) Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL, audit]** In the audited early-career use case, "the competency scores are then used to rank the candidates, with the highest-ranked progressing to an in-person interview, the lowest-ranked being rejected, and the remainder referred to a human reviewer." — [ORCAA](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Scores take about 30 minutes to process after submission. — [HireVue Assessment Score Error Legend](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169163879828-HireVue-Assessment-Score-Error-Legend)

**What the recruiter sees (T2O "AI scored")**
- **[OFFICIAL]** Two modes. *Recommendations*: "AI models provide a suggested score for each question. After reviewing the candidate's response, reviewers can confirm or change the suggested score". *Ranked recommendations*: "AI models provide a final score for candidates on each question set. Candidates are ranked based on scores from highest to lowest. Reviewers cannot change candidate scores". — [AI scored comparison](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490645383316-AI-scored-comparison)
- **[OFFICIAL]** In ranked mode the AI "combines the competency scores from each question to form a single AI scored question set score", used "to stack rank candidates on the interview stage page". Candidates that could not be scored are listed first, marked "Unable to Score", for manual review. HireVue adds that scores "are not intended to be the sole or exclusive basis for candidate ratings or selection decisions". — [AI scored question sets](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490662475156-AI-scored-question-sets); [AI scored ranked recommendations](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490657158548-AI-scored-ranked-recommendations)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Candidates can be sent a Candidate Feedback Report; recommended setting is On. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)

### Inferences
- The score is norm-referenced: a candidate is ranked against other applicants to the same employer scored by the same model, at the moment of scoring. Applying early or late changes the comparison pool but HireVue does not re-rank.
- Because the model is trained to imitate human BARS ratings, the BARS is the real marking scheme. The AI is an approximation of "what a trained rater using this rubric would give", correlating about .66-.77 with those raters, i.e. far from perfectly.
- Because Communication is scored on every answer and correlates .74 with everything else, clarity and organisation of every answer feeds the overall result even when the question is nominally about teamwork or adaptability.
- A bottom-tier result on the classic platform can mean nobody watches the video (HireVue's own diagram makes viewing conditional for the low tier, and its FAQ confirms automated reject actions exist). Whether a given employer does this is an employer configuration choice.
- T2O "AI scored" looks like the scoring system inherited from Modern Hire (it sits alongside Virtual Job Tryout and uses different competency names and statistics from CUSTARD). HireVue does not state this lineage explicitly.

### Gaps
- The full list of 19 competencies is not published in the Explainability Statement; only the 15 in Liff et al. and the 8 in ORCAA are named.
- The Recruiter Report and Candidate Feedback Report examples (Appendix A) are images and could not be read.
- The Landers audit report itself was not opened; quotes are via HireVue's own summary.
- The 2025 statement no longer mentions bag-of-words features (the 2022 statement and Liff et al. do); whether they were removed from production models is not stated.

## 3. What does HireVue's own documentation say produces a high score?

### Takeaway
HireVue documents no formula of the "say X, score Y" kind. What is documented: scores track how closely the content of the answer matches the behaviours on the competency's BARS; there is a hard minimum of intelligible content below which an answer is not scored at all; and keyword stuffing is claimed to be ineffective because meaning is modelled in context. Nothing official says longer answers score higher beyond that minimum.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** The published Communication BARS (definition: "the ability to express ideas or a message in a clear and convincing manner"). Key behaviours with Novice vs Expert anchors include: *Delivers Clear & Concise Message* ("disorganized, lacks a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is not delivered in a logical sequence" vs "well organized, has a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is delivered in a logical sequence"); *Exhibits Professionalism*; *Uses Appropriate Communication Style*; *Shares Information* (novice "Interacts and shares information only when asked" vs expert "Encourages others by facilitating an environment that fosters sharing information"); *Verifies Understanding* (expert "proactively seeks feedback and follows up on the message to confirm correct interpretation"); *Engages Others* (expert "proactively seeking the ideas of others"); *Tailors Message to Audience*. — [2025 Explainability Statement, Appendix B](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Level meanings: Novice = "unlikely to be successful in situations requiring this competency"; Intermediate = "likely to demonstrate this competency well, but may need assistance in more difficult situations"; Expert = "with extreme effectiveness in moderate to complex situations". — [2025 Explainability Statement, Appendix B](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** How HireVue explains what drives scores: it perturbs inputs and measures score changes. "the word 'team' is a strong positive input for the teamwork model"; at phrase level, "'handle change very easily' and 'deal with challenges' are scored as important features in the adaptability model". Conclusion: "our models are well-aligned with the BARS used by human evaluators". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** On gaming: the model's ability to generalise "makes it more difficult for candidates to 'game' the video interview process by mentioning particular words or phrases". Candidate FAQ: "there's no course you can take that will help you game an AI-driven job assessment". HireVue blog: "You can't game the system – so don't try to keyword stuff." — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf); [candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq); [HireVue blog Q&A with CEO Kevin Parker](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/candidates/candidate-questions-answered-live-youtube-qa-with-hirevue-ceo-kevin-parker-and-andrew-lacivita)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** Counterpoint from HireVue's own paper: every published model included binary bag-of-words predictors (word present / absent) alongside contextual embeddings, and the authors note overlap between competencies arises "merely because the same word or NLP category appears in responses to different competency-based questions". — [Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Minimum content rule: "our AI system must detect enough content in a candidate's response... some may simply fail to give an adequately long answer or respond to a question in the allotted time, some candidates may fail to speak clearly enough to be understood, and some may have technical issues with their microphone". If not met, "the candidate's response will not be scored with AI and instead it will be flagged for human review." — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** T2O numeric thresholds: not scored "If the interview transcription is projected to be less than 70% accurate" or "If the candidate response was less than the minimum threshold of 150 characters". — [AI scored recommendations](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490672413844-AI-scored-recommendations)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Classic platform: causes of an unscoreable transcript are "Low volume audio; Background noise, especially a consistent hum; Not enough words spoken in their answer; Very strong accent; Unintelligible voice; Speaking in a different language than expected". One bad question fails the whole interview: "Any question that fails to meet our quality standards will cause the entire interview to fail with an Insufficient Data Error." Frequency: "On average, 1-2% of interviews". — [Insufficient Data Error (Thresholding) FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070693370132-Insufficient-Data-Error-Thresholding-FAQ)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** Typical answer length in the data the models learned from: "three minutes were allotted by the system as the total response time allowed per question and respondents averaged around one minute and forty-five seconds per response." — [Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Outliers are mis-scored: "Some candidates' answers will be so unique that the algorithm won't know how to score them... someone who provides a response that is overly technical or contains excessive industry jargon." HireVue also concedes "the model could be making a wrong prediction, either over OR under scoring candidates." — [HireVue Assessments FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070724859796-HireVue-Assessments-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Evidence that generic but on-topic content scores mid-range: "The average Hirevue candidate scores in the Intermediate range... (i.e., a score of 3 on a scale from Novice - 1 to Expert - 5). When given just the interview question with no further instructions, ChatGPT also scored in the range of an average candidate. When given better prompting, ChatGPT was able to achieve a score of 5, but in the vast majority of instances still scored in the average range" (ChatGPT 3.5; adaptability, problem solving, negotiation). — [HireVue white paper "Do Cheaters Prosper?"](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Accent/clarity: "There can be an impact on the accuracy of the transcription if the candidate is hard to understand... Candidates should be encouraged to speak as clearly as possible to maximize the accuracy of the speech transcription." — [Accuracy of the HireVue Assessment Score FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169148272148-Accuracy-of-the-HireVue-Assessment-Score-FAQ)
- **[REPORTING]** The Center for Democracy & Technology judged that the statement gave reasonable detail on video-interview model development but "mostly fails to explain what it does", especially for games and for disabled candidates. — [CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/hirevue-ai-explainability-statement-mostly-fails-to-explain-what-it-does/)

### Inferences
- The documented levers are all content levers: (1) describe concrete behaviours that sit on the target competency's rubric, (2) organise the answer in a logical sequence with a stated purpose, since Communication is rated on every question, (3) say enough clearly intelligible words to be scored at all.
- Competency vocabulary matters in the weak sense that naturally describing the behaviour uses the words the model has learned ("team", "deal with challenges"). HireVue's "keywords don't work" claim and its own bag-of-words features are in tension; the defensible reading is that relevant words used in a coherent behavioural account help, and isolated keyword lists are unlikely to.
- Since the questions and the training-set selection criteria are STAR-shaped, a Situation/Task/Action/Result answer is the format raters, and hence the model, were calibrated on.
- Highly technical or jargon-heavy answers are a documented risk for mis-scoring; plain-language behavioural description is safer for the algorithm.
- Because audio features are not used, pauses, filler words and pace have no documented direct effect on the AI score. Fillers are transcribed as words, and their effect on the score is undocumented.
- Test-retest reliability of .72 means the same person re-interviewed can land in a different tier; scores have real noise.

### Gaps
- No HireVue document reports the correlation between word count / answer length and score, or says whether using the full time limit helps. Liff et al. do not report it. This is the most important unanswered mechanical question.
- The Appendix B "example of how an interview answer can be scored along the response timeline" is a graphic and could not be extracted.
- The 150-character and 70%-accuracy thresholds are documented for T2O "AI scored"; the equivalent numeric thresholds for classic CUSTARD assessments are not published.
- BARS for competencies other than Communication are not public.
- Hickman et al. (2022) was located but not read in full; the SIOP summary says only that models trained on interviewer ratings (not self-reports) showed validity and that the authors urged caution. It studied Big Five personality in mock interviews, not HireVue's product. — [SIOP research brief](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/research-brief-of-hickman-et-al-s-2022-automated-video-interview-personality-assessments/)

### Inferences
- Detectable and documented: leaving the browser window/tab, pasting text, a different face appearing mid-interview, IP/VPN anomalies, ID mismatch, near-duplicate transcripts across candidates.

Claims on prep sites that "HireVue tracks your eyes" have no official support and conflict with HireVue's statement that no video analysis is used in scoring. A human reviewer can, however, simply see a candidate reading.
- "AI-generated answer detection" is asserted in 2026 marketing/trade coverage but its mechanism is undocumented. The documented behavioural signals (focus loss, paste) detect the act of fetching text, not whether spoken words originated from an LLM.
- HireVue's own data implies a generic LLM answer earns an average score, so the realistic penalty for a scripted generic answer is mediocrity plus the risk that a human reviewer notices reading.
- Interview Insights' metrics (talk times, question consistency, tagging by interviewers) suggest it is aimed mainly at live interviews; whether highlights/summaries are generated for on-demand recordings is not stated.

### Gaps
- The HireVue product page was summarised twice with conflicting results on whether it lists "AI detection" by name; the page should be re-read directly before quoting that phrase.

## 5. Candidate-side mechanics and what human reviewers can see and do

### Takeaway
Almost every candidate-facing parameter (prep time, answer time, retakes, number of questions, whether AI is used) is set by the employer, not HireVue. HireVue's own recommendation for AI-scored interviews is generous (unlimited retries, at least one minute of prep), but banks and graduate schemes are widely reported to use about 30 seconds of prep, 90-180 seconds of answer time, and one or few attempts. Only the last recording is kept; stopping early is explicitly allowed; reviewers can change playback speed, rate with stars and yes/maybe/no, and share videos with other evaluators.

### Cited Findings
**Timing, attempts, practice (official)**
- **[OFFICIAL]** Employer configuration options and HireVue's recommended values for AI-scored interviews: "Preparation Time for each Interview Question (0 - 5 minutes, or Unlimited Prep Time): No Restrictions on Preparation Time Recommended (1 Minute Minimum)"; "Interview Question Retries (Yes/No): Unlimited"; "Rating Guidelines... Recommended On and with 5 Star Guidelines (BARS) On"; "Data Retention: Recommended 2 years". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Typical AI-scored assessment: "4-6 video interview questions (delivered asynchronously) and 2-3 game questions... typically 15-25 minutes." — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf). Candidate FAQ: "Most assessments have five to eight questions", "several minutes to respond" per question, most take 20-30 minutes. — [candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq)
- **[OFFICIAL]** T2O: response time can be unlimited or a set limit; "When the time limit expires, the system ends recording and moves to the next question." Recording Mode: "The default is Multiple Retakes, which means that the candidate can re-record an answer an unlimited number of times. ### Inferences
- Stopping before the timer ends is a designed feature and carries no documented penalty; nothing in HireVue's documentation scores "time used". The only length-related rule is the minimum-content threshold for AI scoring.
- Because only the final take survives, re-recording replaces rather than adds; there is no documented way for a recruiter to view earlier takes.
- Reviewers jump between questions via per-question links and can change playback speed, so partial or accelerated viewing is technically easy. The specific speed options (1.5x, 2x) are not documented.
- When an employer uses HireVue's recommended "5 Star Guidelines (BARS)", human reviewers are rating against the same rubric the AI was trained on, so the same content strategy serves both.
- Where the employer has not licensed AI scoring, the evaluation is entirely the star ratings and yes/maybe/no recommendations of one or more people, averaged.
- The discrepancy between HireVue's recommended settings (unlimited retries, at least 1 minute prep) and the 30-second/one-attempt formats reported at banks suggests many finance employers deliberately run stricter, more "spontaneous" configurations.

### Gaps
- No official document states whether reviewers can see how many attempts a candidate used. It was listed as a future concept ("recording count") in HireVue's cheating white paper; current status unknown.
- No official statistic on how much of each video reviewers actually watch, or at what speed.
- The 30-second-prep figures for banks come from one news article without named sources and one self-described second-hand guide; no employer documentation was found.
- The classic-platform article on creating on-demand interviews confirms a setting for "the number of times the candidate has to re-record a video response" but its default prep/response values were not captured. — [Creating OnDemand Interviews](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43071336338580-Creating-OnDemand-Interviews)

## 6. Game-based assessments, Virtual Job Tryout, and coding assessments

### Takeaway
Games measure cognitive ability (and optionally Big Five personality) in 6-15 minutes and are scored by a regression on in-game performance; they are commonly bundled with the video questions into one overall score. Coding challenges (CodeVue) are scored on tests passed and time, with keystroke playback for reviewers. Virtual Job Tryout is the ex-Modern Hire simulation/situational-judgement assessment scored 1-5.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Games take "approximately 6-15 minutes" in total versus 30-45 minutes for traditional tests; "Each HireVue game takes only a few minutes". Cognitive games are adaptive: "If a candidate completes one level in a game, the next level... will be more difficult. If they fail a task, they will be given an easier one." Example: "Working Memory with our Digitspan game". "Portrait" measures Big Five traits "using images and adjectives". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Scoring inputs: "The candidate's score and other key game metrics including the ratio of levels lost and won, the total number of levels played, selected answers and/or highest level completed are fed into a ridge regression model", trained on panel participants who also took traditional cognitive tests. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Combination with video: for pre-built assessments, "Once we have scored each of the measured competencies, we use these scores to generate the overall HireVue score." HireVue notes that games let an employer "follow up with a person who has scored exceptionally well on the games but poorly on the interviews". — [HireVue Score and Percentile Ranking FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169134508436-HireVue-Score-and-Percentile-Ranking-FAQ); [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Candidates can opt out of AI-scored games "and be evaluated by other, non-AI scored elements". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** CodeVue report shows "The number of tests the candidate passed" and "The length of time the candidate took to finish each test". Reviewers get keystroke-by-keystroke playback where "The playback timeline... highlights when a candidate navigated away from the challenge", and a Code Similarity score "calculated based on the entire set of code submissions in the system". HireVue recommends following code with an on-demand video question asking the candidate to explain their answer. — [Candidate Report for Managers](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070599963796-Understanding-the-Candidate-Report-for-Managers); [Discouraging and Detecting Plagiarism](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169248282004-Discouraging-and-Detecting-Plagiarism); [Do Cheaters Prosper?](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Virtual Job Tryouts include "situational judgment, work history, work style, and simulation items", scored 1-5 and "meant to last 20 minutes"; a recruiter card example reads "VJT assessment score of 4 (Excellent Fit)"; the Candidate Profile Report has sections "Overall Fit", "Competency Fit", "Work History Probes". ChatGPT 3.5 scored 1-2 on VJTs in HireVue's test. — [Do Cheaters Prosper?](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf); [Rating and review](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490682567572-Rating-and-review)
- **[REPORTING]** CDT criticised the Explainability Statement for giving "virtually no explanation" of how game-based assessments were devised, operated or tested. — [CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/hirevue-ai-explainability-statement-mostly-fails-to-explain-what-it-does/)

### Inferences
- Where games are bundled, a strong or weak game result moves the same overall percentile/tier as the video answers; the video is not scored in isolation.
- Requesting a disability accommodation on a game-bearing assessment removes the games and routes the candidate to manual review, which changes how they are evaluated.

### Gaps
- The weighting between game scores and interview-competency scores in the overall HireVue Score is not published (employers can weight competencies).
- The full current list of HireVue games and what each records was not retrieved.

## 7. Regulation: does it make UK/EU employers more or less likely to use automated scoring?

### Takeaway
Regulation has not banned AI scoring of interviews anywhere relevant, but it has added consent, notice, audit and human-oversight duties that fall on the employer. In the EU the high-risk obligations for recruitment AI were postponed to 2 December 2027; emotion inference in recruitment is already banned. The practical effect in the UK/EU is that AI scoring, where used, comes with a visible consent/opt-out screen and human review; no source quantifies how many employers switch it off.

### Cited Findings
- **[REPORTING]** EU AI Act: recruitment and selection AI stays in the high-risk category. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) was published 24 July 2026 and entered into force 27 July 2026, moving application of high-risk obligations for Annex III systems (which include employment) from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act); [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/technology/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-formally-adopted/); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/rules-high-risk-ai-delayed-under-eu-omnibus-deal)
- **[REPORTING]** Critics note that systems placed on the market before December 2027 may largely escape the new rules. — [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/eus-ai-act-delays-let-highrisk-systems-dodge-oversight)
- **[REPORTING]** The Article 5 prohibition on emotion recognition in the workplace (including recruitment) has applied since 2 February 2025, with fines up to EUR 35 million or 7% of turnover; it covers inference of emotions from biometric data. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue positions itself as a GDPR "processor" with the employer as "controller", and "as a 'provider' under the EU AI Act". It states its platform "does not make recruitment decisions" and that challenges must go to the hiring company. — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The classic platform has a dedicated status "Opted Out of Assessments - GDPR: Candidate has requested to be evaluated without a HireVue Assessment." — [Score Error Legend](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169163879828-HireVue-Assessment-Score-Error-Legend)
- **[REPORTING / regulator]** UK ICO, 6 November 2024: after consensual audits of AI recruitment tool providers (August 2023 to May 2024) it made "almost 300 recommendations", all accepted or partially accepted. Findings: some tools let recruiters filter out candidates with protected characteristics, inferred gender and ethnicity from names, and collected far more data than necessary and kept it indefinitely. The ICO published key questions for employers procuring such tools. Law-firm summaries (read via search-result summaries, not opened in full) note the audit excluded tools processing biometric data and stressed human oversight so tools are not relied on for automated decisions they were not designed for. — [ICO news release](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2024/11/ico-intervention-into-ai-recruitment-tools-leads-to-better-data-protection-for-job-seekers); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/ico-audit-on-ai-recruitment-tools/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2024/11/27/ai-in-recruitment-icos-recommendations-and-key-questions)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue's first Explainability Statement was reviewed by the UK ICO before release (per HireVue). — [HireVue press release, 2022](https://www.hirevue.com/press-release/hirevue-launches-ai-explainability-statement-in-hr-industry-first)
- **[OFFICIAL]** NYC Local Law 144: requires an independent bias audit within the prior year, notice to candidates at least 10 days before use, disclosure of characteristics evaluated, and allowing candidates "to request an alternative selection process or accommodation". HireVue: the law applies when "filling a position using HireVue's interview- or game-based assessment technology", and "would not apply to customers only using Video Interviewing or HireVue Builder products." — [HireVue NYC Law FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43071241107348-NYC-Law-FAQ)
- **[REPORTING]** Illinois: the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act applies to employers using AI to analyse video interviews for Illinois-based positions. Separately, HB 3773 (amending the Illinois Human Rights Act, effective 1 January 2026) requires notice when AI is used in recruitment/hiring decisions and bars discriminatory AI use; it does not require bias audits. — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/illinois-steps-up-ai-regulation-in-employment-key-takeaways-for-employers/); [SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/tools/express-requests/illinois-amends-the-human-rights-act-to-limit-the-use-of-predict); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/illinois-anti-discrimination-law-address-ai-goes-effect-1-january-2026)
- **[REPORTING]** UK graduate employers: the Institute of Student Employers' 2023 survey found 28% of employers using AI in hiring, up from 9% in 2022; analysing video interviews was among the common uses; about three quarters of employers reported worry about bias. ISE also reports reasoning tests are far more often fully automated (numerical 57%, situational judgement 53%, verbal 49%). — [ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insight/180/employers_using_ai_to_recruit_graduates_and_apprentices_triples); [ISE insight](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insight/505/how_are_employers_using_ai_in_early_careers_recruitment); [HRreview](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/future-of-work-hr-news/employers-using-ai-in-hiring-triples/373489)

### Inferences
- UK/EU regulation pushes toward "AI assists, human decides": consent screens, opt-out to human rating, and middle-tier human review are how HireVue and its customers avoid solely automated decision-making. A UK/EU candidate who is AI-scored should therefore see an explicit AI consent page; absence of one is good evidence that only humans will evaluate the video.
- The compliance burden (data protection impact assessments, transparency, adverse-impact monitoring, from December 2027 the AI Act deployer duties) is a reason for some EU/UK employers to license video interviewing without AI scoring, but high-volume graduate recruiters have the strongest efficiency incentive to keep it. No data found settles the net direction.
- The delay of EU high-risk obligations to December 2027 removes the near-term deadline that might have prompted employers to switch AI scoring off for the 2026-27 recruiting cycle.
- Opting out is a legally protected route to human review, but an opted-out candidate is visibly tagged as such to the recruiter.

### Gaps
- Details of the Illinois AI Video Interview Act (consent, explanation, deletion on request) were not verified against the statute in this research.
- Changes to UK automated-decision-making rules under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 were not researched.
- No ISE or CIPD figure more recent than 2023 was found for the share of UK employers that AI-score video interviews specifically.
- A claim seen in search results that "some enterprise legal teams have opted to disable AI scoring" could not be traced to a reliable source.

## 8. What fraction of HireVue customers or interviews actually use AI scoring?

### Takeaway
HireVue has never published a share of customers. Its own statements point to a clear minority: a HireVue blog says "only about 1 in 5" interviews use an algorithm, and HireVue's 2022 figures (5 million AI assessments against 26 million video interviews) give the same ratio. Most on-demand videos are evaluated only by humans.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Only about 1 in 5 HireVue interviews actually use an algorithm", and such interviews "always come with a disclaimer". (HireVue blog summarising a Q&A with then-CEO Kevin Parker; undated on the page.) — [HireVue blog](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/candidates/candidate-questions-answered-live-youtube-qa-with-hirevue-ceo-kevin-parker-and-andrew-lacivita)
- **[OFFICIAL]** 2022 statement: "more than 26 million video interviews and 5 million AI-based candidate assessments for over 700 customers". — [Short-Form (2022) Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** 2025 statement: "more than 40 million video interviews and over 200 million candidate assessments for over 1100 customers". — [2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- **[REPORTING]** October 2019: "More than 100 employers now use the system" (the AI assessment), with more than a million job seekers analysed. — [Washington Post via Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2019/10/22/face-scanning-algorithm-increasingly-decides-whether-you-deserve-the-job/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue help text treats human video review as the norm even among assessment customers: "For customers that review videos (most), interviews with Insufficient Data Error should be manually reviewed." — [Insufficient Data Error FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070693370132-Insufficient-Data-Error-Thresholding-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL]** AI scoring is a separate licence: "AI scored is a licensed application feature and may not be available for your application." — [AI scored](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490688121748-AI-scored)
- **[OFFICIAL]** HireVue distinguishes "customers only using Video Interviewing" from assessment customers for legal purposes. — [NYC Law FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43071241107348-NYC-Law-FAQ)
- **[OFFICIAL, conflicting]** Customer counts: "over 1,150" customers and "over 60% of the Fortune 100" in a 2025 press release, versus "1,000+" and "40% of Fortune 100" on the customers page. — [HireVue 2025 AI report press release](https://www.hirevue.com/press-release/hirevues-2025-ai-report); [HireVue customers page](https://www.hirevue.com/our-customers)

### Inferences
- 5 million / 26 million is about 19%, matching the "1 in 5" statement; roughly 100 of about 700 customers in 2019-2022 is about 15% of customers. Both imply roughly four in five HireVue on-demand interviews of that era were human-reviewed only.
- The jump to "200 million candidate assessments" by 2025 almost certainly reflects the addition of Modern Hire's Virtual Job Tryout volumes and games, not a tenfold rise in AI-scored video interviews; it cannot be used to update the 1-in-5 figure.
- The reliable per-interview test is observable: an AI-scored interview must show an AI consent/opt-in screen (HireVue design, plus Illinois/NYC/GDPR duties). No consent screen means human review only.

### Gaps
- No current (2025-2026) figure from HireVue or journalists for the share of customers or interviews using AI scoring. The "1 in 5" blog is undated and predates the Modern Hire merger.
- No breakdown by sector (e.g. investment banks, asset managers) or by region (UK/EU vs US) was found. Which specific finance employers license AI scoring is not publicly documented.
