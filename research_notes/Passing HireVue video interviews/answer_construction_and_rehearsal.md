# Answer construction and rehearsal for one-way recorded video interviews (finance roles)

Reading key for source quality used throughout:
- **[PRIMARY]** = official/primary document (government scoring guide, HireVue's own statement, a university careers service page, a peer-reviewed study or its official summary).
- **[TRADE]** = trade press / long-running industry site with no direct product to sell on the page (eFinancialCareers) or with a product but long track record (Mergers & Inquisitions — sells courses; treat as informed but commercial).
- **[VENDOR]** = paid-coach / prep-product marketing content. Weighted lower.
- **(snippet)** = I only saw the search-engine extract, the page itself was not fetched (several sites returned HTTP 403: igotanoffer, Wall Street Oasis, Bath careers blog). Treat as unverified detail.

Research date: 26 Nov 2026.

## 1. Answer frameworks and how they map to marking (STAR/CAR/STAR-L, PREP, answer-first, time proportions, real marking schemes)

### Takeaway
Published marking schemes reward the same four things: evidence of what *you personally* did ("I", not "we"), enough specific action detail to count as "positive evidence", an outcome, and (for top marks) something that "exceeds expectations at this level" plus lessons learned; careers services convert that into a time budget of roughly Situation 20% / Task 10% / Action 60% / Result 10%. Where the machine scores (HireVue), it scores the *transcript only* against a behaviourally anchored scale whose top band is defined by organisation, clear purpose and logical sequence — which favours an explicitly structured, answer-first spoken answer.

### Cited Findings

**Time proportions inside STAR**
- MIT Career Advising gives explicit shares: Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60% ("should be most of your answer"), Result 10% (outcomes, ideally measurable, plus what you learned). — [PRIMARY: MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)
- University of Washington careers lists the same Task 10% / Action 60% split (snippet). — [PRIMARY: UW Careers](https://careers.uw.edu/resources/star-method-of-interviewing/)
- A resume-tool vendor translates this into seconds for a 90-second answer: ~10s Situation, ~10s Task, ~45s Action, ~15s Result (snippet). — [VENDOR: Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/star-method-generator)
- Disagreement on Result: Built In says Result should be no more than ~10% of the answer (snippet), MIT also says 10%, but the Civil Service scheme explicitly says panels are "interested in the outcome and any lessons learned" and Resume Worded gives Result ~17%. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/star-method); [PRIMARY: COPFS Success Profiles page](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)
- MIT's listed common mistakes: saying "we" so much that individual contribution is unclear; vague/generalised answers instead of a specific example; saying what you think will impress rather than what happened; "memorizing scripted stories, which can limit your flexibility and sound unnatural". — [PRIMARY: MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)

**Real marking scheme 1 — UK Civil Service Success Profiles 7-point scale (verbatim descriptors as published by the Crown Office & Procurator Fiscal Service, which uses the standard Civil Service scale)**
- 1 Not Demonstrated: "No positive evidence and/ or substantial negative evidence demonstrated"
- 2 Minimal Demonstration: "Limited positive evidence and/ or mainly negative evidence demonstrated"
- 3 Moderate Demonstration: "Moderate positive evidence but some negative evidence demonstrated"
- 4 Acceptable Demonstration: "Adequate positive evidence and any negative evidence would not cause concern"
- 5 Good Demonstration: "Substantial positive evidence of the behaviour"
- 6 Strong Demonstration: "Substantial positive evidence; includes some evidence of exceeding expectations at this level"
- 7 Outstanding Demonstration: "The evidence provided wholly exceeds expectation at this level"
— [PRIMARY: COPFS, "How your application is assessed – Success Profiles"](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)
- Pass benchmark there: 4 or more in all behaviours; a single 3 may be accepted if others are high; a panel can set a higher bar (example given: 5 on lead behaviours, or 20 across four behaviours). — [PRIMARY: COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)
- Same page's instructions to candidates: structure interview answers with STAR; say "I" rather than "we" so the panel can see what you personally did; be ready for probing follow-ups; panels are interested in the outcome and lessons learned. — [PRIMARY: COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)
- Cabinet Office guidance to recruiters: "Experience, technical skills and behaviours should be scored using the 7 point scale. Strengths should be scored using the 4 point scale. Panel members should observe, record, classify, evaluate and score each question independently," then reconcile by arithmetic average. — [PRIMARY: gov.uk, Guidance – Application of Success Profile Guides (EO–Grade 6)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f746b1ae90e0740caae75a7/Guidance-Application_of_Success_Profile_Guides_during_delegated_grade__EO-Grade_6__recruitment_v0b.pdf)
- Anecdotal calibration: an HMRC careers-forum responder says the number of candidates scoring 7 is "miniscule" across several thousand monthly interviews (snippet; forum post, not official statistics). — [HMRC careers forum](https://careers.hmrc.gov.uk/discussions/how-are-people-scoring-7-in-the-interview-questions-for-customer-service-advisor)

**Real marking scheme 2 — HireVue's own Behaviourally-Anchored Rating Scale (BARS), published as Appendix B of its Explainability Statement (example competency: Communication)**
- Five proficiency levels: Novice ("unlikely to be successful in situations requiring this competency"), Developing, Intermediate ("likely to demonstrate this competency well, but may need assistance in more difficult situations"), Advanced, Expert ("with extreme effectiveness in moderate to complex situations"). — [PRIMARY: HireVue Explainability Statement (short form), Appendix B](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- "Delivers clear & concise message" anchors: Novice = "Message delivered is disorganised, lacks a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is not delivered in a logical sequence"; Intermediate = "reasonably organised, has a clear purpose and importance, and is delivered in a logical sequence"; Expert = "well organised, has clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is delivered in a logical sequence." — [PRIMARY: HireVue, Appendix B](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- Other key behaviours on the same scale: uses proper grammar/"pace, volume, diction"; shares information (Expert: "proactively seeks improvement in communication skills… encourages others"); verifies understanding (Expert: "proactively seeks feedback and follows up… to confirm correct interpretation"); engages others (Novice: "excessively dominates group discussions"; Expert: "proactively seeking the ideas of others"); tailors message to audience. — [PRIMARY: HireVue, Appendix B](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- What the algorithm reads: "our AI relies only on what is said by the candidate and does not use any video analysis or other audio characteristics (meaning that we do not assess a candidate's facial expressions, body language, their background and surroundings, or tone-of-voice)." Answers are transcribed, then analysed by NLP; the models are trained on human evaluators' BARS ratings. — [PRIMARY: HireVue Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- Output is a tier, and a human still decides: Top tier 67–100% ("Recruiter to advance candidate"), Middle 36–66% ("Recruiter to view interview and advance, hold or reject"), Low 0–33% ("Recruiter to view interview (if volumes are insufficient) and put on hold, or reject"); "Insufficient Data" triggers a retake; "Final decision… taken entirely by the hiring company." — [PRIMARY: HireVue Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- Sample competencies HireVue says it can test: "adaptability, problem solving, communication and willingness to learn"; CDT's review notes HireVue claims 20+ competencies but does not publish the list or how each is measured. — [PRIMARY: HireVue](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf); [Center for Democracy & Technology critique (snippet)](https://cdt.org/insights/hirevue-ai-explainability-statement-mostly-fails-to-explain-what-it-does/)
- Caveat: the PDF pages carry a 2022 copyright line although the file sits under a 2026/01 path and the landing page lists a "version 2.0"; individual banks decide whether to switch AI scoring on at all, and that is not disclosed per bank. — [HireVue legal landing page](https://legal.hirevue.com/product-documentation/hv-4-explainability-statement)

**Real marking scheme 3 — strengths-based interviews (used by Barclays, EY and others)**
- Civil Service scores strengths on a separate 4-point scale, not the 7-point evidence scale. — [PRIMARY: gov.uk guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f746b1ae90e0740caae75a7/Guidance-Application_of_Success_Profile_Guides_during_delegated_grade__EO-Grade_6__recruitment_v0b.pdf)
- Manchester careers: strengths interviewers look at "choice of language, facial expression, body language and sense of enthusiasm"; questions are deliberately open (snippet). — [PRIMARY: University of Manchester Careers](https://www.careers.manchester.ac.uk/applicationsinterviews/interviews/types/strengthsrecruit/)
- Warwick careers publishes a strengths scorecard that rewards "authenticity" (genuine enjoyment, not forced) and "compelling detail to describe specific actions" (snippet). — [PRIMARY: Warwick Careers blog](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/careers/blog/how_to_answer_strengths_based_questions_in_interviews)

**Answer-first / pyramid / PREP**
- Pyramid Principle (Minto): one governing thought at the top, 2–3 supporting arguments below; one guide notes the practical benefit that leading with the main point means it still lands if you are interrupted (snippet). — [VENDOR: Management Consulted](https://managementconsulted.com/pyramid-principle-explained/)
- Disagreement: StrategyCase argues answer-first suits analytical answers but not personal stories — opening a leadership story with "I'm a strong leader for three reasons" "falls flat"; stories need a narrative arc. CaseBasix argues a top-down behavioural answer (conclusion, 2–3 key actions, result/learning) works (snippets; both vendors). — [VENDOR: StrategyCase](https://strategycase.com/the-pyramid-principle-case-interview/); [VENDOR: CaseBasix](https://www.casebasix.com/pages/pyramid-principle-behavioral-answers)
- PREP = Point, Reason, Example, Point: start and end with the main point; stock connectors "I would say… because… for example… that's why" (snippet; personal blog, low authority). — [Blog: canro91](https://canro91.github.io/2025/09/13/Answering)
- Commercial-question shape from a finance prep site: "What happened → Why it matters → Where it could flow next", 45–60 seconds on camera (snippet). — [VENDOR: Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/hirevue-finance-interview-tips-guide)

### Inferences
- Mapping the Civil Service scale to behaviour: a 4 is a complete, credible STAR with nothing worrying in it; a 5 needs *volume and specificity* of positive evidence ("substantial"); 6–7 require evidence of operating above the level expected — for a student that means scale, stakes, initiative beyond the brief, or an unusually quantified result plus reflection. A mid-score answer is typically one that is true and structured but thin on personal actions.
- Because the HireVue algorithm (where enabled) only sees the transcript, verbal signposting ("The situation was… What I did was three things: first… The result was…") is not a stylistic nicety: it is the only way "logical sequence" and "clear purpose" become visible in text. Conversely, tone, eye contact and energy matter only for the human reviewer who watches middle-tier (and some top/low-tier) videos.
- A workable hybrid that reconciles the pyramid disagreement: one headline sentence that answers the question and names the story ("The clearest example is when I led X and we did Y"), then STAR with the 20/10/60/10 budget, then one line of learning/link to role. This front-loads the conclusion without turning a story into a list of abstract claims.
- For a 2-minute limit used at ~100 s: Situation ≈ 20 s, Task ≈ 10 s, Action ≈ 60 s, Result+learning ≈ 10–15 s.

### Gaps
- No Goldman Sachs / JP Morgan / Morgan Stanley / Barclays *internal* HireVue marking guide is public; I found no bank-published assessor rubric. The Big Four strengths frameworks are described by universities but the firms' scoring sheets are not published.
- I could not retrieve the Civil Service 4-point strengths scale descriptors verbatim.
- The "candidates overspend on Situation" claim is ubiquitous in careers advice but I found no empirical study measuring it; it rests on careers-service experience (MIT/UW percentages).
- CAR, STARR/STAR-L and SOAR variants: nothing found that shows assessors score them differently from STAR; they appear to be relabelings that add Reflection/Learning or rename Task as Obstacle.

## 2. The finance HireVue question bank (2023–2026) and how many stories cover it

### Takeaway
Reported bank HireVue questions cluster into about seven categories, dominated by motivation ("why this bank/division/city") and a small set of behavioural themes (failure, teamwork/conflict, leadership, integrity/"doing the right thing", initiative, persistence, prioritisation), with one markets/deal question typical and light technicals occasional. Expert story-count recommendations range from 3 (M&I) to 3–5 (MIT) to 5–8 (general behavioural-interview guides); for the bank lists below, about six well-chosen stories, each tagged to 3–4 themes, cover every behavioural prompt.

### Cited Findings

**Format facts**
- 3–5 on-screen questions, 30 s prep, 90 s answer, 10–15 minutes total; invitation typically arrives right after applying with a few days to complete. — [TRADE: Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- "You'll have 30 seconds to prepare your answer, and either 90 or 120 seconds to deliver it" at most banks; buy-side reverses it — Blackstone 180 s prep / 30 s answer, BlackRock 180 s prep / 90 s answer (article dated 14 Jan 2025). — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/how-long-to-answer-hirevue-interview)
- Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley: 30 s prep, 90 s answer; BlackRock 180 s prep, 90 s answer (article dated 9 Sep 2026). — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)
- Vendor table (lower confidence, internally inconsistent on JPM): Goldman 3–6 questions/30 s prep/~2 min; JP Morgan 4–6 questions/30 s/1–3 min; Morgan Stanley 3–5/30 s/1–2 min; Bank of America 3–5/30 s/1–2 min. — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)
- JPMorgan's own careers page says recorded responses are valid for 6 months so a candidate may not need to retake for similar roles (snippet). — [PRIMARY: JPMorgan careers – interview tips](https://careers.jpmorgan.com/in/en/how-we-hire/interview-tips)

- MIT CAPD: prepare 3–5 stories based on the skills in the job description, outlined as bullets, each adaptable to different questions. — [PRIMARY: MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)

### Inferences
- Collapsing the eFC 2026 and 2023 lists gives these recurring behavioural themes: (1) failure/struggle/regret + learning, (2) teamwork incl. a struggling team, (3) conflict/difficult colleague/challenging authority, (4) leadership, (5) integrity/"doing the right thing"/confidentiality, (6) initiative/proactive problem-solving/process improvement, (7) persistence/pressure/prioritisation, (8) adapting/learning something new/unfamiliar project, (9) persuasion/explaining complex information simply, (10) diversity/multicultural teams. A 6-story × 10-theme matrix where each story is tagged to 3–4 themes gives ≥2 options per theme; M&I's three stories is the minimum viable set and leaves integrity, persuasion and D&I uncovered.
- Integrity is a visibly over-represented theme in 2026 bank lists (Goldman confidentiality ×2, JPM and Morgan Stanley "doing the right thing"), and Goldman's are *hypothetical* (situational-judgement style) rather than past-behaviour questions — so candidates need a rehearsed decision procedure (stop, contain, escalate/tell manager and compliance, do not conceal) as well as a past story.
- Non-story prepared items needed alongside the matrix: CV walk-through; why bank / division / city; strengths ×3, weaknesses ×3; one deal, one macro/market view, one stock or investment pitch, one "recent thing I read"; and 3–4 one-line technical definitions (DCF, WACC, yield curve, three statements).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch igotanoffer or Wall Street Oasis pages (403), so candidate-level question reports for asset managers and hedge funds (beyond BlackRock/Blackstone timing) are thin; no 2024–2026 question list specific to hedge funds or sales & trading desks was verified.
- eFC lists are crowd-sourced from Glassdoor/WSO/Reddit and paraphrased; none is confirmed by the banks.
- No published bank competency list was verified in this pass (e.g. JPMorgan "behaviours", Barclays values) — a JPM summary naming "teamwork, analytical skills, drive for results, presentation skills" appeared only as an unverified secondary snippet.

## 3. "Why this firm" and commercial-awareness answers: what separates strong from generic

### Takeaway
The consistent test is substitution: if the bank's name can be swapped and the answer still works, it is generic. Strong answers are built from (a) named people with title and office, (b) a specific deal with parties, size and the bank's role, and (c) an explicit link to the candidate's own background; strategy claims are riskier because they invite challenge.

### Cited Findings
- M&I calls the question "dumb" because banks are similar, but says the answer matters because the job is often "more about not screwing up than doing anything right"; a dumb answer can ruin an otherwise good interview. — [TRADE: M&I, "Why our bank?"](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)
- M&I's weak answers: liking "the culture" ("way too vague"); "the people are smart and hard-working"; "I applied everywhere" (worst); "exposure to bigger and more complex deals" (boutique→bulge) and "more hands-on client interaction" (bulge→boutique) are "less bad but still too generic". — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)
- M&I's strong-answer order: People (named contacts, titles, offices, how you met, e.g. "a VP in the New York office" at an information session) → Deals (a recent deal the bank advised on, with companies and size) → Your interest (tie to your sector interest or experience, e.g. "I'm really interested in oil, gas, and mining"). — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)
- M&I detail rules: senior names help more but any name works; contacts in another office or who have left can still be named; for "why this group" cite that group's people and deals; don't cite M&A deals for a division that doesn't do M&A; never invent names or meetings; if you have no contacts, discuss strategy (expansions, policies that differ from peers) but expect to be challenged; for middle-market banks, "more deal execution volume" alone only explains why *any* middle-market bank. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)
- Swap test and deal depth: "if your answer would work just as well for Goldman or Morgan Stanley with a name swap, it's not specific enough"; for any deal you name, know acquirer, target, deal value and the bank's role, and expect follow-ups (snippet). — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/why-this-bank-specifically-answer)
- Weak vs strong contrast published for Deutsche Bank: weak = "Deutsche Bank is a leading European investment bank with a strong global presence."; strong = a dated, sourced claim (2025 profit, a named award, quarter-on-quarter advisory revenue change) (snippet; Exponent article reposted by a university career site). — [University of Miami career site repost of Exponent](https://customcareer.miami.edu/blog/2026/04/08/how-to-answer-why-deutsche-bank-interview-question-2026-guide/)
- Same family of guides: "If you admire the strategy, explain what the strategy is. Vague admiration signals you do not have specific reasons." (snippet). — [VENDOR: Exponent](https://www.tryexponent.com/blog/why-bank-of-america-interview-question)
- Commercial awareness (law careers source, transferable): "A strong answer will identify a specific news piece and make an explicit link to your interviewing firm by drawing upon knowledge of their operations." (snippet). — [The Lawyer Portal](https://www.thelawyerportal.com/blog/commercial-awareness-interview-questions-and-answers/)
- Finance prep site: pick macro or corporate/deal stories, avoid stories you barely understand; prepare a 60-second answer with 45- and 90-second versions spoken out loud, and a division-specific "landing line"; hold a view while acknowledging uncertainty (snippets). — [VENDOR: Finbound news-story guide](https://www.finbound.org/blog/news-story-finance-interview-answer-guide); [VENDOR: Finbound commercial awareness guide](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)
- Evidence that banks ask firm-specific commercial questions on HireVue: "recent news affecting that division" (JPM), "effect of economic news on the bank" and "a tech trend Citi should adopt" (Citi), "a recent M&A deal and your view of it" (UBS), "Tell me about a current news story involving [our firm]" (Cambridge list). — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking); [PRIMARY: Cambridge Careers](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/files/example_interview_questions_for_banking_and_financial_markets.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 90-second "why this firm" template consistent with the sources: headline (one sentence, two reasons) → person evidence (name, title, office, one thing they said that changed your view) → deal/strategy evidence (parties, size, bank's role, why it interested you) → link back to own experience and the specific division → one-line close. Each element should fail the name-swap test.
- For commercial questions the scoring-relevant move is the *link and the view*: what happened (1–2 sentences with a number), why it matters (mechanism), what it means for this firm/division or its clients, your view and what would change your mind. Because several banks now ask about news affecting "the division you applied to", the landing line must be division-specific and prepared in advance.
- The same deal can do double duty ("why this bank" evidence and "a deal you followed"), which reduces prep volume.

### Gaps
- No quotes from named bank recruiters or current bankers were verified; the strongest material is from M&I (ex-banker, commercial) and vendors. Canary Wharfian, Bright Network and AmplifyME material did not surface in searches.
- No published example of a full weak-vs-strong *commercial awareness* answer transcript from an authoritative (non-vendor) source.

## 4. Timing: answer length vs time allowed, words per minute, being cut off

### Takeaway
Experts disagree on how much of the window to use: M&I targets ~150 words in 90 seconds (100–125 wpm) and says finishing a 2-minute slot in 1 minute is fine, while vendor guides say use 80–90% of the limit. All agree to finish before the limit; nothing authoritative describes the cut-off mechanics, so structure should make the last 15 seconds expendable.

### Cited Findings
- "Aim for 100-125 words per minute"; in a 90-second answer the maximum is "180-190 words (ideally closer to 150)". — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- M&I's author says (comments) that finishing a 2-minute answer in 1 minute is fine. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- Aim for 80–90% of available time; for a 90-second limit finish around 75–80 seconds; "tell me about yourself" and STAR examples 60–90 seconds. — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)
- Commercial/markets answers: 45–60 seconds on camera (snippet). — [VENDOR: Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/hirevue-finance-interview-tips-guide)
- One coaching site: "walk me through your resume" ~2 minutes, all other behavioural answers ~1 minute (snippet). — [VENDOR: IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/investment-banking-hirevue-interviews)
- LSE: recorded-interview answers are "commonly 30-90 seconds"; "there is usually a time limit imposed"; "there is usually no way to stop and restart the recording"; watch the on-screen timer but "try not to look at the clock too often"; speak more slowly, loudly and clearly than usual. — [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- eFC: the timing "might seem tight, and it is." Blackstone gives only 30 seconds to answer after 180 seconds of prep. — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/how-long-to-answer-hirevue-interview)
- Wharton: keep a professional expression and keep looking at the camera for a few seconds after finishing — "You won't know exactly when the recording stops." — [PRIMARY: Wharton Communication Program](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- General speaking-rate reference point: coaching articles on filler words cite ~150 wpm as an average professional speaking rate (snippet only; I could not confirm which of the two articles below carries the figure, and the original study was not located — low confidence). — [Toastmasters magazine, Sept 2017](https://toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2017/september-2017/umah); [Medium / Fusion Accelerator post](https://medium.com/fusion-accelerator/like-um-and-ah-discover-simple-tactics-to-identify-and-avoid-the-not-so-silent-killers-86f73aac041f)
- HireVue's AI can return "Insufficient Data", which triggers a retake rather than a score — i.e. there is a floor on how little usable speech an answer can contain. — [PRIMARY: HireVue Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Word budgets at M&I's 100–125 wpm: 30 s ≈ 50–60 words (Blackstone); 60 s ≈ 100–125; 90 s ≈ 150–190; 120 s ≈ 200–250. Using the vendor 80–90% rule on a 2-minute limit gives a 96–108 s target ≈ 170–220 words. Script-drafts longer than this cannot be delivered at a natural pace.
- The disagreement (short and tight vs 80–90%) is reconcilable: M&I optimises for a bored human screener; the 80–90% rule gives a transcript-scoring model more evidence. A defensible default is 75–90% of the limit for behavioural stories, 50–70% for motivation and markets answers.
- To make a cut-off costless: headline answer in the first sentence; Result stated before the reflection; the final sentence should be optional (learning / link to role). If the timer shows ~15 s left mid-Action, jump straight to "The result was…".
- For Blackstone-style 30-second answers the structure collapses to PREP: point, one reason, one fact, restate.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found on what the platform does at time-out (hard stop mid-sentence vs warning), or on how assessors treat a truncated answer.
- No empirical evidence found linking answer length to HireVue scores.
- The ~150 wpm "average" figure was not traced to a primary study.

## 5. Using the 30-second prep window

### Takeaway
Only one source gives an explicit routine (pick the example, jot 2–3 STAR bullets, take a breath); the sources converge that the 30 seconds are for *selection and ordering*, not composition — which only works if the story bank is already built.

### Cited Findings
- Prep-window routine: pick the example that fits the question; jot 2–3 bullets using STAR; take a breath before recording. — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)
- "Most banks will only give you 30 seconds to prepare", which is why knowing likely questions in advance matters. — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/11/hirevue-questions-barclays-deutsche-bank-ubs)
- M&I drill that trains the window: write sample questions on note cards, shuffle, answer 5 with 30 s prep and 90 s answer on a stopwatch. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- LSE: you usually get only a short time to read each question before recording; you can usually choose when to start each answer (platform-dependent) — check the rules before beginning. — [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Anderson (TED): if working from bullets, "Focus on remembering the transitions from one bullet point to the next." — [PRIMARY: Chris Anderson, HBR June 2013](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)

### Inferences
- A concrete 30-second routine consistent with the sources: 0–5 s read the question twice and name the theme being tested (from the ten themes in §2); 5–10 s choose the story from the matrix (first choice, or second if already used in this interview); 10–25 s write 4 keywords — headline, two action verbs, the result number; 25–30 s breathe out, eyes to lens, say the headline sentence in your head.
- Where prep is 180 s (BlackRock/Blackstone), the extra time should go to drafting the first and last sentence and trimming to the word budget, not to adding content.
- Keeping a one-page story matrix (theme × story, with each story's result number) beside the keyboard makes the selection step a lookup rather than recall.

### Gaps
- No careers service, recruiter or bank source describing a prep-window routine was found; the "identify the competency → pick story → 3–4 keywords" routine is a synthesis, not a documented expert method.
- No evidence on whether banks' HireVue configuration lets candidates start early or forces the full countdown.

## 6. Script vs bullet points vs improvisation: producing natural-sounding prepared speech

### Takeaway
Experts split two ways: careers services and M&I say outline, don't script; TED's Chris Anderson says full memorisation is best *if* you rehearse past the "valley of awkwardness", and otherwise use bullets and memorise the transitions. The half-memorised script is the one state everyone agrees fails. Memory and fluency research supports learning the meaning/intentions of each chunk and repeating the same answer aloud under shrinking time limits.

### Cited Findings

**Experts' positions**
- Anderson lists three modes: read from script/teleprompter, bullet points that map each section, or memorise verbatim. "My advice: Don't read it… people will know you're reading. And as soon as they sense it, the way they receive your talk will shift." — [PRIMARY: Anderson, HBR June 2013](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)
- Anderson: "Many of our best and most popular TED Talks have been memorized word for word. If you're giving an important talk and you have the time to do this, it's the best way to go." — [PRIMARY: Anderson, HBR](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)
- Anderson's "valley of awkwardness": most people pass through a stage "where they haven't quite memorized the talk. If they give the talk while stuck in that valley, the audience will sense it. Their words will sound recited, or there will be painful moments where they stare into the middle distance, or cast their eyes upward, as they struggle to remember their lines." Getting past it "is just a matter of rehearsing enough times that the flow of words becomes second nature." — [PRIMARY: Anderson, HBR](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)
- Anderson's fallback: "if you don't have time to learn a speech thoroughly and get past that awkward valley, don't try. Go with bullet points on note cards. As long as you know what you want to say for each one, you'll be fine. Focus on remembering the transitions from one bullet point to the next." And on tone: "it's usually much better to just sound conversational. Don't force it. Don't orate." — [PRIMARY: Anderson, HBR](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)
- Scale of rehearsal in Anderson's exemplar: Jill Bolte Taylor did "many hours of solo practice" then "rehearsed her talk dozens of times in front of an audience". — [PRIMARY: Anderson, HBR](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf)
- M&I: avoid reading from a script because video "makes it very obvious that you're not 'natural'"; outline responses rather than writing full scripts because outlines transfer to other questions. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- MIT lists "memorizing scripted stories" as a mistake that limits flexibility and sounds unnatural; outline stories in bullets. — [PRIMARY: MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)
- LSE: "over-rehearsed answers can easily sound robotic or wooden." — [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Vendor middle position: don't script every word; "memorize key bullet points and transitions" and deliver conversationally. — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)
- Public-speaking pedagogy: extemporaneous delivery from a keyword/key-phrase outline is taught as the most spontaneous-sounding mode; risks are forgetting details and using more words than necessary if phrasing wasn't refined in practice; memorised delivery risks the speaker focusing on recall rather than the audience; practise from the outline until main points and their order come without effort (snippets). — [Univ. of Minnesota open textbook, Delivery Modes](https://open.lib.umn.edu/commpractice/chapter/delivery-modes/); [Lumen Learning, Methods of Speech Delivery](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/wm-publicspeaking/chapter/methods-of-speech-delivery/)
- Wharton on retakes: "you want your responses to seem genuine. Your tenth version won't necessarily be better than your first or second." — [PRIMARY: Wharton Communication Program](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)

**How actors make learned lines sound spontaneous (Noice & Noice)**
- Actors "seldom" rote-memorise; they focus "not on the words of the script, but on those words' meaning", breaking the script into "a series of logically connected 'beats' or intentions". — [PRIMARY: APS Observer summary of Noice & Noice, Current Directions in Psychological Science](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/to-be-or-or-um-line)
- "Active experiencing" = using "all physical, mental, and emotional channels to communicate the meaning of material to another person"; students who imagined conveying material to someone who needed it retained more than rote memorisers. — [PRIMARY: APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/to-be-or-or-um-line)
- Lines learned while making an appropriate movement were better remembered later, and the motion did not need repeating at recall; in one small cast study actors free to move recalled 59% of dialogue verbatim vs 46% seated (six performers; snippet). — [PRIMARY: APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/to-be-or-or-um-line); [The American Scholar, "Much Ado About Acting"](https://theamericanscholar.org/much-ado-about-acting/)
- Caveat: in a 1997 Noice study with students, deliberate memorisation outperformed the actors' strategy on verbatim recall; the method's advantage is for experienced users and for meaning-driven delivery (snippet). — [Noice & Noice, Current Directions (PDF)](https://homepage.villanova.edu/diego.fernandezduque/Teaching/CognitivePsychology/Lectures_and_Labs/sssAppliedIssues/CurrDirActing.pdf)

**Repetition and fluency research**
- 4/3/2 technique: deliver the same monologue three times with the time limit cut from 4 to 3 to 2 minutes. Nation (1989) found higher speech rate on the last delivery; later work found fewer hesitations; Tran & Saito (2021) conclude it gives "robust fluency development but not accuracy development" (snippets; second-language research, so transfer to native-speaker interview answers is an extrapolation). — [De Jong & Perfetti (2011), Language Learning](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf); [Tran & Saito (2021) OASIS summary](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10156231/1/OASIS_Summary_Tran_and_Saito_2021.pdf)
- Retrieval practice beats re-reading for delayed recall: in Roediger & Karpicke (2006) self-testers read a passage ~3.4 times vs ~14.2 for re-readers yet remembered more a week later (snippet of secondary summary). — [Roediger & Karpicke 2006 (PDF)](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf); [WashU press summary](https://source.washu.edu/2006/03/repeated-testtaking-better-for-retention-than-repeated-studying-research-shows/)
- M&I's volume benchmark for HireVue specifically: practise 10–20 recorded questions in total before the real interview, in sets of 5. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)

### Inferences
- Decision rule from Anderson applied to a HireVue with ~15–25 possible answers and days (not months) of notice: full verbatim memorisation of every answer is infeasible, so the candidate is on the bullet-point branch — except for the 3–4 answers that are *certain* to be needed (CV walk-through, why firm, why division), which are worth taking past the valley.
- A protocol that fits the evidence: (1) write each answer for the ear at the word budget; (2) reduce to 4–6 keywords = beats, each labelled with its *intention* ("show I spotted the problem first", "show the number"); (3) memorise verbatim only the first sentence, the transition into Action, and the last sentence; (4) rehearse by retrieval — notes face-down, speak aloud, check afterwards — not by re-reading; (5) run a 4/3/2-style compression (e.g. 2:30 → 2:00 → 1:30) to force fluency and cut filler; (6) deliberately re-word the middle on each take so the structure, not the wording, is what is stored; (7) do some takes standing/gesturing, since movement during learning aids recall.
- "Scripted-and-stiff" is Anderson's valley (partial memorisation); "improvised-and-rambling" is the textbook risk of an outline whose phrasing was never refined aloud. Both are cured by more spoken repetitions of a beat outline, not by more writing.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures the number of repetitions needed for an interview answer to sound fluent; Anderson says "dozens" for an 18-minute talk, M&I says 10–20 questions total, pedagogy says "until effortless". No quantitative threshold exists in the sources.
- Nothing located on "shadowing" or broadcast "writing for the ear" from a named broadcaster or style guide in this pass; no direct evidence for spaced (vs massed) rehearsal of speeches specifically (the Cepeda distributed-practice meta-analysis was not retrieved).
- No debate-pedagogy source retrieved.

## 7. Camera delivery as a trainable skill

### Takeaway
The specific, sourced techniques are: lens-level camera with a physical marker beside the lens; break gaze downward not sideways; hide self-view; calibrate energy by having others score it (self-ratings run about twice observers' ratings in media training); and replace fillers with a deliberate silent pause after awareness training. The "camera eats 20–30% of your energy" figure is a trainer's rule of thumb with no measured basis; the best-documented version is "speak 10–15% louder".

### Cited Findings
- "Maintain eye contact with the lens. If you need to break eye contact, look down, not to the side or up." "Avoid staring at the camera. Remember to blink naturally." — [PRIMARY: Wharton Communication Program (from Janet Vasil's On-Camera Interviewing Workshop, Oct 2019)](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- "Speak to the camera, NOT to the computer screen (a common mistake). You may find it helpful to put a sticker, picture or even a small stuffed animal as close to the lens as you can, to remind you to look there." — [PRIMARY: Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- Notes placement: "stick a couple post-it notes under the camera lens" for company points or supporting evidence. — [PRIMARY: Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- Contradiction on notes: LSE says avoid notes where possible — if the interviewer sees you referring to them, "they might assume you are underprepared or not committed." — [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Framing: camera at eye level or slightly above (prop on books); at least arm's length away; eyes about one-third down from the top of the frame; frame from chest or waist up so gestures are visible and keep hands within frame; too much headroom "will diminish your presence"; camera too low "you look arrogant". — [PRIMARY: Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- Contradiction on gestures: M&I says limit hand gestures because they look strange on a fixed camera; Wharton says frame so gestures are visible; LSE says keep hand movements modest. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/); [PRIMARY: Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf); [PRIMARY: LSE](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Self-view: M&I — turn off any feature that shows your own video because it is distracting, and look "above" your normal viewing angle into the webcam; LSE — look into the lens and avoid staring at your own image. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/); [PRIMARY: LSE](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- End of answer: hold a professional expression and keep looking at the camera for a few seconds after you finish. — [PRIMARY: Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)
- Energy on camera — media trainer Brad Phillips (*The Media Training Bible*): "television tends to make people appear more muted than they do in person"; try speaking 10–15 percent louder; trainees who feel "comically loud" usually look right on playback; overdoing it is rare; "Passionate need not be loud"; aim for the most energetic version of you, as when talking with an old friend. — [Throughline Group (media-training firm; commercial but specific)](https://www.throughlinegroup.com/2014/07/03/how-much-energy-is-appropriate-for-media-interviews/)
- Phillips's calibration drill: after an on-camera practice, the trainee rates their own energy 1–10 (typically 8–9); the others in the room rate it (typically 4–5); the gap shows self-assessment of energy is unreliable. — [Throughline Group](https://www.throughlinegroup.com/2014/07/03/how-much-energy-is-appropriate-for-media-interviews/)
- Other energy numbers in circulation are unsupported rules of thumb: a Dale Carnegie Tokyo podcast says the lens costs ~20% of your energy and suggests speaking with 50% more energy (snippet; single trainer's assertion). — [Dale Carnegie Tokyo podcast ep. 241](https://dalecarnegietokyopresentationsjapan.libsyn.com/241-presenting-on-video)
- LSE: speak more loudly, clearly and slowly than usual; smile deliberately because it is harder on camera "and comes through in your voice". — [PRIMARY: LSE](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Filler words — behavioural method: awareness training (first identify your own filler sounds, then learn to notice when/where you produce them), optionally adding a deliberate silent pause as the replacement response; cited to Montes et al. (2019) and Spieler & Miltenberger (2017), both in the *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis* (snippet; effect sizes not checked). — [QBS summary](https://qbs.com/news-and-blog/is-your-presentation-filled-with-ums-a-method-to-reduce-filled-pauses-when-speaking-in-front-of-a-group/)
- Disagreement on whether fillers matter on HireVue: eFC says don't worry about "ums and ahs" (its premise: AI transcribes and assesses the content); M&I says keep "um, ah, uh, like" and stuttering to a minimum and check for them on playback. — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking); [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- M&I also claims automated systems "can detect issues such as lack of eye contact or speaking too quickly"; HireVue's own statement says its AI does not use video or audio characteristics at all — the M&I claim appears outdated. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/); contradicted by [PRIMARY: HireVue](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Reconciling the notes disagreement: Wharton's post-its *under the lens* limit eye travel to a downward flick (the same direction Wharton says is the acceptable way to break eye contact), whereas LSE's objection is to visible reading. So: maximum 4–6 keywords per note, in large type, directly below the lens; never full sentences.
- Because self-rated energy is roughly double observer-rated energy in Phillips's drill, the candidate cannot calibrate alone by feel — it has to be done from playback or by a second person's 1–10 score, and the target is the take that *feels* slightly too big.
- Energy, eye-line and vocal variety are scored only by the human reviewer (HireVue's model ignores them), but the BARS does include "pace, volume, diction" under communication for human raters, so they are not irrelevant where a recruiter watches.

### Gaps
- No broadcaster or peer-reviewed source supports a specific "camera eats 20–30% of your energy" figure; the only numbers found are trainer heuristics (10–15% louder; "20%"; "50% more").
- No sourced vocal-variety drill (pitch/pace exercises) or sourced "how to open and close an answer" technique from a broadcast trainer was found in this pass.
- No evidence on optimum speaking rate for perceived credibility was retrieved.

## 8. Self-diagnosis: content vs delivery, and practice platforms

### Takeaway
The documented way to separate content from delivery is to split the channels on playback — sound off (visual audit), screen off (audio audit), then combined — plus a word count/transcript check against the rubric; automated tools measure only pace, fillers and sometimes eye contact, and HireVue's own practice mode gives no feedback at all.

### Cited Findings
- Separate-channel review: do an audio audit ("only listen to the audio (don't look at the video)") and, separately, a visual audit with the volume at zero to judge body language (snippet). — [SSW Rules](https://www.ssw.com.au/rules-beta/practice-makes-perfect)
- MIT Communication Lab: listen back with the screen blacked out or eyes closed and ask about voice, volume and clarity (snippet). — [PRIMARY: MIT Communication Lab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/be/commkit/public-speaking-how-to-practice/)
- Three-pass variant: audio pass for filler words and pacing, then a combined pass checking whether words and body language match (snippet; vendor). — [VENDOR: Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-practice-public-speaking-at-home)
- M&I playback checklist: eye contact, video/audio quality, **word count**, attire, filler words, background distractions. — [TRADE: M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- Observer-vs-self rating gap (energy 8–9 self vs 4–5 observers) is a ready-made blind-rater test. — [Throughline Group](https://www.throughlinegroup.com/2014/07/03/how-much-energy-is-appropriate-for-media-interviews/)
- Civil Service panel method that a peer rater can copy: "observe, record, classify, evaluate and score each question independently" before discussing. — [PRIMARY: gov.uk guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f746b1ae90e0740caae75a7/Guidance-Application_of_Success_Profile_Guides_during_delegated_grade__EO-Grade_6__recruitment_v0b.pdf)

**Platforms and what they measure**
- HireVue's built-in practice (OnDemand interviews only): unlimited practice questions before starting; three generic questions cycle and are not specific to the role; you can replay answers; practice answers "are never uploaded or submitted to anyone" and are deleted when you leave the playback screen; no review is possible once the real interview begins (snippets of HireVue's help centre/FAQ). — [PRIMARY: HireVue candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq); [PRIMARY: HireVue support](https://hirevuesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028433411)
- LSE: "The interviewer won't see your practice recordings"; LSE also points students to Assessment Day (record and privately review) and its Interview Stream subscription (build custom interviews from a question database). — [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)
- Shortlist.Me (provided by LSE, Dundee, Edge Hill and others): video practice interviews, some written by LSE career consultants; feedback via AI and self-reflection against criteria (tone, pace, concision, handling pressure); Edge Hill says the automated feedback covers speech rate and filler words (snippets). — [PRIMARY: LSE Shortlist.me page](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/online-tools/Shortlist.me); [Edge Hill University](https://edgehill.ac.uk/?p=41562)
- Graduates First (Bath, Warwick): record and review answers to competency and strengths questions; Bath notes it may not look like the employer's software; no scoring method described (snippets). — [Bath Careers blog](https://blogs.bath.ac.uk/careers/2023/10/19/how-to-succeed-in-a-recorded-video-interview); [Warwick Careers](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/careers/applications/interviews/practice)
- Yoodli: tracks pace, filler words and eye contact; offers AI role-play with follow-up questions; a competitor's review flags community concerns about how recordings may be used under its terms (snippet; source is Big Interview, a competitor). — [VENDOR: Big Interview review of Yoodli](https://www.biginterview.com/resources/yoodli-review-for-organizations-can-you-use-it-to-scale-interview-prep)
- Google Interview Warmup: free; transcribes answers in real time and gives basic ML feedback; does not ask follow-ups or grade body language; one blog claims Google retired it in April 2026 — unconfirmed (snippets). — [Google](https://grow.google/interview-warmup); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/talent-acquisition/pages/new-google-ai-tool-helps-job-seekers-prepare-for-interviews.aspx); [retirement claim: Dymesty blog](https://dymesty.com/blogs/articles/ai-interview-practice-tools-methods-guide)
- Big Interview: AI feedback is a premium add-on scoring answers on a set of "key criteria" that the brochure extract did not list (snippet). — [SIU career centre brochure](https://careerdevelopment.siu.edu/_common/documents/interviewing/ai-brochure.pdf)

### Inferences
- A content-vs-delivery diagnostic that follows from HireVue's design: transcribe one recorded answer (any dictation tool), strip the video, and score the *text* against (a) the Civil Service 1–7 descriptors and (b) the BARS "organised / clear purpose / logical sequence" anchors, plus counts — % of words in Situation vs Action, number of "I + verb" action statements, number of quantified facts, presence of result and learning. If the transcript scores ≥5 but the video feels weak, the problem is delivery; if the transcript scores ≤4, no delivery work will fix it. This mirrors what the algorithm sees (text only) and what the human sees (video).
- Then split delivery: sound-off pass (eye-line, stillness, expression), screen-off pass (pace, fillers, monotone, trailing endings), and an independent rater giving 1–10 for energy and a 1–7 for the behaviour without seeing your self-scores.
- Tool limits: the automated platforms measure surface delivery (rate, fillers, gaze) — none of the sources shows any of them scoring evidence quality against a behavioural rubric the way a Civil Service panel or HireVue's BARS-trained model does. They are useful for the screen-off pass only.

### Gaps
- No source describes transcript-and-rubric self-scoring as an established method; it is my synthesis from the marking schemes.
- No independent accuracy evaluation of Yoodli / Big Interview / Shortlist.Me feedback was found.
- Whether Google Interview Warmup is still live (and whether it is US-only) was not confirmed against a Google source.
- InterviewStream's feature set was not examined beyond LSE's one-line description.

## 9. Legitimate ways to obtain or predict questions, and how much firms reuse them

### Takeaway
There is no verified evidence of a fixed bank reused verbatim year to year, but the *themes* are highly stable across 2023–2026 lists and across banks, and legitimate crowd-sourced lists (eFinancialCareers' annual compilation, Glassdoor, WSO, university question banks) are refreshed each season. Paid "leaked question" lists are warned against by the main forum itself.

### Cited Findings
- eFinancialCareers publishes a seasonal compilation gathered from Glassdoor, Wall Street Oasis and Reddit of questions that were, "as far as we can tell", asked in the current recruiting season (latest: 9 Sep 2026, "for 2027"); earlier editions exist for 2023 and are redistributed by university career centres such as MIT's. — [TRADE: eFinancialCareers 2026](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking); [MIT CAPD repost, Mar 2023](https://capd.mit.edu/blog/2023/03/16/new-hirevue-interview-questions-at-goldman-sachs-jpmorgan/)
- Stability evidence across editions: "Why did you choose [bank]", walk through your resume, failure/regret, leading a team/project, working under pressure, teamwork, conflict, a deal/stock pitch and "how would you invest $X" appear in both the Nov 2023 and Sep 2026 lists and in M&I's category list. — [eFC 2023](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/11/hirevue-questions-barclays-deutsche-bank-ubs); [eFC 2026](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking); [M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)
- Vendor claim (unsourced): JP Morgan draws from a pool of 100–150 possible questions so content varies between candidates. — [VENDOR: IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)
- Vendor claim: Morgan Stanley "has shown consistency in their question bank" while some firms rotate annually (snippet). — [VENDOR: The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=6574)
- Wall Street Oasis's "Guide to HireVues" warns that people beg or pay for questions and calls out paid lists of "FAKE QUESTIONS"; recommends practising on camera against a general list (snippet; page 403). — [Wall Street Oasis forum](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/guide-to-hirevues)
- HireVue's design intent is that real questions are hidden in advance and each candidate gets the same time to review, prepare and respond (snippet). — [PRIMARY: HireVue candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq)
- HireVue says interview questions and scoring are "jointly designed by Hirevue and hiring company", against competencies chosen per role. — [PRIMARY: HireVue Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)
- JPMorgan says recorded responses are valid for 6 months and may be reused for similar roles (snippet). — [PRIMARY: JPMorgan careers](https://careers.jpmorgan.com/in/en/how-we-hire/interview-tips)
- University careers services hold their own reported-question banks (Cambridge's 170-question banking list) and platforms with employer-style question databases (LSE's Interview Stream; Shortlist.Me interviews written by LSE consultants). — [PRIMARY: Cambridge Careers](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/files/example_interview_questions_for_banking_and_financial_markets.pdf); [PRIMARY: LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)

### Inferences
- Because questions are written against a per-role competency set (HireVue) and banks' published values change slowly, predicting the *competency* is more reliable than predicting the wording: read the bank's stated values/behaviours and the job description, and expect one question per competency plus motivation and one markets item.
- The JPM 6-month validity implies one recording may serve multiple JPM applications — so the first JPM HireVue of a season is the one to over-prepare for.
- Legitimate prediction workflow: (1) latest eFC seasonal list for the bank; (2) Glassdoor interview reviews filtered to the programme and last 12 months; (3) the university's question bank; (4) the bank's own careers-page values; (5) assume one firm-specific news/deal question for the division applied to.

### Gaps
- No bank or HireVue statement on question-pool size or year-to-year rotation; the "100–150 pool" figure is an unsourced vendor claim.
- Could not verify Glassdoor or WSO thread content directly (403), so the degree of verbatim repetition within a season is unconfirmed.
- Whether any banks send questions in advance (some employers do for accessibility adjustments) was not researched.
