## Ownership

### *** MAJOR RECENT DEVELOPMENT — M Unit Plan termination (30 July 2026) ***
PRIMARY SOURCE (verbatim), Allianz media release 30 July 2026:
https://www.allianz.com/en/mediacenter/news/media-releases/financials/260730-allianz-to-terminate-pimco-m-unit-plan-and-purchase-m-units.html (accessed 2026-08-31)
> "Allianz today announced that it has exercised its right to terminate the legacy PIMCO employee equity plan (M Unit Plan) and will purchase outstanding PIMCO M Units for cash. M Unit options were awarded to employees between 2008 and 2020, and the outstanding M Units held by former and current employees represent a **9.4 percent ownership in PIMCO**."
> "Current PIMCO employees may elect to retain their M Units under a new, separate contractual regime until after termination of employment. As former employees hold a **4.4 percent ownership in PIMCO** in total, Allianz will pay a consideration of **at least 1.4 billion euros in cash at closing** in accordance with the M Unit Plan rules."
Trade press (secondary): ~$1.61bn equivalent (AM Best, Investing.com); The Insurer headline "Allianz to pay at least €1.4bn for former Pimco staff's 4.4% stake".
=> IMPLICATION: PIMCO was NOT 100% Allianz-owned; a legacy employee equity plan held 9.4%. Allianz is now consolidating that. INFERENCE (mark as inference): this simplifies Allianz's capital structure and removes a retention instrument dating to the post-Gross era; it may increase reliance on cash/deferred-cash compensation for senior PIMCO staff going forward. Do NOT assert motive — Allianz's stated framing is only that it "exercised its right".

### KEY ANALYTICAL POINTS (for section 3.1/3.2)
1. **FX is a huge swing factor.** PIMCO earns mostly in USD; Allianz reports in EUR. FY2025: net inflows +€139bn and markets +€94bn were largely eaten by **−€170bn of FX translation**. Headline OP +3.3% vs FX-adjusted +6.9%. Always read the FX-adjusted number.
2. **The 2022 shock is the cleanest illustration of "flows matter".** 2022 was the worst bond bear market in decades: 3P AUM fell €331bn, net OUTflows of €81.4bn, revenue −1.9%, OP −8.3%, CIR jumped from 58.4% → 61.2%. Revenue fell less than AUM because average AUM (what fees are charged on) lags year-end AUM.
3. **Operating leverage is visible.** 2Q26: revenue +19.3% internal → OP +19.8% (+23% FX-adj), CIR down to 60.2%. Costs did not rise proportionally. Conversely in 2022 revenue −1.9% → OP −8.3%. This is the high-fixed-cost / high-operating-leverage signature.
4. **Performance fees are a swing item** — explicitly cited as a driver of the 2022 decline ("lower performance fees") and the 2Q26 increase ("higher performance fees").
5. Allianz FY2025 group context (verbatim boilerplate): "our asset managers PIMCO and Allianz Global Investors manage about 2.2 trillion euros of third-party assets"; "We are one of the world's largest investors, managing around **791 billion euros on behalf of our insurance customers**"; Allianz group 2025: 156,000 employees, total business volume €186.9bn, operating profit €17.4bn.
   → Allianz's own €791bn insurance general account is the pool from which PIMCO's affiliated (non-third-party) assets are drawn. PIMCO total AUM $2.33tn vs third-party $1.92tn ⇒ **~$0.41tn (~18%) is affiliated/related-party money**. INFERENCE: a large share of that is Allianz insurance balance-sheet money. NOT independently confirmed — flag as inference.
