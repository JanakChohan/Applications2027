## Q1. Revenue mix and growth by firm (FY2025, H1 2026): recurring vs transaction share, organic growth, margins

### Takeaway
LSEG is a subscription-led group (73% recurring in FY2025; D&A + FTSE Russell + Risk Intelligence c.60% of H1 2026 income) with a Markets arm at 40% of income that is now its fastest-growing and second-highest-margin division. Its group organic growth (7.1% FY2025, 8.4% H1 2026) is respectable but below most venue-heavy peers in the 2025–26 high-volatility, high-volume period (Cboe +25% in Q2 2026, HKEX +19% in H1 2026, Nasdaq +15%, Deutsche Börse +11% ex treasury). In data and index it is also growing more slowly than the index specialists (FTSE Russell +9.1% vs S&P DJI +20% and MSCI Index +17.5%).

### Inferences
- **Recurring share ladder (latest available, definitions differ):** Nasdaq c.80% Solutions (Q2 2026) > LSEG 73% recurring (FY2025) ≈ MSCI (FY2025 73% recurring subscriptions + 25% asset-based) > Euronext 56% non-volume (Q1 2026) > ICE c.51% recurring (Q2 2026) > Cboe c.24% Data Vantage (Q2 2026) > CME c.12% market data (FY2025). LSEG is the only one of these whose recurring base is mainly a *desktop/feeds* data business rather than software (Nasdaq), indices (MSCI) or mortgage/fixed-income data (ICE).
- **Growth gap is cyclical as well as structural.** The 2026 volatility boom lifted transaction-heavy peers (Cboe +25%, HKEX +19%) far more than LSEG, because only 40% of LSEG is transactional. LSEG's own Markets division (+11.9% H1 2026) grew in line with or faster than ICE (H1 c.+12.5%) and Deutsche Börse (+11% ex treasury), so the group gap comes from D&A (+5.1%).
- **The index gap is the most striking new finding.** In H1/Q2 2026 S&P DJI grew c.20% and MSCI Index c.17.5% while FTSE Russell grew 9.1% organically. The likely reason is mix: peers' index revenue is more asset-based (ETF AUM fees that rise with markets and flows), while FTSE Russell is more subscription-based. This is an inference; FTSE Russell's asset-based share was not checked in this round.
- **Margins:** LSEG's group margin (52.7% H1 2026, partly one-off) is below exchange-only peers (ICE adjusted operating margin 61–63%) because D&A (42%) dilutes it; the Markets division itself (58.6%) is in the exchange range.

## Q2. What LSEG claims is unique, in its own words, and which claims hold up

### Takeaway
LSEG's distinctiveness pitch rests on five claims: an unmatched combination of trade-lifecycle and data value chains ("end-to-end"), open access, customer partnership, "LSEG Everywhere" distribution, and being "more valuable in an AI world" because c.90% of data revenue is real-time or proprietary. The combination, open access and partnership claims hold up well against evidence; the proprietary-data claim is plausible but self-defined and unverified; the AI-world claim is not yet proved in revenue and is the claim markets doubt most.

- "More valuable in an AI world" is the title of both the FY2025 results and Q1 2026 presentations; core thesis: data, compute and models are AI's ingredients; compute is an arms race, models commoditise, and trusted licensed data gains value — [LSEG FY2025 presentation](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/presentation/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-presentation-26feb2026.pdf); [LSEG H1 2026 call transcript](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-results/transcripts/lseg-h1-2026-interim-results-analyst-investor-call-transcript.pdf)

- **Peers make the same "trusted data in AI" pitch:** S&P Global announced on 15 Jul 2025 that its data (Capital IQ financials, earnings call transcripts) would be available in Claude via a Kensho-built MCP server, for existing S&P subscribers only — [S&P Global press release](https://press.spglobal.com/2025-07-15-S-P-Global-and-Anthropic-Announce-Integration-of-S-P-Globals-Trusted-Financial-Data-into-Claude); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/s-p-global-to-share-data-with-anthropic-for-new-claude-offering)

### Inferences
Assessment of each claim:
1. **"End-to-end" / combination: holds up.** No peer in the set owns terminal-scale data, a global index house, multi-asset venues including FX dealer-to-client and interbank, and a global OTC rates CCP together (see Q6). But "end-to-end" is a description of breadth, not of integration: under open access each step is sold separately, so the combination does not lock customers in the way a vertical silo does.
2. **Open access: holds up, and it is a real choice with a real cost.** LSEG accepts losing flow (Euronext clearing) in exchange for being the neutral utility that clears for everyone (SwapClear). It is distinctive among large groups; Deutsche Börse calls itself "vertically integrated", and CME and ICE clear mainly their own venues' products.
3. **Customer partnership: holds up and is the most clearly unique feature.** Bank equity and revenue-share at LCH and Post Trade Solutions has no equivalent found at CME, ICE or Eurex (not exhaustively checked).
4. **LSEG Everywhere: holds up as a distribution strategy but is not unique.** S&P has the same Anthropic/MCP route (and was earlier, July 2025); FactSet and others are on the same platforms. What LSEG has that is harder to copy is licensed real-time and venue-generated data (FX, Tradeweb, LSE, LCH) feeding those channels.
5. **"More valuable in an AI world" / 90% proprietary: unproven.** The 90% figure is LSEG's own, with its own definition of "proprietary"; it was not independently tested. Revenue proof is still thin: Workflows grows c.3%, AI pricing is only now being built into renewals, and the H1 2026 subscription growth (ASV 6.1%) is not visibly accelerating because of AI.

### Gaps
- No independent breakdown of what share of LSEG's data revenue is truly proprietary (venue-generated or exclusive) versus aggregated third-party content.
- The May 2026 investor deck mentioned in the brief was not located or read in this round; the April 2026 Q1 update and July 2026 interim results were used instead.
- No disclosed AI revenue figure from LSEG as of 9 October 2026.

## Q3. Strategic bets compared: Microsoft partnership vs peers' cloud, AI and M&A bets; status and evidence of payoff, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
LSEG's bet is unusual because Microsoft is both its cloud provider and a shareholder and product co-developer, aimed at putting LSEG data and Workspace inside Microsoft's productivity and AI tools. Peers' cloud deals (CME–Google, Nasdaq–AWS) are mainly about infrastructure and hosting; their big 2026 bets are M&A that deepens one lane (ICE buying MarketAxess in fixed income; Deutsche Börse buying Allfunds in funds). Evidence of LSEG's payoff is mainly in usage and partnerships, not yet in revenue, and investors describe the Microsoft rollout as disappointing.

- Outputs by mid-2026: Workspace in Teams, Excel and PowerPoint add-ins, Open Directory (20+ customers onboarded), Autex and Digital Markets Infrastructure on Azure, data in Microsoft Fabric, Copilot Studio connector — [LSEG FY2025 RNS](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf); [LSEG H1 2026 Interim Report](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)

**Where the combination is an advantage (mechanism):**
1. **Own-venue data into own distribution.** Tradeweb, FXall/FX Matching, LSE and LCH generate proprietary prices that LSEG sells through Workspace, feeds and now MCP/AI channels. CME's +20% market-data growth shows how valuable venue data is; LSEG has the venues *and* the distribution layer. This underpins the "90% proprietary/real-time" claim.
2. **Clearing network effects plus partnership.** SwapClear's near-monopoly in GBP and dominance in USD swaps rests on netting scale; bank equity/revenue-share keeps the users from backing rivals. Neither depends on owning data, so this edge would exist even if LSEG were split.
3. **FX across the workflow.** Dealer-to-client (FXall), interbank (FX Matching), clearing (ForexClear), plus data and analytics. ForexClear +31.4% in 2025 shows growth; no peer has all four steps.
4. **Index + data + analytics in fixed income.** FTSE Russell's WGBI and fixed income indices use LSEG pricing data and Yield Book analytics; Tradeweb distributes in the same asset class.

**Where it does not matter (or is a drag):**
1. **Desktop competition with Bloomberg:** owning LCH or the LSE does not win terminal seats; Workflows growth of c.3% shows the combination has not lifted Workspace.
2. **Index selection by ETF issuers:** brand and fee drive choice; FTSE Russell grows more slowly than MSCI and S&P DJI despite the group's breadth.
3. **Listings:** London's weak IPO flow is not helped by group data assets.
4. **Valuation:** the market currently applies a conglomerate-style discount rather than a premium (Q5), and activists have floated separation ideas (Elliott reportedly does not want a sale or spin-off — [RTÉ / Reuters–FT](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0211/1557838-activist-investor-elliott-builds-stake-in-lseg-ft/)).
5. **Open access limits cross-sell lock-in:** because each service is sold separately and neutral access is a regulatory and strategic commitment, LSEG cannot bundle clearing with trading the way a silo can. The combination creates information and distribution value, not tying.

**Genuinely distinctive design choices (summary for the report writer):**
- (a) The breadth of the combination, especially desktop data + global OTC CCP + FX across the workflow: unique in the peer set.
- (b) Open-access clearing at global scale: shared only partly by others; distinctive among the big groups.
