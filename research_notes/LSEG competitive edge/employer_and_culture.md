## 1. Purpose, values and the behaviour framework (exact wording)

### Takeaway
LSEG's Code of Conduct (2024 edition) does not just list four values: it turns each into "everyday" colleague behaviours and People Leader behaviours. That gives a candidate concrete, quotable language. The strongest hook is the Integrity line "our word is our bond", which echoes the London Stock Exchange's historic motto (*Dictum Meum Pactum*).

### Cited Findings
- **Purpose (verbatim):** "driving financial stability, empowering economies and enabling customers to create sustainable growth." The Code says "Everything we do at LSEG must start with our purpose. It's what we're all about and what we're trusted to deliver." (CEO foreword by David Schwimmer, Code of Conduct; PDF created 6 Aug 2024) [PRIMARY] — [LSEG Code of Conduct 2024](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
- **Value definitions (verbatim, identical on the Code and the website):** [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024 §4.1](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf); [LSEG Purpose and values page](https://www.lseg.com/en/about-us/purpose-values) (page undated, read 9 Oct 2026)
  - **Integrity:** "We stand by our principles and deliver on our promises. We earn trust by acting responsibly."
  - **Partnership:** "Our open model is integral to how we do business. We forge long-term relationships; we work together to solve evolving needs and deliver strategic outcomes."
  - **Excellence:** "Our breadth of capabilities sets us apart globally. We achieve industry-leading outcomes by combining unique, diverse perspectives and knowledge across markets."
  - **Change:** "We embrace change. We combine human ingenuity, technology, risk management and insight to create the products and services that lead and shape the industry."
- **"What does this look like every day?" (all-colleague behaviours, verbatim, Code §4.1):** [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
  - Integrity: "We are accountable for our impact on our industry, society and the environment. We are clear, balanced and fair in our decision making. We stand for what we believe is right. We do what we say – our word is our bond."
  - Partnership: "We are customer centric. We build deep connections with customers, partners and each other. We collaborate across boundaries. We anticipate problems and take pride in delivering timely outcomes."
  - Excellence: "We uphold the highest standards. We embrace diverse perspectives. We take ownership of delivery and empower each other to do the same. We learn and grow from our successes and failures."
  - Change: "We are curious and challenge the status quo. We combine creativity with technology to invent new possibilities. We have a growth mindset. We find opportunities in complexity and uncertainty."
- **People Leader expectations (headline per value):** "Lead responsibly to earn trust" (Integrity); "Collaborate for customer outcomes" (Partnership); "Drive performance and growth" (Excellence); "Inspire innovation and resilience" (Change). Example leader statements include "I invite challenge, listen and adapt accordingly", "I look out for the team and their wellbeing", "I simplify complexity and help others see the big picture", "I encourage new ideas and learning from experimentation." [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
- **How the CEO frames the values:** "As we strive for excellence, we must always stand by our principles. Delivering excellence does not mean doing things at any cost or acting irresponsibly. And it means always having the courage to speak up if things don't feel right." and "LSEG's reputation is one of our greatest assets." (Schwimmer, Code foreword, 2024) [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
- The Code's culture section says "the right culture is critical for us to achieve our ambitions. This means creating an inclusive environment where we can all perform, grow and develop and bring our whole selves to work." It also commits to a Speak Up and Whistleblowing framework with no tolerance of retaliation. [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024 §4, §4.2](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
- The website says the purpose "is the foundation on which our culture is built" and the values "go to the heart of who we are and guide our decision making and everyday actions". [PRIMARY] — [Purpose and values](https://www.lseg.com/en/about-us/purpose-values)
- **Earlier culture framework (historic, for context only):** the 2021–22 graduate brochure used three "culture pillars": "Connect" ("We work as one company to serve our customers… We break down silos"), "Create opportunity" ("We have the courage to think differently. We are proud of our heritage yet not constrained by it") and "Deliver excellence" ("Good enough isn't good enough… We know that financial communities depend on the integrity of our infrastructure and data – and never compromise on stability and resilience"). These have since been replaced by the four values. [PRIMARY, dated Sep 2021, modified Jun 2022] — [LSEG Graduate Brochure](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/brochures/lseg-graduate-brochure.pdf)

### Inferences
- The everyday behaviours map closely onto strengths-based interview themes: "anticipate problems", "take ownership of delivery", "learn and grow from successes and failures", "growth mindset", "find opportunities in complexity and uncertainty". A candidate can name a value and then quote its behaviour line, which sounds specific without sounding scripted.
- "Our word is our bond" ties Integrity to 300+ years of exchange history. It is a good way to link the job to what makes infrastructure different (trust is the product).
- The old "never compromise on stability and resilience" wording shows that resilience was part of the culture story before the current values, which fits the purpose's "financial stability".

### Gaps
- I found no published LSEG document defining "leadership expectations" beyond the Code grid, and no named Markets leader speech that walks through the values.
- No date for when the four values replaced the three pillars (likely around 2022–23; not confirmed).

- Recurring themes across LSEG's own testimonials and independent intern reviews: real ownership early, work used by senior people, supportive managers, and a sustainable workload ("never so much that I felt overly stressed"). - Social life is the weakest-rated area in the reviews, so the in-person Ignite offsite and cohort events matter more than they might seem. That is a good question to ask at assessment centre.
- Note the shift from rotations to day-one divisional alignment. A candidate should not promise to "rotate across businesses" unless the specific pathway says so.

## 3. People practices: hybrid, benefits, volunteering, Foundation, sustainability, diversity, engagement

### Takeaway
The 2025 Group Sustainability Databook gives hard 2025 numbers: about 27,000 employees, an engagement score of 74 (flat), a 78% survey response rate, 14% total turnover, 82,718 volunteer hours with 32% of staff volunteering, and £3.9m in LSEG Foundation grants. It also shows some weak spots: women in senior leadership fell from 41% to 36%, against a 40% target. The UK statutory gender pay gaps (April 2025 snapshot period) are a mean of 3–14% and a median of 9–18%, depending on the entity.

## 4. What employees say (Glassdoor themes; integration, reorganisations, offshoring)

### Takeaway
Glassdoor rates LSEG at about 3.6/5 from roughly 3,250 reviews, close to the financial services average of 3.7 and slightly below CME's 3.9 [SNIPPET, undated]. Praise centres on pension, benefits, flexibility, interesting fintech and data work, and supportive teams. Complaints centre on bureaucracy, the Refinitiv integration and redundancies, layered management, and opaque promotion. The workforce has a large APAC weighting (58% in 2025), which candidates should describe neutrally.

### Cited Findings
- **LSEG Glassdoor [SNIPPET, undated, likely 2026]:** 3.6/5 from about 3,250 reviews; about two-thirds would recommend to a friend; 72% approve of CEO David Schwimmer. — [Glassdoor LSEG (E11860)](https://static.glassdoor.com.au/Reviews/LSEG-London-Stock-Exchange-Group-Reviews-E11860.htm)
  - Pros: "great" pension scheme; subsidised cafeteria; flexible hours and salary; interesting work for building fintech and data skills; "supportive team", collaborative atmosphere. [ANECDOTE]
  - Cons: "extremely bureaucratic"; poorly joined-up mergers and partnerships; a former Refinitiv/Thomson Reuters employee described a high-performing team broken up by redundancies; upper management described as insular and middle management as bloated; obscure promotion cycles; painful onboarding because of outsourced technology. [ANECDOTE]
- **Peer comparison: CME Group Glassdoor [SNIPPET, newest reviews visible from May 2026]:** 3.9/5 from about 1,322 reviews; 74% recommend; 78% CEO approval (Terry Duffy). Pros are work-life balance, hybrid working and benefits; cons are office politics, limited promotion and layoffs. — [Glassdoor CME Group](https://static.glassdoor.co.uk/Reviews/CME-Group-Reviews-E7796.htm)
- **Job cuts:** in Oct 2024 Bloomberg reported LSEG was weighing about 200 job cuts (less than 1% of staff), mainly in sales, after a global business review. — [BNN Bloomberg, 25 Oct 2024](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2024/10/25/london-stock-exchange-group-weighs-cutting-200-jobs-globally). Earlier, the CEO warned that the Refinitiv integration would lead to job cuts (no figure given). [SNIPPET] — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=1606021)
- **Footprint:** 58% of employees were in APAC in 2025 (57% in 2024), against 32% in EMEA. [PRIMARY] — [Databook 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/sustainability/lseg-group-sustainability-databook-2025.pdf). The 2021–22 brochure described the footprint as extending "from India to the United States, Poland to Costa Rica". [PRIMARY] — [Graduate Brochure](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/brochures/lseg-graduate-brochure.pdf)

### Inferences
- Balanced framing for interview: LSEG is a merged, global organisation still simplifying itself. The upside for a graduate is working across time zones and teams from day one (Partnership: "collaborate across boundaries"); the downside is process and layers. A candidate who acknowledges this and points to "find opportunities in complexity" (Change) will sound informed rather than naive.
- Glassdoor scores for LSEG and CME are both within the industry band, so ratings are not a differentiator. Programme design and purpose are.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–26 reporting found on specific offshoring moves to Bengaluru or Manila. I found no source and invented none. Glassdoor ratings for ICE, Deutsche Börse, Euronext, Nasdaq, Bloomberg, S&P Global and MSCI were not retrieved (Glassdoor blocks direct fetches).
- No Glassdoor sub-ratings (work-life balance, culture) for LSEG were retrieved.

## 5. Market infrastructure ("the plumbing") vs a bank or asset manager

### Takeaway
No rigorous public comparison of hours exists. Credible signals point one way: careers guides describe investment banking hours as long and market-driven even at entry level, while infrastructure roles follow market and settlement cycles, and LSEG's own graduates describe responsibility "never so much that I felt overly stressed". The distinctive pull of infrastructure is systemic importance (the purpose's "financial stability"), a neutral open-access position serving the whole market (Partnership: "open model"), and a regulated-utility mindset (resilience over speed).

### Cited Findings
- TARGETjobs: in investment banking "it is not unusual… to spend much of their life at work, even at entry level. Weekend and late evening shifts often become necessary." [SNIPPET] — [TARGETjobs: investment banking or insurance](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/graduate-job-investment-banking-or-insurance-which-should-i-choose)
- Market-infrastructure hours tend to follow trading hours. One trading-technology employee wrote that "work hours correspond pretty closely to trading hours… most of the systems are running during trading hours". [ANECDOTE, non-LSEG firm] — [Prosple review](https://careers-cqu.prosple.com/graduate-employers/imc/reviews/working-hours). Clearing roles also run on daily cycles; for example, a Deutsche Börse ECC student role supports "daily settlement and limit management" in the late afternoon. [SNIPPET] — [Deutsche Börse careers](https://career.deutsche-boerse.com/ecc/job/Leipzig-Working-Student-%28fmd%29-Clearing-Services-%28Economics%2C-Finance%2C-Business-Administration%29/961547301/)
- LSEG on its own role: "we enable businesses and economies… to fund innovation, manage risk and create jobs"; "financial communities depend on the integrity of our infrastructure and data – and never compromise on stability and resilience". [PRIMARY 2021–22] — [Graduate Brochure pp.3–4](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/brochures/lseg-graduate-brochure.pdf). The Code says that as "a leading global financial markets infrastructure and data provider, we have a responsibility to protect our people, customers, partners and shareholders and to contribute towards wider economic stability." [PRIMARY] — [Code of Conduct 2024 §1.0](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)
- Client mix: Capital Markets serves issuers "from multi-national enterprises to £10 million businesses"; secondary-market clients are "banks and brokers"; LCH works with "sell-side clearing members and buy-side clients". FTSE Russell indices were "used by 98 of the top 100 asset managers". [PRIMARY 2021–22, figures dated] — [Graduate Brochure p.8](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/brochures/lseg-graduate-brochure.pdf)
- Daniel Maguire (Group Head of Markets, CEO of LCH Group) joined LCH in 1999, when it had about 100–150 people. He started in treasury operations and rose to CEO in 2017, which is evidence that infrastructure careers can be built from operations roles. ### Inferences
- Compared with an IB summer internship, the honest argument is breadth and system-level view, not prestige: an LSEG intern sees issuers, banks, brokers, asset managers and regulators as clients of one neutral venue or clearing house, rather than one side of a deal. The trade-off is lower pay than IB (see WS6_WS7 pay data) and more process.
- "Systemic importance" ties directly to Integrity and Change: when the infrastructure fails, the whole market feels it, so resilience and risk management are core to the work, not side functions.
