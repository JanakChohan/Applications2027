## 1. LCH SwapClear: share by currency, why liquidity concentrates there, the Eurex and CME challenges, and the 2025 founding-bank deal

### Takeaway
SwapClear is close to a monopoly in USD (c.96% of USD OIS volume in Q2 2026) and GBP (99.995% of SONIA OIS in Q1 2026), dominant but contested in EUR (87.6% of all cleared EUR swaps, 93.3% ex-FRAs, Q2 2026), and genuinely contested in JPY (51.8% vs JSCC 48.2%, Q2 2026). The moat is netting: every new trade is cheapest to clear where the offsetting risk already sits, so liquidity begets liquidity. The two policy-driven challenges look to be plateauing in EUR (Eurex stuck around 12-13%) but biting in JPY, where the CFTC's September 2025 decision to let US firms clear at JSCC has driven a 7.7-point swing in a single quarter.

**Mechanisms: why liquidity concentrates**
- *Netting.* Initial margin is charged on the net risk of a portfolio at one CCP. A dealer whose USD book already sits at LCH pays incremental margin only on the marginal risk of a new trade, often reducing margin if the trade offsets. Clearing the same trade at CME creates a new, un-netted position at a second CCP, so margin is charged twice on what is economically a hedged book [Inferred mechanism; consistent with the share data above]. The IM figures show the scale: c.US$263bn at SwapClear vs US$41.9bn at CME IRS [Snippet, above].
- *Compression.* LCH guide: between April 2019 and March 2020 SwapClear compressed more than US$946trn of swaps, keeping notional outstanding at about US$382trn; notional outstanding grew only US$31trn in that year — [SwapClear compression quickguide](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/post-trade/en_us/documents/lch/guides/swapclear-compression-quickguide.pdf) [Snippet of a primary document]. Q1 2022: US$226trn compressed across a record 1.57m trades [Snippet, same search]. Compression only works across trades held at the same CCP, so a larger book gives more offsetting trades to tear up, cutting gross notional, leverage-ratio and G-SIB capital charges [Inferred mechanism]. LSEG's FY2025 text attributes OTC Derivatives growth (+11.6%, £641m) to "growth in clearing and compression activity across all asset classes" — [FY2025 prelim](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf) [Primary].
- *Breadth of currencies.* SwapClear clears 28 currencies (WS3 S141). A multi-currency dealer can margin cross-currency risk in one default fund and one operational set-up; a challenger that offers only one currency (JSCC in JPY, Eurex mainly EUR) cannot match that [Inferred].
- *Default record.* Lehman 2008 and "9 member defaults... within the initial margin held" are already in WS8 (S351, S354); not repeated.
- *Futures-swaps link (new angle).* CME's counter-moat is SOFR futures (99.22%); LSEG's attack on that flank is clearing FMX futures at LCH so that futures can eventually be margined against SwapClear swaps. FMX's 0.78% share shows this is still marginal — [Clarus, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/rates-derivatives-ccp-shares-show-mixed-q2-dynamics/) [Specialist; strategic reading Inferred].

**The SwapClear founding-bank revenue-share deal**
- Already in WS3/WS8: c.30% → 15% (2025) → 10% (2026-2045), £1.2bn (£921m 2025 + £250m 2026), +100bp group EBITDA margin, 2-3% EPS accretive in 2025.
- New detail: the old c.30% entitlement ran "through to 2035"; the 15% applied retroactively from 1 January 2025 — [LSEG press release, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2025/lseg-announces-partnership-and-investment-in-post-trade-solutions) / [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/lsegs-post-trade-solutions-to-receive-investment-from-11-global-banks/) [Primary/Reported]. The press coverage gives £1.15bn (£0.9bn + £0.25bn); the FY2025 prelim gives £1.2bn (£921m + £250m) — **[minor discrepancy, likely rounding/FX; use the prelim figure]**.
- The extension to 2045 was agreed "with the majority of founding members" — [FY2025 prelim](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf) [Primary]. Not all founders signed.
- Financial footprint in H1 2026: Markets cost of sales fell 38.0% to £87m because of the deal; Markets adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 58.6% from 51.9%, of which 350bp came from the SwapClear change. Group-level: 140bp of the 320bp group margin gain is the SwapClear change [Primary, same document]. H1 2026 also carried £58m amortisation of a new "SwapClear intangible asset" [Primary, same document, note to p.~40].

### Inferences
- Why the deal matters: the 10% share to 2045 keeps the dealers' incentive to route swaps to SwapClear for another 20 years, precisely when EMIR 3 (EUR), the CFTC (JPY) and CME (USD) are giving them alternatives. LSEG bought down the cost of loyalty but did not abolish it. Interview line: "LSEG paid £1.2bn to make the banks cheaper partners rather than to end the partnership."
- The challenge pattern is regulatory, not commercial: Eurex's gains come from an EU mandate, JSCC's from a CFTC permission, SHCH/CCIL from onshore policy. Where the choice is purely commercial (USD, GBP), LCH holds 96-100%. That is the cleanest evidence that the moat is netting economics.
- Eurex's EUR share is inflated by FRAs (reset-risk management); on risk trading it is 6-7%. Quoting "93% ex-FRAs" vs "87.6% headline" shows real fluency.
- Biggest live risk in 2026 is JPY, not EUR: a 7.7-point quarterly swing is the largest move in any major currency.
- H1 2026 Markets margin (58.6%) flatters the run rate; expect a step down in Q4 2026 as LSEG itself warns.

- Clarus Q2 2026 GBP CCP split not published in the posts I read; Q1 2026 (99.995%) remains the latest.
- Exact list of founding members who did not extend to 2045 not disclosed.

## 2. ForexClear, RepoClear, CDSClear, LCH SA: position and growth

### Takeaway
ForexClear is effectively the world's NDF clearing utility (99.1% of cleared NDF volume, Q4 2025) and grew notional 45% in H1 2026. RepoClear is one half of a European repo-clearing duopoly with Eurex, and won the ECB as a member for 2026. CDSClear is the challenger to ICE in index CDS and is growing fastest via US FCMs. LCH SA's cash-equity clearing lost Euronext, which still drags the Securities & Reporting line.

### Inferences
- ForexClear's moat is the same as SwapClear's (netting, members) plus a regulatory push: uncleared margin rules make bilateral NDFs costly, so clearing wins; with 99% of cleared NDFs there is no second netting pool to go to. Growth (45%) is outpacing SwapClear (29%).
- The Euronext loss shows the limit of the horizontal model: a vertically integrated exchange can take its own flow home. Contrast: SwapClear flow has no "home" venue to be pulled back to.
- RepoClear is the one LCH business where an EU CCP (Eurex) has comparable scale; EMIR 3 does not currently mandate euro repo, but it would be the next obvious target [Inferred].

## 3. LSEG FX: FXall, FX Matching, Forward First Fixing, WM/Reuters fix; competition

### Takeaway
LSEG is the only group with both a large dealer-to-client venue (FXall) and an interdealer central limit order book (Matching), and its reported spot volume is now well above CME's EBS. But in D2C, Bloomberg FXGO is bigger and growing fast (record US$970bn ADV in June 2026 vs LSEG FX total US$561bn in H1 2026; measures differ). The WM/Reuters 4pm fix is LSEG-owned and is the most widely used FX benchmark, but it appears to be run as a benchmark business on the FTSE Russell/data side rather than inside Markets; its moat is contractual embedding in fund valuation and index methodology.
