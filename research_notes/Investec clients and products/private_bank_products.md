# Investec UK Private Bank: what it sells, and to whom (research notes, compiled 2026-10-07)

**Labels.** **Confirmed** means a primary source I could read (an Investec RNS, or a partner page fetched in full). **Reported** means press, a broker or another third party. **Snippet** means the text was seen only in search-result extracts from investec.com, because every investec.com page returned HTTP 403 when fetched. These are Investec's own words, but I have not read the full page. **Inferred** means my own reasoning. All URLs were accessed on 2026-10-07. Publication dates of the investec.com case studies were not visible.

These notes do not repeat the following, which are already covered: client numbers (8,200, with about 5,000 to be added), the 95k-household pool, the "mortgage banker to relationship manager" quotes, Rathbones moving to an assets-under-advice model, and the RM vacancy wording. ## 1. Plain-English summary for the PDF

Investec's UK Private Bank is a bank for people who **earn a lot but in awkward ways**: bonuses, carried interest (a share of a fund's profits), shares that vest over time, dividends from their own company, or pay in dollars or euros. A high-street bank's computer often cannot make sense of that income and turns them down, or lends too little. Investec puts a human banker on the case who reads the whole picture and then lends, usually £1m or more against a home.

The core product is the **mortgage**. Around it Investec sells savings accounts, foreign exchange, a premium current account, loans against investment portfolios, and (through Rathbones) investment advice. It is now adding credit cards and rewards so that it becomes the client's main bank.

**Caveats on products**
- A search extract described an en_gb "multicurrency pockets" press release (May 2025) as offering "four currencies on one credit card" and "Investec Rewards". The May 2026 plan, however, describes credit cards and rewards as **new** for the UK. That feature may belong to Investec South Africa. Treat it as **unverified for the UK** and do not use it.
- **PE / GP commitment loans.** I found no published UK *Private Bank* product that lends to individuals to meet a fund commitment. This lending sits in **Fund Finance / Fund Solutions** (the corporate side): "GP financing" helps GPs "meet their commitments into the funds they manage" and covers "co-investment or liquidity release" (Snippet, [GP financing](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/private-equity/fund-finance/gp-financing.html)). Investec says it "can advance the GP commitment against management fee income", and its loans to PE professionals are "typically ... greater than £5 million", up to £50–75m (Snippet/search extract). The PE-professionals private-bank page itself offers the Voyage Account and mortgages that count carried interest (Snippet, [PE professionals page](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/individuals/bank-accounts/our-clients/private-equity-professionals.html)).
- **Old example of a PE loan.** In Oct 2011 Investec Fund Finance lent **£14m** over 4 years to the management of Clyde Blowers Capital so they could personally co-invest in their **£350m Fund III**. Simon Hamilton: the loan "will enable the management team to personally invest more in the fund which ... leads to a stronger alignment with their investors" (Reported, [Financial Reporter, 10-Oct-2011](https://2025.financialreporter.co.uk/savings-and-investments/investec-provides-14m-funding-to-management-of-clyde-blowers-capital.html)). This was the corporate Fund Finance team, not the Private Bank, and it is dated.

## 3. Who the clients are

**Eligibility (published).** Minimum annual earnings of **£300k**, net worth **over £3m**, and **UK resident**. This is stated on the Voyage, business-owner and Rathbones pages (Confirmed on [Rathbones](https://www.rathbones.com/en-gb/wealth-management/private-banking-with-investec); Snippet on investec.com). The portfolio loan also requires being an existing Investec W&I/Rathbones client. Money Helpdesk reports some flexibility for "high-potential professionals (doctors, lawyers)" (Reported). Investec does not publish whether £300k and £3m are "and" or "or" requirements: the Finder and Investec snippets list both. There is no account-balance minimum beyond Investec Save's £5,000.

**Client segments.** investec.com has a "Who We Work With" hub ([our-clients](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/individuals/bank-accounts/our-clients.html)). Pages seen:
- **Business owners and entrepreneurs**: "a private banking service created by entrepreneurs, for entrepreneurs"; lending decisions "within days" (Snippet).
- **Private equity professionals**: carried interest and co-investment income counted. In Investec's GP Trends survey, **43.5%** of PE professionals said lenders struggle to understand their income (Snippet).
- **Hedge fund and asset managers**: "complex and volatile income streams" (Snippet, [hedge fund page](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/focus/my-money/what-private-banking-support-can-investec-provide-to-hedge-fund-managers.html)).
- **Management consultants / "city professionals"** (Snippet, [consultants page](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/focus/my-money/advantage-of-private-banking-for-management-consultants.html)).
- **International clients** (Snippet, [international page](https://www.investec.com/en_gb/individuals/bank-accounts/our-clients/international-clients.html)).
- **Investment bankers, and lawyers paid in USD** (from the case studies and client stories in §4).
- For history, the 2018 segments were "corporate executives and entrepreneurs; internationals and professionals; and financial services professionals" (Reported, see relationship_model.md).
- Note: Investec's doctor, medical-practice and "R800,000 income" segment pages are **South African**. Do not use them for the UK.

## 5. Why Investec chooses these clients

- **Statements by Investec or close to it**
  - Investec, via a search extract of its GP Trends survey: 43.5% of PE professionals feel "lenders struggle to understand their income". Investec "is able to consider things like bonus payments, carried interest, co-investment or foreign currency income ... looking at a client's entire situation" (Snippet).
  - Specialist banks "can take different forms of income into account ... and can offer a higher LTV than mainstream lenders" (Snippet, fund managers page).
  - The broker view: "They do not use standard credit scorecards" (Reported, Money Helpdesk).
  - Tholet (2018) on targeting income-led wealth creators rather than wealth preservers is already in relationship_model.md.
- **Inferred economics** (Investec does not state these in public)
  - (a) **Less competition, better pricing.** Scorecard lenders reject or under-lend to these borrowers, so Investec competes with a few private banks, not the whole market.
  - (b) **Good credit risk.** Clients are rich (over £3m) and high-earning, LTVs are often brought down through capital reductions, and repayments come from big bonuses.
  - (c) **Cross-sell.** One mortgage leads to deposits, FX on foreign pay, portfolio loans, and advice through Rathbones (see #15).
  - (d) **Stickiness.** A banker who understands the client's carried interest is hard to replace.
  - (e) **Future business.** Today's PE partner or founder may later bring their company or fund to the corporate bank.

## 6. Link to the corporate / investment bank

- The Rathbones-hosted Investec page offers business owners, on the same page as private banking: asset finance, direct lending, corporate FX, corporate broking, private capital, IPO advisory, working capital, and M&A advisory (Confirmed, [Rathbones page](https://www.rathbones.com/en-gb/wealth-management/private-banking-with-investec)). It is one bank offering both personal and company services.
- Fund Finance / Fund Solutions lends to PE funds (capital call, NAV) **and** to the GPs personally (GP commitments, co-investment), as in the Clyde Blowers example. Meanwhile the Private Bank sells mortgages to the same PE professionals (Snippet, Reported).
- Corporate Relationship Directors are told to partner "with Private Client teams when personal and business needs intersect" (Confirmed vacancy, in relationship_model.md Q2).
- The client story about the tech executive's USD lump sum (#15) is the clearest published example of a liquidity event handled across FX and wealth.
- **Gap.** I found **no published UK example** of a founder whose company was a corporate-bank client and who then became a private-bank client after an exit. I also found no numbers on referrals between the private and corporate sides. "One Investec" is a strategy term in the RNS, but no UK case study shows it in action.

## 7. Gaps and cautions
- None of the investec.com case-study pages could be fetched (403), so the details come from search extracts and dates are unknown. Wording should be checked in a browser before quoting it verbatim in the PDF.
- No published interest rates, fees on mortgages, max loan size, or bridging terms.
- No published launch date or terms for the 2026 credit card and rewards.
- No individual "capital-call loan" product appears on the Private Bank side. That sits in Fund Finance.
- The surgeon/doctor examples found were South African and are excluded.
