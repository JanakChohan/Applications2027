## 1. Remuneration: the EVA bonus pool, deferral, share awards, malus/clawback — and what is rewarded

### Takeaway
Investec bonus pools are not a cut of revenue or sales: they are funded from **risk-adjusted Economic Value Added (EVA)** — roughly, profit left over after charging each business for the capital and risk it uses — and then divided among people using qualitative factors that explicitly include **client outcomes, culture, risk consciousness and treating customers fairly**. Large awards are deferred into shares for years and can be cut (malus) or taken back (clawback), so a banker who books risky, capital-hungry or badly-conducted business shrinks their own pool and may lose deferred pay later.

### Plain-English jargon
- **EVA (Economic Value Added):** profit minus a charge for the capital tied up to earn it. If a desk earns £10m but uses capital that "costs" £8m, its EVA is £2m. A desk can be profitable on paper but have zero or negative EVA.
- **Bonus pool:** the total pot available for bonuses in a team/division, later split among individuals.
- **Deferral:** part of the bonus is paid years later, usually in shares.
- **Malus:** cancelling/reducing bonus that has been awarded but not yet paid out (unvested). **Clawback:** recovering bonus already paid (vested).
- **MRT (Material Risk Taker):** a regulator-defined person whose job can materially affect the bank's risk profile; tighter pay rules apply.
- **STI / LTI:** short-term incentive (annual bonus) / long-term incentive (share awards vesting over several years, often with performance conditions).

### Cited Findings
**How the pool is funded (EVA)**
- [Confirmed] For Investec Bank plc, discretionary bonuses: "The amounts available to be distributed are based on the Bank-wide risk adjusted Economic Value Added (EVA) model which is, at a high level, based on revenue less risk adjusted costs, and overall affordability." — [Pillar 3 2024, p.78](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Factors for overall remuneration levels in the Bank: "Financial measures of performance: – Risk-adjusted EVA model – Affordability. Non-financial measures: – Market context – Specific input from the risk and compliance functions." — [Pillar 3 2024, p.79](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] The capital framework links capital directly to pay: internal capital is used for "Rewarding performance, taking into account the relative levels of risk adopted by forming a basis for the determination of economic value added at a transactional level, and hence the basis for discretionary variable remuneration", alongside "Allocating capital according to the optimal expected marginal risk-based return" and "Determining transactional risk-based returns on capital." — [Pillar 3 2024, capital management section](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Reported] Older Investec remuneration report (IAR 2015/16) as surfaced in search: rewards are "distributed from pools of realised earnings generated in excess of targeted thresholds which reflect usage of risk-adjusted capital", via an EVA model "in operation for about 17 years" (i.e. since c.1998), and pools are set by finance "taking into account risk-adjusted capital requirements and after eliminating unrealised gains." — [Investec IAR 2015 remuneration report (page timed out, HTTP 522; search snippet only)](http://reports.investec.co.za/iar2016/1_remuneration_report.php). Note "unrealised gains" = paper profits not yet turned into cash; excluding them stops people being paid for marks that may reverse.
- [Confirmed] A real EVA formula in action: in *Brogden & Reid v Investec Bank plc* (Court of Appeal, 20 Oct 2016), two structured equity derivatives bankers' contracts set bonuses at "40% of EVA generated by the Equity Derivative business" in year 2 and "30%" from year 3. The bank defined EVA as "revenue minus costs, minus the cost of capital, all calculated before tax"; EVA was computed by the bank's automated desk-level P&L, including an internal funding rate set by Central Treasury. The desk showed an EVA loss in year 3, so **no bonus was paid**; the court held EVA meant the desk's EVA "using the method normally used by the Bank" and dismissed the appeal. — [EWCA Civ 1031](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2016/1031)
- [Confirmed] The EVA pool is still live in 2026: the Senior Reward Manager (req 13645, London, 30 Gresham Street) must "Co-ordinate the EVA bonus pool and Long-Term Share Awards overall pool determination and divisional or team allocation, working closely with the CEO and CFO", and prepare proposals "for review and approval by the Investec Group Remuneration Committee", "supporting the effective operation of several Remuneration Committees in partnership with the Global Head of Reward", including "reward submissions to the PRA and FCA". — [Investec Careers, fetched 2026-10-07](https://careers.investec.co.uk/jobs/vacancy/senior-reward-manager-13645-london---30-gresham-street/13663/description/)

**How individuals are allocated their share (what is rewarded)**
- [Confirmed] "At an individual level the bonus allocations are determined based on performance against qualitative and quantitative factors. Qualitative measures include adherence to culture, including supporting belonging, inclusion and diversity, **client outcomes**, market context, contribution to performance and brand building, attitude displayed towards **risk consciousness and effective risk management**." — [Pillar 3 2024, p.78](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Non-financial factors for individual total compensation include: "Alignment and adherence to our culture and values", "The level of cooperation and collaboration fostered", "Attitude displayed towards risk consciousness and effective risk management", "Adherence to internal control procedures", "Compliance with the Bank's regulatory requirements … including **treating customers fairly**", "The ability to grow and develop markets and **client relationships**", "**Multi-year contribution** to performance and brand building", "**Long-term sustained performance**", and "Specific input from the risk and compliance functions." — [Pillar 3 2024, p.79](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] "Variable remuneration of employees in the audit, risk and compliance functions is set independently of the business they oversee." — [Pillar 3 2024, p.78](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Guaranteed bonuses are allowed only if "Exceptional", "In the context of hiring new staff" and "Limited to the first year of service"; severance "must … be designed in a way that does not reward failure". — [Pillar 3 2024, p.79](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] All-employee philosophy (DLC Remuneration Committee): remuneration "designed to foster an exceptional performance culture that enables an entrepreneurial spirit as well as a strong sense of ownership, whilst operating within our risk appetite"; to "attract and retain culturally aligned … people who adhere and subscribe to our culture, risk appetite, values and philosophies"; "Reward will appropriately account for risk and potential risk"; "Performance-related variable pay will be subject to discretion and malus for all employees, with clawback provisions being included for Material Risk Takers". — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] "Risk consciousness and management is embedded in the organisational culture, from the initiation of transactional activity through to the monitoring of adherence to mandates and limits"; "Non-financial performance is more heavily weighted for non-revenue generating employees when determining their bonus." — [Group remuneration report 2024, Chair's statement](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Code-principle table: "Risk and conduct considerations are taken into account in the setting of any award"; the Committee "has evidenced the application of downward discretion in 2023 and previous years"; culture "is incorporated in the risk modifier in both the STI and LTI" (the 2024 policy renamed it a "Risk and culture modifier"). — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Executive scorecards include "Culture and values" as a weighted measure (e.g. 5% in the STI, 4% in the LTI), and the 2024–27 policy is built around "Connected client ecosystems", "Growth initiatives" and "Optimisation of returns". — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)

**Deferral and share awards**
- [Confirmed] Non-MRTs: bonuses above a set threshold have **60% of the excess deferred**, paid as short-term share awards (or cash) in three equal tranches over ~three years, subject to malus. — [Pillar 3 2024, p.78](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] MRTs: if variable pay ≥ £500,000, at least 60% deferred; otherwise at least 40% (unless de minimis); at least 50% of deferred and non-deferred pay in shares; deferral vests over **four to seven years** plus typically 12 months' retention; "All variable remuneration is subject to clawback"; "All deferred variable remuneration is subject to malus". 45 MRTs at Investec plc and 40 at IBP at 31 Mar 2024. — [Pillar 3 2024, p.78](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Executive directors: "All variable remuneration is awarded in shares with deferral of up to seven years and a further one year of retention"; malus can be applied for up to 7 years and clawback for up to 10 years after award. Triggers include "employee misbehaviour or material error", "a material downturn in financial performance", "a material failure of risk management", "conduct which resulted in significant losses to Investec" and failure of fitness and propriety. Shareholding requirement raised to 300% of (reduced) fixed pay. — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] For FY24 the Committee "considered significant losses, write-downs and risk events" against pre-set criteria; "None of the malus and clawback thresholds were triggered". — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] 2024 policy used the PRA/FCA's removal (Oct 2023) of the 2:1 bonus cap for UK MRTs to cut fixed pay and raise the share of pay at risk; EU-entity MRTs stay at 2:1. — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] FY2026 awards (approved by the Remuneration Committee 27 May 2026, granted 28 May 2026): staff STI upfront forfeitable shares (12-month retention); STI deferred shares vesting years 1–4; LTI conditional shares vesting years 3–5; LTI performance shares with a 1 Apr 2026–31 Mar 2029 performance period. Executive LTIs vest years 3–7 plus 12-month retention. The June 2023 LTI vested at **66.16%** of the original award (e.g. CEO Fani Titi 188,236 → 124,540 shares). — [RNS 2992G via FCA NSM, 29 May 2026](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/91f6f80b-b7ac-419b-87b3-67c01963429e.html)
- [Reported] CEO Fani Titi's single-figure pay fell from £5.2m (FY24) to £3.4m (FY25) after the new policy. — [BusinessTech (search snippet)](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=829890)

**Long-Term Share Awards (broad-based)**
- [Confirmed] "In principle, all employees are eligible for, and the majority receive, long-term share incentives; this is designed to give our people a sense of ownership, so they feel invested in the organisation." — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf); 2022 wording: "We believe strongly in share ownership among our employees and therefore all employees are, in principle, eligible to participate in our long-term incentive scheme." — [Group remuneration report 2022](https://cdn.financialreports.eu/financialreports/media/filings/5231/2022/10-K/5231_10-k_2022-06-30_f4a86c79-7c52-43d3-8f7f-aa737491d734.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Because the pool starts from *risk-adjusted* EVA (with a capital charge and, historically, excluding unrealised gains), a team that grows volume by using lots of capital, taking concentrated risk or booking paper profits does not automatically grow its pool. That nudges bankers towards capital-light, fee-earning, repeat-client work — consistent with a relationship ethos.
- [Inferred] The 2:1 cap removal and the shift to lower fixed pay/more at-risk pay increases the sensitivity of pay to outcomes; combined with long share deferral this ties bankers to multi-year results, but it is also a stronger incentive overall, so it relies on the risk/culture modifier and malus to keep behaviour in check.
- [Inferred] Tension: EVA is a *team* P&L measure, and *Brogden* shows how internal transfer prices (funding rates, cost allocations) decide pay; that can create internal disputes and silo behaviour, which may cut against "One Investec" cross-selling unless allocations reward collaboration (the Pillar 3 lists "cooperation and collaboration" as a factor).

### Gaps
- The **2025 and 2026 Investec Group remuneration reports** (DLC Remuneration Committee) could not be downloaded (investec.com returned 404 pages for the guessed URLs). FY26-specific chair's statement, scorecard outcomes and any changes to the EVA description are therefore not verified; the 2024 report and 2024 Pillar 3 are the latest full texts read.
- The exact EVA formula used today (capital charge rate/cost of equity, share of EVA that goes to the pool, treatment of unrealised gains) is not disclosed in current documents; the "about 17 years"/"realised earnings" wording is from an older report seen only as a snippet.
- No public statement found that explicitly says "we do not pay on sales volume"; the evidence is the design (EVA + qualitative factors), not a stated prohibition.

## 2. Employee share ownership

### Takeaway
Staff ownership is structural, not decorative: management and staff took control of the business in 1988; staff share trusts are among the largest shareholders today (IGL Share Scheme 8.0% of Investec Limited at 31 March 2026, plus a further 5.8% in a 2002 option trust), and most employees receive long-term share awards.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] Investec Limited's largest shareholders at 31 March 2026 include "IGL Share Scheme (ZA)" with 23,158,400 shares, **8.0%** (3rd largest after the PIC 15.2% and Allan Gray 12.8%), and "Investec Limited Security Purchase and Option Scheme 2002 Trust (ZA)" with 16,987,875 shares, **5.8%**. — [Investec Limited Annual Report 2026, shareholder analysis p.174](https://apis.bse.co.bw/storage/disclosures/06/2026/6950.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Purpose of the staff share schemes: "to promote an esprit de corps within the organisation, create an awareness of the Investec Group's performance and provide an incentive to maximise individual business unit and Group performance by allowing all staff to share in the risks and rewards of the Group." Awards are forfeitable shares, conditional shares and share appreciation rights, settled from share-scheme trusts that buy shares in the market. — [Investec Limited Annual Report 2026, note 8](https://apis.bse.co.bw/storage/disclosures/06/2026/6950.pdf)
- [Confirmed] At 31 March 2021: Investec staff share schemes held **4.1% of Investec plc** and **7.1% of Investec Limited**; executive directors 0.3%/0.5%; non-executive directors 0.5%/0.1%. — [Investec Corporate Profile 2021](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2021/Investec-Corporate-Profile-2021-Online.pdf)
- [Confirmed] At 31 March 2019: staff share schemes held 1.6% of Investec plc and 3.9% of Investec Limited. — [DLC Annual Report 2019](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2019/investec-dlc-vol-1-annual-report-2019.pdf)
- [Reported] "In 1988, Investec Bank was restructured into Investec Group, giving Investec management and staff control of the company"; management and staff "own approximately 10%" of group shares (date of that figure unclear, likely mid-2010s); an Investec Employee Share Trust was part of the Nov 2003 BEE deal (with Tiso, Peu and an Entrepreneurship Development Trust) that took 25.1% of Investec Limited. — [Search synthesis of Investec/third-party pages, e.g. Financial Mail 2018](https://www.financialmail.businessday.co.za/fm/features/cover-story/2018-10-11-inside-investecs-big-split/); not verified against a primary document.

- [Confirmed] Kagiso Tiso Holdings still held 2.7% of Investec Limited at 31 March 2026 (BEE partner legacy). — [Investec Limited Annual Report 2026](https://apis.bse.co.bw/storage/disclosures/06/2026/6950.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] With staff trusts holding high single-digit percentages of the SA-listed company and most staff receiving LTIs that vest over 3–7 years, employees' wealth rises and falls with long-run group value, which supports "owner" behaviour (protecting the franchise and the balance sheet) over short-term deal volume.
- [Inferred] Some trust holdings are pools used to *fund* future awards rather than shares already owned by named individuals, so "staff own X%" figures overstate direct individual ownership.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) figure found for staff share-scheme holdings in **Investec plc**, nor a single verified "management and staff own x% of the group" figure from a primary document.
- Founders' (Koseff, Tapnack, Kantor, Bernard Kantor) historic stake sizes not verified.

## 3. Risk culture: know your client, risk appetite, credit committees, concentration, TTC loss range, three lines

### Takeaway
Credit is decided by independent committees that need **unanimous consent** and include voting members independent of the business; the stated appetite is lending to "clients we know and understand", mostly secured and **held to maturity**, inside board-set limits. Losses are managed to a published through-the-cycle range (25–45bps), and the FY26 outcome (36bps) sat within it.

### Plain-English jargon
- **Risk appetite:** the board's written limits on how much and what kind of risk the firm will take.
- **Three lines of defence:** (1) the business owns its risks; (2) independent risk & compliance challenge it; (3) internal audit checks the whole system.
- **Credit loss ratio (CLR), bps:** annual loan-loss charges as a % of loans; 36bps = 0.36%. **Through-the-cycle (TTC) range:** the average band expected across good and bad years.
- **Concentration risk:** too much lent to one borrower, sector or place.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] "Independent credit committees exist in the UK … operate under Board-approved delegated limits, policies and procedures. There is a high level of executive involvement and oversight in the credit decision-making forums … It is our policy that **all credit committees include voting members who are independent of the originating business unit. All decisions to enter into a transaction are based on unanimous consent.**" — [Pillar 3 2024, credit risk section](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Client assessment "includes consideration of their **character, integrity**, core competencies, track record and financial strength. A strong emphasis is placed on the historic and ongoing stability of income and cash flow …". "We are client-centric in our approach and **originate loans mainly with the intent of holding these assets to maturity**, thereby developing a 'hands-on' and long-standing relationship." Target clients: HNWIs, "active wealth creators", high-income professionals, "owner managers in small to mid-cap corporates", etc. — [Pillar 3 2024](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] "We target a diversified loan portfolio, **lending to clients we know and understand**. Credit and counterparty risk is always assessed with reference to the aggregate exposure to a single counterparty or group of related parties to manage concentration risk … we will consider a sell-down of exposures … if required." "Our risk appetite continues to favour lower risk, income-based lending with exposures well collateralised and credit risk taken over a short to medium term." — [Pillar 3 2024, principal risks](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Board-set limits "regulate the maximum exposures we would be comfortable to tolerate"; breaches or near-breaches "are specifically highlighted for attention, with remedial actions reported at IBP BRCC, DLC BRCC and the respective Boards"; supporting forums: Watchlist Forums, Forbearance Forum, Impairment Decision Committee, Models Forum with independent model validation. Annual review of risk appetite frameworks by IBP ERC, Group ERC, IBP and DLC BRCC and Boards. — [Pillar 3 2024](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Three levels of defence: "Level 1 – Business unit management: responsible for identifying and managing risks…"; "Level 2 – Independent risk and compliance functions: … challenging the business lines' inputs to, and outputs from, the Group's risk management…"; "Level 3 – Independent internal audit". Risk appetite frameworks use "both a top-down and bottom-up approach" and are approved by the Board "at least annually". — [Pillar 3 2024](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] "Investec has a limited appetite for unsecured debt, thus the credit risk mitigation technique most commonly used is the taking of collateral, with a strong preference for tangible assets." — [Corporate Profile 2021, principal risks](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2021/Investec-Corporate-Profile-2021-Online.pdf)
- [Confirmed] FY26: credit loss ratio on core loans **36bps** (FY25: 38bps), "within the Group's through-the-cycle (TTC) range of 25bps to 45bps"; FY27 guidance is for the CLR to stay in that range; "robust capital and liquidity levels well above Board-approved minimums". — [Final Results FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)
- [Reported] A Global Credit Committee chaired by the Group Risk and Finance Director meets **twice a week**; single-name limits of 7.5% of CET1 (plc) / 5% of tier 1 (Limited, up to 10% with board-committee approval). — [Investec IAR 2017 risk management (snippet, c.2017; see WS2 note S111)](http://reports.investec.co.za/iar2017/2_risk_management_02.php). Not re-verified in 2024–26 documents.
- [Confirmed] Investec Bank plc pay: "Specific input from the risk and compliance functions" feeds both pool size and individual awards (see Q1), linking risk culture to pay. — [Pillar 3 2024, p.79](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed, sentence only partly extracted] The market-risk section states "Our strategic intent is that proprietary trading should be …" (rest of sentence cut off in text extraction) and that trading desks act as principal mainly to serve client flow; FY26 customer-flow trading income rose 10.9% to £169.9m. — [Pillar 3 2024 market risk](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf); [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Unanimous-consent committees with independent voters plus an originate-to-hold model mean the banker who brings the deal must live with it for its whole life and persuade sceptics up front — a strong mechanical push towards knowing the client deeply rather than chasing volume.
- [Inferred] The counterweight: it also means Investec carries concentrated, often unrated exposures to entrepreneurs and property, so a single bad name can show up in results (FY26 commentary cites ECLs on "a small number of large single name exposures"); the culture is the control, which is why pay is tied to it.

### Gaps
- Current (FY26) single-name and sector/CRE concentration limits and current credit committee meeting frequency were not found in documents read; the "twice a week" and 7.5% CET1 figures are older.

## 4. Structure: DLC, no cross-guarantees, IBP, specialist teams, Mumbai, and capital discipline history

### Takeaway
Investec is two listed companies (Investec plc in London, Investec Limited in Johannesburg) run "as if it is a single unified economic enterprise", but with **ring-fenced creditors, no cross-guarantees and no flow of capital or liquidity between them**. Each half must stand on its own balance sheet. The group has repeatedly pruned businesses that did not earn their capital (Kensington and Australia 2014), separated a business that was better standalone (Ninety One 2020), and swapped UK wealth for a stake in Rathbones (2023).

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] "Investec plc and Investec Limited are separate legal entities and listings, but are bound together by contractual agreements and mechanisms"; "Investec operates as if it is a single unified economic enterprise"; shareholders have "common economic and voting interests as if Investec plc and Investec Limited were a single company"; "Creditors, however, are ring-fenced to either Investec plc or Investec Limited as there are no cross-guarantees between the companies." — [DLC Annual Report 2019](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2019/investec-dlc-vol-1-annual-report-2019.pdf); same wording in [Corporate Profile 2021](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2021/Investec-Corporate-Profile-2021-Online.pdf)
- [Confirmed] "Capital and liquidity are prohibited from flowing between the two entities and thus capital and liquidity are not fungible." Rating agencies rate Investec Bank plc (IBP) and Investec Bank Limited (IBL) separately. — [Corporate Profile 2021](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2021/Investec-Corporate-Profile-2021-Online.pdf)
- [Confirmed] DLC created July 2002; Investec Bank plc is the main UK banking subsidiary of Investec plc; IBP has its own Board, BRCC, Remuneration, Nomination and Audit committees reporting into the DLC committees. — see [WS2 / WS8 background notes](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS8_centre_postings.md); IBP Remuneration Committee pre-approves pay for IBP executive directors, PDMRs and SMCR Senior Managers — [Pillar 3 2024](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Stated culture: "We aim to sustain our distinctive, out of the ordinary culture, entrepreneurial spirit and **freedom to operate, with the discipline and obligation to do things properly for the whole of Investec**"; "All relevant Investec resources and services are on offer in every single client transaction." — [Corporate Profile 2021](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2021/Investec-Corporate-Profile-2021-Online.pdf). Careers wording: culture "defined by material ownership, freedom to operate with accountability and open and honest dialogue" — [WS8 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS8_centre_postings.md)
- [Confirmed from postings; label Inferred] 15 of 52 live central roles (Oct 2026) are in Mumbai (Investec Global Services India, IGSI), almost all non-client-facing (credit hub, finance, technology), i.e. London leads and Mumbai delivers at scale; central functions such as Reward, Company Secretarial and the Credit Hub serve the whole bank. — [WS8 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS8_centre_postings.md)
- [Reported] Capital discipline history (details in WS2):
  - Kensington (UK sub-prime lender) bought Aug 2007 for ~R4.2bn, sold to Blackstone/TPG Sept 2014 for £180m; Koseff: "the timing of the Kensington deal was very unfortunate". — [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/INVESTEC-PLC-4006220/news/Investec-Sale-of-Kensington-Group-19014449/); [WS2 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS2_firm_business.md)
  - Investec Bank (Australia) sold to Bank of Queensland for A$440m, completed 31 July 2014. — [WS2 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS2_firm_business.md)
  - Asset management demerged and listed as **Ninety One** in March 2020 (Investec retained 25%, later distributed 15% to shareholders). The 15% distribution boosted TNAV in the 2021 LTI assessment. — [WS2 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS2_firm_business.md); [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
  - UK & Channel Islands wealth (IW&I UK) combined with **Rathbones** 21 Sept 2023 in an all-share deal; Investec held 41.25% economic interest (voting capped at 29.9%). FY26 equity-accounted earnings accrued at 43.15% effective interest after Rathbones' buyback/treasury shares; share of Rathbones PBT £76.6m. — [Investegate RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/rathbones-group--rat/completion-of-the-combination/7769142); [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] No cross-guarantees mean UK bankers cannot lean on South African capital: Investec plc's lending has to be funded and capitalised by itself (on the standardised approach, so more capital per £ lent), sharpening the EVA capital charge in the UK and pushing teams towards niches where they can price for that capital.
- [Inferred] "Freedom to operate" works because the hard limits are elsewhere — capital allocation, independent unanimous credit committees and EVA-based pools. Teams are entrepreneurial inside a tightly fenced field.
- [Inferred] The 2014–2023 disposals show a pattern: when a business needs scale or capital Investec cannot justify (Kensington, Australia), or would be worth more alone or with a partner (Ninety One, Rathbones), the group exits or partners rather than doubling down — which is what an EVA culture would predict.

## 5. Targets and "dynamic capital management": what they mean for growth choices

### Takeaway
Investec manages to a return-on-equity range (13–17% medium term), not to a balance-sheet size or market-share target, and says it will use "dynamic capital management" — deploying capital where returns are highest and returning surplus (dividends at a 35–50% payout, buybacks) — to reach the upper end by FY2030. Growth is therefore chosen by return on capital, which is the same logic as the EVA bonus pool.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] FY26: ROE **13.6%** (FY25 13.9%) "within the Group's medium-term **13% to 17%** target range"; ROTE 15.7% within 14–18%; total dividend 38.5p, 46.4% payout "within the Group's current 35% to 50% payout policy"; the "c.R2.5 billion / £110 million share buy-back announced in May 2025" was completed. — [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)
- [Confirmed] "We are on track to achieve returns at the **upper end of our target range by FY2030** … Through our **dynamic capital management** approach, we will continue to deploy capital optimally to advance returns." — [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)
- [Confirmed] FY27 guidance: Investec plc ROTE 12.5–13.5% "near the lower end of the target range"; cost-to-income 52–54%; CLR within 25–45bps TTC. FY28 guidance: ROE 13.8–14.6%, ROTE 15.8–16.6%, helped by SA Corporate mid-market; "In the UK, we expect our Private Client and Corporate mid-market propositions to generate positive incremental returns into FY2030. Our dynamic capital management, including ongoing optimisation of capital is also expected to contribute to the progression of returns." — [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)
- [Reported] FY2030 ambition: ROE about 16%, ROTE about 18%; UK mid-market push to add ≥200bps of group ROE by FY2030. — [WS2 note](/home/user/Applications2027/investec/research/WS2_firm_business.md)
- [Confirmed] Capital plans go to Capital Committees **monthly**, with three-year plans stressed under conditions "most likely to cause Investec plc duress", agreed by IBP, Investec plc and DLC Capital Committees; capital is allocated "according to the optimal expected marginal risk-based return". — [Pillar 3 2024](https://www.investec.com/content/dam/investor-relations/financial-information/group-financial-results/2024/Investec-plc-group-and-Investec-Bank-plc-Pillar-3-disclosure-report-March-2024.pdf)
- [Confirmed] Executive LTIs have been measured on TNAV growth and **Return on Risk-Weighted Assets (RoRWA)** plus non-financial measures (culture and values, franchise development, governance and regulatory relationships); the 2021 LTI was capped at 135% of on-target. — [Group remuneration report 2024](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/Portal/NI-000099890/NI-000099890.pdf)
- [Confirmed] FY26 capital: Investec Limited CET1 13.6% (AIRB), Investec plc CET1 13.0% (standardised). — [FY26 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/investec--invp/final-results-31-03-2026/9578809)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Because both executive LTIs (RoRWA, TNAV) and staff bonus pools (risk-adjusted EVA) reward return on capital, the firm is wired to prefer capital-light growth (advice, fees, transactional banking, deposit-rich relationships) over balance-sheet growth — matching the 2025–26 strategies to add current accounts, transactional banking and advice for UK private and mid-market clients.
- [Inferred] Trade-off: an ROE discipline can make Investec slower to chase market share and quicker to exit businesses (as in 2014), which may look less "long-term" to staff in those units even if it protects the franchise overall. Investec plc's ROTE guidance at the low end of the range (FY27) also creates near-term pressure to grow returns in the UK.

### Gaps
- No precise public definition of "dynamic capital management" beyond the RNS wording (capital optimisation, deployment to highest returns, distributions).
- How group ROE targets cascade into divisional EVA hurdles (cost-of-capital rate used) is not disclosed.
