Chapter 2

Distinctive thing one: both halves of the market

To see why this matters, you need one picture of how the market is divided.

The market has two halves

  • The sell side: the dealers. Mostly big banks. They quote prices and trade with customers.
  • The buy side: the investors. Pension funds, asset managers, hedge funds. They manage other people's money.

For most of history, an interdealer broker only ever touched the first half. "Interdealer" means "between dealers". The investors were the banks' customers, not the broker's.

How to read this diagram Three boxes. On the left are the investors. On the right are the banks. TP ICAP, in the dark box, has a business facing each. Liquidnet faces left. Global Broking, where your internship is, faces right. A traditional interdealer broker has only the right-hand arrow.

The pieces, in plain English

PieceWhat it is
Global BrokingThe classic interdealer business. Brokers arrange trades between banks. This is your division.
LiquidnetA network TP ICAP bought in 2021. Investment firms use it to trade large blocks of shares and bonds with each other, anonymously. More than 1,100 firms are members.
NeptuneA data network TP ICAP bought in 2025. It carries information from 35 banks about which bonds they want to buy or sell.
RealQLaunched June 2026. It puts Neptune's bank data together with Liquidnet's investor connections, so banks and investors can find each other in the bond market. Nine banks own 30% of it.

Why this is distinctive

  • Tradition is, as far as public reports show, an interdealer broker through and through.
  • BGC has an electronic arm, Fenics, and its own futures exchange, FMX. Its bet is technology and exchanges. It does not own an investor network like Liquidnet.
  • TP ICAP is the one of the three that bought its way directly onto the investors' desks.

Why it matters for the future

The wall between the two halves is slowly coming down. More trading now happens directly between banks and investors, and even between investors themselves. TP ICAP's own annual report lists four ways of connecting people:

Short nameWho trades with whom
Dealer to dealerBank with bank. The traditional interdealer market.
Dealer to clientBank with investor.
Client to clientInvestor with investor. This is Liquidnet's home ground.
All to allAnyone with anyone.

A firm that only knows the dealers can serve the first row. A firm that knows both halves can serve all four.

Why a junior broker should care

In plain English A broker's career is a list of people who trust them. At a firm with one half of the market, that list can only ever contain bank traders. At a firm with both halves, the kinds of client you could serve over twenty years is wider. You are choosing the size of the room you will work in.
Be honest about the limits Liquidnet is a separate division from Global Broking. As an intern you would be on interdealer desks, not on Liquidnet. And Liquidnet's revenue was roughly flat in 2026. So do not claim you would be "working with investors" on day one. The honest claim is about direction: the two sides are being joined up. The evidence is RealQ, and the fact that in late 2026 some Liquidnet desks were moved into Global Broking.
Chapter 3

Distinctive thing two: broking turned into data

This one is quietly brilliant, and it starts with something a broker creates by accident.

The by-product

Every time a broker arranges a trade, a price is created. In a public market, everyone can see prices. In the private markets where TP ICAP works, nobody outside the trade sees them. So the firm in the middle ends up holding something valuable: a record of what things are really trading at.

Who would pay for that?

  • Banks, to value the positions they hold.
  • Investors, to check they got a fair price.
  • Risk and compliance teams, to prove things to regulators.

TP ICAP packages those prices and sells them through its division Parameta Solutions.

What "benchmark administrator" means

A benchmark is an official reference price that contracts are tied to. Because so much money depends on benchmarks, the regulator decides who is allowed to run them.

In 2022 Parameta was authorised by the UK regulator, the FCA, as a benchmark administrator. TP ICAP said this made it the first interdealer broker to run benchmarks for private markets. Its first ones were interest rate swap benchmarks built from prices in Global Broking.

In plain English The prices that TP ICAP's brokers create are trusted enough that the regulator lets the firm publish them as official reference rates. That is a stamp on the quality of what the broking desks produce.

Chapter 5

Make it yours

A fact about a firm is not a reason to work there. It becomes a reason only when you connect it to something you want. This is the step that stops an answer sounding forced.

Four safety rules

  • Say "what stands out to me", not "the only firm that".
  • Never run a rival down. "A different bet" is the phrase.
  • Say "about" before the numbers.
  • Only use a "what I want" that is true. If none of the four fits, tell the truth about what you do want, and find the fact that matches.
