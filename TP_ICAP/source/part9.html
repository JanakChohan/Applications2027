Chapter 2

The shop

Do not undersell this one. Years of real selling, starting at 14, is rare. Most candidates have never sold anything to anyone.

What it was, in plain words

Say it like this "From the age of 14 I worked in a shop selling school uniform to parents. I did it for [number] years."

How it matches the broker's job

In the shopOn a broking desk
You talked to customers all day.A broker talks to clients all day.
The same families came back each year.A broker deals with the same clients again and again.
Parents trusted you to tell them what they really needed.A client trusts a broker to give a straight price.
Some weeks were a rush, such as just before term.Markets have rushes too, when news breaks.
Some customers were stressed, or said no.A broker hears "no" many times a day.

How to read this table Left is what you did. Right is the same thing in broking. Pick the two rows that are most true for you. You do not need all six.

Link to the job advert

The advert asks for someone who enjoys a "high pressured sales" environment, has "a passion for working with people", and is "resilient". The shop is your proof for all three.

Link to the firm

In plain English TP ICAP does not sell a product. It earns a fee each time a client chooses to trade through it. So it only does well if clients keep coming back. You learned that lesson in a shop: look after the customer and they return next year.

Ready to say

20 seconds "From the age of 14 I worked in a shop selling school uniform to parents. The same families came back every year, so I learned early that being straight with a customer matters more than one sale. A broker works the same way: the same clients, every day, and you only earn if they trust you enough to come back."

Details that would make it stronger

  • How many years you worked there.
  • One real moment: a difficult parent, a very busy day, or a customer who came back and asked for you.
  • A number: how many customers on a busy day, or how many seasons you worked.
  • Why you started at 14. That shows drive. The advert's word is "entrepreneurial".

Chapter 3

Pictet Asset Management

This is your strongest link, because of where you sat: in the middle. And the people on one side of you were exactly the kind of institutions TP ICAP serves.

What it was, in plain words

Say it like this "I did a summer internship in the Global Client Group at Pictet Asset Management. I sat between the clients, which were pension funds and sovereign wealth funds, and the portfolio managers inside the firm."

How it matches the broker's job

At PictetOn a broking desk
You sat between two groups: clients and portfolio managers.A broker sits between two sides of a trade.
The two groups spoke differently. You had to put one side's message into words the other could use.A broker carries messages between two sides until they agree.
The clients were huge institutions.A broker's clients are huge institutions.
The relationship was built on trust and getting details right.The same.
You saw the world from the client's chair.A good broker understands what the client needs before they ask.

Link to the job advert

  • The advert says the firm's clients include "pension and hedge funds, and asset managers". You have already worked with pension funds, from inside an asset manager.
  • It asks for someone who can "understand and translate data to a wide-ranging audience". Passing a portfolio manager's thinking on to a client is exactly that.
  • It asks for "excellent attention to detail". [Add what you did at Pictet that needed care.]

Link to the firm

In plain English TP ICAP sits in the middle of the market, between big institutions. Through its Liquidnet business it connects more than 1,100 investment firms. You have worked inside one of those kinds of firm. You know what they care about. Few candidates can say that.

Ready to say

25 seconds "At Pictet Asset Management I interned in the Global Client Group. I sat between the clients, which were pension funds and sovereign wealth funds, and the portfolio managers. My job was to help each side understand the other. That middle seat was the part I liked most, and it is what a broker does all day, only faster."

The honest contrast, and why it helps you

They may ask: "If you liked Pictet, why not stay in asset management?" Have a true answer ready. Here is one shape. Only use it if it is how you really feel.

If this is true for you "At Pictet I liked being the bridge. But those relationships move slowly, over months. What draws me to broking is the same bridge, live, many times a day, with a result I can see."

Chapter 4

Debating

You said you love this one but cannot see the link. There is a strong link. You just have to look at how a debate works, not what it is about.

What it was, in plain words

Say it like this "At university I compete in British Parliamentary debating. You get the topic fifteen minutes before you speak, and you are told which side to argue."

How it matches the broker's job

In a debateOn a broking desk
You get the topic fifteen minutes before. You must think fast with little information.News breaks and prices move. A broker must react in seconds.
You do not choose your side. You have to understand both.A broker stands between a buyer and a seller and must understand what each one wants.
Other speakers interrupt you with questions while you are talking.A trading floor is constant interruption. You answer and carry on.
You have a strict time limit, so every word must count.A broker has to be short, clear and exact.
You must listen closely to everyone before you, or your reply misses the point.Listening is most of a broker's job.
Four teams are ranked. You lose rounds, and go again an hour later.A broker hears "no", and picks up the phone again.
You work with a partner.A broker works as part of a desk.
How to read this table Seven links. The first three are the strongest: thinking fast, seeing both sides, and handling interruptions. Choose two and you have your answer.

Link to the job advert

  • "Comfortable with ambiguous, ever-changing situations in a fast-paced environment." An unseen topic and fifteen minutes is exactly that.
  • "The ability not only to work with others, but to influence." Debating is practice at influencing people.
  • "Analytical thinking and problem-solving." You break down a topic you have never seen and build a case.

Link to the firm

In plain English At TP ICAP both sides of every trade are clients. So a broker cannot take one side. They have to see the deal from both ends. Debating trained you to do that, because you never get to choose which side you argue.

Ready to say

25 seconds "I compete in British Parliamentary debating. You get the topic fifteen minutes before, and you don't choose your side. So I've learned to think quickly with little information, and to understand both sides of an argument. A broker stands between a buyer and a seller, in a market that moves fast, so I think it is good training."
One trap to avoid A debater wants to win the argument. A broker wants two people to agree. Do not make yourself sound like someone who argues with clients. Lead with thinking fast, listening, and seeing both sides.

Details that would make it stronger

  • How long you have debated, and roughly how many competitions.
  • A result, if you have a good one.
  • One round that went badly and what you did in the next one. That is a resilience story.

Use it for these questions

"A time you worked under pressure."   "A time you had to think on your feet."   "What do you do outside your studies?"   "Why should we choose you?"
