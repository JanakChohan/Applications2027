The three steps, once more

How to read this diagram Left to right. A fact, then what follows from it, then why it matters to you. The dark box is the one most people forget. Without step 3, it is just trivia.
Sentences 1 to 5

The kind of work

1
What it is. "Broking means connecting big buyers and sellers who want to trade with each other. The broker finds the other side."
2
How it earns money. "It earns a small fee, called commission, on each trade it arranges. It does not bet its own money."
3
Who the clients are, and what they want. "Banks, hedge funds, asset managers and energy firms. They want the other side of their trade found fast, at a fair price, without anyone knowing it is them."
4
How it differs from the two nearest jobs. "A trader takes the risk. A broker takes none. A bank salesperson offers one bank's prices. A broker sits between many banks."
5
One thing changing it, and what you think. "More simple trades are moving onto screens. I think that makes people matter more for the big, difficult trades, where a client needs someone they trust."
The test Say these five to someone who does not work in finance. If they can say it back, you understand it.
Add your step 3 to sentence 4 "...which is why I want broking and not trading. I like being in the middle, getting two sides to agree, more than I like taking a bet."
Sentences 6 to 10

The firm

6
What the firm is. "TP ICAP is one of the world's largest interdealer brokers. That means it is a broker between big banks and other large firms. It made about £2.35 billion in 2025."
7
Its parts, and where you would be. "It has four parts. Global Broking is the biggest, at about 58%. The others are Energy and Commodities, Liquidnet, and Parameta Solutions. I would be in Global Broking, in London."
8
What makes it different from two rivals you can name. "Its main rivals are BGC and Tradition. Two things stand out to me. It owns Liquidnet, which serves investors and not just banks. And it sells the price data its own brokers create, through Parameta." Which means: "it earns from more than commission, and it reaches both sides of the market."
9
Its values, and one you can prove. "Accountability, Adaptability and Authenticity. The firm calls them Triple-A. Under Accountability it says: speak up when things are not right." Your proof: one time you spoke up, or owned a mistake. One sentence.
10
One recent thing, with the date. "On 3 November 2026 it reported revenue of about £1.9 billion for the first nine months, up 9%. Global Broking grew 16% in the third quarter." Which means: "the part I am applying to is the part growing fastest."
Three numbers is plenty For the whole interview, pick three: £2.35 billion (size), 58% (Global Broking's share), 16% (its growth last quarter). More than three and you sound like you are reading a list.
Say "one of the largest" Not "the largest". A rival, BGC, reported similar revenue.
Sentences 11 to 14

The job

11
What the job produces, and for whom. "Trades that are matched for clients, and commission for the desk. As an intern I would be learning how the brokers do that."
12
Three things you would do on a normal day. "Sit with brokers and watch how they deal with clients. Move between the Rates, Credit, Equities and FX desks. Go to workshops to learn the products."
13
What is hard about it. "It is fast and high-pressure. You hear 'no' a lot. And your results are seen every day." Which is why I: "...think I'd suit it. I'd rather be measured openly than not know how I'm doing."
14
What makes someone good at it. In the advert's own words. "Someone resilient and entrepreneurial, who builds relationships with ease. Good in a high-pressure sales team. Careful with detail. Comfortable when things keep changing."
Sentences 15 to 20

You

Only you can write these. Here is the shape of each. Fill the gaps with true things.

15
Who you are and what you are aiming at. "I'm a final-year [subject] student at [university]. I want to start my career as a broker."
16
Why this kind of work. Where it started. "It started when I [what you did]. I liked [the part that is like broking]."
17
Why this firm. Sentence 8, then "which is why I". "TP ICAP reaches both banks and investors, and sells its own data. Which is why I want to start here: [what that gives you]."
18
Why this job. Sentence 14, matched to two things you have done. "The job needs someone who builds relationships and stays calm when it is busy. I've [thing one], and I've [thing two]."
19
Two strengths with proof. One weakness with what you are doing about it. "I'm good at [strength]. For example [proof]. I'm also [strength]. For example [proof]. I'm working on [weakness] by [action]."
20
Where you want to be, and why start here. "In a few years I want my own clients on a broking desk. This internship leads straight to a desk, so it is the right place to start."

3. One news story and your view

Say it like this "On 5 November the Bank of England raised interest rates to 4%. It was the first rise since 2023. What interests me is not the price but the trading. Trading in sterling interest rate swaps had more than halved earlier this year, while the Bank sat still. Now the Bank has changed direction. A broker is paid when clients trade, so I expect that trading to pick up again."

Four answers, ready to shape

Each is about 60 seconds. The words in square brackets are yours to add.

"Tell us about yourself." "Thanks for watching. I'm a final-year [subject] student at [university], and I want to start my career as a broker. It started when I [what you did]. I liked being the person in the middle, getting two sides to agree. The job needs someone who builds relationships and stays calm when it is busy. I've [thing one], and I've [thing two]. That is why I'm applying."
"Why broking?" "Thank you for watching. Broking means connecting big buyers and sellers and finding the other side of a trade. The broker earns a fee and does not take the risk. I want that because of [what you did]. I found I'm happiest in the middle, getting two people to agree. A trader takes a bet. I'd rather build clients who call me first."
"Why TP ICAP?" "Thanks for watching. Two things stand out to me against rivals like BGC and Tradition. TP ICAP owns Liquidnet, which serves investors and not just banks. And it sells the price data its own brokers create. Which means it reaches both sides of the market and earns from more than commission. Which is why I want to start here. [What that gives you.] And Global Broking grew 16% last quarter, so it is a good time to learn."
"Why should we choose you?" "The advert asks for someone resilient, who builds relationships with ease and is careful with detail. I can show each one. [Proof one.] [Proof two.] [Proof three.] And I know the job is fast and that you hear no a lot. That is the kind of place I do my best work."
