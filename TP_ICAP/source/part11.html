The broker's question For every headline, ask: "Who has to trade because of this?" A broker is paid when clients trade. So news matters when it forces banks and funds to change what they hold.

Desk 1

Rates

The biggest desk at TP ICAP. This is the safest topic to pick.

What happened

  • The US central bank meets on 8 to 9 December. Markets put the chance of a rate rise at about 96% or more. Its rate is 3.75% to 4% now.
  • The Bank of England meets on 17 December. It raised its rate to 4% on 5 November.
  • UK inflation for October was 3.6%. That is the highest in a year, but a little below the 3.7% expected.
  • So a December rise in the UK became slightly less certain. Reports say the chance slipped from about 90% to about 80%.
  • UK 10-year government borrowing costs eased to about 5.15%, the lowest in over two weeks.

Which means

When people are sure what a central bank will do, there is little to trade. When they are unsure, banks and funds keep adjusting. The UK has just become a little less sure. That doubt is what keeps a rates desk busy.

Say it like this. About 30 seconds "I've been following the two central bank meetings in December. Markets are almost certain the US will raise rates on the 9th. The UK on the 17th is less clear. Inflation came in at 3.6%, just under forecast, and the chance of a rise slipped from about 90% to about 80%. For a broker that doubt matters more than the rate itself, because banks and pension funds have to keep adjusting until they know."
If you want a deeper version Part 4 has your full story: sterling swap trading more than halved earlier this year, then the Bank of England changed direction. This week's news fits straight on the end of it.
Desk 2

Equities

The loudest story of the week.

What happened

  • Technology shares sold off sharply. On Wednesday 25 November the Nasdaq 100, an index of big US technology firms, was briefly about 20% below its October high. It recovered most of the day's fall by the close.
  • The fear gauge jumped. The VIX, which measures how wild investors expect shares to be, closed around 28 after touching about 35.
  • The worry: companies have spent and borrowed huge sums to build AI data centres. Investors are asking whether that will pay back. Higher interest rates make it worse.

Which means

When shares swing like this, investors rush to buy protection. Protection means options. Options are what the Equities desk at a broker deals in. So a scared stock market is a busy equity derivatives desk.

Say it like this. About 30 seconds "This week technology shares fell hard. The Nasdaq 100 was briefly about 20% below its October high, and the VIX, the fear gauge, closed around 28. The worry is whether the money spent on AI will pay back. For a broker, that means investors rushing to buy protection through options, which is what the equities desk deals in. It was already TP ICAP's fastest-growing desk this year."
A neat extra, if you want it Use the rule of 16 from Part 3. Divide the VIX by 16 to get a typical daily move. 28 divided by 16 is about 1.75. So the market expects shares to move about 1.75% a day. Normal is under 1%.
Desk 3

Credit

The quiet story that few candidates will mention. It links straight to the equities one.

What happened

  • Risky companies suddenly pay more to borrow. The extra interest they pay over a safe government, called the credit spread, rose from about 3.3% to about 3.8% in the week to 20 November.
  • Safer companies barely moved. Their spread went from about 0.90% to about 0.98%.
  • The weakest companies were hit hardest.
  • The worry is the same as in shares: heavy borrowing to pay for AI building.

Which means

Lenders are getting pickier. When spreads move like this, investors sell the bonds they are worried about and buy insurance against companies failing. Both go through a Credit desk. And because many bonds have no screen price, people need a broker to find a buyer.

Say it like this. About 30 seconds "What caught my eye this week was the bond market, not just shares. The extra interest that risky companies pay to borrow rose by nearly half a percentage point in a week, while safer companies barely moved. So lenders are getting pickier, mostly over debt taken on to build AI. For a broker that means investors selling the bonds they are worried about, and in credit many bonds have no screen price, so they need someone to find the buyer."
Desk 4, and one extra

FX, and oil

FX: what happened

  • The pound is about $1.31 and about €1.19.
  • Against the euro it hit its highest level of 2026 on 18 November, then slipped.
  • On scared days, money moved into the Japanese yen and Swiss franc. These are seen as safe.

Which means

Currencies follow interest rates. With the UK and the US both raising rates, the question is who goes faster. Every change in that guess moves the pound against the dollar, and companies and funds have to re-fix their currency deals.

Say it like this. About 20 seconds "The pound is around $1.31. What moves it now is which central bank raises rates faster, the UK or the US. Each time that guess changes, firms have to re-fix their currency deals, and that is what an FX desk arranges."

Oil: what happened

  • Brent, the main oil price, is in the low to mid $90s. It rose about 3% on Wednesday.
  • Iran said two tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. That claim has not been confirmed. Attacks on Saudi oil sites were also reported.
  • But a surplus of oil is expected next year, which holds the price down.

Which means

Oil is not one of your four desks. It belongs to Energy and Commodities. But it drives inflation, so it sits behind the whole rates story. And it is over half of that division's revenue.

Careful Oil news changes by the hour, and some claims are unconfirmed. If you mention it, say "reports of" and do not quote a precise price.

If they ask a follow-up

They askYou say

"What is the VIX?""A measure of how much investors expect US shares to move. It is called the fear gauge."
"What is a credit spread?""The extra interest a company pays to borrow, compared with a safe government."
"Is falling share prices bad for a broker?""Not directly. A broker is paid on how much clients trade, and they trade more when markets move."
"What happens if the Bank does not raise in December?""Then everyone who bet on a rise has to undo it. That is trading too."
