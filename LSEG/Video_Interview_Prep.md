# LSEG video interview: the questions that come up

R0123386 Business Management Summer Internship. Invite received; 7 days to complete, one sitting, on the Capp platform (cappassessments.com). Built 9 Oct 2026 from candidate reports and LSEG's own pages.

**Honest caveat first.** LSEG does not publish its questions and they rotate. No source gives the full set for this internship and year. What follows is (1) every question candidates have actually reported, labelled with where it came from, then (2) the questions most likely in a Capp strengths interview, mapped to what LSEG says it is testing. If you prepare stories for section 2, you will be ready for anything in section 1.

## 2. Questions candidates have reported (LSEG, recent cycles)

Grouped by type. "Intern" means a summer intern review; "Grad" a graduate programme review. All from Glassdoor, Wall Street Oasis or The Student Room search snippets: anecdotes, not official.

**Motivation**
1. Why do you want to work at LSEG? (Intern, several)
2. Why this role / why this programme? (Grad, TSR)
3. Where do you see yourself in 5 years? (Intern)
4. What would you improve about the finance sector? (Grad, TSR: "later questions were about finance sector and what you'd improve about it")

**Strengths and working style**

5. How do you gain credibility in your work? (Intern/Grad, April 2026)
6. Do you consider yourself more of a cooperative person or a strong independent person? / Are you more inclined to work individually and take full responsibility, or collaborate with others? (Grad)

**Scenarios ("imagine you are at LSEG...")**

7. Imagine you are working at LSEG and you have been given your first project to manage. How would you make sure that your team members feel confident in you as you work on the project? (London, recent)
8. How would you lead a team if you were meeting them for the first time? (Grad)

**Experience**

9. Describe a difficult challenge you faced (and how you resolved it). (April 2026; also older)
10. Tell me about a time you faced a problem. (Grad, TSR)
11. A time of challenge or failure and how you dealt with it. (WSO, older)
12. A time you did something new to improve a process. (WSO, older)
13. Your experience working in groups. (WSO, older)
14. Describe a challenging project, the steps you took to overcome obstacles, and the outcome. (Glassdoor)

One reviewer said the scenario questions "required no understanding of LSEG". Even so, the motivation questions plainly do, and LSEG lists "what inspires you about a career at LSEG" as a theme.

## 3. The likely set for your interview

A Capp strengths interview usually mixes three kinds of prompt: "what energises you", "how would you handle X", and "tell me about a time". Combining LSEG's four themes with the reports above, expect roughly this set of eight:

| # | Likely question | Theme | What they score |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Why LSEG, and why this internship? | Motivation | Specific, real interest; knows LSEG is more than "the stock exchange" |
| B | What inspires you about a career in financial markets / at LSEG? | Inspiration | Genuine energy, not recital |
| C | What do you enjoy doing most, and what drains you? / When are you at your best? | Strengths | Self-awareness; energy that matches the work |
| D | How do you feel when you're working with data / a large amount of information? | Strengths (JD: "curiosity for data") | Enjoyment of analysis; how you get to a conclusion |
| E | First project / new team / credibility scenario (Q7, Q8, Q5 above) | Scenario | Trust-building, listening, clarity, follow-through |
| F | Tell me about a challenge or setback and what you did | Experience / resilience | Ownership, learning, calm |
| G | Plans change at short notice / lots of tasks at once: how do you handle it? | Strengths (JD: "fast paced... flexibility") | Prioritising, staying steady |
| H | Team vs independent / how do you work with others? | Strengths (JD: "collaborate") | Honest preference plus adaptability |

Also possible: "What would you improve about financial markets?" and "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

## 4. How to answer each (outlines, not scripts)

Shape for strengths questions: **answer in one line, then give a real example, then say why it energises you, then link it to the seat.** Aim for about 90 seconds. Unlike a competency answer, the "why I enjoy it" part counts as much as the example.

**A. Why LSEG / why this internship**
- Hook: the specific thing you noticed (from the pack, Part 12): LSEG owns venues (London Stock Exchange, Tradeweb, FXall), but its clearing house LCH must, by its own rules, clear for rival venues on fair terms, and banks still sit on its risk committee. That trust is why SwapClear handles over 90% of cleared interest rate swaps and cleared a record $1,941trn in 2025.
- Why this seat: business management sits where those businesses join, and Markets is the fastest-growing division (+11.9% in H1 2026).
- Close: one thing you'd like to work on, e.g. measuring whether FX clients have moved their workflow from FXall into Workspace.
- Avoid: prestige, "the building", calling it LSE.

**B. What inspires you about a career here**
- One real moment that got you interested in how markets work (a lecture, a news story, a trade you followed). Then one live LSEG issue you find interesting (LSE 24 trading from H1 2027, T+1 settlement from 11 Oct 2027, London listings reform), and why you'd want to be near it.

**C. What energises you / drains you**
- Name a true energiser that fits (making sense of messy information, sorting out a problem for someone, organising a group). Give a short example. Then name a real drainer that isn't central to the job (e.g. repetitive work with no feedback), and say how you manage it.
- Avoid: "I have no weaknesses", or a drainer that is the job ("dealing with clients", "detail").

**D. Data**
- An example where you took raw data or information, found the "so what" and acted on it. Say what you enjoyed (the moment the pattern appears). Link it to the JD: "curiosity for data... draw conclusions and propose solutions".

**E. First project / new team / credibility**
- Listen first (one-to-ones, learn what each person cares about), agree the goal and who owns what, deliver a small early win, be open about what you don't know, follow through on every promise. Credibility comes from doing what you said and getting facts right. Use a real example if you have one ("When I led X...").

**F. Challenge or setback**
- Short situation; what *you* did (not "we"); the result, with a number if possible; what you'd do differently. Keep the setup under 20 seconds.

**G. Changing demands / many tasks**
- How you triage (deadline and impact), when you tell people early about a clash, and one real example. The pack's priority rule: surface the clash, rank by consequence, offer options, confirm in writing.

**H. Team vs independent**
- Give an honest preference, then show you can switch: "I'm at my best owning a piece of work end to end, but I check in early so it fits the team's plan." The JD says interns are "individual contributors" and also "work as a team", so both halves matter.

**Five years / improve finance**
- Five years: building expertise in market infrastructure (e.g. through LSEG's graduate programme), more client or strategy responsibility. Keep it believable.
- Improve finance: pick one concrete thing with both sides, e.g. cost and transparency of market data (FCA 2024 study) or getting more UK companies to list. State a view in one line.

## 5. Recording checklist

- Laptop at eye level, light in front of you, plain background, phone on silent, water nearby.
- Use the unlimited prep time: write 3 or 4 bullet words, not a script. Reading on camera shows.
- First sentence answers the question. Smile at the start and the end. Look at the lens.
- If you stumble, keep going; one stumble doesn't matter, but stopping does.
- Keep your energy consistent across all eight questions. Strengths reviewers look for answers that sound true, not rehearsed.
- Do it within the 7 days, and earlier is better: LSEG reviews on a rolling basis.

## 6. Before you record: prepare 6 stories

Have six stories ready that you can reshape for any question: a data or analysis task you enjoyed; a team you led or organised; a setback; a time you changed how something was done; a time you dealt with a difficult person or client; a time you juggled competing deadlines. For each, know the result and why you enjoyed it.
