What the posting really says, what a day in each of the four seats looks like, who sits around you in the Markets division, and the lines you must never cross.

This part does three jobs. First, it reads the job description (JD) line by line and translates each sentence into concrete work at a named LSEG unit. Second, it shows the seat in motion: a worked day for each of the four possible placements, how a request travels through the team, and what happens when two people want your time at once. Third, it maps the people and teams around the role, using only names and facts that appear in public sources, with every box tagged by how sure we are.

Key term

Markets division: one of LSEG's four reporting segments since 1 January 2025, formed by merging the old Capital Markets and Post Trade segments. The other three are Data & Analytics, FTSE Russell and Risk Intelligence [S22, S23]. Inside Markets, LSEG runs separate cash-generating units (CGUs), the smallest groups of assets that earn money on their own: Digital & Securities Markets, FX, Tradeweb, LCH Ltd, LCH SA, Post Trade Solutions and Regulatory Reporting [S22]. That list is the best public map of how Markets is run.

Figure 2 shows what every one of the four seats has in common: work arrives as a request from someone inside LSEG (or from a client through an account manager), it gets triaged, analysed and turned into a product, and the product is judged by an outcome.

The insight that changes how you prepare

The four "exposure" bullets in the JD are not a description of one London team. The New York Business Management internship (R0123464) uses the same four bullets word for word, the same five businesses and the same "dynamic nature of the division" line [S9]. The London graduate programme R0123406 reuses them as "exposure/rotations" [S6]. Confirmed The bullets describe a pool of Markets placements. The JD then says how you land in one: "To allow us to place you in the right area, during the recruitment process we will get to know your skills and interest and how they align with the needs of the business" [S1, S2].

So the recruitment process is also a matching process. Two things follow. First, you need a credible answer for all four seats, because you do not know which one you will get. Second, you can shape the match: an assessor who hears a specific, evidenced reason for preferring one seat has something to work with. Inferred

Key term

EBITDA and margin: EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, a rough measure of operating profit. The margin is EBITDA divided by income. A 58.6% margin means about 59p of every £1 of Markets income is left after operating costs.

"As an intern, you will join one of our flagship trading services - London Stock Exchange, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise - or LCH our global leading multi-asset clearing houses."
Means Five brands, which map onto the CGUs above. The Annual Report lists the venues: "the London Stock Exchange, AIM, Turquoise, FXall, FX Matching and Tradeweb", combined with "multi-asset class clearing infrastructure" [S23]. Confirmed

LCH Group, parent of LCH Ltd (UK) and LCH SA (France), is about 94% owned (94.4% per the Annual Report, 94.2% per the 2024 results), rising above 95% after a €70m minority buy-in agreed in July 2026 [S300, S121, S221]. Confirmed
Structural fact These are separate regulated legal entities with their own boards. That shapes everything you do: data, people and decisions do not move freely between them [S300, S304].
Trap Do not call LCH "the LSEG clearing division". Name LCH Ltd and LCH SA as separate companies in different countries [S22, S28].
Key term

RIE (Recognised Investment Exchange): a UK exchange recognised by the FCA under the Financial Services and Markets Act. It must run fair and orderly markets and police its own rules. London Stock Exchange plc operates its markets "in its capacity as a Recognised Investment Exchange" [S13, S95].

Key term

MTF (multilateral trading facility): a regulated trading venue run by an investment firm rather than an exchange. It brings many buyers and sellers together under non-discretionary rules. Turquoise is an MTF; so are LSEG FX's venues, authorised by the FCA in the UK and the Central Bank of Ireland in the EU [S95, S341].

Means This is almost word for word the Annual Report's Markets introduction ("We help customers across the trade lifecycle optimise their financial resource consumption and risk, manage their regulatory reporting obligations ... Unit by stage Raise capital: LSE plc primary markets. Trade: LSE, Turquoise, FXall, FX Matching, Tradeweb. Clear: LCH Ltd and LCH SA. After the trade: Post Trade Solutions (compression and optimisation) and Regulatory Reporting [S22, S300].
Structural fact "Clearing and reporting obligations" are legal duties on LSEG's customers. LSEG sells compliance as a product.
Use it If you can walk an interviewer through the trade life cycle and name the LSEG unit at each stage, you have shown you understand "how it fits together", which the JD asks for later.
Key term

Trade life cycle: the stages a trade goes through. Pre-trade (raising capital, getting prices), execution (agreeing the trade on a venue), clearing (a central counterparty steps in between buyer and seller), settlement (cash and asset actually swap) and post-trade (reporting, compression, record-keeping). Liquidity pool: a place where buyers and sellers meet, so you can trade size without moving the price much.

Use it Prepare one story where you reacted fast and one where you planned over months. The strengths interview tests both.

Key term

FXall: LSEG's dealer-to-client FX platform. A buy-side firm or corporate asks several banks for prices at once (RFQ, request for quote), sees streamed prices, or uses an anonymous order book and algorithms (TWAP, Iceberg) [S342]. It also handles post-trade steps: confirmations, settlement instructions, reporting [S340].

Key term

FX Matching: LSEG's dealer-to-dealer venue, an anonymous central limit order book where banks trade with each other [S22, S303]. Liquidity provider (LP): a bank or trading firm that quotes prices others can trade on.

Key term

ADV (average daily volume): total value traded divided by trading days. LSEG's main FX volume KPI: $525bn in 2025, $561bn in H1 2026 [S301, S302]. STP (straight-through processing): a trade flows from execution to the client's booking and settlement systems with no manual re-keying. FXall offers STP via "Trade Notification" [S340].

Key term

TCA (transaction cost analysis): measuring how much a trade really cost against a benchmark, to prove best execution. FXall's version is Trade Performance Reporting, to "evaluate the cost and effectiveness of your execution strategies" [S340]. Post-sale activity: everything after the contract is signed: onboarding, permissions, connectivity, renewals, billing queries.

Structural factAt a clearing house, risk and revenue move together. SwapClear cleared a record $1,941trn of interest rate swap notional in 2025 and holds over 90% of the cleared market [S22, S23]. Net Treasury Income (interest earned on margin cash) rose 12.6% in H1 2026, "driven by strong growth in cash collateral balances supported by elevated market volatility" [S24]. Volatility lifts revenue and default risk at the same time.
Key term

CCP (central counterparty): a clearing house that "sits in the middle of trades as the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer" [S304]. If a member defaults, the CCP takes over its positions. LCH has managed nine member defaults, all within the initial margin it held [S351].

Key term

Margin: collateral a member must post. Initial margin covers the likely loss if the member defaults; LCH sets it at 99.7% confidence (99% for Listed Rates) [S351]. Default fund: a pool all members pay into, used only after the defaulter's own margin and LCH's own capital. Notional: the face value a derivative is based on, not money that changes hands.

How the work splits

Three splits matter for an intern.

  • London versus the hubs. 19 of the 45 open Markets roles are in Taguig City, Manila: operations, onboarding, PMO and control roles [S4]. India holds 8,541 of LSEG's 28,516 staff, the UK 5,043 [S300]. London keeps product, client, regulatory and leadership roles. Your "operational improvement" project will probably involve a team you meet on video. Inferred
  • Business versus the centre. Risk ownership is assigned per principal risk to named executives: central counterparty risk to the Head of Markets; technology to the CIO; data to divisional heads and the COO [S302]. First-line teams inside Markets own their risks; Group Risk provides "Coverage (Data and Analytics, Markets, Post Trade and Operations)" [S314].
  • Client coverage. A new Group Head of Sales and Account Management (Chris Coleman, January 2026) suggests account management is being pulled towards a central model [S306]. Inferred for the implication. Ask about it.

Why have a business management function at all? Figure 6 shows the mechanism. Markets is a set of separate regulated companies plus central functions. Without someone who owns the numbers and the calendar, every party has to coordinate with every other party.

Figure 7 follows one request through the team. It is an FX client issue because that seat has the most moving parts, but the shape (log, triage, route, fix, confirm, learn) is the same for a strategy question.

The scarce resource in any business management team is analyst time. Interns hit this in week two, when two people ask for something by the same day. Figure 8 gives you a rule to use and, in interview, a strengths answer about competing priorities.

The compliance and control dimension

Markets is the most regulated corner of LSEG. LSE plc is an RIE supervised by the FCA; LCH Ltd is a Recognised Clearing House supervised by the Bank of England; the FX venues are MTFs licensed in the UK and Ireland [S13, S304, S341]. As an intern you will see information that could move markets or that a client has trusted LSEG with. The rules below are bright lines: there is no grey area for an intern, because the answer is always "stop and ask".

Key term

MAR (UK Market Abuse Regulation): the law against insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information and market manipulation. Inside information is "information of a precise nature, which has not been made public" that is "likely to have a significant effect on the prices" if it were (Article 7) [S349].

Key term

Insider list: a record issuers and their advisers must keep of "all persons who have access to inside information", with the reason and "date and time" of access, kept for "at least five years" (MAR Article 18) [S348]. If you work on a live deal, your name may go on one.

Key term

Three lines of defence: "Accountability for risk management ... sits within the first line of defence, independent oversight and challenge with the second line and objective, independent assurance with the third line" [S300]. First line is the business (you); second is Risk and Compliance; third is Internal Audit.

Key term

STOR (suspicious transaction and order report): a report a venue files with the regulator when trading may be abusive. Market Supervision files them for LSE and Turquoise trading "as outlined in the UK Financial Conduct Authority's Code of Market Conduct and MAR" [S10].
