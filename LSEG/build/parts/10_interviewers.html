Reading the room and adapting live

In a live interview you get signals. Use them to give the interviewer what they need, not to manage them. All points below are Inferred from the stage formats.

SignalLikely meaningGood response
They ask for detail ("How exactly did you...?", "What was the number?")Probably a practitioner, scoring depth.Give the number and the reason. Shorter context, more mechanism.
They ask about motivation and valuesProbably scoring fit.Use LSEG's value words, linked to a real story [S271].
They look down or start typing while you speakOften note-taking against a score sheet.Keep going at an even pace; pause briefly after key points so they can write.
They cut in or move on earlyThey have what they need, or time is short.Stop, do not finish the speech. "Happy to say more on that later."
A follow-up challenges your viewTesting reasoning under pressure, not your conclusion.Take the point seriously, say what would change your mind, hold your view if it still stands.
A question you cannot answerTesting honesty."I don't know that figure. My reasoning would be..." Never invent a number.
Silence after your answerThinking, writing, or waiting to see if you add more.Ask "Would you like me to go into more detail on any part?"
In the group exercise, someone dominatesThe observers are watching how others respond [S292].Bring in a quiet person by name; summarise to move the group on.
Don't do this

No manipulation tactics. Deliberate mirroring, flattery, or dropping the names of senior leaders you have never met will look false, and assessors are trained to notice Inferred. The aim of reading the room is to answer the question they are really asking.

The pattern

Across the quotes, four themes repeat: steady growth in Markets, new venues and digital products (LSE 24, PSM, tokenisation), end-to-end workflow, and trust through risk management and resilience Inferred. An answer that links growth to trust ("new products only work if clients trust the infrastructure") speaks the same language as the people who run the division.

Referencing a public statement appropriately

Say this in the interview

"In the LSE 24 release in July, LSEG said it wanted to give clients flexibility beyond normal trading hours. I wondered how you test demand for an overnight session before launch."

It cites the source (a release), uses the idea, and asks a question. The person's name is optional; the source is not.

Don't say this

"As Julia Hoggett said..." to someone who does not work on LSE, or "I saw Daniel Maguire worked at J.P. Morgan". Careers of senior people are not interview content. Comments on activists, share price or rumoured deals are also off limits Inferred.

  • Name the document, not the person, unless the interviewer works directly in that leader's area and the quote is on point.
  • Quote accurately or paraphrase. If you are unsure of exact words, say "LSEG said something like..." Never invent a quote.
  • Date it. "In the July release" shows currency and lets them correct you if things have moved on.
  • Turn it into a question. A public statement is a starting point for curiosity, not proof of insider knowledge.
  • Stay professional. Use only what leaders said in their roles. Nothing personal, nothing from social media.
