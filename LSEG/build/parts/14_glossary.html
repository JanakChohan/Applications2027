Part 14

Glossary

Every term in this pack in plain English, with the LSEG business it touches, so you can explain any of them to an interviewer in two sentences.

This glossary is alphabetical. Each entry gives a plain-English definition first. Where the term matters to a specific part of LSEG, an LSEG angle line tells you which business it touches and gives one fact you can use. Numbers and dates carry source markers that match the source list in Part 15. Definitions with no marker are standard market vocabulary rather than facts about LSEG.

How to use this part

Do not learn it front to back. Before each stage, cover the definitions and test yourself on the twenty terms most likely to come up for a Markets intern: CCP, clearing, settlement, margin, default waterfall, open access, MTF, RFQ, FX spot, FX forward, fixing, interest rate swap, IPO, ISM, PISCES, T+1, EMIR 3, net treasury income, organic growth and three lines of defence. If you can say each in one sentence and name the LSEG business it belongs to, you are ahead of most candidates.

Key term

The one-line test: for any term, an interviewer is checking two things. Do you know what it is, and do you know why LSEG cares? The LSEG angle lines are there for the second half. Use one fact, not five.

Terms A to E

A

Active account (EMIR 3): An EU rule that makes some large EU firms hold, and actually use, an account at an EU clearing house for certain euro and Polish zloty interest rate derivatives. It applies from 25 June 2025, with at least five trades per sub-category per reference period going through the EU account [S161]. LSEG angle: it is designed to pull euro swap clearing away from LCH SwapClear in London. First reports covering 25 June 2025 to 30 June 2026 were due by 31 July 2026 [S257].

ADV (average daily volume): The total traded on a platform in a period, divided by the number of trading days. Bonds and FX are measured in money (dollars or pounds), futures in contracts. LSEG angle: a headline Markets KPI. In H1 2026 Tradeweb ADV was $3.2trn (+24.8%) and LSEG FX average daily total volume $561bn (+5.8%) [S24].

Adjusted EPS (AEPS): Earnings per share after removing items management treats as one-off or non-cash, such as amortisation of intangible assets bought in acquisitions. LSEG angle: FY2025 AEPS was 420.6p (+15.7%) against reported EPS of 238.4p [S72]. Much of the gap is amortisation from the Refinitiv deal Inferred [S70].

AIM: London Stock Exchange's market for smaller, growing companies, founded in 1995, with lighter admission rules than the Main Market. AIM companies work with a nominated adviser (a "nomad") [S78]. LSEG angle: 14 of London's 23 IPOs in 2025 were on AIM [S192]. LSE's Primary Market and AIM Regulation team (PMAR) oversees it [S13].

ASV (annual subscription value): The yearly value of all subscription contracts live at a point in time. Its growth rate is an early signal of future subscription revenue. LSEG angle: ASV growth was 6.1% at June 2026 [S70]. Slower ASV growth was blamed for a sharp share fall on 31 July 2025 [S102, S103].

B

BI (business intelligence): Turning raw business data (volumes, revenue, client activity) into dashboards and reports that managers use to decide things. LSEG angle: one of the four JD exposure areas is "Delivery of business intelligence, strategic initiatives, and operational improvements" [JD]. The sibling Business Analyst internship names Power BI and Tableau [S5].

Bond: A loan turned into a tradable security. The issuer borrows money, pays interest (the coupon) and repays the face value at maturity. When market yields rise, existing bond prices fall. LSEG angle: bonds are listed on the Main Market, ISM and Sustainable Bond Market, and traded on Tradeweb [S15, S177].

Buy side and sell side: The buy side invests money (asset managers, pension funds, hedge funds). The sell side is banks and dealers that make prices and sell trading services. LSEG angle: FXall connects buy-side clients to more than 200 bank liquidity providers [S340]. Banking customers produce 72% of LSEG revenue [S303].

Buyback: A company buys its own shares and cancels them. This returns cash to shareholders and raises earnings per share. LSEG angle: £2.1bn in 2025, £2.1bn in H1 2026 and up to £1.35bn more by February 2027, taking buybacks since 2022 above £8bn. The Board cited a "dislocation" between the value of the business and the share price [S72, S70].

C

Cappfinity: A UK assessment company (formerly Capp) whose tests measure strengths: what you do well and what energises you. LSEG angle: LSEG's "Assessment Preparation Hub" link opens a page titled "Cappfinity Preparation Hub", so the Immersive Online Assessment is very likely Cappfinity's Inferred [S264, S265].

CCP (central counterparty): A clearing house that steps into the middle of a trade, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. If one side defaults, the other still gets paid. It collects margin and a default fund to cover that risk. LSEG angle: LCH Ltd (London) and LCH SA (Paris). Member margin and default fund collateral across LSEG's CCPs was £277bn at 31 December 2025 [S74].

CDSClear: LCH SA's service for clearing credit default swaps, contracts that pay out if a borrower defaults. LSEG angle: run from Paris and led alongside DigitalAssetClear [S28]. A London Business Programme Manager role supports its transformation programme across Risk, Operations, Product, Technology and other teams [S12].

Clearing: The step between agreeing a trade and settling it, where obligations are confirmed, netted and risk-managed. Central clearing puts a CCP in the middle. LSEG angle: LCH. After the 2008 crisis, G20 rules pushed standard over-the-counter derivatives into central clearing, which is why SwapClear is so large Inferred.

CLS: The system that settles FX trades payment-versus-payment, operating since September 2002. It settles over $8.0trn of payments a day in 18 currencies for over 75 members [S204, S345]. LSEG angle: ForexClear's CLSClearedFX service was redesigned in July 2025 to put cleared FX into the main CLS settlement session [S304].

Commercial companies category: see ESCC.

Compression: Tearing up derivatives trades that offset each other and replacing them with fewer trades carrying the same net risk. It cuts notional outstanding, capital use and admin. LSEG angle: OTC Derivatives revenue rose 11.6% in 2025, "driven by growth in clearing and compression activity" [S301]. 7.8m SwapClear trades were compressed in 2023; no 2025 figure was found [S356]. Post Trade Solutions sells compression and optimisation for uncleared trades [S300].

Consolidated tape: One feed that combines trade data from every venue for an asset class, so investors see the whole market. LSEG angle: the UK bond tape, run by ETS Connect UK, went live on 22 June 2026. The equity tape is due to be procured in early 2027 and to operate "in 2027 or early 2028", with income shared with data contributors [S155, S157]. LSE, Turquoise and Tradeweb feed tapes, and a cheap public tape can compete with paid venue data Inferred.

COO office: The team supporting a chief operating officer with performance reporting, governance, planning and executive papers. LSEG angle: a Markets Operations and Controls Business Manager advert asks the holder to "own the production of high-quality executive materials" and keep "KPIs, MI, and performance reporting" consistent [S18]. The exact COO-office structure in Markets was not confirmed (Part 16).

Critical third parties (CTP): Suppliers, mostly cloud providers, that HM Treasury designates for direct oversight by UK financial regulators, because many firms depend on them. LSEG angle: the regime started on 1 January 2025 and the first designations took effect on 13 July 2026 [S170]; those named were reported as AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle [S171]. LSEG runs on Microsoft Azure under a 10-year partnership [S79].

CSD (central securities depository): The institution that keeps the official record of who owns which securities and moves them from seller to buyer at settlement. LSEG angle: LSEG is not the main UK CSD (that is Euroclear UK and International) Inferred. It is building a Digital Securities Depository for tokenised assets [S70].

D

D2C and D2D: Dealer-to-client platforms let investors trade with banks. Dealer-to-dealer platforms let banks trade with each other. LSEG angle: FXall is LSEG's D2C FX platform and FX Matching its D2D platform [S22].

Default fund: A pool of money paid in by a CCP's clearing members, used if a defaulting member's own margin does not cover its losses. LSEG angle: LSEG says its default funds are sized by stress tests to cover "multiple defaults in extreme market circumstances" [S74].

Default waterfall: The fixed order in which a CCP uses resources to absorb a member's default. LSEG angle: at LCH the defaulter's collateral and default fund contribution go first, then LCH's own funds, then the default fund contributions of the other members [S93]. LCH says it has managed 9 member defaults, all within the initial margin held [S351].

Digital gilt (DIGIT): A UK government bond issued and recorded on a distributed ledger, as a pilot. LSEG angle: HSBC Orion is the issuer depository and LSEG's Digital Securities Depository the investor depository under an MoU, with issuance by Q1 2027 [S246, S221]. Rachel Reeves said the UK would be the first G7 country to issue a digital sovereign bond [S245].

Digital Markets Infrastructure (DMI): LSEG's ledger-based platform for private funds, built on Microsoft Azure. LSEG angle: launched September 2025; six fund providers onboarded by H1 2026 [S72, S70].

Digital Securities Sandbox (DSS): A Bank of England and FCA regime that lets firms test DLT-based trading and settlement under adjusted rules. It came into force on 8 January 2024 and opened to applicants on 30 September 2024, running to December 2028 unless extended [S169, S168]. LSEG angle: HSBC Orion was the first firm approved to go live as a Digital Securities Depository on 13 July 2026, linking to LSEG for DIGIT [S246].

DLT (distributed ledger technology): A shared database copied across many participants and updated under agreed rules. Blockchains are one type. It can record ownership and settle trades almost instantly. LSEG angle: sits behind LSEG's digital projects: DMI, the Digital Securities Depository and DIGIT [S70, S246].

DORA: The EU Digital Operational Resilience Act, which sets rules on IT risk, incident reporting, resilience testing and oversight of tech suppliers. It applies from 17 January 2025 [S173]. LSEG angle: relevant to LSEG's EU entities such as LCH SA and Turquoise Europe Inferred.

Dual listing: A company whose shares are listed on two exchanges, often its home market plus a second market to reach more investors. LSEG angle: LSE's Equity Primary Markets team covers IPOs and secondary listings, and LSE claims "the highest proportion of international companies among its issuers of any major exchange" [S16] Reported.

E

EBITDA: Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. A rough measure of operating profit before investment costs. "Adjusted" removes one-off items. LSEG angle: adjusted EBITDA £4,523m in 2025, a 50.3% margin; 52.7% in H1 2026 [S72, S70].

EMIR, UK EMIR and EMIR 3: EU rules on derivatives clearing, trade reporting and CCPs. The UK kept its own version (UK EMIR) after Brexit. EMIR 3 is the EU revision in force from 24 December 2024 [S161]. LSEG angle: the Bank of England consulted in July 2025 on moving UK EMIR CCP rules into its own FMI Rulebook [S197].

Equivalence: An EU decision that a non-EU rulebook is good enough, so EU firms can use that country's infrastructure. LSEG angle: the EU extended equivalence for UK CCPs on 31 January 2025 to 30 June 2028 [S160]. Without renewal, that is a cliff edge for LCH's EU clients Inferred.

ESCC (equity shares in commercial companies): The single main category for listing company shares under the UK Listing Rules from 29 July 2024, replacing the old premium and standard segments. The FCA's own text says "commercial companies category"; ESCC is practitioner shorthand [S150] Reported. LSEG angle: part of the reform package meant to win IPOs back to London.

ETF (exchange-traded fund): A fund whose shares trade on an exchange all day, usually tracking an index. LSEG angle: FTSE Russell earns asset-based fees from ETFs tracking its indices: $2.19trn of ETF assets at June 2026 [S70].

ETP (exchange-traded product): The umbrella term for anything fund-like traded on an exchange, including ETFs and exchange-traded notes and commodities. LSEG angle: ETPs will be the first asset class on LSE 24 in H1 2027, subject to regulatory approval [S196].

Eurobond: A bond issued in a currency other than that of the country where it is sold, placed with international investors. LSEG angle: the market began with Autostrade in July 1963 ($15m, 15 years, 5.5%), lead-managed from London by S G Warburg [S174], but listed in Luxembourg, not London [S175] Reported.

Exchange (regulated market): The most heavily regulated kind of trading venue, where securities are admitted to trading under fixed, non-discretionary rules. LSEG angle: London Stock Exchange plc operates its markets as a Recognised Investment Exchange [S13].

Terms F to M

F

First line (of defence): The business teams that own their risks day to day: running controls, assessing new products, finding root causes of incidents. LSEG angle: a Post Trade Solutions role must "maintain the PTS control library within the GRC system" [S19]; LCH Ltd has a First Line Business Continuity Management team [S318]. See three lines of defence.

Fixing: A benchmark exchange rate set at a fixed time each day, such as the WM/Reuters 4pm London fix. Asset managers often trade at the fix so their trades match the rate used to value their funds Inferred. LSEG angle: LSEG owns WM/Reuters through WMR [S221].

FMI (financial market infrastructure): The plumbing of markets: exchanges, trading platforms, clearing houses, depositories, payment and data systems. They make trading safe rather than taking bets themselves. LSEG angle: LSEG describes itself as "a leading financial markets infrastructure and data provider" [S74].

Forward First Fixing (FFF): An FXall workflow that lets asset managers arrange the forward part of fixing-related trades electronically, hours ahead. LSEG angle: passed $1.5trn cumulative volume by May 2026, with 30+ clients and 18 partner banks; fees waived for the rest of 2026 [S238]; a record $330bn+ in Q2 2026 [S221].

ForexClear: LCH Ltd's service for clearing over-the-counter FX derivatives. LSEG angle: $48.1trn notional cleared in 2025 (+31.4%) and $33.3trn in H1 2026 (+45.2%), with 41 members [S301, S221].

Free float: The share of a company's shares available for the public to trade, excluding stakes held by founders, governments or strategic owners. It affects liquidity and index inclusion. LSEG angle: Airtel Money is listing with about 16.5% to 17.5% in public hands [S232]; FTSE Russell cut the minimum free float for non-UK incorporated companies from 25% to 10% from June 2026 [S253].

FTSE Russell: LSEG's index business, which designs and calculates benchmarks such as the FTSE 100 and Russell indices and licenses them. LSEG angle: £954m income in 2025 at a 66.6% EBITDA margin, the group's most profitable division [S72]. FTSE Group was established in 1995 and Frank Russell bought in December 2014 [S78].

FX forward: An agreement to exchange currencies at a rate fixed today for a date later than spot. Companies use forwards to lock in the cost of a future payment.

FX Global Code: A voluntary set of 55 principles of good practice for FX markets, maintained by the Global Foreign Exchange Committee and first published in 2017 [S204]. A review completed in December 2024 strengthened guidance on settlement risk; the updated Code came out in January 2025 [S164]. LSEG angle: principles on last look (P17), confidentiality (P19, P20) and confirmations (P46, P48) are your anchors in any FX client scenario [S343].

FX Matching: LSEG's anonymous dealer-to-dealer central limit order book for FX, which came with Refinitiv [S303, S300]. LSEG angle: reported to have over 1,000 subscribers Reported. FX revenue (FXall plus Matching) was £272m in 2025 [S22].

FX spot: Exchanging currencies for near-immediate delivery, usually in two business days. LSEG angle: spot was 31% of global FX turnover of $9.6trn a day in April 2025 [S163]. LSEG's US Streaming FX Spot business made its first trades on AWS Outposts [S24].

FX swap: A spot trade combined with a reverse forward trade. In effect, borrowing one currency against another for a period. LSEG angle: FX swaps were 42% of global FX turnover in April 2025, the biggest single product [S163].

FXall: LSEG's dealer-to-client FX trading platform, offering request-for-quote, bank price streams, an order book and post-trade services. LSEG angle: 2,400+ institutional clients, 200+ liquidity providers, 500+ currency pairs [S340]. A large share of its functionality is now inside Workspace [S302].

G

Gilt: A UK government bond. LSEG angle: the 10-year gilt yield reached 5.38% in late September 2026, near two-decade highs [S243]. Gilt volatility tends to lift rates trading and sterling swap hedging, which feeds Tradeweb and SwapClear volumes Inferred. See also digital gilt.

I

Index: A single number tracking the value of a defined basket of securities, such as the FTSE 100. Index providers license their indices to fund managers who build index funds and ETFs. LSEG angle: FTSE Russell, with its index revenue split between subscription licences and asset-based fees [S72].

Initial margin: Collateral posted up front to a CCP to cover the losses it might face while closing out a defaulting member's positions. LSEG angle: LCH sets initial margin at 99.7% confidence for most services (99% for Listed Rates), above the 99.5% regulatory minimum for OTC products [S93].

Inside information: Under UK MAR Article 7, information "of a precise nature, which has not been made public" and which would be "likely to have a significant effect on the prices" if it were [S349]. LSEG angle: Primary Markets staff see issuer pipelines before they are public, so this matters from day one Inferred [S13].

Insider list: A list issuers must keep of everyone with access to inside information, including advisers, with the date and time of access, kept for at least five years (UK MAR Article 18) [S348].

Interest rate swap (IRS): Two parties swap interest payments on a notional amount, typically one paying a fixed rate and the other a floating rate such as SONIA. Used to hedge or take a view on rates. LSEG angle: SwapClear cleared a record $1,941trn of IRS notional in 2025 (+21.2%) [S72].

IPO (initial public offering): A company's first sale of shares to the public, with admission to trading on an exchange. LSEG angle: London had 23 IPOs raising £2.1bn in 2025 [S192]. Airtel Money priced at £1.96 a share on 1 October 2026 (market value £5.3bn), with Main Market admission expected on 14 October [S232].

ISM (International Securities Market): LSE's market for debt sold to professional investors, launched in May 2017 [S176]. It is a UK MTF operated by the Exchange as an RIE; admission is decided by the Exchange and ISM securities are not on the FCA's Official List [S346]. LSEG angle: LSE markets it with "3+2" turnaround (comments within three days, then two) [S347], and calls ISM a "key engine of growth" [S15].

K

KPI (key performance indicator): A number used to judge progress against a goal. LSEG angle: Markets publishes UK value traded, Tradeweb ADV, FX volume, SwapClear notional and client trades, ForexClear members and collateral balances [S301].

L

Last look: A final window in which a liquidity provider can accept or reject a trade request at its quoted price. LSEG angle: FX Global Code P17 says those using it "should be transparent regarding its use and provide appropriate disclosures to Clients" [S343]. GFXC guidance says it should not be used for information gathering [S344] Reported.

LCH Ltd and LCH SA: LSEG's two clearing houses. LCH Ltd in London (SwapClear, ForexClear, RepoClear, EquityClear) is a Recognised Clearing House supervised by the Bank of England [S304]. LCH SA in Paris (CDSClear, euro repo) is regulated in France by the ACPR [S105] Reported. LSEG angle: LSEG owns about 94% of LCH Group (94.2% after a 2024 purchase, 94.4% at end 2025), rising above 95% after a July 2026 deal [S121, S23, S70].

Leeds Reforms: A UK financial services reform package announced on 15 July 2025 alongside Rachel Reeves' Mansion House speech, aiming to make the UK the top destination for financial services by 2035 [S180]. Whether the new Chancellor, John Healey, keeps it unchanged is not known (Part 16).

Listing particulars: A disclosure document used for some admissions that are not public offers, lighter than a full prospectus. LSEG angle: LSE's ISM information pack compares admission particulars, full prospectus and listing particulars across its markets, but uses pre-2026 terms; the January 2026 ISM rulebook uses the new POATRs language [S347, S346].

Lit and dark: On a lit venue, orders are visible to everyone before trading. In a dark pool, orders stay hidden until they execute, which helps large investors trade without moving the price. LSEG angle: Turquoise runs both lit and dark (Turquoise Plato) books Inferred. Its lit and dark volume split was not found.

LSE 24: A new, separate London Stock Exchange venue trading 17:00 to 07:50, Monday to Friday, with a pause from 18:30 to 19:00 [S227]. LSEG angle: client testing by end 2026; ETPs first in H1 2027, then equities, subject to approval. It "intends to draw on elements of central limit order book and request-for-quote functionality" [S196]. Tokenised xStocks are planned to trade there in 2027 [S228].

LSEG Everywhere: LSEG's plan to deliver AI-ready data wherever customers work, including through MCP (Model Context Protocol, an open standard for plugging data into AI tools). LSEG angle: partners include Anthropic, Databricks, Microsoft, OpenAI, Rogo and Snowflake, then Amazon Quick and Google Gemini; over 200 customers were engaged on the MCP server by July 2026 [S72, S70].

M

Main Market: London Stock Exchange's flagship regulated market for shares and bonds. LSEG angle: 9 of London's 23 IPOs in 2025 were on the Main Market [S192]. Airtel Money is due to list there [S232].

Mansion House: The Chancellor's annual City speech, and the pension pledges named after it. The Compact (10 July 2023) aimed for 5% of default funds in unlisted equities by 2030; the Accord (13 May 2025) for 10% in private markets by 2030, half in the UK [S181]. LSEG angle: Rachel Reeves gave the 14 July 2026 speech [S245], then was replaced as Chancellor by John Healey [S243, S244]. Never call Reeves the current Chancellor.

MAR (UK Market Abuse Regulation): Bans insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information and market manipulation; issuers must disclose inside information promptly and keep insider lists. LSEG angle: LSEG Market Supervision monitors all secondary trading on LSE and Turquoise for possible abuse and files STORs (suspicious transaction and order reports) [S10].

Margin (two meanings): (1) Profit margin: profit as a share of revenue. Markets' adjusted EBITDA margin was 58.6% in H1 2026 [S70]. (2) Clearing margin: collateral posted to a CCP; see initial margin and variation margin. Say which one you mean.

MiFID II and UK MiFIR: EU rules from 3 January 2018 on investment firms and trading venues: more transparency, trade reporting and trading obligations [S203]. The original MiFID (1 November 2007) opened the door to competing venues such as Turquoise. The UK kept the rules as UK MiFIR and has since changed them, for example revoking the share trading obligation on 29 August 2023 [S167].

MTF (multilateral trading facility): A trading venue that brings many buyers and sellers together under fixed rules, regulated more lightly than an exchange. LSEG angle: Turquoise is an equity MTF; AIM and ISM are MTFs run by LSE [S346]; FXall runs an EU MTF in Ireland and a UK MTF [S341].

Terms N to S

N

NDF (non-deliverable forward): An FX forward where the two currencies are never exchanged in full. At maturity, the difference between the agreed rate and a fixing rate is paid in a major currency, usually dollars. Used for currencies with capital controls. LSEG angle: LSEG's FX surveillance product covers "Spot, Forward, Swap, NDF, Options" [S14], and FXall onboarding includes NDF Matching participants [S317].

Net treasury income (NTI): "Income earned on cash deposited with LCH as margin and default funds as part of the risk management process" [S23], after paying members their share. LSEG angle: £257m in 2025 and £143m in H1 2026 (+12.6%), driven by higher cash collateral in volatile markets [S72, S70]. It falls if interest rates fall Inferred.

Netting: Offsetting buys against sells so that only the net amount is settled or margined. It reduces risk and the cash needed. LSEG angle: the more trades sit in one CCP, the more can be netted, which lowers margin. That network effect is why SwapClear's scale matters Inferred.

Non-controlling interest: The share of a subsidiary's profit that belongs to outside shareholders. LSEG angle: LSEG consolidates all of Tradeweb's revenue, and the outside owners' share shows up here: £334m (adjusted) in 2025 [S72].

Notional: The face amount a derivative is based on, used to calculate payments. It is not the money at risk. LSEG angle: SwapClear's $1,941trn notional cleared in 2025 sounds huge because it is a notional sum [S72]. Never compare it with revenue or GDP.

O

Open access: A CCP clears trades from any venue on fair, non-discriminatory terms, rather than only its owner's venues. LSEG angle: written into LCH's articles when LSEG took control: "No exchange will be favoured over any other" [S305]. It cuts both ways: Euronext moved its clearing in-house, cutting LSEG's Securities and Reporting revenue 3.0% in 2025 [S301].

Operational resilience: The ability to keep important business services running through disruption, within a set "impact tolerance" (the maximum tolerable outage). LSEG angle: UK firms had until 31 March 2025 to meet their tolerances [S172]. The Bank of England's FMI supervision priorities include meeting impact tolerances "under cyber and extreme scenarios" [S94].

Order book (CLOB): A central limit order book continuously matches anonymous buy and sell orders by price, then time. Most shares trade this way. LSEG angle: LSE, Turquoise and FX Matching are order books; LSE 24 will mix order book and RFQ features [S196].

Organic growth: Growth with acquisitions and disposals removed and exchange rates held constant ("constant currency"). LSEG also strips out "recoveries", fees it collects for third-party content and passes on. LSEG angle: +7.1% in 2025, +8.4% in H1 2026, 2026 guidance raised to 7.0% to 7.5% [S72, S70].

OTC (over the counter): Trades negotiated directly between two parties, often a bank and a client, rather than on an exchange order book. Most FX, bonds and swaps trade OTC. LSEG angle: LCH's OTC Derivatives line earned £641m in 2025 [S22].

OTF (organised trading facility): A venue for bonds and derivatives where the operator has some discretion over how orders meet. LSEG angle: not a focus for LSEG; interdealer brokers are the typical operators Inferred.

P

PISCES: The Private Intermittent Securities and Capital Exchange System: a UK legal sandbox letting private companies' shares trade during set trading windows. In force from 5 June 2025 (FCA rules PS25/6 from 10 June 2025), it expires on 5 June 2030 unless made permanent [S152]. Trading is secondary only: no new money is raised [S154].

PMO (programme management office): The team that runs the governance of a project or programme: plans, budgets, RAID logs (risks, actions, issues, decisions) and steering committee papers. LSEG angle: Post Trade Solutions and Markets Operations Transformation PMO roles are advertised, many in Manila [S4].

Post Trade Solutions (PTS): LSEG's business for uncleared derivatives and post-trade efficiency, including Quantile, Acadia, TradeAgent and RepoAgent. LSEG angle: 11 banks bought 20% for £170m in October 2025, "replicating the original LCH model" [S72]; over 80 customers were added in H1 2026 [S70].

Primary and secondary market: In the primary market, new securities are sold for the first time and the issuer gets the cash (an IPO, a new bond). In the secondary market, existing securities change hands between investors. LSEG angle: listings and debt capital markets teams work in the primary market; LSE, Turquoise and Tradeweb trading is secondary.

Private Securities Market (PSM): London Stock Exchange's PISCES venue. LSEG angle: LSE was the first operator granted a PISCES Approval Notice, on 26 August 2025 [S96]. The first auction ran on 25 March 2026 [S154]; Wayve ran an $85m auction for employees in July 2026 [S235].

Prospectus: The legal document describing a security and its issuer for investors. LSEG angle: from 19 January 2026 the Public Offers and Admissions to Trading Regulations (POATRs) replaced the UK Prospectus Regulation, with FCA rules in PS25/9 [S151].

PvP (payment versus payment): Both legs of an FX trade settle at the same moment, or neither does. It removes settlement risk (also called Herstatt risk): paying one currency and not receiving the other. LSEG angle: in April 2025, $5.2trn a day settled via PvP and about $1.4trn (10%) settled with no protection [S205].

R

Recurring revenue: Revenue from contracts that repeat, such as subscriptions and licences, rather than one-off fees. LSEG angle: 73% of total income including recoveries in 2025 [S74].

Repo (repurchase agreement): A sale of securities with an agreement to buy them back later at a slightly higher price. In effect, a short-term secured loan where the price difference is the interest.

RepoClear: LCH's repo clearing service, run across LCH Ltd and LCH SA. LSEG angle: €334.2trn nominal cleared in 2025 (+7.8%) [S301]; 86 live members at LCH Ltd, including 16 Sponsored Members [S304]. RepoAgent, built by PTS with Tradeweb, helps reduce repo settlement fails [S301].

Revenue capture: Revenue earned per unit of volume (revenue divided by volume). LSEG angle: LSEG does not publish a capture rate in the documents read. If you compute one, for example FX revenue against FX ADV, say clearly it is your own metric Inferred [S301].

RFQ (request for quote): A client asks a few dealers for a price and picks the best. Most bonds, swaps and much FX trade this way. LSEG angle: Tradeweb's core model and FXall's QuickTrade [S342].

RIE (Recognised Investment Exchange): UK legal status for an exchange, recognised and supervised by the FCA. LSEG angle: London Stock Exchange plc is an RIE [S95] Reported; its PMAR team gives "first line primary regulatory oversight" of its markets in that capacity [S13].

RNS (Regulatory News Service): LSE's service for publishing issuer announcements such as results and inside information. LSEG angle: roles there require "ability to respect confidentiality at all times" [S20]; the July 2024 Workspace outage disrupted RNS [S98].

S

Settlement: The actual exchange of cash and securities that completes a trade, usually at a CSD (or through CLS for FX). See T+1.

Skin in the game: A CCP's own capital placed in the default waterfall ahead of other members' default fund money, so the CCP shares the pain. LSEG angle: LCH sets aside 25% of its minimum regulatory capital as skin in the game [S93].

SJT (situational judgement test): A test that sets out a work scenario and asks what you would do. Cappfinity-style tests may also ask how much you would enjoy each option [S289]. LSEG angle: likely part of the Immersive Online Assessment Inferred.

SLA (service level agreement): A written promise of a service standard, such as uptime or response time, between a provider and a client or between internal teams. LSEG angle: useful vocabulary for FX client and operations scenarios Inferred.

SONIA: The Sterling Overnight Index Average, the benchmark overnight sterling interest rate that replaced LIBOR for sterling contracts. LSEG angle: LCH SwapClear had a 99.995% share of cleared GBP SONIA swaps in Q1 2026 [S140] Reported.

Speak Up: LSEG's whistleblowing channels: confidential, optionally anonymous, with a 24-hour hotline. LSEG angle: 192 reports were received in 2025 [S300]. The Code of Conduct says you "must follow the Speak Up Policy and not look the other way" and that LSEG "does not tolerate retaliation" [S307].

STP (straight-through processing): A trade flows from execution to confirmation, booking and settlement without anyone re-keying it. LSEG angle: FXall sends trades to clients' order and treasury systems "with full STP" [S340]; David Schwimmer cites "straight through FXall execution capabilities into ForexClear" [S284].

Strengths-based interview: An interview that asks what you do well and what energises you, not only what you have done. LSEG angle: LSEG's video stage uses "a strength-based-interview style" [S260]. One candidate anecdote reports about 8 questions, one take each, 1 to 2 minutes per answer [S291] Reported.

Subscription: A recurring fee for access to data, software or index licences. LSEG angle: Data and Analytics, FTSE Russell and Risk Intelligence together are about 61% of income Inferred [S120]; subscription revenue retention was 92.8% at June 2026 [S70].

SwapClear: LCH Ltd's interest rate swap clearing service, "founded in collaboration with clearing members 25 years ago" [S304]. LSEG angle: over 90% of cleared IRS notional outstanding [S303]. In October 2025 LSEG paid £1.2bn to cut the founding banks' share of SwapClear's revenue surplus from about 30% to 10% from 2026 to 2045 [S72].

Terms T to W

T

T+1: Settlement one business day after the trade date. LSEG angle: the UK and EU both plan to move to T+1 from 11 October 2027 [S158, S159]. In the UK only the draft statutory instrument (November 2025) is confirmed. Less time to fix failed trades means more automation for venues and LCH Inferred.

TCA (transaction cost analysis): Measuring how much a trade cost against a benchmark, to prove best execution. LSEG angle: FXall's Trade Performance Reporting lets clients "evaluate the cost and effectiveness of your execution strategies" [S340].

Three lines of defence: A risk model in which the business owns its risks (first line), Risk and Compliance oversee and challenge (second line), and Internal Audit gives independent assurance (third line). LSEG angle: "LSEG operates a three lines of defence model, providing appropriate segregation of duties and clear roles and responsibilities" [S74].

Tokenisation: Representing a security or other asset as a digital token on a ledger, so it can move between digital wallets and potentially trade around the clock. LSEG angle: on 1 September 2026 LSE announced UK tokenised equity structures, including xStocks (1:1 backed tokenised shares), and a partnership with Payward [S228].

Trade repository: A database that collects reports of derivatives trades so regulators can see risk building up. LSEG angle: LSEG's UnaVista operates in this space Inferred; Regulatory Reporting is a separate unit inside Markets [S22].

Tradeweb: An electronic marketplace for rates, credit, equities and money markets, separately listed on Nasdaq (TW). LSEG angle: LSEG holds about 50.9% of the economics [S74] and about 89.9% of the votes [S137], so say "majority-owned". Revenue $2,052.4m in 2025 (+18.9%) [S137]; ADV $3.18trn in H1 2026 [S70].

Turquoise: LSEG's pan-European equities MTF, founded in 2006 by nine banks, with Turquoise Europe in Amsterdam [S95]. LSEG angle: 84.2% owned by LSEG [S300]. Tom Stenhouse was appointed CEO in March 2026, subject to regulatory approval [S32].

U

UK Listing Relief: A three-year exemption from the 0.5% stamp duty reserve tax (SDRT) on shares of companies newly listed on a UK regulated market on or after 27 November 2025 [S182] Reported. LSEG angle: one of the incentives for IPOs in London.

UKLR (UK Listing Rules): The FCA rulebook for listed companies, in force from 29 July 2024. The FCA called it "the most significant changes to the UK's listing regime in over 3 decades" [S150]. See ESCC.

V

Variation margin: Cash paid daily (or more often) to settle gains and losses on open positions, so losses never pile up. LSEG angle: LCH can make "multiple intraday margin calls" in volatile markets [S351].

Vertical silo: A group owns both the trading venue and the CCP that clears it, so trading and clearing are tied together. LSEG angle: Deutsche Börse describes its "vertically integrated business model" (Eurex trading, Eurex Clearing, Clearstream) [S127]; LSEG chose open access instead [S300, S305].

W

Workspace: LSEG's desktop data and analytics platform, the successor to Eikon (Eikon access ended on 30 June 2025) [S72]. LSEG angle: FXall functionality, FTSE Russell indices and LCH data are now available inside it [S300]; AI Search had 17,000 active users in H1 2026 [S70]. Its Workflows line grew only 2.8% in H1 2026 [S70].

World-Check: LSEG Risk Intelligence's database for screening customers against sanctions lists and politically exposed persons. LSEG angle: in 2024 a hacking group claimed to hold 5.3m World-Check records; LSEG said it "was not a security breach of LSEG/our systems" but a third party's copy [S99] Reported. The Business Analyst internship mentions World-Check under Markets, which is unexplained [S5].

Don't say this

"SwapClear cleared $1,941 trillion, so it is worth more than the world economy." Notional is a reference amount, not money at risk. Also avoid "LCH is LSEG's clearing division" without naming LCH Ltd and LCH SA as separate legal entities in two countries [S22, S28].

Say this in the interview

"Open access means LCH clears for any venue on fair terms, even rival ones. That is why banks trust it, and why it lost Euronext's clearing when Euronext built its own. It is a deliberate trade-off, not an accident" [S305, S301].
