Part 4

How LSEG stands out

LSEG is not the biggest, fastest-growing or leanest exchange group, but it is the only one that combines a global swaps clearing utility, terminal-scale data, a global index house and multi-asset venues under one roof.

How to use this part

"Why LSEG and not ICE or CME?" and "Who are our competitors?" are standard interview questions. A weak answer lists firms. A strong answer says which business line competes with whom, on what measure, with a number, and admits where LSEG is behind. This part gives you the comparison tables, the design choices that separate the groups, six claims only LSEG can make, the places it does not lead, and a line-by-line competitor map.

Read the numbers with care

Every firm reports in its own currency and defines revenue differently (net revenue, revenue less transaction costs, total income excluding recoveries). The research keeps each number in its home currency [S120]. Where this part converts to US dollars, the rate is the author's round-number assumption (£1 = $1.30, €1 = $1.15), marked Inferred. Say "roughly" when you compare sizes out loud. Market caps are a single aggregator snapshot from October 2026 [S148] Reported.

Size against staffing

Revenue per employee is a rough measure of how labour-heavy a business model is. Exchanges and clearing houses run on automated systems, so a small team can handle huge volumes. Data businesses need people to collect, clean, sell and support content.

Four design choices, and where each rival sits

Exchange groups differ less in what they do than in how they are built. Four design choices separate them. Figure 22 places each firm on each axis; the text below gives the evidence.

Axis 1: transaction-heavy or subscription-heavy revenue

At the transaction end: CME (81% from clearing and transaction fees), Tradeweb (82.8% transaction fees), Cboe [S122, S137, S132]. At the subscription end: MSCI (73% recurring subscriptions), Nasdaq (about 77% Solutions) [S136, S130]. LSEG is near the data end, with about 61% of income from its three subscription divisions; ICE sits in the middle at about 51% recurring [S120, S124]. Why it matters for Markets: the Markets division is LSEG's volume-driven, volatility-sensitive part, and it grew faster (+8.9%) than D&A (+5.0%) in 2025 [S120].

Axis 2: vertical silo or open access clearing

Deutsche Börse calls its model "vertically integrated" [S127]. Euronext pulled cash equity clearing out of LCH SA into its own CCP, which LSEG names as the reason Securities & Reporting revenue fell 3.0% [S120]. LSEG is the open-access end: SwapClear has "over 100 members" and clears 28 currencies [S141]. Watch out: the silo model is gaining in euro swaps, where Eurex rose to 13.5% in Q3 2025 from 8.4% a year earlier [S139] Reported.

Axis 3: exchange-owned or user-partnered clearing

LSEG owns about 94% of LCH [S121, S300], but keeps users inside: founding banks share 10% of SwapClear's revenue surplus until 2045, and 11 banks bought 20% of Post Trade Solutions for £170m [S120]. No comparable bank-equity structure was found at CME, ICE or Eurex clearing Inferred, though this was not checked exhaustively.

Axis 4: strength as a listings venue

London had 23 IPOs raising £2.1bn in 2025, up 170% on £777.7m in 2024 [S142], and 7 IPOs raising £577m in H1 2026 [S143]. Hong Kong raised HK$286.9bn from 119 IPOs, ranked first in the world [S134]; US proceeds rose 38% and led the world by value [S144]. 88 companies left London's main market in 2024 [S145] Reported. This is why listings are a small part of LSEG's revenue and why the JD's research track mentions debt listings.

A fifth difference: the listed subsidiary and the tech partner

LSEG controls Tradeweb with about 89.9% of the votes but only about half the economics [S137, S300]; CME and ICE own their fixed income venues outright [S122, S124]. And LSEG's cloud deal is a product partnership as well as infrastructure: Microsoft owns about 4% of LSEG, LSEG committed to $2.8bn of minimum cloud spend, and Workspace now runs inside Teams and Excel [S147, S120]. CME is moving to Google Cloud [S122].

Six things only LSEG can claim

Each claim below is phrased so that it is true and checkable. "Only" claims are the author's comparison across the peer set in Table A and B, so say "no other exchange group I looked at" rather than "no one in the world". Inferred

1. It runs the world's swaps clearing utility

Evidence

SwapClear cleared a record $1,941 trillion of interest rate swap notional in 2025 (+21.2%) and 5.3 million client trades (+33%) [S120]. Clarus data: 99.995% of GBP SONIA swaps in Q1 2026; CME's best-ever share of USD OIS was only 4.3% [S140]. LSEG says SwapClear clears "95 percent of all 'vanilla' OTC interest rate swap products" [S141] (undated company claim).

Why it matters

Clearing has network effects: offsets improve as more trades sit in one pool (Figure 19). That is a moat built over 25 years. The 2025 surplus deal with founding banks added 100bps to group EBITDA margin [S120].

2. One group owns terminal-scale data, a global index house, multi-asset venues and a global CCP

Evidence

D&A £3,978m, FTSE Russell £954m, plus Tradeweb, FXall and FX Matching, LSE, Turquoise and LCH [S120]. ICE has data, exchanges and clearing but no FX venue or desktop of this scale; Deutsche Börse has 360T, Eurex and an index business but no terminal; Bloomberg has a terminal, FXGO and bond trading but no CCP or exchange. Inferred

Why it matters

It is the basis for cross-selling and for distributing data everywhere customers work: "The combination of our trade lifecycle and data value chains is unmatched" [S303].

3. A bank-partnership model built into its infrastructure

Evidence

Banks share 10% of the SwapClear revenue surplus until 2045 and own 20% of Post Trade Solutions [S120]; clearing members and clients sit on LCH's risk committee, with no constituent holding a majority [S304]. No comparable structure found at CME, ICE or Deutsche Börse clearing Inferred.

Why it matters

Banks are 72% of LSEG's income [S303]. Giving them a seat and a share makes them less likely to back a rival CCP, as they once did when founding Turquoise and Tradeweb.

4. A controlled, separately listed subsidiary worth over $20bn

Evidence

Tradeweb: revenue $2,052m (+18.9%), ADV $2.6trn, market cap $22.28bn [S137, S120, S148]. LSEG holds about 89.9% of votes and 50.9% of the economics [S137, S300]. No other exchange group in the peer set consolidates a listed company of that size. Inferred

Why it matters

Tradeweb is the fastest-growing big piece of Markets, but about half of its profit belongs to outside shareholders, and it has its own board. Coordinating with it (RepoAgent, Workspace integration) is a live business management task [S301, S302].

5. Microsoft as shareholder and product partner

Evidence

About 4% stake, 10-year term, $2.8bn minimum cloud spend [S147]; Workspace in Teams, Excel and PowerPoint add-ins, Autex on Azure, data on Microsoft Fabric [S120]; AI data partnerships with Anthropic, Databricks, Microsoft, OpenAI, Rogo and Snowflake [S120].

Why it matters

It puts LSEG's data inside the tools customers already use, which is LSEG's answer to the fear that AI tools will bypass terminals. Some investors see the rollout as slower than promised (see Part 2).

6. FX across the whole workflow

Evidence

FXall (dealer-to-client) plus FX Matching (dealer-to-dealer): $525bn average daily volume in 2025; ForexClear cleared $48,113bn notional, up 31.4% [S120]. Deutsche Börse (360T) and Cboe FX lack an interbank matching platform of this scale plus FX clearing in the same group; CME has EBS but no dealer-to-client platform like FXall. Inferred

Why it matters

The FX customer track in the JD sits here. A client can trade, confirm and clear FX within LSEG businesses, but each step is still a separate service under open access.

Don't say this

"LSEG is the oldest exchange in the world" or other history claims that the competitor research did not verify. And do not say Tradeweb is wholly owned: it is majority-owned and separately listed [S137].

Practice: "Who would you say are LSEG's biggest competitors?"
Testing Whether you see LSEG as a set of businesses, not one firm. Structure "It depends on the business line" then three lines with a rival and a number each: swaps clearing (Eurex, 13.5% of euro swaps), FX (Bloomberg FXGO), equities trading (Cboe Europe, 25%). Close with data (Bloomberg) and index (MSCI). Weave in Open access as the reason LCH competes for flow from every venue. Trap Naming only "other exchanges like Nasdaq and NYSE"; LSEG's biggest fights are with Bloomberg, Eurex and Cboe.
Practice: "Why LSEG and not ICE or CME?"
Testing Whether you know what makes LSEG different, with evidence. Weave in Figure 22: LSEG sits at the open, user-partnered end. Trap Saying LSEG is "the biggest"; it is fifth by market cap among exchange operators [S148].
Practice: "Where is LSEG vulnerable to competitors?"
Testing Honesty and judgement. Structure Pick two: euro swaps (Eurex rising from 8.4% to 13.5%) and data (Bloomberg first; FCA says users may overpay). Say what LSEG is doing about each: open access and margin efficiency in clearing; Workspace in Microsoft tools and long-term data contracts. Weave in Euronext leaving LCH SA shows open access cuts both ways. Trap Saying "AI will kill the data business" without numbers; LSEG says 90% of data revenue is real-time or proprietary [S71].
