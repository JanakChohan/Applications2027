Part 11

The scenario playbook

One answer method, one priority order, and 36 rehearsed situations built from how LSEG Markets actually works, so a hard question meets a steady frame instead of a scramble.

Every later stage of this process puts you in a situation and watches what you do. The Immersive Online Assessment is very likely a Cappfinity-style simulation: one storyline, emails and data to read, and "what would you do" choices scored against a strengths model [S264, S265, S289] Inferred. The strengths video gives you one take of roughly one to two minutes per question, according to a candidate anecdote [S291] Reported. The virtual assessment centre includes group work, where (on another LSEG track) two assessors watched group behaviour more than content [S292] Reported.

Across all three, the assessor scores two things: what you would do, and how you hold yourself while you decide. The JD says it directly: they want people who "recognise that what you achieve is equally important as how you achieve it" [S260]. This part gives you a fixed method so that the "how" stays calm even when the "what" is hard.

The frame-holding method

Use the same four steps for every scenario, in every stage. In a live interview the whole answer runs 45 to 75 seconds. In the recorded video you can let the "Act" step run longer, up to about two minutes in total, but keep the shape.

Key term

Frame: the structure you hold an answer in. If you have a frame (steps, an order of priorities, a closing line), a hostile follow-up only moves you inside the frame. Without one, every follow-up feels like starting again. Situational judgement: a test format that describes a work situation and asks what you would do. It scores judgement, not knowledge.

The priority order when good things collide

Most scenario questions are not about right versus wrong. They are about two good things that cannot both win: a client's speed against a check, a senior person's request against another's, helpfulness against confidentiality. Decide the order now, at your desk, so you never have to invent it on camera.

For LSEG Markets the order follows from what the businesses are. LSEG's venues and clearing houses are regulated market infrastructure: London Stock Exchange plc is a Recognised Investment Exchange, LCH Ltd is a Recognised Clearing House supervised by the Bank of England, and the FX venues are MTFs authorised by the FCA and the Central Bank of Ireland [S300, S304, S341] Confirmed. The Bank of England supervises CCPs to "protect and enhance financial stability in the UK" [S357]. LSEG names its own cyber risk as one that could "pose systemic risks" [S302]. A shortcut here is not a private matter between you and one client.

Why this order, tier by tier

  • Tier 1 beats everything because the firm's licence to operate is the product. LSEG's Integrity value reads: "We stand by our principles and deliver on our promises. We earn trust by acting responsibly" [S307]. The Code of Conduct prohibits trading on "confidential, proprietary, inside and/or material non-public information" [S307], and UK MAR defines inside information as precise, non-public and likely to have a significant effect on prices [S349].
  • Tier 2 beats process because fairness is written into LSEG's structure, not just its values. When LSEG took control of LCH, the OFT recorded that "No exchange will be favoured over any other and LSEG's trading services users will not be favoured over any other exchange's users" [S305]. LSEG's Partnership value begins: "Our open model is integral to how we do business" [S307]. In FX, the Global Code requires firms to "clearly and effectively identify and appropriately limit access to Confidential Information" (Principle 19) [S343].
  • Tier 3 beats speed because LSEG puts accountability for risk "within the first line of defence", with second-line oversight and third-line assurance [S300]. As an intern you are in the first line. Your job is to follow the control, escalate, and write it down, not to decide that a control does not apply today.
  • Tier 4 is still a duty. The JD says the division needs "instant reaction and long-term vision" [S260]. Never use the order as an excuse to be slow. The best answers say what you would do in the next ten minutes.

Don't say this

"I'd just use my judgement." It sounds like you will make up the order on the day. Say the order: "Rules first, then fairness, then the process, then speed, and I'd push hard on speed inside those."

Eight ways interviewers break a frame

Good interviewers push. They are not trying to catch you out for sport. They want to see whether your answer was a real decision or a memorised line. Each push below has a counter. The counter always does the same thing: it accepts the new information, then puts it back inside your frame.

How to tell a better reason from pressure

The hardest skill is knowing when to change your mind. Change it when the interviewer gives you a new fact ("the client is already approved for that route", "the control owner has signed a waiver"). Hold it when they give you only new pressure ("the MD is angry", "everyone else does it", "you'll lose the client"). Say which one it is: "That's new information, so I'd change step two" or "I understand that's uncomfortable, and I'd still do it this way." Assessors are often scoring exactly this distinction Inferred.

Changing your mind well

"That's a fair challenge. If the owner has already approved an exception, then yes, I'd use it and make sure it's logged. I'd change my answer on that point."

Changing your mind badly

"Oh, OK, then I suppose I would just do it." This tells the assessor that your first answer was a guess and that pressure works on you.

Staying calm physically

Your body leaks stress before your words do. Prepare the room and your breathing the way you prepare content.

Breathing

  • Before each question (or while the video prep timer runs): breathe in through the nose for four, out through the mouth for six. Twice. A longer out-breath slows you down.
  • When a hard follow-up lands: breathe out first, then say your Steady line. The out-breath is your three seconds.

Posture on video

  • Camera at eye level (books under the laptop). Face lit from the front, not a window behind you.
  • Sit back in the chair, then lean in slightly. Feet flat. Hands visible and still on the desk or moving gently with your points.
  • Look at the camera lens when making your main point and when closing, not at your own face on screen.

Notes

  • One index card behind or beside the camera: the four steps, the priority order, three numbers. Not a script: reading shows on camera.
  • For the virtual assessment centre: a blank sheet for notes during reading time, with your priority order written at the top.

Water and pace

  • A glass of water in reach. A sip is a legitimate two-second pause in a live interview.
  • Speak slower than feels natural. Pause at full stops. Under stress most people speed up by a third.
  • In the recorded video, the anecdote is one take per answer [S291] Reported. Use the untimed prep to sketch the four steps, not to write sentences.
Why this matters

In a strengths-based format, assessors watch for energy and authenticity as well as content [S264, S289]. Calm, unhurried delivery reads as confidence. A rushed, perfect script reads as rehearsal.

The scenario ladder: 36 cards

Each JD duty line, plus the two behaviour lines, gets three scenarios: easy (do you know the basics), medium (two goods collide), hard (pressure or an ethical edge). Practise them aloud with the four-step protocol, timing yourself. The figure is the map; the cards follow in the same order.

How to read a card

Setup is the situation. Trap is the answer most candidates give. Say is a model answer in the four-step shape: adapt the words, keep the order. Why gives the LSEG fact behind it. Twist is the follow-up push, and Hold is how to keep your frame.

Line 1: Solve problems for FX customers and understand their goals

1E. "What can FXall do for us?"Easy
Setup A mid-sized asset manager is new to FXall. Their treasurer says: "We need to show our board we get good prices, and we spend too long on manual work."
Trap Reciting a feature list before you know which feature matters.
Say "Can I check I have the two goals right: evidence of good execution, and less manual work? For evidence, FXall's Trade Performance Reporting lets you evaluate the cost and effectiveness of your execution. For manual work, trades can flow straight through to your own systems by FIX API or your treasury system, with confirmations and settlement instructions handled on the platform. I'd suggest we agree one measure for each goal now and look at it together in three months."
Why FXall offers confirmations, settlement instructions, Trade Performance Reporting and STP (straight-through processing: no re-keying) [S340]. The JD asks you to "understand their business goals" [S260]. Leading with their goals, not your product, is the Partnership value: "we work together to solve evolving needs" [S307].
Twist "Just tell us which execution method is cheapest." Hold: "It depends on your trade sizes and currency pairs. FXall has request-for-quote, bank price streams, an anonymous order book and algorithms like TWAP [S342]. I'd rather show you your own data than give a general answer that's wrong for you."
1M. Too many rejected trades (last look)Medium
Setup A hedge fund client says one liquidity provider keeps rejecting their trades after quoting. "They're using last look against us. What are you going to do about it?"
Trap Agreeing the bank is cheating, or defending the bank. Either way you have judged a third party on one client's say-so.
Say "I take that seriously, so let me get the facts: which provider, which pairs, what times, how many rejects. Last look is a final chance for a provider to accept or reject a request at its quoted price, and the FX Global Code says providers using it should be transparent and disclose it to clients. So I'd pull your own Trade Performance Reporting so you can see the reject pattern, point you to that provider's last-look disclosure, and raise the pattern internally with the team that manages liquidity providers. I'll come back to you by Thursday with what I've found."
Why FX Global Code Principle 17 (Dec 2024) [S343]; GFXC guidance says last look "should not be used for purposes of information gathering" [S344] Reported. Reporting data comes from FXall [S340]. The split (provider owns disclosure, venue supplies data) is this pack's reading Inferred.
Twist "Just tell us which other banks don't do it." Hold: "Which providers you trade with is your choice, and I'll help you set that up. I can't rank providers using other clients' trading. Your data, yes; theirs, no."
1H. "Which bank should we send our flow to?"Hard
Setup A corporate treasurer wants you to name the best bank among FXall's providers and hints that FXall should rank banks using what it sees across all clients. "You see everything. Help us."
Trap Feeling helpful and giving a name, or hinting at what other clients see. Both break fair treatment (tier 2) and confidentiality.
Say "I want you to get the best result, and I'll do that with your data, not anyone else's. FXall has over 200 liquidity providers; your own execution reports will show which fill you well on your pairs and sizes. What I can't do is use other clients' trading to rank banks for you: that's confidential to them, in the same way yours is to you. If it helps, I'll walk your team through reading your own reports next week."
Why 200+ liquidity providers, 2,400+ institutional clients [S340]. FX Global Code P19 and P20: limit access to confidential information and do not disclose it externally [S343]. Code of Conduct: never disclose confidential information without a legitimate need to know [S307]. A venue that picks favourites among providers loses the trust that makes it a venue Inferred.
Twist "Another platform would do this for us." Hold: "Then I'd ask how they protect your data too. The rule that stops me sharing theirs is the rule that protects yours."

Line 2: Maintain relationships and keep customers informed

2E. Prepare a client business reviewEasy
Setup Your manager asks you to draft slides for a quarterly business review with a large FXall client next week.
Trap Ten charts of volumes with no story, and no link to what the client said they wanted last time.
Say "I'd start from the goals the client set last quarter. Then three sections: what they traded and how it changed; where their workflow had friction, such as rejects, breaks or support tickets; and two options for next quarter. I'd check every number against the source system before it goes in, and ask you which commercial topics, like new products, it's right to raise."
Why LSEG's FX Senior Account Manager ad says the role leads "business reviews" and should "identify upsell, cross-sell, and new business opportunities across FX trading, workflow, data, and related LSEG solutions" [S313]. A new Group Head of Sales and Account Management joined in Jan 2026 [S306].
Twist "Client cancelled. Send it by email instead." Hold: "I'd send a one-page summary with the three points and the numbers, and offer a 15-minute call. Same content, shorter."
2M. FXall outage: the client calls at 8:05Medium
Setup 8:05 London time. A client can't connect to FXall and has month-end hedges to do. They're angry and want a fix time.
Trap Guessing the cause, promising a fix time you don't own, or going quiet until you know more.
Say "I'd say: 'I hear you, and I'm on it now. I'll call you back by 8:20 with what we know, even if it isn't much.' Then I'd log it through the incident process straight away, check whether it's one client or many, and ask the support team whether there's an approved alternative route for them to trade. At 8:20 I call back, as promised. I won't guess the cause or promise a fix time I don't control."
Why LSEG's H1 2026 principal risks include technology risk, where "system outages... could impact customer access" [S302]. The 2024 Workspace outage disrupted RNS publication and drew market-wide attention [S98] Reported. In infrastructure, reliability is the brand Inferred.
Twist "Can't you just do the trade for me over the phone?" Hold: "I can't execute trades for you; that isn't my role. What I can do is find out in the next few minutes which approved routes are open to you."
2H. "Off the record, what is Bank X doing?"Hard
Setup Over coffee, a long-standing, friendly client asks: "Off the record, is [big bank] pulling back from emerging market FX? You must see it."
Trap A hint, a smile, or "I can't say, but...". A nod is disclosure.
Say "I'd say it plainly and warmly: 'I can't talk about any other client's activity, on or off the record, and you'd want me to say exactly the same about you.' Then I'd move to what I can offer: our published volume figures, public market data, or a look at their own trading."
Why FX Global Code P19 and P20 [S343]; Code of Conduct need-to-know rule [S307]. Confidentiality is part of how LSEG's structure was approved: information barriers so rivals can deal with LCH "assured of the confidentiality of those discussions" [S305].
Twist "Come on, just yes or no." Hold: "Even yes or no is information. The answer's the same, and I'd still like to help with what I can share."

Line 3: Deal with post-sale activity

3E. New price stream not showingEasy
Setup A client asked to receive a new bank's price stream on FXall last week. It still isn't showing.
Trap Promising "it'll be on today" without knowing who has to approve what.
Say "I'd find the request first. Permissioning on FXall runs through FXall Admin, the reference data and permissioning tool, as a price stream entitlement request. I'd check whether it was submitted, who still has to act, for example the bank agreeing to stream to this client, and tell the client today exactly what is pending and with whom."
Why LSEG's FX Venue Onboarding ad lists "Price Stream Entitlement requests" in "FXall Admin" [S317]. PriceStream is disclosed bank streams [S342]. That the bank must agree is this pack's reading of "disclosed" Inferred.
Twist "Can't you just switch it on yourself?" Hold: "I don't have that access and I wouldn't want it. Entitlement controls protect both you and the bank."
3M. Trade break at 4pmMedium
Setup A client's back office says the confirmation for a large EUR/USD forward shows a different value date from their record. Settlement is close.
Trap Telling the client which side is right, or trying to amend the trade yourself to be helpful.
Say "Steady: 'Let me take the trade ID and both versions.' Sort: the risk is settling on the wrong date, so time matters, and amendments must be controlled. Act: I'd send it straight to the operations team that owns confirmations, and make sure the counterparty bank is contacted through the proper channel. Close: I tell the client who is handling it and when they'll hear back, and I log it so we can see whether the same break keeps happening."
Why FX Global Code P46: confirm trades "as soon as practicable"; P48: "identify and resolve confirmation and settlement discrepancies as soon as practicable"; P45: amendments "in a carefully controlled manner" [S343]. FXall provides confirmations and STP [S340].
Twist "The bank says it's our error." Hold: "It might be. The fix is the same: find the source, correct it through the controlled process, and if it's ours, say so and fix the cause."
3H. Invoice dispute and an exit threatHard
Setup A client says this quarter's invoice is far higher than they expected and they will move to a rival venue unless it's fixed. They want a credit on this call.
Trap Offering a discount you have no authority to give, or blaming the billing team.
Say "I'd acknowledge it and not argue on the call. Then I'd get the invoice and the contract, find exactly which line they dispute, and check with billing whether it's higher usage, a fee change they were told about, or an error. I'd come back by a set time with the explanation. If it's our error, we fix it. Any credit or price change is for my manager to decide, and it has to be consistent with how we treat other clients."
Why FX revenue mixes transaction fees (about 75%) with recurring membership and data fees (about 25%) [S303], so a bill can rise with usage. LSEG is building a "unified revenue and billing platform" [S301]. LSEG FX fee schedules were not found in research.
Twist "Your competitor offered us half price." Hold: "I'll pass that on accurately today. I won't match a price on the call; I'll make sure the person who decides hears it."
Source uncertain

How LSEG FX handles billing queries, and its fee schedules, were not found in public sources. Treat 3H as a general method, not a description of LSEG's process.

Line 4: Research into global markets and debt listings

4E. ISM or Main Market? A one-page answerEasy
Setup Your manager asks for a one-page comparison for an overseas issuer choosing where to list a bond.
Trap Copying an old LSE brochure that still talks about the "UKLA" and the "Prospectus Directive".
Say "I'd set out three rows. Who it's for: the International Securities Market is for qualified, professional investors; the Main Market can serve a wider investor base. Who decides admission: on ISM, the Exchange; it isn't on the FCA's Official List. What document: ISM uses admission particulars, and exempt issuers don't need them. I'd use the January 2026 rulebook terms, because the older info pack predates the current regime, and I'd flag the timings as 'check current'."
Why ISM Rulebook (Jan 2026): MTF for qualified investors, admission "determined by the Exchange", not on the Official List, admission particulars not required for exempt issuers [S346]. The ISM info pack cites UKLA and the Prospectus Directive and is out of date [S347].
Twist "So which is better?" Hold: "It depends on who the issuer wants to sell to. I'd lay out the trade-offs and let the issuer decide with its advisers."
4M. "Can we list on ISM by Friday?"Medium
Setup Monday morning. An issuer's adviser emails: they want ISM admission on Friday. They haven't submitted draft particulars yet.
Trap Saying yes to keep them happy.
Say "I'd do the arithmetic first. The Exchange aims to comment within three days of a first submission and two days of later ones, and wants final documents and a signed ISM Form 1 by 9am one business day before admission. Submit today, and first comments could come by Thursday, with the final-documents deadline also Thursday at 9am. So Friday is at serious risk. I'd tell them that honestly, say what would have to be true for it to work, check with the admissions team whether the express route or an exemption applies, and give a realistic date."
Why "Competitive turnaround times (3+2)"; 9am one business day before; decision via a Dealing Notice on RNS [S347]; express route exists [S346]. The info pack is older than the 2026 rulebook, so current timings need checking.
Twist "Their CEO is very well known. Can't you push it through?" Hold: "Every issuer gets the same review. I can make sure nothing on our side adds delay and that they know exactly what we need."
4H. "Pick a window that looks better"Hard
Setup Your research for an external client presentation shows London listings are recovering from a low base. A senior colleague asks you to choose a time window that makes the trend "look stronger".
Trap Quietly cherry-picking, or refusing in a way that embarrasses them.
Say "I'd offer a version that is both strong and fair: 'Can I show the recent rise and the longer period side by side, with context? For example, 2025 UK IPO proceeds were up 170% to £2.1bn, but from a weak 2024. If we only show the flattering window and a client checks, we lose more than a slide.' Then I'd build it that way."
Why 2025: 23 UK IPOs raising £2.1bn, up 170% on £777.7m [S192]; 88 companies left the LSE in 2024 [S200] Reported. Integrity: "We earn trust by acting responsibly" [S307]. LSEG is itself a listed company, so its external numbers are scrutinised Inferred.
Twist "It's only internal, relax." Hold: "Then let's label the window clearly, so nobody reuses it externally by mistake."

The composure set: five hard scenarios

These five are the ones most likely to rattle you, because each puts a senior person, a client or your own mistake in the way of the right answer. Each has a decision tree. The first two trees below are drawn; the other three are written as steps. Learn the questions in the trees, not the wording.

C1. Two senior people want the same scarce thing

Setup. Two directors each ask you, on the same morning, to spend tomorrow on their analysis. Or two account managers each want the one client slot left in next week's product demo. Each says theirs is the priority.

#Ask yourselfIf yesIf no
1Do I know each request's real deadline and what it's for?Go to 2.Ask both: "What decision does it feed, and by when?"
2Is there a real clash, or can both be done with a small change (half a day each, a later slot)?Go to 3.Propose the split to both, in writing.
3Does one request involve a control, a regulator or a client commitment already made?That one goes first (priority tiers 1 to 3). Tell the other person why, and when you'll start theirs.Go to 4.
4Is it my place to choose between two senior people?(It rarely is.)Take it to your line manager with both asks and a suggested order. Copy nobody into a fight.
Say this in the interview

"I wouldn't pick between two directors myself. I'd find out what each piece is for and when it's needed, see if both can be done with a small change, and if not, take both requests to my manager with a suggested order. Both directors would hear the same thing from me."

C2. A senior person asks you to skip a control

Setup. A senior colleague, under deadline, says: "Just send the client file without the second sign-off. I'll take responsibility."

Say this in the interview

"I'd want to help them hit the deadline, so I'd first check exactly which check it is and whether there's an approved exception. If there isn't, I'd tell them I can't skip it, and I'd escalate straight away to get the second signer moving. 'I'll take responsibility' is kind, but it doesn't change who owns the control."

C3. You overhear possible inside information or another client's flow

Setup. In a shared kitchen, or on an open Teams call, you hear that a named company is about to announce a bond deal or a takeover, or how much a named bank traded yesterday. Later a friend at university, who trades shares, asks "anything interesting at work?"

Key term

Insider list: a record that issuers (and people acting for them) must keep of everyone with access to inside information, with the reason and the date and time of access, kept for at least five years [S348]. Information barrier: rules and systems that stop confidential information flowing between teams that shouldn't share it [S305].

C4. A mistake you made is in a report already sent to a client

Setup. On Tuesday you find that a chart in Monday's client report used the wrong month's volume. Your manager sent it to the client.

#Ask yourselfIf yesIf no
1Am I sure it's an error? (Five minutes to check, not a day.)Go to 2.Note what you checked; mention it to your manager anyway.
2Could it change a client's decision, or touch a regulated report?Tell your manager within the hour, in person or by call, then in writing.Still tell your manager today. Small errors hidden become big errors found.
3Has my manager decided how to tell the client?Prepare the correction: what was wrong, the right figure, what it changes. Short and plain.Offer a draft correction; don't contact the client yourself.
4Do I know why it happened?Fix the cause: a check step, a source label, a template.Find out before the next report goes.
Say this in the interview

"I'd tell my manager straight away: what's wrong, what the right figure is, and whether it changes anything for the client. I wouldn't quietly resend it. Then I'd fix the cause, probably by adding a check against the source before anything goes out. Integrity at LSEG is 'deliver on our promises', and a correct report is the promise."

C5. Acting on incomplete information against a deadline

Setup. At 4pm you need to send a recommendation by 5pm. One key data point won't arrive until tomorrow.

#Ask yourselfIf yesIf no
1Do I know what decision the deadline serves, and who makes it?Go to 2.Ask now: one line to the requester.
2Is the decision easy to reverse if the missing data changes the answer?Recommend on the best estimate, label every assumption, say what would change it, and when you'll update.Go to 3.
3Does it touch a regulatory filing, a client commitment, or risk limits?Escalate now: ask for the deadline to move or for a senior person to accept the gap on record.Send a range with the missing piece clearly named, and a time for the update.
Say this in the interview

"I'd first ask whether the decision can be undone. If it can, I'd send my best answer on time with the assumptions in bold and a time for the update. If it can't, or it touches risk or a regulator, I'd say so now and ask for either more time or a senior decision to go ahead with the gap. Late and labelled beats on time and wrong."

The CV grill

At the assessment centre interview, expect at least one line of your CV to be tested three levels deep. Your CV details aren't in this pack, so this section gives the method, eight attacks with frame-holding answers, and a drill. The worked examples are placeholders: replace them with your own.

How a three-levels-deep attack works

LevelWhat they askWhat they are testing
1. What"Tell me about the investment society role."Can you explain it plainly in 20 seconds?
2. How and how much"How did you grow attendance? From what to what? How do you know?"Is there a real number, and did you measure it honestly?
3. Why you, and what if"Wasn't that just because term started? What would have happened without you? What would you do differently?"Can you separate your contribution from luck and the team, and do you learn?

The frame for a CV grill is the same four steps, with one rule added: never claim more at level 3 than you claimed at level 1. Inflation shows up at the third question, not the first.

Eight attacks and how to hold the frame

A1. "So what did you do? Not the team. You."
Hold "The team did the event; my part was the sign-up process and the reminder emails. That's where attendance moved." Name your part precisely. Credit others without shrinking yourself.
A2. "That number seems high. How did you measure it?"
Hold "From the sign-up sheet, comparing the four weeks before with the four weeks after. It isn't a controlled test, so some of it may be the time of term." Give the method and its limit before they do.
A3. "That sounds like an easy job."
Hold "Parts of it were. The hard part was [one specific thing], and that's where I learned [skill]." Don't defend everything; locate the hard bit.
A4. "Why did you leave after only eight weeks?"
Hold "It was a fixed eight-week role; I finished it and handed over [what]." State the fact, then the handover. No apology, no story.
A5. "You say you're interested in markets, but nothing here shows it."
Hold "Fair: it's not in a job title yet. It shows in [one habit or project]. For example, I've been following how London's FX share held at 37.8% in the latest BIS survey [S162] and what that means for a venue like FXall." Then stop.
A6. "Tell me about this grade / this gap."
Hold "That module went badly because [one honest reason]. What I changed was [habit], and my next result was [result]." One sentence of cause, two of change.
A7. "What would your manager there say your weakness was?"
Hold "Probably that I took too long to ask for help early on. By the end I was checking in after the first hour on anything unclear." Real, specific, already improving. No disguised strengths.
A8. "Why should any of that matter to us at LSEG?"
Hold "Because the internship asks for [JD phrase, for example 'curiosity for data... to draw conclusions and propose solutions'] [S260], and this is where I did that: [one line]." Map to the JD's own words.

The four-line drill for every CV bullet

For each bullet on your CV, write four lines on a card. If you can't fill a line, the bullet is weak: change the bullet or prepare harder.

LineQuestion it answers
1. WhatWhat was it, in one plain sentence a stranger understands?
2. NumberWhat changed, from what to what, measured how? (If no number exists, say what you would have measured.)
3. Your partWhich decisions or actions were yours, not the team's?
4. DifferentlyWhat would you do differently now, and why?
Worked placeholder 1: replace with your own

PLACEHOLDER bullet: "Events officer, university finance society: grew weekly market briefing attendance."
What: I ran a 30-minute weekly briefing on one market story for society members.
Number: PLACEHOLDER: average attendance went from about 15 to about 40 over one term, from the sign-up sheet.
My part: I changed the format to one chart and one question, and moved sign-up to a form with a reminder the day before.
Differently: I'd have kept the old format for two weeks alongside, so I could tell how much was the format and how much was the start of term.

Worked placeholder 2: replace with your own

PLACEHOLDER bullet: "Retail assistant: cut weekly stock-check time."
What: I suggested a new order for the weekly stock count in a busy shop.
Number: PLACEHOLDER: the count went from about three hours to about two, measured over four weeks.
My part: I timed each section, spotted that we walked the floor twice, and proposed one route. My manager approved it; the team ran it.
Differently: I'd have asked the evening team first. They had a reason for the old order (deliveries), and the first week went badly because I hadn't asked.

Why this matters

Placeholder 2 is deliberately close to scenario 8M (the old spreadsheet). The best CV stories double as scenario evidence: when an assessor asks "what would you do?", you can say "I've done a version of this" and give the four lines. That is what a strengths-based assessor hopes to hear [S264].

Don't say this

"We increased engagement significantly." No owner, no number, no method. Under a level-2 question it collapses. Every bullet you can't defend at level 3 should come off the CV before you submit.
