Part 16

Gaps, open questions and self-check

What this pack could not establish, where its sources disagree, how the research was done, and what to re-check before each stage.

A research pack is only as good as its honesty about what it does not know. This part collects every "Gaps" section from the seven research workstreams in one place, groups them by theme, and tells you two things for each: why it matters for you, and how you could close it yourself. It then lists the places where sources disagreed and how the pack settled each one, so that you never repeat a figure an interviewer could correct.

Why this matters

Interviewers at an infrastructure business value people who know the limits of their data. If you say "the annual report gives 28,516 employees, although the job advert still says 25,000", you show exactly the "curiosity for data" the JD asks for [JD]. Saying "I couldn't find that, so I'd like to ask you" is a strength, not a weakness.

What could not be established

Each table lists the gap, why it matters to you, and the quickest way to close it. "Ask at AC" means it is a good, genuine question for the assessment centre, where LSEG says you will "meet our people" [S264].

Tags and how to read them

Confirmed means read in a primary or official source (or two credible outlets). Reported means a single secondary source or a search snippet that was not fully read. Inferred means the pack's own reasoning from sourced facts. The same document sometimes has several IDs because each workstream numbered its own sources: for example, the 2025 preliminary results are S22, S72, S120 and S301.

Limits on the research

  • Search budget ran out late in several workstreams. WS1 ran 11 searches before the 12th and 13th were refused, and made up for it with direct fetches and the Workday API. WS2 ran out before repeating the Refinitiv integration and FTSE index-error checks. WS6 lost planned searches on YouTube vlogs, Bright Network pay and extra forum threads. WS8 ran out before reading LSEG FX's last-look disclosure. WS3 ran out of source IDs, so the SIX and Aquis deal was left uncited. WS4 used 37 searches and 15 fetches plus 13 full-page downloads.
  • Sites that blocked scripted access (HTTP 403): Glassdoor, TheStudentRoom, Reddit, Bright Network, Gradcracker and Tradeweb.com. Anything from them is a snippet and marked as anecdote. WallStreetOasis returned nothing usable.
  • Paywalls: FT and Bloomberg (for example S55, S87, S102, S131, S225, S234), Risk.net and FX Markets [S240], Coalition Greenwich and the full Burton-Taylor reports. Only headlines were used, and never as the sole basis for a claim.
  • Other failures: McKinsey's report returned HTTP 503 [S187]; londonstockexchange.com pages are rendered by JavaScript and gave no content; Etrading Software's site was behind Cloudflare [S248]; LCH's 2008 press release domain no longer resolves [S355]; old.lseg.com and Forbes returned 403 [S51, S233]; the lseg.com culture page returned 404.
  • Candidate CV details were not available, so CV-based answers in this pack use placeholders and a method, not your real examples.
