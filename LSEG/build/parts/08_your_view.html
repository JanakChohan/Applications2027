Part 8

Forming your own view

Eleven live arguments about LSEG, each with both sides in numbers, the signpost that would change your mind, and a position you can defend in a minute.

Interviewers at LSEG are not testing whether you can recite the annual report. The JD asks for people who can "connect the dots and think strategically" and "analyse information to draw conclusions and propose solutions" (JD). The fastest way to show both is to hold a view on a live question, back it with one number, and name the risk to your own view. This Part gives you eleven of those questions. For each one you get the bull case (the argument that it is good for LSEG), the bear case (the argument that it is bad), the signpost you would watch, and a suggested position in a purple Your opinion goes here box. The suggestions are a starting point. Change them if the evidence persuades you otherwise: an opinion you have argued yourself into sounds very different from one you memorised.

Key term

Bull case / bear case: the optimistic and pessimistic arguments about a company or decision. Analysts use the words for any two-sided debate, not just share prices. Signpost: a specific, dated piece of evidence that would tell you which side is winning. Naming one is what separates an opinion from a guess.

How to form and defend a view in 60 seconds

Every answer in this Part follows the same five-step shape. Learn the shape once and you can use it on any question, including ones not in this pack.

StepWhat you sayTimeWorked example (Argument 11)
1. ClaimYour answer in one sentence. Pick a side.5 s"Markets is the part of LSEG I would bet on for the next two years."
2. MechanismWhy it is true: the cause, not an adjective.15 s"Its revenue comes from trading and clearing volumes, and from customers who post margin, so it grows when markets are busy and it is not the part AI tools threaten."
3. NumberOne figure, with its date.10 s"In H1 2026 it grew 11.9%, against 5.1% for Data & Analytics, at a 58.6% margin." [S70]

Say this in the interview

"My view is X, because Y. The number that persuades me is Z. The best argument against me is W, so the thing I'd watch is V." Five sentences, under a minute, and every one carries information.

Don't say this

"There are pros and cons on both sides." This is the most common non-answer in assessment centres. It tells the interviewer you read both sides and could not decide. Decide, then show you know what would change your mind.

Two rules make the method work under pressure. First, the mechanism carries the answer. "LSEG is well positioned" is an adjective; "LCH must clear for rival venues on non-discriminatory terms, so banks trust it with their swaps" is a mechanism [S305]. Second, conceding the risk makes you more credible, not less. Interviewers at an infrastructure firm spend their days managing risk. A candidate who names the risk to their own view sounds like a colleague.

The argument map: where to spend your preparation

Not every argument deserves equal effort. Figure 26c places the eleven on two axes: how much the question matters to LSEG's value and strategy, and how settled the evidence is today. The top-left corner (big and still open) is where a well-argued opinion earns most credit, because the interviewer cannot simply look up the answer. Why it is live. On 11 February 2026 reports emerged that Elliott, the activist hedge fund, had built a stake [S86]. It reportedly pushed for a portfolio review and a £5bn buyback [S226] (RTÉ wrote "$5 billion", so the currency differs between sources [S222]). LSEG announced a £3bn buyback on 26 February [S72]. Non-controlling interest (minority interest): the share of a subsidiary's profit that belongs to its other shareholders. LSEG consolidates 100% of Tradeweb's revenue but owns only about 50.9% of its economics [S74].

Airtel Money, priced on 1 October 2026, is the biggest test so far [S232]. This matters for the JD's research track ("debt listings, valuations, and shareholder and geographic analysis").

Key term

UKLR: the UK Listing Rules, in force 29 July 2024, which replaced the premium and standard segments with one category for commercial companies [S150]. POATRs: the Public Offers and Admissions to Trading Regulations, which replaced the UK prospectus regime on 19 January 2026 [S151]. PISCES: a sandbox that lets private companies' shares trade at occasional auctions; LSE's version is the Private Securities Market (PSM) [S152].

5. Open access: strength or leak?

Why it is live. When LSEG took control of LCH in 2012-13, the competition regulator (the OFT) recorded that open access would go into LCH's articles: services "must be offered on terms that are fair, reasonable, open and non-discriminatory... No exchange will be favoured over any other and LSEG's trading services users will not be favoured over any other exchange's users" [S305]. That neutrality is why the whole market uses LCH. It also means customers can leave, and one did: Euronext moved its cash equity clearing out of LCH SA.

Key term

Open access vs vertical silo: in a vertical silo, an exchange group owns the trading venue and its clearing house and keeps the trades in-house (Deutsche Börse describes its "vertically integrated business model" [S127]). Under open access, the clearing house clears trades from any venue on equal terms, and the venue can use any clearing house.

Bull case: open access is the moatBear case: open access lets value walk out
Neutrality wins the flow. SwapClear has over 90% of cleared interest rate swap notional outstanding (LSEG's figure) [S303]; $1,941trn notional cleared in 2025, up 21.2% [S301]. In GBP SONIA swaps the share was 99.995% in Q1 2026 [S140] Reported.Euronext left. Securities & Reporting revenue fell 3.0% on "the final impact of the termination of the Euronext clearing agreement", and Net Treasury Income fell on "loss of business from Euronext" [S301].
Netting rewards one big pot. The more trades sit at one clearing house, the more they offset and the less margin each member posts Inferred. That is why members stay even when they could leave.The silo is gaining in euros. Eurex's share of EUR swaps rose to 13.5% in Q3 2025 from 8.4% a year earlier; LCH fell to 86.5% from 91.6% [S139] Reported.
It is LSEG's identity. "When other exchange groups focused on vertical integration of trading and clearing, we championed open access" [S300]. LSEG's value "Partnership" is defined as "Our open model is integral to how we do business" [S307].Politics can force a leak. EU EMIR 3 requires some EU firms to hold an "active account" at an EU clearing house from 25 June 2025 [S161]. The EU Commissioner said the aim is "reducing our exposure to UK CCPs" [S160].
Regulators trust neutrality. The EU extended equivalence for UK clearing houses to 30 June 2028 [S160].LSEG venues get no advantage. Under open access LCH must not favour LSEG venues [S305], so LSEG cannot bundle clearing to win trading.
What would change your mind
  • The first EMIR 3 active-account reports, due by 31 July 2026: did euro swap volume actually move to Eurex? [S257] Reported.
  • Clarus quarterly CCP share data for EUR swaps [S139]: a steady climb above 15% for Eurex would be a warning.
  • The EU decision on UK clearing house equivalence before 30 June 2028 [S160].
Your opinion goes here

Suggested position: A strength, and the leak is the price of it. Euronext's exit cost a few per cent of one revenue line; neutrality is what earns SwapClear its dominant share. The real threat is political (EMIR 3), not commercial.

One sentence: "Open access is why banks trust LCH with over 90% of cleared swaps; losing Euronext's equity clearing was the price of that trust, and a fair one."

Concede: Eurex's euro share has risen [S139]. If you are asked, say you would watch whether that is policy-driven box-ticking or real migration.

7. Tokenisation: hype or real?

Why it is live. On 1 September 2026 London Stock Exchange announced UK tokenised equity structures, including xStocks (1:1 backed tokenised versions of listed shares), and a partnership with Payward to connect regulated infrastructure to digital-native distribution [S228]. In July, HSBC Orion became the first firm approved to go live as a Digital Securities Depository in the Bank of England's sandbox, with LSEG's depository as the investor-side link for the UK's first digital gilt (DIGIT), due by Q1 2027 [S246, S221].

Key term

Tokenisation: representing a security as a digital token on a shared ledger (a blockchain), so ownership can move and settle almost instantly [S246]. Depository: the record of who owns which securities. Digital Securities Sandbox (DSS): a Bank of England and FCA regime for testing this; it opened on 30 September 2024 [S168].

Bull case: it is realBear case: it is mostly hype
The state is issuing. Rachel Reeves's Mansion House speech (14 Jul 2026) committed to the UK being the first G7 country to issue a digital sovereign bond "by early next year" [S245].The numbers are forecasts. Morgan Stanley and Oliver Wyman's $2.3trn of tokenised real-world assets by 2030 is a base case, mostly collateral mobility [S191] Reported.
LSEG is building plumbing. Digital Markets Infrastructure launched Sept 2025, six fund providers onboarded; Digital Settlement House launched H1 2026; Digital Securities Depository planned H2 2026 [S72, S70].Trust has to be built. The Bank of England frames it cautiously: the technology can be adopted, but trust must be earned [S184]. Hoggett herself: tokenisation "must develop in a way that preserves the trust, rights and role of regulated markets" [S228].
Collateral is the killer use. The Bank of England is consulting on "permitting tokenised assets as eligible collateral for CCPs" [S184]. LCH holds £277bn of member collateral [S74], so faster collateral movement matters directly Inferred.It could disintermediate incumbents. Oliver Wyman: tokenisation could shift 35% of current FMI revenue (about $45bn) to new token-native infrastructure [S185].
Rivals are moving. ICE and OKX reportedly plan 24/7 tokenised US stocks [S251] Reported.Early and small. xStocks trading on LSE 24 is planned for 2027, subject to approval [S228]. No revenue figure exists yet.

8. Will T+1 hurt or help?

Why it is live. T+1 means a trade settles (cash and securities change hands) one business day after the trade, rather than two. HM Treasury published a draft statutory instrument on 20 November 2025 to make T+1 standard in the UK "from 11 October 2027" [S158]. The EU moves on the same day [S159]. That is during your graduate year if you convert.

Bull case: it helps LSEGBear case: it hurts

LSEG sells the fix. RepoAgent (Post Trade Solutions with Tradeweb) "helps banks and clients reduce settlement fails in the repo market" [S301]. Tighter deadlines make that product more valuable Inferred.Operating cost and strain. LCH, venues and their clients must adapt processes and systems [S158] Inferred.
UK and EU align. The same date on both sides avoids cross-border mismatches [S158, S159].Interim deadlines. T+0 allocations and confirmations milestones are reported for December 2026 [S247] Reported, snippet.
Source uncertain

Your opinion goes here

Suggested position: A short-term operational headache and a long-term help. LSEG's Markets businesses make money from volume and from solving post-trade pain, and T+1 creates pain that customers will pay to remove.
