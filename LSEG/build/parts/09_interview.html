Stage 1: the application and CV

What is confirmed

  • You apply on Workday with an application and a resume (CV). The posting went live on 21 September 2026 and closes on 30 October 2026, but "may close early" [S260] Confirmed.
  • "Please review all the graduate programme opportunities and make one application." If you apply more than once, LSEG reviews only the first [S260]. Pick this posting deliberately. Do not apply to the Business Analyst internship as a backup.
  • Eligibility: penultimate-year undergraduate due to finish in summer 2028; LSEG says ideal candidates have the right to work in the UK without restrictions for the programme [S260].
  • Hybrid working, 3 days a week in the office at 5 Canada Square [S260].

What the screen is probably looking for

LSEG does not say how CVs are screened. The safest reading is that the screen checks the JD's own list Inferred. Stage 2: the Immersive Online Assessment

What it is

LSEG calls it an "untimed Immersive Online Assessment" designed "to give you an insight into what it's like to work at LSEG" [S260]. Its hints page describes "an interactive assessment designed to help us understand your strengths, potential and approach to solving real-world challenges", and tells you to visit an "Assessment Preparation Hub" first [S264] Confirmed.

Key term

Immersive assessment: an online test built as a story. You play a new joiner, read emails and data, and answer questions as the day unfolds. There is no separate "maths test" and "verbal test"; the numbers sit inside the story [S289]. Situational judgement test (SJT): a question that gives you a work scenario and asks what you would do, or how you would rank possible actions.

Who probably runs it: Cappfinity

The words "Assessment Preparation Hub" on LSEG's page link to hub.preparationplus.com. That page's HTML title is "Cappfinity Preparation Hub" and its description reads "Powered by Cappfinity" [S264, S265]. So LSEG's partner for this stage is very likely Cappfinity (formerly Capp) Inferred, high confidence. LSEG does not name the vendor in text.

Source uncertain

Three test-prep sellers say LSEG uses SHL timed numerical, verbal and logical tests, and one mentions a telephone interview [S286, S287, S288]. One unverified snippet claims a shift to Aon and SHL [S299]. These do not match LSEG's own 2026 wording (untimed, immersive, video) and may describe older cycles or other roles. The live Cappfinity link is the stronger evidence for the 2027 cycle. Practical answer: prepare mainly for an immersive strengths simulation, with a little timed numerical practice as insurance.

What it probably looks like

This is Cappfinity's general format, from a third-party guide that does not mention LSEG [S289] Reported:

  • One storyline set in a realistic role, with situational "strengths" questions.
  • Numerical and verbal tasks built into the story (for example, read a short data table in an email, then answer a question about it).
  • Sometimes a written or short video task.
  • Sections "often generously timed or untimed".
  • Some questions ask both what you would do and how much you would enjoy doing it.

One Glassdoor snippet from an LSEG graduate candidate describes a "Behavior Assessment with personality, behavior and logic tests without time limit", with mainly behavioural questions that were "very well-crafted" [S291] Reported, anecdote.

Stage 3: the strengths-based video interview

Format, from what candidates report

LSEG confirms the style and themes: strengths-based questions about "your experiences, motivations, strengths and what inspires you about a career at LSEG" [S260, S264] Confirmed. The mechanics come only from anecdotes:

  • About 8 questions, no overall time limit, "unlimited time to prepare", but "only one attempt to record an answer of 1-2 minutes" (Glassdoor, graduate programme) [S291] Reported, anecdote. The count may differ by year or track.
  • The invite tended to arrive about a day after the online assessment; waiting 2 to 3 days with no invite was seen by some as a bad sign (TheStudentRoom, 2026 graduate threads) [S293] Reported, low confidence.
  • Cappfinity describes its own video product as "fully asynchronous", meaning you record alone and someone reviews later [S290]. Whether LSEG uses Cappfinity for the video too is not stated Inferred, medium confidence.
Key term

Strengths-based interview: an interview that asks what you are naturally good at and what gives you energy, not only what you have done. The idea is that people perform best in work that uses their strengths and that they enjoy. Competency interview: the older style, asking for past evidence of set behaviours ("Tell us about a time you..."), usually answered with STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Recording checklist

  • Test camera, microphone and connection the day before.
  • Camera at eye level, face lit from the front, plain background.
  • Use the untimed preparation window to jot 3 bullet prompts, then look at the camera, not the notes.
  • Aim for 90 seconds. The reported window is 1 to 2 minutes [S291].
  • Answer first, in one sentence. Then one example. Then the link to LSEG.
  • If you stumble, keep going. A small recovery looks human; you only get one take [S291].
  • Smile where it is natural, especially when talking about what you enjoy.
  • Do all questions in one sitting so your energy stays even.
Say this in the interview

For "What inspires you about a career at LSEG?" (LSEG's own theme [S264]): "I get most energy from understanding how a system works end to end. LSEG is one of the few places where you can see a trade from the venue, through clearing at LCH, to reporting, inside one group. The internship lets me see that from the inside." Then give your own example of enjoying a system or process.

Virtual AC habits that help

  • In the group exercise, say other people's names when you build on their points ("Building on Priya's point about cost..."). It shows listening on camera, where body language is hard to read Inferred.
  • Offer to keep time early. It is a real contribution and it is visible Inferred.
  • In the case, state the recommendation in the first 20 seconds of your presentation. A summary without a decision is a common way to lose marks (see H2).
  • Keep a printed one-page fact sheet (H1 2026 Markets numbers, the five businesses, the four values) beside your screen for the interview, but do not read from it.

Questions A to B: motivation and Markets

A. Motivation and why LSEG (6)

Q1 (A1). Why LSEG?
Testing whether your interest is specific and real, and whether you see LSEG as more than "the stock exchange". Structure (1) one concrete moment that got you interested in market infrastructure; (2) one thing about LSEG's model you find interesting; (3) what you want to learn in 9 weeks and how it links to your next step. Weave in the Markets Division "combines the Group's flagship trading and clearing businesses" (LSE, Turquoise, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, LCH) with risk, collateral and reporting capabilities [S273]; LCH must clear on "fair, reasonable, open and non-discriminatory" terms, even for rival venues [S305]. Trap talking only about prestige or the building; reciting the purpose statement with no personal link; confusing LSEG with LSE (the university).
Q2 (A2). Why this internship and not an investment bank?
Testing self-awareness, and whether infrastructure is a choice, not a fallback. Structure what draws you to the "plumbing" (venues, clearing, data) over deal-making; one example of you enjoying systems, process or client service; what this internship lets you see (the trade lifecycle end to end). Weave in the JD: interns "help customers across the trade life cycle", across clearing and reporting [S260]. Trap criticising banks; giving "work-life balance" as the main reason.
Q3 (A3). What do you know about the business areas an intern could join?
Testing whether you read the JD and can describe each business in one plain sentence. Structure name the five (LSE, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise, LCH) [S260]; one line each; the two that interest you most and why. Weave in H1 2026 Markets income £1,920m, up 11.9% at constant currency [S285]; Markets is about 40% of group revenue [S71]. Trap a list of names with no understanding; a fixed preference so strong you seem inflexible (LSEG places you using what it learns) [S260].
Q4 (A4). What inspires you about a career at LSEG?
Testing energy and motivation; this is LSEG's own wording for the video stage [S264]. Structure one thing that energises you; a short story that shows it; a link to a specific LSEG activity. Weave in LSE 24, a planned venue running 17:00 to 07:50, with ETPs first in H1 2027, subject to approval [S278]; the Private Securities Market's first transactions in H1 2026 [S285]. Trap "fast-paced and dynamic" with nothing behind it.
Q5 (A5). Where do you see yourself after this internship?
Testing whether you are likely to convert and stay. Structure you hope to return on the Business Graduate Programme; show you know its shape; stay open on the exact area. Weave in 12 months, Commercial and Business Analyst pathways, an 8-week group project [S268]; "Ignite" mid-point offsite and 5 study days on the FTSE Russell version [S261]. Trap naming a different employer as your long-term plan.
Q6 (A6). Tell us about yourself.
Testing concise communication and a link to the role. Structure present (degree, one interest), past (two relevant experiences), future (why this role now). 60 to 90 seconds. Weave in the JD's "demonstrable interest in financial markets ... through your studies, work experience or extracurricular activities" [S260]. Trap reading your CV line by line.

B. Why this division: Markets (5)

Q7 (B1). Which of LSE, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise or LCH would you most like to join, and why?
Testing specific interest plus flexibility. Structure first choice with a reason tied to a skill; a second choice; say you are open. Weave in (pick one) FX revenue £145m in H1 2026, up 7.6%, with average daily volume $561bn [S285]; ForexClear notional cleared $33,311bn, up 45.2% [S285]; UK equities average daily value traded £6,671m, up 33.5% [S285]. Trap picking a business you cannot describe.
Q8 (B2). The JD mentions solving problems for FX customers. What are an FX client's goals?
Testing client empathy and basic FX. Structure who the clients are (asset managers, corporates, banks); what they want (good price, low cost, reliable execution, a clean audit trail); how a venue helps. Weave in Forward First Fixing did over $330bn in Q2 2026, a record [S285]; LSEG cites a 90%+ cut in manual processing for its largest FFF client [S238]. Trap talking about FX as retail speculation.
Q9 (B3). How would you approach research into debt listings, valuations, and shareholder and geographic analysis?
Testing analytical thinking and knowing what a debt listing is. Structure define the question; pick data sources; describe the analysis; say how you would present it. Weave in the JD's exact words [S260]; Airtel Money's Main Market IPO, priced 1 Oct 2026 at £1.96 a share, about 16.5% to 17.5% in public hands [S232], as a live example of shareholder analysis. Trap overclaiming technical skills.
Q10 (B4). What do risk teams in a market infrastructure business do?
Testing why risk matters at a clearing house or venue. Structure monitor (exposures, margin, operational risk); analyse (data, stress); report and deliver projects. Weave in JD: "monitoring and analysing risks, and interpreting data in readiness for the delivery of risk projects or reporting" [S260]; LSEG runs a "three lines of defence model" [S74]. Trap treating risk management as compliance only.
Q11 (B5). What is the difference between a trading venue and a clearing house, and why might one group own both?
Testing joined-up thinking: the JD asks you to "connect the dots" [S260]. Structure a venue matches buyers and sellers; a clearing house stands between them after the trade; owning both supports end-to-end workflow, but LCH runs on open access. Weave in CEO on the Q1 2026 call: "straight through FXall execution capabilities into ForexClear" [S284]; LCH's open-access rule: "No exchange will be favoured over any other" [S305]. Trap claiming LSEG sells venue and clearing as a bundle (no LSEG statement says so, and open access makes it unlikely) [S305].

Questions C: strengths

General scaffold for every strengths question: a genuine answer about what you enjoy and do well, then a short real example, then how often you use it [S289, S290] Inferred. Short, honest, specific. These questions have no "right" answer, only consistent and believable ones.

Q12 (C1). What energises you?
Testing real motivators, and whether they fit a fast, client-facing, data-heavy role. Structure name the thing; why; a recent example; how it would show up at LSEG. Weave in the JD's "curiosity for data" and "connect the dots" [S260]. Trap answering what you think they want; "I love challenges" with no detail.
Q13 (C2). What drains your energy?
Testing self-awareness and honesty. Structure a real drain; how you manage it; why it will not stop you doing the job. Weave in nothing from LSEG; this answer is about you. Trap a fake weakness ("perfectionism"); naming something central to the role, such as talking to clients.

F. Markets knowledge for beginners (8)

Practise each of these to "explain it to a friend in 30 seconds" level. Plain words beat jargon.

Q35 (F1). What does a central counterparty (CCP) do?
Testing the core LCH idea. Structure after a trade, the CCP becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer; it collects margin (collateral); if a member defaults it uses that member's margin and default fund first, then its own funds, then other members' default fund [S93]. Weave in LCH says it has "managed 9 member defaults and, in all cases, losses were within the initial margin held" [S351]; Daniel Maguire led the unwinding of Lehman's LCH-cleared bond and repo portfolio from 1 Sep 2008 [S273]. Q36 (F2). What is a spot FX trade versus a forward?

Weave in LSEG FX average daily volume $561bn in H1 2026 [S285]. Trap mixing up FX venues and banks.
