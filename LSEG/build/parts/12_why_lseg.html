Part 12

Why LSEG, in three lengths

An answer built from how LSEG works, not from adjectives: compressed to 30 seconds, expanded to 2 minutes, and finished with one specific thing you would work on.

"Why LSEG?" is the question every candidate prepares, so it is the one where most answers sound the same. The way to stand out is to answer with mechanisms: how the business is built, why customers pay, and where the work actually happens. A mechanism is something a rival could not say about itself. "LSEG is a global leader" could be said by ICE, CME or Deutsche Börse. "LCH has to clear for rival venues on non-discriminatory terms, which is why banks trust it with their swaps" could only be said about LSEG [S305].

This Part gives you the four mechanism answers in compressed form, then the same material at three lengths: 30 seconds (video interview opener), 2 minutes (a full interview answer), and a close that names one thing you would work on. Then it covers the follow-ups: why this programme rather than a bank, why not a rival, and the honest case against LSEG.

Key term

Key term

The one-liners

  • Customer value: "Customers pay for safety that has been tested: nine defaults, all inside initial margin."
  • The centre: "Business management sits where those businesses and the central functions meet, which is the part I want to learn."

Why LSEG in 30 seconds

Use this as the opening of a video interview answer, or when an interviewer says "briefly". About 90 words: 30 to 35 seconds at a calm speaking pace. Read it aloud three times, then say it without the page.

Say this: 30 seconds

"What draws me to LSEG is how it's built. It runs trading venues like the London Stock Exchange, Tradeweb and FXall, but its clearing house, LCH, has open access in its rules. It has to clear for rival venues on fair terms, and banks still sit on its risk committee. That neutrality is why SwapClear clears over 90% of cleared interest rate swaps. Markets is now 40% of the group and its fastest-growing part, and business management sits right where those businesses meet. That's where I want to learn."

Sources behind each sentence: open access [S305, S304]; members and clients on the LCH risk committee [S304]; over 90% of cleared IRS notional outstanding, LSEG's own figure [S303]; Markets 40% [S71] and fastest-growing at +11.9% in H1 2026 [S70].

Don't say this

"LSEG is a world-leading, innovative and diverse company with great values." Every word could apply to a rival, and the interviewer will hear it from most candidates that day.

About 300 words. The time marks show where you should be; if you are running long, cut the fourth paragraph first. Contractions ("it's", "isn't") are deliberate: they make it sound spoken, not read.

Why this matters

The fifth paragraph is what makes the answer sound like an analyst rather than a fan. You name two real criticisms with sources, then turn them into a description of the job. It also matches the JD's line that "what you achieve is equally important as how you achieve it".

The close: one specific thing you would work on

The JD says LSEG will "get to know your skills and interest and how they align with the needs of the business" before placing you. A specific close does that work for them. Pick the one that matches the area you most want, and be ready for "why that one?". Each close has the same shape: the thing, why it matters now, what you would measure.

Close A. FX customers: FXall moving into Workspace

Say "If I could pick one thing, it would be FXall moving into Workspace. LSEG says a large proportion of FXall's functionality is now there. I'd like to help measure whether clients are actually moving their workflow: which client groups have switched, where onboarding and permissioning slow them down, and whether it changes retention or what else they buy from LSEG."

Why credible The integration is stated in the H1 2026 report [S302]. FX account managers are asked to "identify upsell, cross-sell, and new business opportunities across FX trading, workflow, data" [S313]. Onboarding runs through FXall Admin, including "Price Stream Entitlement requests" [S317].

Measure Share of active FXall clients using it through Workspace, by segment; onboarding tickets and time to first trade; client retention after the switch Inferred.

Follow-up If asked about pricing, use Forward First Fixing: over $1.5trn cumulative volume and fees waived for the rest of 2026 [S238]. "When does adoption justify switching fees on?" is a good question back.

Close B. Research: debt listings on the International Securities Market

Say "I'd like to work on debt listings, specifically the International Securities Market. It promises comments within three days of a first submission and two days after that, with no listing agent needed. I'd want to look at where its issuers come from, and whether turnaround has held up since the new prospectus regime started in January."

Why credible ISM is LSE's market for debt sold to professional investors, with admission decided by the Exchange, not the FCA [S346]. The "3+2" review windows are LSE's own promise [S347]. POATRs came into force on 19 January 2026 [S151]. An LSEG debt origination ad cites "1,300 global borrowers in over 60 countries that raise $500-600 billion annually" [S316] Reported.

Measure Issuers by country and sector; days from first submission to admission against the 3+2 promise; share of programme vs one-off issues Inferred.

Caution The ISM information pack predates current rules (it cites the UKLA and the Prospectus Directive), so say "the published 3+2 windows" and check current timings [S347, S346].

Say "I'd like to help build the view of what drives Markets growth. The division grew 11.9% in the first half, partly on volatility. I'd split each line into volume and revenue per unit of volume, for example FX revenue against FX daily volume, to see which businesses are growing structurally and which are riding the cycle."

Why credible LSEG publishes the volume KPIs: FX ADV $525bn (2025) and $561bn (H1 2026); Tradeweb ADV $3.2trn; SwapClear notional; ForexClear members [S301, S302]. FX revenue was £272m in 2025 [S301].

With T+1 coming in October 2027, how quickly that reporting picks up problems matters more each year."

Don't say this

"I'd like to help with digital transformation and AI." It is not specific enough to measure, and the interviewer cannot picture you doing it. Why this programme, not a bank's graduate scheme

Expect this as a follow-up, often phrased as "Where else are you applying?" The interviewer wants to know whether LSEG is a real choice or a fallback. Do not criticise banks: banks generate about 72% of LSEG's revenue [S303], and some of your interviewers may have come from one.

Say this

"A bank would teach me one desk's view of the market. LSEG lets me see the market from the infrastructure underneath it. Banking customers are about 72% of LSEG's revenue, so in a Markets role I'd be working with many banks at once, on the venues and clearing they all rely on. The business earns fees for running trusted infrastructure rather than taking positions with its own balance sheet, so the questions are about customers, reliability and risk, which is what interests me. And the programme spans four areas, from FX clients to risk reporting, which is the breadth I want at this stage."
