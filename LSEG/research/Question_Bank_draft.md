# LSEG Business Management Summer Internship 2027: Question Bank (draft)

Req R0123386, London. Prepared 2026-10-05. Companion to WS6_WS7.md (source IDs S260 to S299 refer to the source table there).

How to use this: these are scaffolds, not scripts. Each question has four parts. "Tested" is what the assessor is really scoring. "Structure" is a shape for the answer. "Weave in" lists LSEG facts you can use if they fit, each with a source ID. "Traps" are common ways to lose marks. Do not memorise answers. In a strengths format (Cappfinity-style, see WS6_WS7.md section 1.2), answers that sound learned score worse than answers that sound real.

Confidence tags: [Confirmed] = stated on an LSEG-owned page or official release that was read in full. [Reported] = third party or forum. [Inferred] = my reasoning from the evidence.

Answer length guide: video interview answers are reported at about 1 to 2 minutes, with one attempt to record (Glassdoor anecdote, S291, [Reported]). Practise to 90 seconds.

## A. Motivation and why LSEG (6)

**A1. Why LSEG?**
- Tested: whether your interest is specific and real, and whether you understand LSEG is more than "the stock exchange".
- Structure: (1) one concrete moment that got you interested in market infrastructure; (2) the one thing about LSEG's model that you find interesting; (3) what you want to learn in 9 weeks and how it links to your next step.
- Weave in: LSEG describes itself as "a leading global financial markets infrastructure and data provider" [Confirmed, S276]. The Markets Division combines London Stock Exchange, Turquoise, LSEG FX, Tradeweb and LCH Group with risk, capital optimisation, collateral and regulatory reporting [Confirmed, S273]. Purpose: "driving financial stability, empowering economies and enabling customers to create sustainable growth" [Confirmed, S271].
- Traps: talking only about the London Stock Exchange building or prestige; reciting the purpose statement without a personal link; confusing LSEG with LSE (London School of Economics).

**A2. Why this internship and not an investment bank internship?**
- Tested: self-awareness, and whether you see infrastructure as a choice, not a fallback.
- Structure: what draws you to the "plumbing" (venues, clearing, data) rather than deal-making; one example of you enjoying systems, process or client service; what the internship lets you see (the trade lifecycle end to end).
- Weave in: the JD says interns "help customers across the trade life cycle" across trading, clearing and reporting [Confirmed, S260].
- Traps: criticising banks; saying "work-life balance" as the main reason.

**A3. What do you know about the business areas an intern could join?**
- Tested: whether you read the JD and can describe each business in one plain sentence.
- Structure: name the five (LSE, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise, LCH) [Confirmed, S260], one line each on what it does, then say which two interest you most and why.
- Weave in: H1 2026 Markets total income £1,920m, up 11.9% on a constant currency basis [Confirmed, S285].
- Traps: listing names with no understanding; claiming a fixed preference so strongly you seem inflexible (the JD says LSEG places you based on skills and interest) [Confirmed, S260].

**A4. What inspires you about a career at LSEG?** (LSEG's own wording for the video stage, S264)
- Tested: energy and motivation (a Cappfinity-style "what energises you" angle) [Inferred from S264, S265].
- Structure: one thing you find energising, a short story that shows it, and a link to a specific LSEG activity.
- Weave in: LSE 24, a planned 24/5 venue, with ETPs first in H1 2027 subject to regulatory approval [Confirmed, S278]. The Private Securities Market hosted its first transactions in H1 2026 [Confirmed, S285].
- Traps: generic "fast-paced and dynamic" language.

**A5. Where do you see yourself after this internship?**
- Tested: whether you are likely to convert and stay.
- Structure: say you hope to return on the Business Graduate Programme, show you know its shape, keep it open on the exact area.
- Weave in: the graduate programme is a 12-month development journey with a mid-point offsite called Ignite and 5 study days [Confirmed, S261]. There is an 8-week group project and two business pathways, Commercial and Business Analyst [Confirmed, S268].
- Traps: naming a different employer as the long-term plan.

**A6. Tell us about yourself / walk us through your background.**
- Tested: whether you can communicate concisely and link your story to the role.
- Structure: present (degree, one interest) then past (two relevant experiences) then future (why this role now). 60 to 90 seconds.
- Weave in: the JD's "demonstrable interest in financial markets ... through your studies, work experience or extracurricular activities" [Confirmed, S260].
- Traps: reading your CV line by line.

## B. Why this division (Markets) (5)

**B1. Which of LSE, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise or LCH would you most like to work in, and why?**
- Tested: specific interest plus flexibility.
- Structure: first choice with a reason linked to a skill; second choice; say you are open.
- Weave in (pick one): FX revenue £145m in H1 2026, up 7.6%, from FXall and Matching; FX average daily total volume $561bn [Confirmed, S285]. ForexClear notional cleared $33,311bn in H1 2026, up 45.2% [Confirmed, S285]. UK equities average daily value traded £6,671m, up 33.5% [Confirmed, S285].
- Traps: picking a business you cannot describe.

**B2. The JD mentions solving problems for FX customers. What do you think an FX client's goals are?**
- Tested: client empathy and basic FX knowledge.
- Structure: who the clients are (asset managers, corporates, banks); what they want (best price, low cost, reliable execution, good audit trail); how a venue helps.
- Weave in: Forward First Fixing, launched about 18 months before H1 2026, did over $330bn in Q2 2026, a record [Confirmed, S285]. FXall integration into Workspace and straight-through execution into ForexClear [Confirmed, S284].
- Traps: talking about FX as retail speculation.

**B3. The JD mentions research into debt listings, valuations, shareholder and geographic analysis. How would you approach that?**
- Tested: analytical thinking and knowing what a debt listing is.
- Structure: define the question, pick data sources, describe the analysis, say how you would present it.
- Weave in: the JD lists "debt listings, valuations, and shareholder and geographic analysis" [Confirmed, S260]. LSE lists international equity, fixed income, ETFs and investment vehicles [Confirmed, S278].
- Traps: overclaiming technical skills you do not have.

**B4. What do risk teams in a market infrastructure business do?**
- Tested: understanding of why risk matters at a CCP or venue.
- Structure: monitor (exposures, margin, operational risk), analyse (data, stress), report and deliver projects.
- Weave in: JD "monitoring and analysing risks, and interpreting data in readiness for the delivery of risk projects or reporting" [Confirmed, S260]. LCH's Chair statement credits LCH with keeping SwapClear operating through COVID-19 volatility [Confirmed, S276].
- Traps: confusing risk management with compliance only.

**B5. What is the difference between a trading venue and a clearing house, and why might one group own both?**
- Tested: joined-up thinking ("connect the dots", a JD requirement) [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: venue matches buyers and sellers; clearing house stands between them after the trade; owning both allows end-to-end workflow, but must preserve open access.
- Weave in: Q1 2026 call described FXall plugged into Tradeweb and straight-through execution into ForexClear [Confirmed, S284]. LCH press material describes an "open access model" [Confirmed, S281].
- Traps: suggesting a vertical silo is always good; ignoring competition and regulation.

## C. Strengths questions (Cappfinity-style) (10)

General scaffold for all strengths questions [Inferred from Cappfinity material, S289, S290]: give a genuine answer about what you enjoy and do well, then a short real example, then how you use it often. Assessors are thought to look for energy (tone, pace, detail) as much as content. Short, honest, specific.

**C1. What energises you?**
- Tested: authentic motivators and whether they fit a fast, client-facing, data-heavy role.
- Structure: name the thing; why; a recent example; how it would show up at LSEG.
- Weave in: JD "curiosity for data", "connect the dots" [Confirmed, S260].
- Traps: answering what you think they want; vague "I love challenges".

**C2. What drains your energy?**
- Tested: self-awareness and honesty.
- Structure: real drain; how you manage it; why it will not stop you doing the job.
- Traps: a fake weakness ("perfectionism"); naming something central to the role (e.g. "talking to clients").

**C3. How do you stay organised when you have lots going on?**
- Tested: planning and focus (JD: "focus, resilience and the flexibility to adapt") [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: your system; an example when it was tested; what you changed afterwards.
- Traps: listing apps with no example.

**C4. Tell us about a time you learned something new quickly.**
- Tested: learning agility, needed to get up to speed on market structure in 9 weeks.
- Structure: context; how you learned (sources, people, practice); proof you got it; what you would do differently.
- Weave in: you can mention how you taught yourself what a CCP is during preparation.
- Traps: choosing something trivial.

**C5. What do you enjoy more: starting a task or finishing it?**
- Tested: natural preferences; there is no correct answer [Inferred from S289].
- Structure: honest preference; example; how you cover the other side.
- Traps: saying "both equally" with no detail.

**C6. How do you feel when you are looking at a big set of data?**
- Tested: data curiosity (JD).
- Structure: honest reaction; what you do first; an example where you found something useful.
- Traps: claiming expertise in tools you have barely used.

**C7. When are you at your best in a team?**
- Tested: collaboration and role awareness (value: Partnership) [Confirmed, S271].
- Structure: your natural role; example; how you adapt when the team needs something else.
- Traps: always being "the leader".

**C8. How do you react when plans change at short notice?**
- Tested: resilience and adaptability.
- Structure: first reaction (honest); how you reset; real example.
- Weave in: Q1 2026 saw exceptional volumes driven by volatility, then Q2 moderated [Confirmed, S285]; the JD stresses "instant reaction and long-term vision" [Confirmed, S260].
- Traps: claiming you never feel stress.

**C9. What would your friends say you are naturally good at?**
- Tested: self-insight; consistency with the rest of your answers.
- Structure: one strength; one example from outside study; one link to the role.
- Traps: a list of five adjectives.

**C10. Do you prefer to work on lots of things at once or one thing in depth?**
- Tested: fit with "individual contributor" plus team work [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: preference; example; how you cope with the other mode.
- Traps: inconsistency with C3.

## D. Behavioural and competency (6)

Use a STAR or CAR shape, but keep "Situation" to two sentences. Spend most of the time on what you did and the result.

**D1. Tell us about a time you solved a problem for a customer or user.**
- Tested: client focus (the FX role is customer facing) [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: the customer's goal; the problem; your action; the result; what you learned.
- Traps: a story where you only escalated.

**D2. Tell us about a time you used data to change someone's mind.**
- Tested: analysis plus communication.
- Structure: question; data; insight; how you presented it; outcome.
- Traps: no actual numbers.

**D3. Tell us about a time you had to work with someone difficult.**
- Tested: Partnership value, maturity.
- Structure: situation; your understanding of their view; what you did; outcome; relationship after.
- Traps: blaming the other person.

**D4. Tell us about a time you failed or something went wrong.**
- Tested: resilience and honesty; JD "what you achieve is equally important as how you achieve it" [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: the failure (own it); what you did next; what changed.
- Traps: a disguised success.

**D5. Tell us about a time you improved a process.**
- Tested: fit with "operational improvements" and "strategic initiatives" in the JD [Confirmed, S260].
- Structure: what was inefficient; your idea; how you got buy-in; measurable change.
- Traps: improvement with no evidence.

**D6. Tell us about a time you had to meet a tight deadline.**
- Tested: prioritisation and calm.
- Structure: deadline; trade-offs you chose; result; what you would change.
- Traps: missing the deadline and not owning it.

## F. Markets knowledge for beginners (8)

**F1. What does a central counterparty (CCP) do?**
- Tested: the core LCH concept.
- Structure: after a trade, the CCP becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer; it collects margin; if a member defaults it uses margin and a default fund; this reduces counterparty risk across the system.
- Weave in: Maguire led the trading and unwinding of Lehman Brothers' LCH-cleared bond and repo portfolio from 1 September 2008 [Confirmed, S273].
- Traps: saying a CCP removes all risk.

**F2. What is the FX market and what is a spot versus forward trade?**
- Tested: basics for the FX desk.
- Structure: exchange of currencies; spot settles quickly (typically two business days for most pairs); forward settles at a future date at a rate agreed today; used for hedging.
- Weave in: FX average daily total volume on LSEG FX $561bn in H1 2026 [Confirmed, S285].
- Traps: mixing up FX venues and banks.

**F3. What does it mean to list a bond on the London Stock Exchange?**
- Tested: the "debt listings" research area in the JD.
- Structure: issuer admits debt securities to a market; gains visibility and investor access, must meet disclosure rules; investors gain transparency.
- Weave in: LSE delivers "international equity, fixed income, exchange-traded funds, and investment vehicles" [Confirmed, S278].
- Traps: confusing listing with trading volume.

**F4. What is the difference between an exchange and an MTF like Turquoise?**
- Tested: market structure.
- Structure: both are regulated multilateral venues; exchanges also run listings; MTFs trade securities listed elsewhere; Turquoise is pan-European.
- Weave in: Turquoise described by LSEG as "pan-European trading venue" [Confirmed, S277].
- Traps: claiming Turquoise lists companies.

**F5. What is an interest rate swap and why clear it?**
- Tested: SwapClear basics.
- Structure: exchange fixed for floating payments; used to hedge rate risk; clearing reduces bilateral risk and is required for many trades.
- Weave in: SwapClear IRS notional cleared $1,165trn in H1 2026, up 29.4% [Confirmed, S285].
- Traps: confusing notional with money at risk.

**F6. What does Tradeweb do?**
- Tested: knowing the biggest Markets revenue line.
- Structure: electronic marketplaces for rates, credit, equities and money markets; moves bond trading from phone to screen.
- Weave in: Tradeweb ADV $3.2trn, up 24.8% [Confirmed, S285]; LSEG describes "electronification of fixed income trading with Tradeweb" [Confirmed, S284].
- Traps: calling Tradeweb wholly owned without checking (ownership not verified in this research) [Gap].

**F7. What is an ETP and why would LSE 24 start with them?**
- Tested: reading the LSE 24 release.
- Structure: exchange-traded products give exposure to an index or asset; global demand across time zones; LSE is a leading ETP hub.
- Weave in: release says ETPs are "a natural starting point" given London's position as "a leading international ETP hub" [Confirmed, S278].
- Traps: confusing ETPs with mutual funds.

**F8. What is the trade lifecycle?**
- Tested: the JD's framing.
- Structure: pre-trade (data, price), execution (venue), clearing (CCP), settlement, reporting.
- Weave in: JD "across the trade life cycle, ensuring compliance and efficiency in clearing and reporting obligations" [Confirmed, S260].
- Traps: skipping clearing or reporting.

## H. Case and assessment centre style (4)

The AC is half a day, virtual, with "several different types of exercises" [Confirmed, S260] and "interactive activities, discussions and exercises" [Confirmed, S264]. Third-party accounts mention a group exercise, a case study and an interview [Reported, S286, S292].

**H1. Group exercise: rank these factors (e.g. economic, political, ESG, technological) by impact on a business.**
- Tested: collaboration more than content (reported) [Reported, S292].
- Structure: agree the goal and time plan; contribute your factor briefly; build on others by name; help the group decide; summarise.
- Traps: talking over others; staying silent; arguing for your factor at any cost.

**H2. Case study: review an information pack and recommend a course of action.**
- Tested: analysis, prioritising, clear recommendation.
- Structure: recommendation first; 3 reasons with numbers; risks and mitigations; next steps.
- Traps: summarising the pack without a decision; running out of time.

**H3. A client is unhappy with a service outage. What do you do?**
- Tested: client handling, integrity, escalation.
- Structure: acknowledge; facts; escalate properly; keep client informed; follow up and learn.
- Traps: promising compensation you cannot authorise.

**H4. You have three urgent requests from different teams. How do you prioritise?**
- Tested: judgement (also a classic immersive SJT format) [Inferred from S289].
- Structure: impact and urgency; clarify; communicate trade-offs; escalate if needed.
- Traps: trying to do everything alone.

## J. Ethics and compliance (4)

**J1. A friend asks you about a listing you have heard about at work before it is public. What do you do?**
- Tested: understanding of inside information and confidentiality.
- Structure: do not share; explain you cannot discuss work; report per policy if needed.
- Weave in: LSEG has a Code of Conduct [Confirmed, S271]. Do not quote specific rules you have not read.

**J2. You notice a colleague's spreadsheet has an error that went into a client report. What do you do?**
- Tested: integrity and team tact.
- Structure: check; raise privately and quickly; escalate if not fixed; focus on the client.
- Traps: ignoring it to avoid conflict.

**J3. Why does a market operator care so much about operational resilience?**
- Tested: understanding systemic role.
- Structure: markets and CCPs are critical infrastructure; outages hurt confidence; regulators expect resilience.
- Weave in: LCH Chair release on resilience through COVID-19 and Brexit [Confirmed, S276]; Carole Machell previously chaired the LCH SA Operational Resilience Committee [Confirmed, S276].

**J4. A client offers you an expensive gift after you helped them. What do you do?**
- Tested: conflicts of interest awareness.
- Structure: thank them; do not accept without checking policy; declare it.
- Traps: accepting quietly.
