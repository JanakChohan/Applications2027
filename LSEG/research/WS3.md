Tags: [Confirmed] = read in a primary source (annual report, 10-K, results release, company page); [Reported] = third-party outlet or search snippet; [Inferred] = my own calculation or judgement from the cited numbers.

Currency note: each firm's numbers are kept in its reporting currency. I have not converted to a common currency, so cross-firm comparisons of size are approximate and should be described that way in interview.

### 1c. Design-choice spectrums (where LSEG sits)

1. **Data-heavy vs transaction-heavy revenue.**
   Transaction end: CME (81% clearing and transaction fees, S122), Tradeweb (82.8% transaction fees, S137), Cboe (derivatives 55% plus cash 19%, S132). Data/subscription end: MSCI (73% recurring subscriptions, S136), S&P Global, Nasdaq (c.77% Solutions, S130). LSEG is near the data end: Data & Analytics + FTSE Russell + Risk Intelligence are c.61% of income, Markets c.39% (S120) [Inferred]. ICE is in the middle (recurring c.51%, S124). Why it matters for an intern in Markets: the Markets division is LSEG's volume-driven, volatility-sensitive engine; it grew faster (+8.9%) than D&A (+5.0%) in 2025 (S120).

2. **Vertical silo vs open access clearing.**
   Deutsche Börse describes its "vertically integrated business model" covering "the entire value chain" (S127) [Confirmed]. Euronext pulled cash equity clearing from LCH SA into Euronext Clearing, which LSEG names as the reason Securities & Reporting revenue fell 3.0% (S120) [Confirmed]. LSEG/LCH is the open-access end: SwapClear has "over 100 members" and clears 28 currencies (S141). Note the silo model is gaining in euro swaps: Eurex share of EUR swaps rose to 13.5% in Q3 2025 from 8.4% a year earlier, while LCH fell to 86.5% from 91.6% (S139) [Reported].

3. **Majority-owned listed subsidiary vs fully owned.**
   LSEG controls Tradeweb through Refinitiv with c.89.9% of voting power (S137) [Confirmed] but only around half the economics (50.7% economic interest per Tradeweb Q1 2025 10-Q, seen in search snippet only, so [Reported]). CME owns BrokerTec and EBS outright; ICE owns its fixed income and data businesses outright (S122, S124). LSEG is the outlier. Consequence: Tradeweb has its own share price, buybacks and board, and LSEG's results show minority interest leakage ("Tradeweb share buyback" lines in S120).

4. **User-governed vs exchange-owned clearing.**
   LSEG owns 94.2% of LCH Group after buying 11.6% from minorities in 2024 (S121) [Confirmed]. Not 82.6% as the brief suggested. The user partnership survives through (a) the SwapClear revenue-surplus share with founding banks, cut from c.30% to 15% in 2025 and 10% from 2026, extended to 2045, for £1.2bn (S120) [Confirmed], and (b) 11 banks buying 20% of Post Trade Solutions for £170m in October 2025, which LSEG calls "replicating the original LCH model" (S120) [Confirmed]. CME Clearing, ICE Clear and Eurex Clearing are wholly exchange-owned [Inferred, not individually sourced].

5. **Cloud / tech partner.**
   LSEG: 10-year Microsoft partnership (Dec 2022), Microsoft bought c.4% of LSEG, LSEG committed to minimum cloud spend of $2.8bn (S147) [Confirmed]. In 2025 this produced the Workspace app in Teams, Excel and PowerPoint add-ins, Autex on Azure and data on Microsoft Fabric (S120) [Confirmed]. CME: migrating to Google Cloud, with risk-factor language on "CME Group's transition to the Google Cloud" (S122) [Confirmed]. Nasdaq: AWS is defined in its 10-K glossary (S130) but I did not source the migration details within range (gap). LSEG's version is distinctive because it is a product co-development deal (Workspace inside Teams/Excel), not only infrastructure [Inferred].

6. **Listings venue health.**
   London: 23 IPOs raising £2.1bn in 2025 (up 170% on £777.7m in 2024) (S142) [Confirmed]; 7 IPOs raising £577m in H1 2026 (S143) [Confirmed]; 88 companies left the main market in 2024 (S145) [Reported, citing EY]. Hong Kong: HK$286.9bn from 119 IPOs and ranked No.1 globally (S134) [Confirmed]. US: proceeds up 38% in 2025 and led the world by proceeds (S144) [Reported]. Europe: 105 IPOs raising $17.3bn (S144). Euronext reports "leading position for equity listings" in Europe (S128). LSEG is at the weak end on IPO volume, which is why listings are a small part of its revenue and the JD stresses debt listings research.

### 1d. Things you can say about LSEG that no single rival can (with evidence)

1. **It runs the global swaps clearing utility.** SwapClear cleared a record $1,941 trillion of IRS notional in 2025, up 21.2%, and 5.3m client trades, up 33% (S120) [Confirmed]. LSEG says SwapClear "clears 95 percent of all 'vanilla' OTC interest rate swap products" (S141, undated company claim) [Reported]. Clarus data: 99.995% of GBP SONIA swaps in Q1 2026 (S140); dominance in USD, GBP, AUD, CAD and 16 other currencies (S139); CME's best-ever USD OIS share was only 4.3% (S140) [Reported]. Why it matters: clearing creates network effects (margin offsets get better the more trades sit in one pot), which is why the 2025 surplus deal added 100bp to group EBITDA margin (S120).
2. **One group owns terminal-scale data, a global index house, multi-asset venues and a global CCP.** D&A £3,978m, FTSE Russell £954m, Tradeweb, FXall/FX Matching, LSE, Turquoise, LCH (S120) [Confirmed]. ICE has data + exchanges + clearing but no FX venue or desktop of LSEG's scale; Deutsche Börse has 360T + Eurex + ISS STOXX but no terminal; Bloomberg has terminal + FXGO + bond trading but no CCP or exchange [Inferred]. Why it matters: cross-selling and "LSEG Everywhere" data distribution.
3. **The partnership model with banks.** Banks share SwapClear surplus until 2045 and own 20% of Post Trade Solutions (S120) [Confirmed]. I found no comparable bank-equity structure at CME, ICE or Deutsche Börse clearing [Inferred; not exhaustively checked].
4. **A separately listed, controlled subsidiary that is itself a $22bn company.** Tradeweb: revenue $2,052m, +18.9%, ADV $2.6trn (S137, S120); market cap $22.28bn (S148). No other exchange group in this set consolidates a listed company of that size [Inferred].
5. **Microsoft as shareholder and product partner.** c.4% stake, 10 years, $2.8bn minimum cloud spend (S147) [Confirmed]; plus 2025 AI data deals with Anthropic, Databricks, Microsoft, OpenAI, Rogo and Snowflake (S120) [Confirmed].
6. **FX across the full workflow.** FXall (dealer-to-client) plus FX Matching (dealer-to-dealer) with $525bn average daily volume in 2025, and ForexClear clearing $48,113bn notional, up 31.4% (S120) [Confirmed]. Deutsche Börse (360T) and Cboe FX lack a matching interbank platform of this scale plus FX clearing in the same group; CME has EBS but not a D2C platform of FXall's type [Inferred].

Not verified in this workstream (do not claim as fact yet): LSE as "oldest major exchange", the International Securities Market and Sustainable Bond Market specifics. See Gaps.

### 1e. Where LSEG does NOT lead

- **Valuation and size:** 5th largest listed exchange operator by market cap ($52.26bn) behind CME, ICE, HKEX and Deutsche Börse; S&P Global ($113.87bn) is over twice its size (S148) [Reported].
- **Share price:** Reuters reports LSEG shares fell 13% in one day on AI fears and were still "23% below a peak hit in 2025" in June 2026, even after a 27% rebound; it trades at c.18x forward earnings, about 30% below Moody's and c.40% below MSCI (S146) [Reported]. Bloomberg ran "LSEG Lags Own Index With Investors Impatient for AI Breakthrough" (S131, PAYWALLED-NOT-READ).
- **Market data:** Bloomberg holds the largest share; LSEG D&A is second (S129) [Reported, based on 2023 data].
- **Growth:** LSEG +7.1% organic in 2025 vs HKEX +30%, Cboe +17%, Nasdaq +13%, Euronext +12.1%, Deutsche Börse +9% ex treasury (S120, S134, S132, S130, S128, S126) [Confirmed; note different growth definitions].
- **Futures:** CME revenue $6.5bn and record 28.1m contracts/day (S122); LSEG has no comparable listed futures franchise [Inferred].
- **Energy:** ICE energy revenue $2.182bn, +16% (S124); Deutsche Börse commodities €612m (S126).
- **European cash equities:** Cboe Europe had a "record yearly Cboe overall market share of 25%" in 2025 (S133) [Confirmed by release]. Turquoise share not found (gap).
- **IPOs:** London £2.1bn in 2025 vs Hong Kong HK$286.9bn (S142, S134).
- **Euro and yen swaps:** Eurex at 13.5% of EUR (Q3 2025, S139); JSCC 40.3% of JPY TONA (Q1 2026, S140).
- **FX D2C volume:** Bloomberg reports FXGO ADV above $830bn (Mar 2025) vs LSEG total FX ADV $525bn for 2025 (S149, S120). Measures are not like-for-like (different counting and periods) [Inferred caution].
- **Revenue per employee:** below every pure venue operator (table 1b).
- **US credit e-trading:** MarketAxess is the specialist (18.4% estimated US high-grade share, S138), though its share slipped and its revenue grew only 3.6% while Tradeweb grew 18.9% [Confirmed].

## 6) Interview angles

1. **"Who are our competitors?"** Answer by business line, not by firm: in swaps clearing the challenger is Eurex (13.5% of EUR, S139); in FX it is Bloomberg FXGO and 360T; in European equities Cboe Europe (25%, S133); in bonds MarketAxess and Bloomberg vs Tradeweb; in listings New York and Hong Kong; in data Bloomberg and S&P Global. Shows you understand LSEG is a group of separate competitive fights.
2. **Open access vs vertical silo.** Use Euronext pulling its clearing from LCH SA (cost LSEG revenue in Securities & Reporting, S120) and Deutsche Börse's own "vertically integrated" language (S127) to show why LCH's open-access, bank-partnered model is a strategic choice with real trade-offs.
3. **The 2025 SwapClear deal as a business case.** LSEG paid £1.2bn to cut the banks' surplus share from c.30% to 10% and extend to 2045, adding 100bp to group EBITDA margin (S120). Good example for a "business intelligence / strategic initiatives" intern: what did LSEG give, what did it get, why would banks agree?
4. **Markets as the growth engine.** Markets grew 8.9% vs D&A 5.0% in 2025 and margin rose to 55.6% (S120). A sharp line: "the data business gets the headlines, but Markets is growing faster and is less exposed to the AI-disruption worry that hit the share price" [Inferred].
5. **Be honest about London listings.** £2.1bn from 23 IPOs in 2025 vs HK$286.9bn in Hong Kong (S142, S134), but H1 2026 proceeds up 215% (S143). Pair this with the JD's "debt listings" research focus: London's debt and international listings matter more than the equity IPO headlines.
6. **Tradeweb ownership quirk.** LSEG controls c.89.9% of votes (S137) but Tradeweb is separately listed: a good question to ask interviewers is how Markets coordinates with a listed subsidiary (for example the RepoAgent tie-up between Post Trade Solutions and Tradeweb, S120).
7. **FX customer role in the JD.** Know: FXall (D2C) + FX Matching (D2D), $525bn ADV, FX revenue £272m (+7.5%), ForexClear +31.4% (S120), and that Bloomberg's FXGO claims more than $830bn ADV (S149). A question to ask: how LSEG FX competes on workflow when Bloomberg bundles FXGO with the Terminal.
