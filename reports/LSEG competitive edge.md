# LSEG wins on design, not on size

LSEG stands out not because it is the biggest, fastest-growing or most highly valued exchange group (it is none of these), but because it made three design choices that no peer has made together. It combines terminal-scale data, a global index house, multi-asset venues, FX from client trading to clearing, and a global OTC clearing house in one group. It runs that clearing house on open access, so LCH must clear for rival venues on equal terms. And it keeps its biggest customers, the banks, inside as part-owners and revenue-sharers. In the Markets division this produces a mechanism a candidate can explain in two sentences. Netting makes every new swap cheapest to clear where the offsetting risk already sits, so SwapClear holds **about 96% of cleared USD OIS and 99.995% of sterling SONIA swaps**. Where LSEG has lost share (euro, yen, yuan, rupee swaps), the cause is regulation or onshore policy, not a commercial rival offering a better deal. The honest counter-case is serious, and the market prices it. LSEG trades at a 12-to-14-year low multiple because investors fear AI will erode its desktop and aggregated-data revenue. Its 2025–26 growth trailed volatility-geared peers. FTSE Russell grew at half the rate of MSCI and S&P DJI. Bloomberg is bigger in desktops and in dealer-to-client FX. Its H1 2026 Markets margin is temporarily flattered by a deal that reverses in Q4 2026. As an employer, LSEG's distinctive pull is the vantage point of neutral infrastructure: one intern sees issuers, banks, brokers and asset managers as clients of the same venue or clearing house. It also offers quotable values language, a decorated graduate programme and stable engagement. The weak spots are bureaucracy, a heavily merged workforce and lower pay than a bank. Several facts in the earlier prep pack are now out of date (headcount, Eurex and JSCC shares, MarketAxess's independence, FXGO volumes, LSE 24 hours). This report flags each one, and the newer evidence wins wherever they conflict.

## The combination is unique, but it earns no premium

Set LSEG against the ten named rivals and none of them has the full kit. The peer-by-peer check below is an assessment built from each firm's 2025–26 disclosures. ICE comes closest, with exchanges, CCPs, fixed-income data and indices, and soon MarketAxess, but it has no Workspace-scale desktop and no FX franchise to match FXall, FX Matching and ForexClear ([ICE Q2 2026](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/Intercontinental-Exchange-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx)). Deutsche Börse has venues, a CCP, a CSD, an index business and 360T FX, but no desktop data, and it describes itself as "vertically integrated" ([Deutsche Börse Annual Report 2025](https://www.deutsche-boerse.com/resource/blob/4932346/c2a3c01b3456eeea6f8747ca5a9cda2d/data/annual-report-2025_tug_jahresfinanzbericht_en.pdf)). Bloomberg has the leading desktop, FXGO and bond trading, but no regulated exchange or CCP. S&P Global and MSCI have data and indices but no venues.

The breadth matters less than three design choices that sit on top of it. The first is **open access by rule**. When LSEG bought control of LCH in 2012–13, the competition regulator recorded that LCH's articles would say "No exchange will be favoured over any other and LSEG's trading services users will not be favoured over any other exchange's users" ([OFT decision](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/555de2f740f0b669c4000047/LSEG.pdf)). LCH still describes itself as operating "an open access model" ([LCH Ltd financial statements 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/post-trade/en_us/documents/lch/annual-reports/2025-lch-limited-financial-statements.pdf)). The choice has a real cost: Euronext pulled its cash-equity clearing out of LCH SA, and LSEG names that as the reason Securities & Reporting fell 3.0% in 2025 ([LSEG FY2025 results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf)). The second choice is **user partnership**. Founding banks keep 10% of the SwapClear revenue surplus to 2045, 11 banks bought 20% of Post Trade Solutions for £170m, and clearing members and clients sit on LCH's risk committee "with no one constituent… having a majority" ([LSEG FY2025 results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf); [LCH Ltd 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/post-trade/en_us/documents/lch/annual-reports/2025-lch-limited-financial-statements.pdf)). The research found no equivalent at CME, ICE or Eurex, though the check was not exhaustive. The third is **a hyperscaler as shareholder and product co-developer**. Microsoft holds 4.1% of LSEG, and LSEG committed to US$2.8bn of minimum cloud spend ([LSEG/Microsoft, Dec 2022](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/press-releases/lseg-and-microsoft-launch-strategic-partnership-12dec2022.pdf); [LSEG Annual Report 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/annual-reports/lseg-annual-report-2025.pdf)). By contrast, CME's Google deal and Nasdaq's AWS work are infrastructure-led ([CME Globex notice, Sep 2026](https://www.cmegroup.com/notices/electronic-trading/2026/09/20260921.html)).

The peers' 2026 strategic bets show how unusual this is. ICE agreed on 30 July 2026 to buy MarketAxess for about US$6.0bn of equity, aiming to combine MarketAxess's c.2,100 institutional clients with ICE's retail bond trading, data and indices ([Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/ice-to-acquire-marketaxess/)). Deutsche Börse agreed in January 2026 to buy Allfunds for c.€5.3bn and fold it into Clearstream Fund Services ([Deutsche Börse](https://www.deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/Deutsche-B-rse-Group-and-Allfunds-Group-Sign-Agreement-on-Recommended-Acquisition-of-Allfunds-Group-4914918)). Cboe is shrinking to derivatives, data and event contracts and exiting Australia, Canada and listings ([Cboe Q2 2026](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2026/Cboe-Global-Markets-Reports-Results-for-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx)). Peers are deepening one lane through M&A. LSEG is betting on distribution and on the joins between lanes.

The numbers show a subscription-led group with a Markets engine. **73% of FY2025 income was recurring**, and Markets is **40% of H1 2026 income** (£1,920m of £4,799m), growing **11.9%** at a **58.6% EBITDA margin**. Data & Analytics grew 5.1% at 42.3% ([LSEG Annual Report 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/annual-reports/lseg-annual-report-2025.pdf); [LSEG H1 2026 interim report](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). That puts Markets in line with the transaction-heavy peers: ICE's H1 net revenue rose about 12.5% and Deutsche Börse's 11% excluding treasury income ([ICE Q2 2026](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/Intercontinental-Exchange-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx); [Deutsche Börse H1 2026](https://www.deutsche-boerse.com/resource/blob/5302530/683d446f9d49b88f194990b24a79aee8/data/gdb-quarterly-statement-q2-2026_en.pdf)). The group gap against Cboe (+25%) and HKEX (+19%) comes from the data side, not from Markets ([HKEX interim 2026](https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/News/News-Release/2026/260819news/260819news_eng.pdf)).

One staffing fact needs correcting from the earlier pack. LSEG had **28,516 employees at end-2025**. The "c.14,200" figure is engineering headcount, so revenue per head is about **£315k, not £633k** ([LSEG Annual Report 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/annual-reports/lseg-annual-report-2025.pdf)). LSEG's own sustainability databook gives 26,976 "total employees" for 2025, apparently on a different basis it does not explain, and the job advert still says 25,000 ([Sustainability Databook 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/sustainability/lseg-group-sustainability-databook-2025.pdf)). Quote the Annual Report figure. The low revenue per head is itself a point worth making: LSEG is a labour-intensive data company with a lean, venue-like Markets division inside it.

## SwapClear's moat is netting, and only regulation dents it

The core Markets mechanism is simple. Initial margin is charged on the *net* risk of a portfolio at one CCP. A dealer whose dollar book already sits at LCH pays margin only on a new trade's marginal risk, and an offsetting trade can even cut margin. Clearing the same trade at CME creates a fresh, un-netted position, so the dealer posts margin twice on what is economically a hedged book. Compression works the same way: trades can only be torn up against other trades at the same CCP, so a bigger book gives more offsets. That cuts gross notional and the leverage-ratio and G-SIB capital charges that come with it. LSEG attributes 2025 OTC derivatives growth (+11.6% to £641m) to "growth in clearing and compression activity" ([LSEG FY2025 results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf)). Add 28 currencies under one default fund and a tested default record and the pool becomes self-reinforcing. LCH "managed 9 member defaults and, in all cases, losses were within the initial margin held" ([LCH Group risk management](https://www.lseg.com/en/post-trade/clearing/risk-management/group-risk-management)). Lehman's US$9trn swap book was closed out using only 35% of its initial margin ([CCP Global](https://ccp-global.org/the-lehman-case)).

The best evidence for the mechanism is the pattern in market share. Where the choice is purely commercial, LCH holds 96–100%. In Q2 2026 SwapClear cleared **US$70.3trn of the US$73.4trn** USD OIS total (about 95.8%; CME about 4.2%). CME's share has swung between roughly 2% and 4.3% and has never sustainably broken 5% ([Clarus, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/q2-2026-cleared-rates-swaps-17-below-the-q1-high-but-up-23-yoy/); [Clarus Q1 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/q1-2026-cleared-rates-swap-volumes-and-ccp-share/)). In sterling LCH cleared 99.995% of SONIA OIS in Q1 2026 ([Clarus Q1 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/q1-2026-cleared-rates-swap-volumes-and-ccp-share/)). Every place LCH loses share has a policy lever behind it:

- **Euro:** Eurex is pushed by the EU's EMIR 3 "active account" requirement.
- **Yen:** JSCC gained after the CFTC's September 2025 decision to let US firms clear there.
- **Yuan and rupee:** Shanghai Clearing House holds 79.2% of CNY and CCIL 31.2% of INR, under onshore policy.

Sources: [Clarus, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/rates-derivatives-ccp-shares-show-mixed-q2-dynamics/); [Eurex EMIR 3.0](https://www.eurex.com/ec-en/rules-regs/emir3-0).

The euro and yen numbers have moved since the earlier pack, in opposite directions. **Euro: Eurex has stalled.** Its share of all cleared EUR swaps *fell* 0.8 points to **12.4% in Q2 2026**, against the 13.5% (Q3 2025) the earlier pack quoted as a rising trend. Excluding FRAs, which are mostly reset-risk management, Eurex holds 6.7% and LCH 93.3%. Clarus calls 12–13% "the new normal" ([Clarus, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/rates-derivatives-ccp-shares-show-mixed-q2-dynamics/)). Measures differ, so do not mix them. A narrower Clarus cut gave Eurex 6.9% of Euribor IRS plus €STR OIS in Q1 2026 ([Clarus Q1 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/q1-2026-cleared-rates-swap-volumes-and-ccp-share/)). Eurex's own marketing claims about 23%, almost certainly on notional *outstanding* ([Eurex Clearing Partnership summary](https://www.eurex.com/resource/blob/143582/a00ae77c9a0341346ce5626aee9c026f/data/Eurex-Clearing-Partnership-Programme-summary-otc-ird.pdf)). Risk.net reported that more than 80% of buy-side euro clearing accounts opened at Eurex were inactive before the first reporting deadline ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/markets/7963712/many-eurex-accounts-remain-inactive-as-reporting-deadline-looms)).

**Yen is now the live risk.** JSCC jumped 7.7 points in one quarter to **48.2%**, leaving LCH with **51.8%**. The earlier pack quoted JSCC at 40.3% for Q1 2026 ([Clarus, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.clarusft.com/rates-derivatives-ccp-shares-show-mixed-q2-dynamics/)). Even so, LCH's absolute volumes keep growing. H1 2026 SwapClear notional was **US$1,165trn (+29.4%)** and client trades rose 23.8% ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). The fair summary: LCH is losing a few points at the margin under regulatory compulsion while the pool keeps growing. The tail risk is political, not commercial.

The October 2025 founding-bank deal shows the partnership model working. LSEG paid **£1.2bn** to cut the banks' share of the SwapClear surplus from about 30% (which ran to 2035) to 15% for 2025 and 10% for 2026–2045. A majority of founders agreed, not all ([LSEG FY2025 results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf); [LSEG release, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2025/lseg-announces-partnership-and-investment-in-post-trade-solutions)). LSEG made its partners cheaper; it did not end the partnership. That keeps the banks motivated to route swaps to SwapClear for another 20 years, at exactly the moment EMIR 3, the CFTC and CME are offering alternatives.

The deal also has a reporting consequence a candidate should know. **350bp of the 58.6% H1 2026 Markets margin**, and 140bp of the group's margin gain, came from the deal's timing, and LSEG warns "this year-on-year uplift will largely reverse in Q4 2026" ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). Do not quote 58.6% as the run-rate.

The same logic runs through the rest of LCH and post-trade:

- **ForexClear** is effectively the world's NDF clearing utility, with **99.1% of cleared NDF volume** in Q4 2025 ([Clarus](https://www.clarusft.com/fx-derivatives-volumes-at-the-end-of-q4-2025/)). Notional grew 45.2% in H1 2026, faster than SwapClear ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)).
- **RepoClear** is one half of a European duopoly with Eurex, and both won the ECB as a member for 2026 ([LCH release, Nov 2025](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/lch/2025/the-european-central-bank-joins-lch-repoclear-sa); [Finadium](https://finadium.com/ecb-to-join-eurex-repo-market-and-lch-repoclear-sa-in-q1-2026/)).
- **Post Trade Solutions** (Acadia, Quantile, SwapAgent, TradeAgent) extends LSEG into *uncleared* derivatives, where LCH does not reach. It is small: 2024 revenue £96m and normalised EBITDA £16m. The banks' £170m for 20% valued it at £850m, about 53 times EBITDA, so it is priced for growth. It added more than 120 customers in 2025 and 80 more in H1 2026 ([LSEG release, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2025/lseg-announces-partnership-and-investment-in-post-trade-solutions); [LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)).

The structural point is that SwapClear flow has no "home" venue to be pulled back to, which is exactly what Euronext did with equity clearing.

## On the trading side, LSEG leads in places and trails in others

The Markets division's venues are not equally strong. Ranked by how hard each moat is to attack, here is the evidence business by business.

| LSEG business | Moat mechanism | Best 2026 evidence | Main rival and where it leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| SwapClear (USD, GBP) | Netting, compression, partnership | c.96% USD OIS (Q2 2026); 99.995% SONIA (Q1 2026) | CME c.4% USD; Eurex 12.4% EUR; JSCC 48.2% JPY |
| ForexClear | Netting, plus uncleared margin rules pushing NDFs into clearing | 99.1% of cleared NDFs (Q4 2025) | None at scale |
| WM/Reuters 4pm fix | Written into fund prospectuses and index rules | "most widely used FX benchmarks" | Bloomberg BFIX |
| Tradeweb | Protocol and asset-class breadth | Rates derivatives ADV +45.5% (H1 2026) | ICE–MarketAxess (pending); Bloomberg |
| LSE debt listings (ISM) | Professional-only market, fast admission | Record c.21,700 issuances in 2025 | Luxembourg, Dublin (not compared) |
| FXall + FX Matching | Workflow embedding; both client and interbank venues | US$561bn ADV (H1 2026) | Bloomberg FXGO bigger in client trading |
| LSE secondary trading | Opening and closing auctions on the listing venue | UK value traded +33.5% (H1 2026) | Cboe Europe for continuous trading |
| Turquoise | Weak; being reset | New CEO, March 2026 | Cboe Europe 25.5% (Q1 2026) |

**FX is the clearest case of LSEG covering the whole chain.** It is the only group with both a large dealer-to-client venue (FXall) and an interbank order book (Matching), plus NDF clearing at ForexClear. LSEG's 2025 spot volume (US$106bn a day) was well above CME's EBS (US$63.8bn spot plus NDF). About 80% of LSEG FX volume is non-spot, mostly on FXall ([The Full FX, Feb 2026](https://thefullfx.com/a-year-of-two-halves-but-fx-venues-thrive-in-2025/)). LSEG also owns the WM/Reuters 4pm fix, which it calls "the most widely used FX benchmarks". Its switching cost comes from being written into thousands of fund and index documents ([LSEG WMR overview](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/factsheets/wmr-overview-and-request-for-feedback.pdf)). The document sits in FTSE Russell's file path, so WMR is probably not reported inside Markets; this is unconfirmed, so do not state it as fact.

The honest weakness: in dealer-to-client trading, **Bloomberg FXGO hit a record US$970bn ADV in June 2026**, against LSEG FX's total of US$561bn in H1 2026. The earlier pack had only FXGO's US$830bn figure from March 2025. The two are not measured the same way, but the direction is clear, and Bloomberg bundles FXGO with the Terminal ([The Full FX, 13 Jul 2026](https://thefullfx.com/bloomberg-reports-new-fx-volume-record/); [LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). LSEG FX revenue grew 7.6% in H1 2026, behind its clearing businesses. LSEG's answer is to bring FXall into Workspace and to sell workflow tools. Forward First Fixing had another record quarter, executing more than US$330bn in Q2 2026 ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)).

**Tradeweb is the fastest-growing big asset, and it now faces a bigger rival.** Q2 2026 revenue rose 9.0% to US$558.9m, ADV reached US$3.0trn (+18.2%), and market-data revenue jumped 22.6% "driven mainly by amendments to the LSEG market data license" ([Tradeweb Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001758730/000175873026000132/tw-2026xq2xex991.htm)). That last line is a concrete case of a venue feeding the group's data business. In US high-grade credit, Tradeweb's total share was 25.7%. MarketAxess estimated its own share at 17.7% for H1 2026, down from 19.2% a year earlier. The bases differ, and MarketAxess disputes how duplicate trade reports are counted ([MarketAxess Q2 2026](https://investor.marketaxess.com/news/news-details/2026/MarketAxess-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). The earlier pack called MarketAxess an independent specialist that was losing ground. **That is now wrong: ICE is buying it, with closing targeted for H1 2027.** A combined ICE credit franchise (MarketAxess plus ICE Bonds, data and indices) is the most direct new threat to Tradeweb. Tradeweb's moat is breadth of trading methods (RFQ, portfolio trading, all-to-all, session trading) across rates, credit, ETFs and money markets, so a client connects once for many markets. LSEG holds 50.9% of Tradeweb's economics but about 89.9% of the votes, so roughly half the profit belongs to outside shareholders ([LSEG Annual Report 2025](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/annual-reports/lseg-annual-report-2025.pdf)).

**The London Stock Exchange's real primary-market strength is debt, not equity.** 2025 was a record year, with **just over 21,700 issuances raising about US$1,178bn**, the most since 2008, driven by the International Securities Market (19,280 issuances; 253 issuers from 44 countries) ([LSE Debt Capital Markets Update 2025](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/eoy-2025-debt-capital-markets-update.pdf)). These primary figures replace the earlier pack's "1,300 borrowers, $500–600bn" line, which came from a job advert. In secondary equities, LSE's edge is the listing itself: opening and closing auctions, which set index and fund valuation prices, happen on the listing venue, and LSEG cites "robust auction volumes" in H1 2026 ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). In continuous pan-European trading, **Cboe Europe is clearly first at 25.5% in Q1 2026**, its best since 2011 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/european-equities-q-1-2026-review/)). Turquoise lost its Plato Partnership in 2025 and got a dedicated CEO in March 2026 with a mandate to expand in Europe, which reads as a reset ([LSEG, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2026/lseg-strengthens-presence-european-equities-trading)).

Two newer products need careful wording. **LSE 24** is a separate 24/5 venue for ETPs from H1 2027, subject to approval, with client testing from end-2026, a central order book plus RFQ, and Main Market hours unchanged ([LSEG, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2026/london-stock-exchange-to-launch-lse-24)). The "17:00–07:50" session the earlier pack stated as fact comes from commentary, not LSEG, so drop it. On **PISCES**, LSE "became the first operator to be granted approval", but the first trade anywhere happened on JP Jenkins' rival platform on 24 February 2026. LSE's own Private Securities Market first transacted on 25 March 2026, with Wayve and Moneybox following in July ([LSEG FY2025 results](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf); [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/pisces-takes-flight-the-first-trades-on-the-uks-new-private-company-trading-platforms); [Wedlake Bell](https://wedlakebell.com/insights/articles/pisces-gathers-momentum-early-transactions-and-the-road-ahead/)). "First approved, not first to trade" is the precise version.

## The market prices LSEG as a good exchange attached to a doubted data business

The counter-case is not a list of minor flaws. It is a coherent thesis that the market has priced. The shares fell from a **12,095p peak (February 2025)** to a **7,170p closing low (February 2026)** and stood around **8,220p on 5 October 2026** ([Kalkine, Oct 2026](https://kalkinemedia.com/uk/stocks/financial/why-is-london-stock-exchange-group-plc-lselseg-bringing-in-a-technology-chief-after-this-weeks-directorate-change)). This happened "without any material change to consensus EPS", which makes it a reset of the valuation multiple, not of earnings ([Investing.com, Feb 2026](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-analysts-see-opportunity-in-lse-despite-aidriven-selloff-4490206)). The trigger was Anthropic's legal plug-in for Cowork on 3 February 2026. Sources put the one-day fall anywhere from about 10% to 15% depending on intraday or closing prices, so say "around 10–13%" ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/lseg-sage-rightmove-shares-fall-as-anthropic-ai-rattles-markets/)). Rothschild & Co Redburn put about 30% of group EBITDA at risk. It split the business the way most analysts now do: real-time data, indices and post-trade "remain structurally protected", while terminals and non-real-time data are "more exposed" ([Investing.com, Jun 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/lseg-shares-drop-4-after-rothschild--co-redburn-warns-on-ai-disruption-risks-4749129)). In June, Reuters put LSEG at about 18x forward earnings, 30% below Moody's and 40% below MSCI ([Reuters via GBAF](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/analysis-lseg-slowly-sheds-ai-risk-tag-drive-show-growth/)). A UBS note reported around 8 October puts it nearer **15x, the lowest in more than 12 years**, and says the overhang will persist until AI produces revenue. That note was seen only as a search summary, so it is unconfirmed ([Proactive Investors](https://www.proactiveinvestors.com/companies/news/1099814/ubs-says-ai-overhang-will-persist-for-lseg-despite-valuation-discount-1099814.html)).

The most telling implication cuts against the "combination" story. UBS's February sum-of-the-parts valued FTSE Russell, Risk Intelligence, the Tradeweb stake and LCH at **£35.8bn, slightly above LSEG's then £35.0bn market cap** ([Proactive via Yahoo](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/elliott-managements-lseg-push-much-160006597.html)). In other words, the market gave close to zero value to Data & Analytics, and to the combination itself. LSEG's US$52bn market cap sits below Deutsche Börse (US$58bn) and HKEX (US$61bn) despite larger revenue ([CompaniesMarketCap](https://companiesmarketcap.com/exchanges/largest-exchanges-by-market-cap/)). Elliott built a stake and asked for a £5bn buyback and a "root-and-branch" portfolio review. It got a £3bn buyback (plus £1.35bn more at the half-year) and no disposals, and the research found no public escalation since February ([Elliott statement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/elliott-management-statement-on-london-stock-exchange-group-plc-302699173.html); [LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)).

Four further weaknesses are sharper than the earlier pack made them:

- **The index gap.** FTSE Russell grew 9.1% in H1 2026, while S&P DJI grew 20% and MSCI Index 17.5% in Q2. FTSE Russell earns about £252m a quarter against roughly US$510–535m at each peer ([S&P Global Q2 2026](https://s29.q4cdn.com/690959130/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/S-P-Global-2Q-2026-Earnings-Release-and-Exhibits-7-28-2026.pdf); [MSCI Q2 2026](https://ir.msci.com/node/22856)). The likely cause is mix: the peers earn more from ETF asset-based fees, which rise with markets. That is an inference.
- **Slow Workspace growth.** Workflows grew only 2.8% in H1 2026, and LSEG remains a distant second to Bloomberg on desktops ([LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/financial-market-data-spending-reaches-record-42bn/)).
- **A latent pricing challenge.** The FCA found market power and margins above 60% in some wholesale data markets, but imposed no remedy ([Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102j24q/fca-concludes-its-wholesale-data-market-study)).
- **Shrinking London equity.** The number of listed companies fell to 1,534 in May 2026 from 2,429 in 2015. CRH, Smurfit Westrock and Flutter completed full exits in 2026. H1 brought 7 London IPOs against 72 in the US ([Fortune, Sep 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/09/11/after-a-wave-of-delistings-can-londons-stock-market-reverse-the-decline/)). Julia Hoggett's rebuttal is that 13 of 21 UK firms that floated in the US since 2014 have delisted and the IPO pipeline is the largest since 2005. That answers small-cap flight, not the large-cap exits.

The best counter-arguments are also concrete. Bulls and bears agree on the *split* of the business; they disagree only on how fast the exposed part shrinks. Markets, LCH and indices, the protected parts, are where the internship sits. Q1 2026 income growth of 9.8% was the strongest in five years. About 90% of covering analysts rate LSEG a buy. More than 200 customers are engaged with its MCP server, and it has published a pricing framework for AI access ([Reuters via GBAF](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/analysis-lseg-slowly-sheds-ai-risk-tag-drive-show-growth/); [LSEG H1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/interim-report/lseg-interim-report-h1-2026-30july2026.pdf)). What is unproven is disclosed AI revenue. Until LSEG shows it, "more valuable in an AI world" remains LSEG's claim, not an established fact. The Q3 trading update on 22 October 2026 falls after this report's date.

## As an employer, the draw is the vantage point, not the prestige

LSEG's employer case is strongest when it rests on the same mechanism as the business case. A bank intern sees one side of a deal. A Markets intern at LSEG works for a neutral venue or clearing house whose clients are issuers, banks, brokers, asset managers and regulators all at once. Banks alone are about 72% of group income ([LSEG investor introduction, May 2026](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/introduction-to-lseg.pdf)). The internship places people in LSE, LSEG FX, Tradeweb, Turquoise or LCH, across four streams: FX customers, research (including debt listings), business intelligence and operational improvement, and risk. The advert says "These are real roles" ([LSEG Workday posting](https://lseg.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/Graduate_Careers/details/Business-Management-and-Sales-Summer-Internship_R0123386)). The career path is real too: Daniel Maguire, now Group Head of Markets and CEO of LCH, joined LCH in treasury operations in 1999 ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/maguire-takes-helm-at-lch-as-new-ceo/)).

For a strengths-based interview, LSEG's values are unusually usable, because its Code of Conduct turns each value into everyday behaviours. Integrity includes "We do what we say – our word is our bond", which echoes the Exchange's historic motto. Partnership opens with "Our open model is integral to how we do business", which is open access restated as a value. Change includes "We find opportunities in complexity and uncertainty" and "We have a growth mindset". Excellence includes "We take ownership of delivery" and "We learn and grow from our successes and failures" ([LSEG Code of Conduct 2024](https://lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/policies/lseg-code-of-conduct-2024.pdf)). These lines map directly onto strengths prompts about setbacks, ownership, ambiguity and collaboration. Pairing a true story with the matching behaviour line sounds specific without sounding scripted.

The early-careers evidence is good but dated. LSEG won ISE's **Best Graduate Development Programme award in 2024** and reported a **42% rise in intern conversions** in one year. That is a relative change; the actual conversion rate is not published ([ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/206/how_lsegs_graduate_programme_is_meeting_future_skills_needs/)). The graduate programme runs 12 months with a global induction, an "Ignite" mid-point offsite and 5 study days ([LSEG Workday, FTSE Russell programme](https://lseg.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/Graduate_Careers/job/London-United-Kingdom/Business-Graduate-Programme--FTSE-Russell-_R0123708)). Current postings align graduates to a division from day one, whereas the 2021–22 brochure described rotations, so do not promise yourself a rotation ([TARGETjobs listing](https://targetjobs.co.uk/jobs/business-analyst-graduate-programme-242317); [LSEG Graduate Brochure](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/brochures/lseg-graduate-brochure.pdf)). Independent intern reviews from 2022–24 rate training and support highly and social life lower ([RateMyPlacement](https://www.ratemyplacement.co.uk/placement-review/171296/lseg-london-stock-exchange-group/business-summer-intern)).

The 2025 people data support a "stable through change" story, with honest blemishes:

- **Engagement and turnover.** Engagement held at **74** through the integration years, though the survey response rate slipped from 81% to 78%. Turnover was 14%, of which 8% voluntary.
- **Volunteering.** Volunteering rose to **32% of staff** and 82,718 hours.
- **Gender balance.** Women in senior leadership **fell from 41% to 36%**, below a 40% target.
- **Footprint.** **58% of employees are in APAC.**

## Interview-ready lines

**Why LSEG in 30 seconds (about 90 words).** "What draws me to LSEG is how it's built. It owns venues like the London Stock Exchange, Tradeweb and FXall, but LCH has open access in its rules: it must clear for rival venues on equal terms, and the banks keep a stake and a seat. That neutrality, plus netting, is why SwapClear clears about 96% of dollar swaps and almost all sterling ones. Markets is 40% of the group and its fastest-growing part. Business management sits where those businesses meet, and that's where I want to learn."

**Five differentiators to say**

## Conclusion

The evidence changes what "standing out" means for LSEG. Its distinctiveness is real, but the market does not reward it. Investors currently value the parts above the whole, which means the combination LSEG markets as its edge is, for now, a claim to be proved rather than a premium already earned. For a Markets candidate that is useful rather than awkward. The parts the bears call "structurally protected" are exactly LCH, Tradeweb, the FX chain and the venues. The strongest proof that the Markets moat is economic, not inherited, is the share pattern: near-total where customers choose freely, and eroding only where a regulator intervenes.

The live questions for 2027 are therefore narrow and testable. Will JSCC's yen surge stop near parity? Will ICE–MarketAxess slow Tradeweb's credit gains? Will AI access charges show up as disclosed revenue before the patience of Elliott and the market runs out? A candidate who can name those three tests, and the mechanism behind each, will sound like an analyst rather than a fan. That is the "connect the dots" skill the job advert asks for, and the "what you achieve is equally important as how you achieve it" balance it says will make an applicant stand out.
