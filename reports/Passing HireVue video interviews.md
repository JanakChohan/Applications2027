# Pass HireVues by answering the marking scheme

A one-way video interview is marked more like an exam than a performance. What your words say, measured against a marking scheme nobody has shown you, decides more of it than how you look or sound, and a share of your rejections were probably never decided by the video at all. You asked four questions; here is each answer in brief, with how far to trust it. The rest of the guide explains the reasoning from zero.

- **Is it my answers or my delivery? Content first.** Where HireVue's optional artificial-intelligence (AI) scoring is switched on, it reads only a typed transcript of your words and ignores your face, eyes, background and tone of voice. That comes from HireVue's own published documentation and is the strongest evidence in this guide. Where people mark the video, which Goldman Sachs and PwC say is their practice, experiments show that better content lifts ratings a lot while voice and appearance each have a small effect. That evidence is moderate, because most of it comes from mock interviews with students and paid volunteers, not real applicants.
- **Script it or speak naturally? Neither extreme.** Prepare the content as thoroughly as a script, then speak it from four to six keywords, with only the first sentence, the turn into your actions and the last sentence memorised. This rests on expert agreement and first-hand accounts, not experiments, so treat it as the best available judgement.
- **Are you meant to know the questions beforehand? No, but you can know the types.** The exact questions are hidden by design. The question types are stable enough across firms and years that about six prepared stories and a handful of set pieces cover nearly all of them.
- **Why has "keep practising" not worked?** It is the weak part of the advice you have been given. The one experiment that separated the two found that practice alone did little, while training in what the markers score improved performance.
- **What about you specifically?** You give seven-minute unscripted debate speeches, so any delivery problem you have is specific to the camera or to reciting a script. The one-week diagnosis in this guide will tell you whether your problem is content, delivery, or something outside the video such as your CV, an online test or timing.

If you read only three parts, read this page, the one-week diagnosis and the checklist at the end.

## What this guide rests on, and where the evidence is thin

You asked for honesty about what is known, so here it is before anything else. Five kinds of evidence sit behind this guide, and they are not equally trustworthy.

| Kind of evidence | What it is good for | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| HireVue's own documents, and a research paper written mainly by HireVue staff | How the software works and what the AI reads | Strong on mechanics, but "official" is not the same as independently checked |
| Firms' own careers pages (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, BlackRock, Barclays, HSBC, Schroders, PwC, Deloitte, KPMG) | Stages, rules, what is banned | Reliable, but they say very little about marking |
| Academic experiments | What raises or lowers interview ratings | Moderate: nearly all use mock interviews with no job at stake |
| Trade press and coaching sites | Formats, reported questions | Mixed: often no named source, and some sell courses |
| Anonymous forums (Wall Street Oasis, The Student Room, Glassdoor, Canary Wharfian) | What reviewers and candidates say happened | Weak: most posts could be read only as search-engine snippets, without dates or context |

Three gaps matter most. **No bank's internal marking sheet is public**, so everything about how banks mark is inferred from the tool they use, from published marking schemes elsewhere and from anonymous reviewers. **Reddit could not be read at all**, and The Student Room and Wall Street Oasis could mostly be read only as snippets, so the pool of first-hand accounts is small: about 25 accounts, of which roughly five are true "I failed, changed something, then passed" stories. And **almost all the firm-specific evidence is about banks**; for asset managers there is a little on BlackRock and Schroders, and for wealth managers there is nothing usable. Where I give a recommendation built on weak evidence, I say so.

One piece of jargon appears throughout. A **correlation**, written *r*, is a number that shows how closely two things move together: 0 means no link, 0.2 is a weak link and 0.5 a substantial one. A minus sign means that as one goes up the other goes down.

## What a HireVue is and what happens to your recording

**HireVue** is an American company whose software lets an employer set interview questions that you answer alone, on camera, with nobody on the other end. This is called a **one-way**, **on-demand** or **asynchronous** video interview: the recording is stored and assessed later. People use "HireVue" loosely for every interview of this kind, but some firms use other suppliers. PwC UK runs its video interview on a platform called SHL, and Schroders' online stages use tools from a company called Aon ([PwC UK](https://www.pwc.co.uk/careers/early-careers/apply/selection-process.html)) ([Schroders](https://www.schroders.com/en-gb/uk/individual/about-us/careers/graduates/faqs/)). The detail about AI below applies to HireVue only.

<!-- figure:routes -->

The employer, not HireVue, sets almost everything you experience: how many questions, how long you get to think, how long you get to answer and how many attempts you have. Banks are reported to give about **30 seconds to prepare and 90 or 120 seconds to answer**, over three to five questions ([Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)); BlackRock is reported to give 180 seconds to prepare and 90 to answer, although candidates on Glassdoor describe about 30 seconds there, so settings differ by programme ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/how-long-to-answer-hirevue-interview)) ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.ca/Interview/BlackRock-Spring-Insight-Program-Interview-Questions-EI_IE9331.0,9_KO10,32.htm)). Three mechanical facts are confirmed by HireVue's help pages. An early screen tells you how many attempts you have per question. You are "not required to use the full response time" and can click "Done Answering". And if you re-record, "the last recording that was made is the video the recruiter will see"; you cannot go back and choose an earlier take ([HireVue support](https://hirevuesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028433751-Getting-Started-with-Your-On-Demand-Interview-Computer)) ([HireVue help centre](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490713395348-Can-a-candidate-make-multiple-interview-recordings-and-select-the-best-one)).

### HireVue is two products: a video player for human markers, and an optional AI score

The single most useful fact for you is that HireVue is two things at once. The first is a recording-and-review tool: people at the firm watch your answers and mark them. The second is an AI assessment that the employer has to license separately; HireVue's help page says "AI scored is a licensed application feature and may not be available for your application" ([HireVue help centre](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490688121748-AI-scored)). HireVue's own statements suggest the AI is used in a minority of interviews: a HireVue blog says "only about 1 in 5 HireVue interviews actually use an algorithm", and a HireVue consultant put it at under 30% in 2022 ([HireVue blog](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/candidates/candidate-questions-answered-live-youtube-qa-with-hirevue-ceo-kevin-parker-and-andrew-lacivita)) ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/10/hirevue-interview-banking)). Both figures are several years old and neither is broken down by industry.

### When people mark it: stars per question, averaged, plus yes, maybe or no

A **rating scale** is a fixed set of marks, such as one to five stars, with a description of what each mark means. A **competency** is a named skill or behaviour the employer has decided the job needs, such as teamwork, problem solving or "drive for results". HireVue's reviewer screen is built around both. The reviewer gives a star rating for each question; the system "averages the ratings for all questions to produce an overall average rating"; the reviewer can type notes and gives an overall recommendation of "No, Maybe, or Yes". The employer chooses a three-, four- or five-point scale ([HireVue help centre](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070569356052-OnDemand-Evaluating-an-Interview-On-a-Computer)) ([HireVue evaluation settings](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43071188493460-My-Company-Settings-Evaluation-Settings)). HireVue recommends that employers switch on written rating guidelines for reviewers, and employers can add "reviewer instructions" describing what an effective and an ineffective answer looks like ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)) ([HireVue help centre](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490705990804-Interview-questions)).

What the reviewer sees is a video player with your answers listed question by question. They can "play and pause the video, jump backward or forward 15 seconds, adjust the volume, and control the playback speed", and on the newer version of the platform the page links to your CV ([HireVue help centre](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070569356052-OnDemand-Evaluating-an-Interview-On-a-Computer)) ([HireVue review page](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490755026580-Review-page-on-demand-interviews)). More than one person can rate you, and their ratings are averaged ([HireVue rating and review](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490682567572-Rating-and-review)). Whether a reviewer can see how many attempts you used is not documented.

Who the reviewers are is known only in outline. Goldman Sachs says its video interviews are "assessed by trained human recruiters and colleagues across our firm" ([Goldman Sachs careers blog](https://www.goldmansachs.com/careers/blog/posts/goldman-sachs-video-interviewing-facts.html)). Anonymous posters who say they have reviewed videos describe being "assigned a group of videos" to rate question by question, and name recruiters, junior analysts, hiring managers and, at one bank, a group of managing directors ([Fishbowl](https://www.fishbowlapp.com/post/do-companies-like-goldman-sachs-and-jp-morgan-actually-go-through-their-hirevue-video-interviews-i-read-somewhere-that)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/do-hirevues-even-matter)). One says "HR tends to require feedback per Q + a general score" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/how-are-hirevue-videos-looked-atrated)). These are unverifiable snippets, but they agree with each other and with the design of the tool.

### When the AI scores it: your words are typed out and compared with past marked answers

Where the AI is switched on, HireVue describes three steps: "(1) transcribing spoken words to text, (2) understanding the meaning of the text, and (3) assessing/scoring the candidate's answers based on expert human rater evaluations of answers to similar competency-based interview questions" ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)). In plain terms: trained people marked many thousands of real interview answers from one to five for each competency, and the software learned to predict the mark those people would give from the words alone. HireVue states that "our AI relies only on what is said by the candidate and does not use any video analysis or other audio characteristics (meaning that we do not assess a candidate's facial expressions, body language, their background and surroundings, or tone-of-voice)" ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)). The research paper behind it, written mainly by HireVue staff, says the same: "We explicitly excluded paraverbal (e.g., pitch, cadence, tone), and nonverbal (e.g., facial expressions) variables" ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)).

The recruiter then sees a ranking, not a mark out of ten. On HireVue's classic platform the score is "a percentile ranking of each candidate's interview responses as compared to all other candidates that have been scored by this same assessment in this account" (a **percentile** says what share of other candidates you beat), shown by default as three bands: bottom third, middle third and top third ([HireVue score FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169134508436-HireVue-Score-and-Percentile-Ranking-FAQ)) ([HireVue Assessments FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070724859796-HireVue-Assessments-FAQ)). HireVue's own diagram has recruiters advancing the top band, watching the middle band, and watching the bottom band only "if volumes are insufficient"; its help page adds that "often customers will use automated actions to advance or reject candidates based on their HireVue score" ([HireVue short-form statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)) ([HireVue Assessments FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070724859796-HireVue-Assessments-FAQ)). So at an employer that uses the AI this way, a bottom-third answer can be rejected without anyone watching it. No bank has confirmed that it does this.

Three details of the AI route are worth knowing. First, **you are told**: an AI-scored interview shows a consent page with "Yes, I consent" and "I do not consent", and if you decline you get the same interview marked by people, though you are visibly tagged to the recruiter as having opted out ([HireVue help centre](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490672413844-AI-scored-recommendations)). It follows, as an inference rather than a HireVue statement, that if you saw no such page, only people marked you. Second, **the AI needs enough clear words**: on the newer platform an answer is not scored if the transcript is "projected to be less than 70% accurate" or is under 150 characters, and on the classic platform causes of failure include "low volume audio", "background noise, especially a consistent hum" and "not enough words spoken" ([HireVue help centre](https://hirevuehelpcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/45490672413844-AI-scored-recommendations)) ([HireVue thresholding FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070693370132-Insufficient-Data-Error-Thresholding-FAQ)). This is the real reason audio quality matters: the transcript is the whole input. Third, **the score is noisy**: the software agrees with trained human markers at about *r* = .66, and the same person re-interviewed about six weeks later gets a score that matches their first at about *r* = .72, so the same candidate can land in a different band on a different day ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)).

### Four myths the evidence disproves

| Myth | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "It analyses your face and expressions." | False for current HireVue scoring. It was true before 2020. | HireVue "stopped using video inputs in new models early in 2020" and announced the removal in January 2021 ([HireVue blog](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/hiring/the-latest-leap-in-hirevues-assessment-technology)) ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/talent-acquisition/pages/hirevue-discontinues-facial-analysis-screening.aspx)). In the EU, inferring emotions from faces in recruitment has been banned since 2 February 2025 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)). |
| "It tracks your eyes to catch you reading." | No documentation supports it. | HireVue describes no gaze analysis anywhere; its own white paper says reading is "something a human reviewer can sense when watching" ([HireVue white paper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)). A person can simply see you reading. |
| "It scores your tone of voice, pauses and ums." | False for the AI. People still hear them. | HireVue said in September 2021 that it would "eliminate speech inputs"; a HireVue consultant said of video, intonation and gaps: "None of that is being scored" ([HireVue blog](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/hiring/the-latest-leap-in-hirevues-assessment-technology)) ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/10/hirevue-interview-banking)). |
| "A bored reviewer decides in the first 15 seconds." | Not supported. The first answer does carry extra weight. | See the next paragraph. |

The rumours have a traceable origin: one out-of-date HireVue help article still says the system analyses "voice tone, facial expressions, eye movements, and speech rates", while a corrected sister article says "only language-based data is scored" ([stale HireVue article](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43169148272148-Accuracy-of-the-HireVue-Assessment-Score-FAQ)) ([corrected HireVue article](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070724859796-HireVue-Assessments-FAQ)). One honest caveat: HireVue said old features were removed from older models "as they come up for review", and no dated statement confirms that every old model has gone.

On speed of decision, the sources conflict. One anonymous reviewer on Wall Street Oasis says "most people watch the first video question and make a decision. If they're on the fence, they'll keep watching"; another replies that they "always watched the whole thing, sometimes at 1.5-2x speed" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/how-are-hirevue-videos-looked-atrated)). Neither says 15 seconds: a first answer is 90 to 120 seconds long. Research on live interviews points the same way. In a study of 166 interviewers and 691 applicants, only **4.9% of decisions were made in the first minute** and about 70% took five minutes or more, and the more structured the interview, the slower the decision ([Frieder et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/joop.12118)) ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/do-interviewers-really-make-hiring-decision-first-four-minutes)). First impressions do influence marks, but the influence is stronger on early questions than on later ones ([Swider et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000077)). No study has measured how quickly people judge a recorded one-way answer. My recommendation: treat your **first answer and the first sentence of every answer** as carrying extra weight, and do not believe that the rest is unwatched.

## You are being ranked against hundreds of thousands, not marked against a pass mark

There is no pass mark. A HireVue consultant put it this way: candidates do not really "fail"; the application is "put at the bottom of a big pile", the firm starts with the best-ranked, and this "explains why some candidates wait months without hearing back" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/10/hirevue-interview-banking)). The piles are very large. Goldman Sachs had **more than 360,000 internship applicants in 2025 and accepted 0.7%** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/exclusive-goldman-sachs-intern-acceptance-rate-falls-below-1-for-third-straight-year/)). JPMorgan is reported to have had **630,000 applicants for 4,100 intern places**, also 0.7%, and Morgan Stanley's rate was 0.5% ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/bank-acceptance-rate)). These are worldwide figures across all divisions; nothing is published for London alone.

No firm publishes a pass rate for the video stage. HireVue's consultant says 60% to 75% of candidates typically go through across all its customers, but that cannot be true at firms that accept under 1% ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/10/hirevue-interview-banking)). Coaching sites quote pass rates such as "roughly 20-25%" for BlackRock with no source, and you should ignore them ([Intervyo](https://www.useintervyo.com/firms/blackrock/hirevue)). A rough sum shows the scale. If one in three to one in five final-round candidates gets an offer, JPMorgan can bring only about 12,000 to 20,000 of its 630,000 applicants to a final round, so the CV and video screen together remove about 97%. That sum rests on my assumption about final-round conversion, not on a published figure. Its meaning for you is plain: **at these firms, being rejected after a HireVue is the normal outcome**, including for good candidates, and many rejections in a row is weak evidence that your videos are unusually bad.

### A rejection after a HireVue is often not a rejection because of the HireVue

Four other things can decide the outcome, and the evidence for each is real.

Your **CV** is judged alongside the video. A self-described screener says "It's a mix of resume and hirevue" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/goldman-sachs-hirevue-process)), and a poster badged as JPMorgan staff says HR writes a recommendation from your CV and a hiring manager then watches the video ([Fishbowl](https://www.fishbowlapp.com/post/do-companies-like-goldman-sachs-and-jp-morgan-actually-go-through-their-hirevue-video-interviews-i-read-somewhere-that)). At Bank of America and JPMorgan the video is effectively part of the application form: JPMorgan's advert says "your application will not be considered for further review until you have completed these three questions" ([JPMorgan listing](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/jpmorganchase/2026-commercial-investment-banking-markets-research-summer-internship-london)). Where everybody gets the video automatically, "rejected after the HireVue" and "rejected on the CV" look identical from the outside.

**Online tests** often sit before or beside the video and are marked by computer. KPMG invites you to its video stage only "if you pass Stage 2"; HSBC, Schroders and Deloitte fold recorded answers into wider online assessments ([KPMG](https://www.kpmgcareers.co.uk/apprentice/applying-to-kpmg/)) ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/careers/students-and-graduates/applying-to-hsbc)) ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/careers/early-careers/early-careers-assessment.html)). A Student Room poster reports that Deutsche Bank "rejects your application THE SAME DAY" if the online assessment falls short ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7616430&page=2)).

**Timing** matters because of **rolling recruitment**, which means the firm reviews applications as they arrive and fills places as it goes instead of waiting for the deadline. Goldman, JPMorgan, HSBC and Schroders all say they recruit this way; HSBC adds that it "may close a programme early if all vacancies are filled" ([JPMorganChase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/careers/how-we-hire/faqs)) ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/careers/students-and-graduates/applying-to-hsbc)). No data shows how fast places fill.

Some videos are **never watched**. One applicant who used data-protection law to obtain his records reports that JPMorgan's reply implicitly admitted his HireVue answers had not been watched across two years of applications ([GitHub write-up](https://github.com/Leader-board/OA-and-Interviews/blob/main/Getting%20the%20feedback%20through%20GDPR.md)). That is one anonymous, undated account for technology roles. It shows this happens; it does not show how often.

Your own sense of how it went is no guide either. One candidate wrote "I absolutely aced it, my answers were as perfect as they could possibly be, but I didn't get invited", while a friend who "was just rambling on" got through; another reports "My answers were ass and I still got AC and offer" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/hirevue-disaster)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/jpm-ibd-sa-2026-are-these-hv-answers-strong-enough-for-ac-has-any-ac-gone)).

### How to tell which it was

| What you observed | Most likely reading | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rejection within about two days of submitting the video | Not a person's verdict on your video: a test score, an eligibility rule or a full programme | Goldman's own guide was about two weeks to hear back; reviewers describe videos handed out in batches ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2018/07/video-interview-goldman-sachs)). This is an inference, not a firm statement. |
| The video link arrived automatically, minutes after you applied | The video was part of the application; the CV screen came with or after it | Bank of America requires application and video "by the application deadline" ([EP Advisory](https://ep-advisory.com/blog/bank-of-america-investment-banking-analyst-application-process/)) |
| An online test came before or with the video | The test is the first suspect | KPMG, HSBC, Schroders and Deloitte stage orders, above |
| Weeks or months of silence, then a rejection near the end of the season | You were ranked but never reached before places filled | HireVue consultant's "bottom of a big pile" |
| You applied or submitted late in the window | Places were already allocated | Firms' rolling-recruitment statements |
| You clicked through an AI consent page | An AI score ranked you; a low band is sometimes not watched | HireVue's own workflow diagram |
| A records request shows no rating or notes against your video | It was not reviewed | The JPMorgan account above |

For your own file there is one more check. The research found nothing either way on whether firms filter on A-level grades or university at this stage, beyond Deloitte saying that candidates must meet its academic criteria to move forward ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/careers/early-careers/early-careers-assessment.html)). So compare each advert's stated school-grade requirement with your A, B, C before you blame the video for that application. That is a check to run, not a finding.

## Why people fail: eight causes, ranked by how well the evidence supports them

The ranking below is my judgement of how much evidence stands behind each cause, strongest first. The studies use different measures, so the order is not a calculation.

| Rank | Cause | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The answer does not show the competency the question is marking | Strongest: several experiments, HireVue's own staff, firms' advice |
| 2 | General claims instead of one specific event | Strong: field study, marking schemes, HireVue's own example |
| 3 | Too little of your own action, and no result | Moderate: published marking schemes, careers services, one first-hand account |
| 4 | Hard to follow: no stated point, no clear order | Moderate: HireVue's published scale, one field study |
| 5 | Wrong length: too short, or padded after the answer has ended | Indicative: one doctoral thesis plus HireVue field data |
| 6 | Reciting a script, or depending on one | Weak to moderate: first-hand accounts and expert opinion; one experiment cuts the other way |
| 7 | Camera delivery: slow start, slow speech, flat energy, eyes off to the side | Moderate evidence, small effects |
| 8 | Process and technical errors | Anecdotal, but 4 of about 25 accounts |

**Cause 1: the answer does not show what the question is marking.** Each question exists to collect evidence of a particular competency, and candidates who work out which one score higher. When researchers simply told candidates which qualities each question was marking, scores rose, with the change explaining roughly 7% to 12% of the differences in marks ([Klehe et al. 2008](https://jlupub.ub.uni-giessen.de/bitstream/jlupub/17122/1/KleheTransparency_2008.pdf)). People who are naturally better at guessing the criteria also score higher (*r* = .23 in one study) ([Ingold et al. 2015](https://www.uni-saarland.de/fileadmin/upload/lehrstuhl/koenig/PDFs-Publikationen/IngoldKleinmannKoenigMelchersvanIddekinge_inpress.pdf)). An independent audit of HireVue found that "some candidates don't understand what the assessment is 'looking for'" ([ORCAA audit](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf)). One conflicting result: a 2025 study found this ability did not predict how much people improved their scores ([Hickman et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.70014)). It looks like this: a fluent, pleasant answer that tells a story about the wrong thing, or answers only half the question. Goldman's recruiters say "Be specific and be sure to answer all parts of the question" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2018/07/video-interview-goldman-sachs)). The fix is to name the competency before you choose your story, and to say the link out loud.

**Cause 2: general claims instead of one specific event.** HireVue's chief data scientist gave the example herself: "A bad answer might be simply, 'I like to work as a team'" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2021/03/hirevue-interview-bank)). A study of 62 real job interviews found recruiters respond positively to genuine stories, yet some candidates fall back on describing how they usually behave instead of recalling one event, and researchers note that applicants often find such stories hard to produce ([Bangerter et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-014-9350-0)) ([Orji 2025](https://doi.org/10.35662/unine-thesis-3214)). HireVue's own test shows where a generic answer lands: the average candidate scores 3 out of 5, and ChatGPT given only the question "also scored in the range of an average candidate" ([HireVue white paper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)). In a ranking where most people are cut, average is a rejection. It looks like this: sentences beginning "I'm someone who...", "I always...", "I believe teamwork is...". The fix is one dated event with named actions.

**Cause 3: too little of your own action, and no result.** Published marking schemes reward "positive evidence" of what you personally did, and careers services budget about 60% of an answer for your actions ([COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)) ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)). The first-hand version: a candidate who prepared for "12+ hours" wrote "First question, I didn't have enough time to finish my answer" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/2nd-hirevue-attempt-for-2021-early-careersand-i-failed-miserably)). Goldman's recording is reported to cut off at exactly two minutes ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/goldman-sachs-hirevue-process)). It looks like this: forty seconds of background, a rushed "so we fixed it", and the clock running out before any outcome. The fix is the time budget in the answer-building section. The widespread belief that candidates spend too long on background comes from careers-service experience; no study has measured it.

**Cause 4: hard to follow.** HireVue's published scale for Communication puts "disorganized, lacks a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is not delivered in a logical sequence" at the bottom ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)). Communication is marked on every question, not on a question of its own, and its scores move closely with every other competency score (*r* = .74), so a muddled answer about teamwork loses marks twice ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)). In a study of 213 real promotion candidates, coaching improved scores through one route: better-organised answers ([Maurer et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.86.4.709)). The fix is a first sentence that answers the question, and spoken signposts.

**Cause 5: wrong length.** In an experiment for a doctoral thesis, people given one minute scored lowest; people given three minutes spoke for about 127 seconds and scored highest; and when candidates kept talking after signalling they had finished, which about 80% did at least once, ratings fell ([Orji 2025](https://doi.org/10.35662/unine-thesis-3214)). Real HireVue candidates given three minutes averaged about 1 minute 45 seconds ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)). It looks like this: either a 40-second answer with half the evidence missing, or a complete answer followed by 30 seconds of repetition into silence. This rests mainly on one thesis, so treat it as indicative.

**Cause 6: reciting a script, or depending on one.** This is the most common self-diagnosis among people who went from failing to passing. A UK candidate who had sat "a countless number" describes a bad Bank of America interview where unexpected questions "got me flustered, as I was relying on my carefully curated pre-prepared answers", and concludes that scripting word for word "is definitely not advisable" ([Canary Wharfian](https://forum.canarywharfian.co.uk/threads/how-to-pass-hirevue-interviews-i-sat-too-many.529/)). A candidate who did pass said that coming across "like a robot" after revising scripts hurt them ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/morgan-stanley-hirevue-interview-went-awful-summer-analyst-ibd)). The conflicting evidence: in the only experiment on reading aloud, people who read out ChatGPT-written answers received "considerably higher" marks for content and overall, their delivery marks did not drop, but they were rated less honest ([Canagasuriam & Lukacik 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.12491)). So a script does not automatically wreck delivery; what fails is a half-learned script, or a script for a question you were not asked. Given your debating, this is the first delivery cause to test in your case.

**Cause 7: camera delivery.** The effects here are real but small. In real interviews, a bundle of voice features (pitch variation, pace, pauses) correlated *r* = .20 with interviewers' judgements, and a bundle of visual features (smiling, gaze, attractiveness, gestures) *r* = .21 ([DeGroot & Motowidlo 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.84.6.986)). Long delays before answering lowered ratings in a study of real interviews, and slow speech was the one behaviour reliably read as nervousness ([Brosy et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/0163853X.2016.1150769)) ([Feiler & Powell 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-015-9403-z)). Looking slightly above or below the camera did not lower ratings in an experiment; looking sideways did ([Basch & Melchers 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-024-09981-4)). Ratings made from recordings ran lower than live ratings of the same interviews, which fits your own experience of being better in a room ([Basch et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-020-09714-3)). Anxiety lowers ratings modestly (*r* = -.19), through looking less assertive and warm and speaking more slowly ([Powell et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/cbs0000108)).

**Cause 8: process and technical errors.** Four of the roughly 25 accounts were self-inflicted: submitting with no answers recorded, a low-battery pop-up pausing a recording, a laptop ringing mid-answer, clicking stop in the belief that a retake would follow ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/2nd-hirevue-attempt-for-2021-early-careersand-i-failed-miserably)) ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7580291)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/hirevue-disaster)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/i-swore-in-a-hirevue-and-it-submitted)). Where AI scoring is on, one unintelligible answer "will cause the entire interview to fail", which HireVue says happens in 1% to 2% of interviews ([HireVue thresholding FAQ](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070693370132-Insufficient-Data-Error-Thresholding-FAQ)). The fix is to use the practice question to learn exactly what each button does before you start.

Two causes that coaching sites assert often, giving a generic "why this firm" answer and answering a different question from the one asked, are plausible, but no first-hand account in the research names either as the thing a candidate fixed before passing.

## How answers are marked, and what lifts a middle answer into the top band

No bank publishes its marking sheet. Two real marking schemes are public, and together they show what markers are trained to look for.

The first is the UK Civil Service scale, used across government interviews and published by one of its departments. It is not a bank's scale; it is here because it is the clearest published UK example of how an answer about behaviour is marked ([COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)).

| Mark | Label | Published description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not Demonstrated | "No positive evidence and/ or substantial negative evidence demonstrated" |
| 2 | Minimal Demonstration | "Limited positive evidence and/ or mainly negative evidence demonstrated" |
| 3 | Moderate Demonstration | "Moderate positive evidence but some negative evidence demonstrated" |
| 4 | Acceptable Demonstration | "Adequate positive evidence and any negative evidence would not cause concern" |
| 5 | Good Demonstration | "Substantial positive evidence of the behaviour" |
| 6 | Strong Demonstration | "Substantial positive evidence; includes some evidence of exceeding expectations at this level" |
| 7 | Outstanding Demonstration | "The evidence provided wholly exceeds expectation at this level" |

The pass line there is 4. Markers are told to "observe, record, classify, evaluate and score each question independently" ([Cabinet Office guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f746b1ae90e0740caae75a7/Guidance-Application_of_Success_Profile_Guides_during_delegated_grade__EO-Grade_6__recruitment_v0b.pdf)). The word that matters is **evidence**. The marker is counting things you said you did, not judging whether you seem impressive.

The second is HireVue's own scale. It is a **behaviourally anchored rating scale** (BARS): a rating scale where each mark is tied, or "anchored", to a description of behaviour. It has five levels, from Novice ("unlikely to be successful in situations requiring this competency") through Intermediate ("likely to demonstrate this competency well, but may need assistance in more difficult situations") to Expert ("with extreme effectiveness in moderate to complex situations") ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)). HireVue has published the full scale for one competency only, Communication. On the line "Delivers clear & concise message", a Novice answer is "disorganized, lacks a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is not delivered in a logical sequence"; an Intermediate answer is "reasonably organised, has a clear purpose and importance, and is delivered in a logical sequence"; an Expert answer is "well organized, has a clear explanation of purpose and importance, and is delivered in a logical sequence" ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)) ([HireVue short-form statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HV_2025_AI_Short-Form_Explainability-Statement-1.pdf)). On other lines the Expert level is marked by doing things unprompted: someone who "proactively seeks feedback and follows up on the message to confirm correct interpretation" and is "proactively seeking the ideas of others", against a Novice who "interacts and shares information only when asked" ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)).

Some firms publish the headings of their own forms. KPMG lists the 12 strengths it assesses, among them Resilience, Initiative, Critical Thinking and Integrity; Barclays says it looks for its "key attributes and Values" ([KPMG](https://www.kpmgcareers.co.uk/apprentice/applying-to-kpmg/)) ([Barclays](https://search.jobs.barclays/internship-graduate-application)). JPMorgan's reported 2026 questions (persisting toward a goal, an innovative solution, doing the right thing when it was hard, showing humility) read like one question per competency, though JPMorgan publishes no scheme ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)).

Put the two scales side by side and five things separate a top-band answer from a middle one. The middle answer is true, relevant and structured, and thin: HireVue says its average candidate sits at 3 of 5, the same place an unprompted ChatGPT answer lands ([HireVue white paper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)). The top answer, first, describes **one specific event**. Second, it has **volume of personal evidence**, "substantial" in the Civil Service wording: several distinct things you did, each a sentence starting with "I" and a verb. Third, it is **visibly organised**, with its purpose stated. Fourth, it shows you **going beyond what someone at your level was asked to do**: starting something unasked, checking that it worked for the people affected, following up afterwards. That is my reading of "exceeding expectations at this level" and of HireVue's "proactively" wording. Fifth, it has **an outcome and something learned**, which Civil Service panels say they are "interested in" ([COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)).

Because marks are averaged across questions, consistency beats brilliance. One blank or rambling answer drags your average down, and nothing above the top of the scale exists to compensate for it.

## Building each type of answer

### The time budget inside an answer, and how long to speak

**STAR** stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result: where you were, what needed doing, what you did, what happened. It is the shape HireVue's markers were trained on; the questions used to build its models had to follow "the Situation-Task-Action-Result interview question structure" ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)). MIT's careers service gives the shares: **Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10%** ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)). Sources disagree slightly on the Result, between 10% and about 17%, and I use the higher end because published marking schemes ask for the outcome and the lesson.

On total length the sources conflict, so here they are together.

| Source | What it says | Kind of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Doctoral thesis experiment ([Orji 2025](https://doi.org/10.35662/unine-thesis-3214)) | With a 3-minute limit, about 127 seconds of speech scored best; 1-minute answers scored worst; talking on after finishing lowered marks | Mock interviews, one study |
| HireVue's research data ([Liff et al. 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/a/web/5pa7UFPYmcygooLJEwwDgW/88Pn9s/psychometric-properties-of-automated-video-interview-competency-assessments.pdf)) | Real candidates given 3 minutes averaged about 1 minute 45 seconds | Real applicants; an average, not a recommendation |
| Mergers & Inquisitions ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)) | 100 to 125 words a minute; about 150 words for a 90-second answer; finishing a 2-minute slot in 1 minute is fine | Commercial site; the author's banking career predates HireVue |
| Coaching site ([IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/hirevue-video-interview-investment-banking-guide)) | Use 80% to 90% of the limit | Vendor |
| Reviewer-side forum post ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/sa27-hirevues)) | "Better to deliver a concise, well-thought-out response in 50 seconds than to ramble for the full 2 minutes" | Anonymous snippet |
| Student Room posters ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7558431)) | One says use all the time; another passed with 90 seconds | Anonymous snippets |
| HireVue's documentation ([HireVue support](https://hirevuesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028433751-Getting-Started-with-Your-On-Demand-Interview-Computer)) | Stopping early is allowed; nothing marks "time used"; there is only a minimum amount of content | Official |

The two camps are closer than they look. Nobody reports being rejected for a complete answer that stopped early, and nobody defends padding. My recommendation: **finish a complete structure, including the result, at 75% to 90% of the limit for a story, and 50% to 70% for a motivation or markets answer, then stop.**

| | 90-second limit | 2-minute limit |
|---|---|---|
| Story answer: aim to finish at | 70 to 80 seconds | 95 to 110 seconds |
| Story answer: words at 125 a minute | 150 to 170 | 200 to 230 |
| Headline sentence | 6 seconds | 8 seconds |
| Situation | 12 seconds | 17 seconds |
| Task | 7 seconds | 9 seconds |
| Action | 40 seconds | 55 seconds |
| Result | 9 seconds | 11 seconds |
| Learning or link to the role (the part you can drop) | 4 seconds | 5 seconds |
| Motivation or markets answer: aim to finish at | 50 to 65 seconds | 60 to 85 seconds |

<!-- figure:timebar -->

Measure your own pace from a transcript before you trust the word counts; debaters often speak faster than 125 words a minute. If the timer shows about 15 seconds left and you are still describing actions, jump straight to "The result was...". If a firm gives you only 30 seconds to answer, as Blackstone is reported to do, collapse to point, one reason, one fact, point again ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/how-long-to-answer-hirevue-interview)).

### Past-behaviour questions ("Tell me about a time when...")

These dominate the reported lists: failure, a struggling team, a difficult colleague, leadership, doing the right thing, initiative, persistence, prioritising, adapting, persuading ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)) ([eFinancialCareers 2023](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/11/hirevue-questions-barclays-deutsche-bank-ubs)). Build each answer as one headline sentence that answers the question and names the event, then STAR on the budget above, then one line of learning. The headline matters because "clear explanation of purpose" is in the top band of HireVue's scale, and because a first-sentence answer survives a hurried viewer. It should be one sentence, not a preview of everything to come.

On "I" against "we" the sources conflict. The Civil Service and MIT both tell candidates to say "I" so markers can see what you personally did ([COPFS](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/careers/how-your-application-is-assessed-success-profiles)) ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)). The only study that measured pronouns found "we" linked to higher ratings and "I" to lower, but it used 69 students rated by untrained online workers for general impression, not trained markers using a scale ([Naim et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.03425)). My recommendation follows the marking schemes: "we" for the setting, "I" for every action.

How many stories do you need? Recommendations run from three to about seven, and six stories each tagged to three or four themes cover the ten recurring themes with a spare for each ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)) ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)). Your own record gives you more raw material than most candidates have. The table lists what each experience could cover; the themes in it are possibilities for you to confirm, because I know only the headline of each experience and not what happened inside it.

| Your experience | Themes it could cover, if the facts support it |
|---|---|
| Portal routing due diligence questionnaires to the owning team, replacing a 400-line Excel tracker (asset manager) | Initiative; improving a process; solving a problem; explaining something complicated simply |
| Progressing 50 segregated market openings (asset manager) | Persistence; prioritising and juggling tasks; working across teams [confirm who you depended on] |
| Validating performance packs on a $900m equity mandate (asset manager) | Producing accurate work under pressure; doing the right thing [only if you found and raised a problem] |
| Modelling sales quotas against a $3.8m regional target (software company) | Solving a business problem with a result; breaking down information; persuading [only if someone had to be convinced] |

| Sector head in the student investment fund; the Urban Outfitters buy pitch | Leadership; persuasion; "pitch a stock"; a view on markets |

| Head Boy | Leadership; handling a disagreement [a specific incident]; working with people unlike you [a specific incident] |

### A worked example: before and after

The question, reported at Goldman Sachs in 2026, is about a time you made a process more efficient ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)). The competency behind it is initiative and drive for results. The "before" below is my illustration of a typical middle-band answer built from your material; it is not a transcript of anything you said.

**Before (about 95 words, roughly 45 seconds of a 2-minute limit):**

> "During my summer internship at an asset manager I worked in the client servicing and onboarding team. The team received a lot of due diligence questionnaires, and these were tracked in a big Excel spreadsheet, which was quite inefficient. We decided to improve this, so we built a portal which made the process much more efficient. I'm someone who always looks for ways to improve things, and I think that's really important in finance. It taught me a lot about teamwork and attention to detail, and I would bring those skills to this role."

Marked against the published scales, this is "adequate positive evidence" at best, a 3 or 4 of 7. Nothing in it is false or badly spoken. It says "we" at the two moments that matter, so the marker cannot tell what you did. It contains one action ("built a portal") and no result. A third of it is general claims about yourself, the pattern HireVue's own data scientist gave as a bad answer. It stops with more than a minute unused and the evidence missing. Better delivery would not move its mark.

**After (200 to 230 words once the brackets are filled, roughly 100 seconds). Square brackets mark facts only you can supply.**

> **Headline (8 seconds).** "The clearest example is from my summer in client servicing at [asset manager], where I replaced a 400-line Excel tracker with a portal that sends each due diligence questionnaire straight to the team that owns it."
>
> **Situation (17 seconds).** "Clients send these questionnaires when they are deciding whether to invest, and every question has to reach the right specialist team. All of that ran through one shared spreadsheet, and the problem I kept seeing was [the specific failure: for example, questions sitting unassigned, or people being chased by email]."
>
> **Task (9 seconds).** "[Say honestly whether you were asked to fix this or proposed it. If you proposed it: 'Nobody had asked me to fix it, so I took the idea to my manager and offered to build a replacement alongside my normal work.']"
>
> **Action (55 seconds).** "I did three things. First, I [how you worked out which team owned which type of question, and how many teams that was]. Second, I built the portal in [tool], so that [what happens when a questionnaire arrives]. Third, before switching over, I [how you tested it with the teams who would use it, and what you changed because of what they told you], and I [how you handed it over so it kept working after you left]."
>
> **Result (11 seconds).** "The portal replaced the tracker for [which team or teams], and [result you measured: time saved, number of questionnaires handled, errors avoided]. [If you measured nothing, say what visibly changed and what your manager did with it.]"
>
> **Learning (5 seconds, drop it if the clock is tight).** "What I took from it is [one specific lesson], which is why [one clause linking to this role]."

What changed is entirely content. The headline answers the question in the first eight seconds. Every action is an "I" sentence. The third action, testing with the users and handing over, is the "proactively seeks feedback and follows up" behaviour from the top band of HireVue's scale. The task line, if you started this yourself, is the "exceeding expectations at this level" evidence that separates a 6 from a 5. For a 90-second limit, cut the learning line and compress the third action to one clause. Do not put a number into the result that you did not measure.

### Motivation questions ("Why this firm?", "Why this division?")

The test is substitution: "if your answer would work just as well for Goldman or Morgan Stanley with a name swap, it's not specific enough" ([IB Interview Questions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/why-this-bank-specifically-answer)). Answers that fail it include liking "the culture" (which one long-running guide calls "way too vague"), "the people are smart and hard-working", and prestige ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)). The same guide's order for a strong answer is people, then deals, then your own interest: a named person with their title and office and how you met them; a specific piece of the firm's work with the parties and size; and a link to your background. It adds two rules: never invent a name or a meeting, and do not cite deals for a division that does not do deals ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-our-bank-investment-banking-interviews)). That advice was written for investment banking. For asset and wealth management, my adaptation is to replace the deal with something equally checkable about that firm's investing business: [a named fund or strategy, a published investment view, or a recent business move, with one number]. Goldman's own email to applicants says its "HireVue best practices" are to review the firm's financial results, business principles and core values ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/goldman-hirevue)).

Unlike a story, a motivation answer suits a list. Use a headline naming two reasons, then the person, then the firm-specific fact, then your own link, in 50 to 85 seconds. Your material is strong here. For "why asset management" you have a summer inside an asset manager, so your reason can be something you saw there: "[what you observed on the $900m equity mandate work that made you want to be closer to the investment side]". For a Goldman answer you have a spring week, so your people evidence can be "[name, title, desk, and one thing they said that changed your view]". One point to prepare: you are applying to asset management, wealth management and banking, and your spring week was in sales and trading. A reviewer who sees your CV beside the video will notice. Each "why this division" answer needs one sentence that makes your path look chosen: "[what the spring week showed you that you wanted more or less of]".

### Hypothetical and integrity questions ("What would you do if...")

A **hypothetical** or **situational** question asks what you would do, not what you did. HireVue advises its clients against them, but banks ask them all the same: Goldman's reported 2026 set includes what you would do after sending confidential information to a client by mistake ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/10/hirevue-interview-banking)) ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)). Doing well on these is partly a matter of reading what the situation demands ([Oostrom et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-015-9410-0)). For integrity scenarios the firm is checking that you would not hide a mistake. No published scheme exists for these, so the following is a reasoned structure, not a documented one: name the issue in one sentence ("this is a client-confidentiality breach, so speed and openness matter more than my embarrassment"); give your steps in order (stop and contain it, tell your manager at once, involve compliance, do not try to fix it quietly yourself); give the reason; and, if you have one, add a fifteen-second real example of owning a mistake. Here a numbered list of steps is the right shape.

### Commercial awareness questions

**Commercial awareness** means showing you follow what is happening in markets and business and can say why it matters to the firm. Reported examples: "recent news affecting the division applied to" (JPMorgan), how interest-rate changes affect a bank's revenue (Citi), a recent deal and your view of it (UBS), and "Why BlackRock?" at BlackRock ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)) ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.ca/Interview/BlackRock-Spring-Insight-Program-Interview-Questions-EI_IE9331.0,9_KO10,32.htm)). One finance coaching site gives the shape "What happened → Why it matters → Where it could flow next", in 45 to 60 seconds ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/hirevue-finance-interview-tips-guide)). The marks are in the link and the view: what happened, with one number; the mechanism that makes it matter; what it means for this firm or division or its clients; your view, and what would change your mind. Prepare one macroeconomic story, one firm-specific story for each firm, and one investment idea. Your Urban Outfitters pitch is the investment idea: "[thesis in one sentence], because [driver one] and [driver two]; [one valuation fact]; the main risk is [risk], and I would change my mind if [trigger]". Authoritative evidence here is thin; the best sources are commercial.

### The CV walk-through ("Tell me about yourself")

This is commonly the first question, which is where a hurried reviewer's attention is highest. The research found almost nothing on how it is marked, so this is my synthesis: one sentence on who you are and what you are aiming at; three beats in time order, each with one concrete fact; and a last sentence that lands on this role. For you: the Economics degree with a first-class average; the placement year at the software company (the quota model against a $3.8m regional target); the summer at the asset manager (the portal, the $900m mandate); then "[why that sequence leads to this division]". Debating and the student fund fit in one clause each. This answer, "why this division" and the "why this firm" template are the three you are certain to need, so they are the three worth polishing furthest.

## Script, bullets or improvise: prepare like a script, deliver from keywords

The three positions in the sources are less far apart than they sound. Careers services say outline, do not script: MIT lists "memorizing scripted stories, which can limit your flexibility and sound unnatural" as a mistake, and LSE warns that "over-rehearsed answers can easily sound robotic or wooden" ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)) ([LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)). The head of TED, Chris Anderson, says the opposite can work: many of the best talks "have been memorized word for word", but only if you rehearse past what he calls the "valley of awkwardness", the half-learned stage where "their words will sound recited, or there will be painful moments where they stare into the middle distance". His fallback: "if you don't have time to learn a speech thoroughly and get past that awkward valley, don't try. Go with bullet points... Focus on remembering the transitions from one bullet point to the next" ([Anderson, Harvard Business Review](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20%28Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw%29.pdf)). Candidates who passed describe the middle: one "wrote their responses out in a notebook" and rehearsed until it did not sound memorised; another "drilled their answers until they were 2nd nature"; others used bullet points only ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/how-do-you-pass-hirevues)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/guide-to-hirevues)). In about 25 accounts, **nobody who passed describes reading a script on camera, and nobody describes pure improvisation**. That absence is weak evidence, since people who read a script and passed have little reason to say so.

No experiment compares a candidate's own memorised, read and keyword-led delivery of the same answer. The decision rule that fits the evidence is Anderson's. You face 15 to 25 possible answers with days of notice, so memorising all of them to fluency is not feasible, and the half-memorised state is the one everyone agrees fails. So: **write every answer out in full once**, to get the content and length right; **reduce it to four to six keywords**; and **memorise word for word only the first sentence, the sentence that turns into your actions, and the last sentence**. The three answers you will certainly need (CV walk-through, why this division, why this firm) are worth taking further.

For you this is good news. Speaking from a structure of points without a text is what a debate speech is. If your HireVue answers have been written out and learned, you have been setting aside your strongest skill and using your weakest method.

### Why "just keep practising" has not worked: practice is not training

The one experiment that separated the two put 202 people into four groups (training or not, practice or not) before a five-question mock one-way interview. **Practice had little effect; training was associated with better interview performance** ([Roulin, Pham & Bourdage 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2023.103912)). Only the summary of that study could be read, so treat it as a strong pointer, not proof. It fits everything else. Coaching that worked in real promotion interviews was coaching about content and organisation ([Maurer et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.86.4.709)). Telling candidates the criteria raised scores ([Klehe et al. 2008](https://jlupub.ub.uni-giessen.de/bitstream/jlupub/17122/1/KleheTransparency_2008.pdf)). And in one experiment, explaining what past-behaviour questions expect raised marks ([Tescari et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.70004)).

The difference is this. **Practice** is recording yourself again and hoping it improves. **Training** is recording yourself, marking the result against the scale the marker uses, changing one named thing, and recording again. Repeating a middle-band answer twenty times gives you a fluent middle-band answer. More takes can even make things worse: a HireVue executive said that with unlimited tries "we found it got worse every time", and Wharton's guide says "your tenth version won't necessarily be better than your first or second" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/finance/hirevue-interview-banking)) ([Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)).

### A rehearsal method built on the evidence

Work through each answer in this order. First, fix the content on paper: name the competency, write the answer to the word budget, and mark the text against the scales in the marking section before you say a word aloud. If the written answer is a 4, rehearsing it is wasted effort. Second, cut it to four to six keywords and give each one a purpose, such as "show I spotted it first" or "give the number"; actors learn lines this way, by breaking a script into intentions and focusing "not on the words of the script, but on those words' meaning" ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/to-be-or-or-um-line)). Third, rehearse by recall, not re-reading: turn the notes face down, say the answer aloud, then check what you missed. Testing yourself beats re-reading for memory a week later ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf)). Fourth, compress: give the same answer three times against a shrinking clock, for example 2 minutes 30, then 2 minutes, then 1 minute 30. This "4/3/2" drill reliably builds fluency in language learners; applying it to native speakers is my extrapolation ([Tran & Saito 2021](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10156231/1/OASIS_Summary_Tran_and_Saito_2021.pdf)). Fifth, change the wording of the middle on purpose in each take, so that what you store is the structure and not the sentences. Sixth, record under real conditions, mark the take, change one thing, and stop when your marks stop rising. One forum poster's rule is to stop once answers start getting worse ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/no-bs-tips-for-passing-hirevues)).

Then drill selection, which is its own skill: write questions on cards, shuffle them, and answer five in a row with 30 seconds to prepare and 90 to answer on a stopwatch. One guide suggests 10 to 20 recorded questions in total, in sets of five, before a real interview ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)). The best-evidenced preparation mistake in the first-hand accounts is preparing for hours but never aloud against the clock ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/sa27-hirevues)).

For the camera itself, four specific techniques have a source. Put a face beside the lens: one candidate who "absolutely bombed" a first interview mounted a photo of a friend around the webcam so there was someone to speak to, and Wharton suggests a sticker "as close to the lens as you can" ([Canary Wharfian](https://forum.canarywharfian.co.uk/threads/how-to-pass-hirevue-interviews-i-sat-too-many.529/)) ([Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)). Hide the picture of yourself ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)). Have someone else score your energy, because in a media trainer's drill people rate their own on-camera energy at 8 or 9 out of 10 while observers give 4 or 5; his fix is to speak "10-15 percent louder", which is a trainer's rule of thumb, not a measured finding ([Throughline](https://www.throughlinegroup.com/2014/07/03/how-much-energy-is-appropriate-for-media-interviews/)). And decide your last sentence in advance, because the urge to keep talking into the silence is where marks leak; that follows from the padding finding and has not itself been tested.

## A one-week diagnosis: is it your content, your delivery, or neither?

Nothing in the research can tell you which problem is yours. These tests can. The transcript test is my construction from the published scales, not an established method, and scoring yourself is biased, which is why a second person appears in it.

**Test 1: mark the words alone.** Record three answers under real conditions (30 seconds to prepare, 90 seconds to answer, one take, no warm-up): one motivation question, one "tell me about a time" question and one hypothetical, drawn at random from the reported lists. Turn each recording into text with any dictation tool. Put the video away and mark only the text: give it a mark from 1 to 7 on the Civil Service scale; judge it Novice, Intermediate or Expert on "organised, clear purpose, logical sequence"; and count. Count the share of words spent before your first action; the number of sentences that are "I" plus a verb; the number of specific facts and figures; whether there is a result; whether every part of the question was answered; and, for the motivation answer, whether it survives swapping the firm's name. This mirrors what the AI sees. **If the text scores 4 or below, your problem is content, and no delivery work will fix it.** Check the dictation itself too: if your own tool garbles your words, a scoring transcript will be garbled as well, and the cause is your microphone or room.

**Test 2: watch with the sound off.** Look at where your eyes go, whether you are still or fidgeting, and how the first three seconds look. Sideways glances are the only gaze pattern with evidence of a penalty.

**Test 3: listen with the screen off.** Note how long you take to start, whether you sound as if you are reciting, whether your pace sags and whether your sentence endings trail away. A Student Room poster describes the same two passes: sound off to check engagement, then without looking, to check that the answer flows and contains a clear example and result ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7693263)).

**Test 4: a blind rater.** Give someone the question, the 1-to-7 table and the video. Ask for three things before you tell them anything: a mark, a score from 1 to 10 for energy, and their answer to "which skill was this answer evidence of?". If they cannot name the skill, your answer did not signal it, which is Cause 1. One candidate said that having a listener flag anything that "didn't flow or link back to the skill made a big difference" ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7693263)). A careers adviser, or someone who has reviewed applications, is a better rater than a friend who likes you.

**Test 5: script against keywords, lens against person.** This is the test for a debater. Answer the same question twice on camera: once reciting a version you have written and learned, once from four keywords. Then give the keyword version a third time on a live video call to a person who reacts. Ask your rater which of the three they would hire. If the keyword and live versions win clearly, your delivery problem is the script, and the fix is the method in the previous section. If the live version beats both camera versions, the problem is the empty lens, and the fixes are the face beside the camera, the hidden self-view and the louder voice.

**Test 6: your own rejection history.** For every past HireVue, list the firm, the date you applied, the order of stages, the date you submitted the video, the date of the rejection, whether an online test sat before it and whether you saw an AI consent page. Read the list against the table in the ranking section.

| Result | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Transcripts score 4 or below | Content. Your answers are complete but thin, general, or aimed at the wrong skill | Rebuild the stories against the scales; delivery can wait |
| Transcripts score 5 or more, but the rater marks the video lower | Delivery | Use Tests 2, 3 and 5 to find which part |
| Recited version loses to the keyword version | The script | Stop learning answers word for word |
| Live version beats both camera versions | The lens | Camera techniques; more recorded takes with a face beside the lens |
| Rater cannot name the skill | You are not signalling the criterion | Name the skill in your headline and your last line |
| Everything scores well, and your history shows fast rejections, tests before videos, or late applications | Probably not the video | CV, test preparation, earlier applications, and a records request |

A caution about the automated practice tools your university offers. Tools such as Shortlist.Me measure speech rate and filler words ([Edge Hill University](https://edgehill.ac.uk/?p=41562)). Nothing in the research shows any of them marking the quality of your evidence against a scale. They help with Test 3 only. HireVue's own practice questions give no feedback at all, and they are never seen by anyone but you ([HireVue support](https://hirevuesupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044807611-Practice-your-interview-FAQ)).

## On the day: the prep window, what the firm can see, what is banned, and predicting questions

### Thirty seconds is for choosing, not composing

In an experiment, preparation time improved ratings only through "active response preparation": making notes and structuring the answer ([Basch et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.12341)). Being offered more time did nothing unless it was used ([Roulin et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/1359432X.2022.2156862)). No recruiter, careers service or successful candidate has published a routine for the window, so this one is my synthesis. In the first five seconds, read the question twice and name the skill it is marking. In the next five, pick the story from your list, taking your second choice if you have already used the first in this interview. In the next fifteen, write four keywords: the headline, two action verbs and the result. In the last five, breathe out, look at the lens and say the headline sentence in your head. This works only if the stories already exist. The candidate who "was stumbling to figure out what to say when trying to give a real example" had no list to choose from ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/2nd-hirevue-attempt-for-2021-early-careersand-i-failed-miserably)).

Where a firm gives three minutes, spend the extra time drafting your first and last sentences and cutting to the word budget, not adding content. Start speaking promptly once recording begins. If retakes are offered, the experiments say use them: people who used more re-recordings performed better ([Roulin et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/1359432X.2022.2156862)). The practitioner sources above say later takes get worse. My recommendation: re-record once if the first take missed its result or lost its structure, never to polish, and remember that only the last take survives. JPMorgan allows "one opportunity to re-record each response"; Goldman was reported in 2018 to allow one attempt ([JPMorgan careers](https://careers.jpmorgan.com/us/en/how-we-hire/prepare-for-an-interview)) ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2018/07/video-interview-goldman-sachs)).

### What the platform can see, and what firms prohibit

Start from the rules, because "not detectable" and "allowed" are different things, and the rules differ by firm.

| Firm | What its own rules say |
|---|---|
| Goldman Sachs | Its 2025 email to applicants, as quoted in press coverage: "Goldman Sachs prohibits the use of any external sources, including ChatGPT or Google search engine, during the interview process" ([Yahoo/Fortune](https://www.yahoo.com/news/goldman-sachs-wants-students-stop-210747496.html)) |
| JPMorgan | Its Digital Handbook, as read by a third party, treats the HireVue as the first-round interview and forbids notes, pre-written documents and answer-generating software ([EP Advisory](https://ep-advisory.com/blog/jp-morgan-investment-banking-analyst-application/)) |
| BlackRock | You "may use AI tools as part of their preparation" but not during a recorded assessment; banned behaviour includes "Reading or paraphrasing AI-generated responses in real time" and "Using another open device or browser for suggestions" ([BlackRock](https://careers.blackrock.com/en/ai-interview-guidance)) |
| Barclays | "Any use will result in your interview being stopped and your application withdrawn" ([Barclays](https://search.jobs.barclays/internship-graduate-application)) |
| PwC UK | AI may not be used to "generate, edit, rewrite, summarise, coach or otherwise complete assessment responses"; video transcripts are compared across candidates ([PwC UK](https://pwc.co.uk/careers/early-careers/apply/video-interview.html)) |
| Deloitte UK | You record an integrity statement; using answers from third-party websites breaches it ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/careers/early-careers/early-careers-assessment.html)) |

What HireVue documents being able to flag: your browser losing focus (switching tab or window), pasted text, snapshots showing a different person or someone looking off to the side while talking to another person, unusual location or internet-address signals, identity-document checks, and answers whose wording nearly matches another candidate's ([HireVue cheating and fraud page](https://www.hirevue.com/platform/cheating-and-fraud)) ([HireVue white paper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)). Press coverage adds unusually long response times ([Bloomberg via Daily Herald](https://www.dailyherald.com/20251122/business/wall-street-wants-everyone-using-ai-except-its-job-applicants/)). HireVue says a flag "may warrant additional review" and that "a single behavior should not automatically be treated as proof of fraud" ([HireVue cheating and fraud page](https://www.hirevue.com/platform/cheating-and-fraud)). Employers choose which checks are switched on. What is not documented anywhere: automatic eye tracking, automatic detection of reading, or detection of a second device that never touches the interview browser. Forum claims that the software "can tell when you're reading from a cheat sheet" are hearsay ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/resources/interviews/hirevue-interview-guide)). The research found no first-hand account of anyone flagged or rejected for AI use in a HireVue, and none credibly claiming it got them through.

The practical conclusions are short. Close every other tab and window before you start, because focus loss is the one signal certainly recorded. Do not read anything aloud, because the detector of reading is a person; one forum commenter advises never having notes in front of you in any video interview because "the temptation is too much" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/job-search/hirevues-etiquette-for-ib-how-to-pass-them)). Do not use AI during the interview at any of these firms. Apart from the rules, HireVue's own test found unprompted ChatGPT answers scored as average ([HireVue white paper](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_Do-Cheaters-Prosper_Whitepaper.pdf)). And keep your wording your own, because transcripts are compared: a model answer shared in a group chat is a detectable risk whatever its quality.

On notes the sources conflict. Wharton's guide says "stick a couple post-it notes under the camera lens" ([Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)). LSE says avoid notes, because a viewer who sees you use them "might assume you are underprepared or not committed" ([LSE Careers](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/interview-assessment-centre-psychometric/video-interviews)). The gaze experiment favours Wharton's placement: a glance down was not penalised, a glance sideways was ([Basch & Melchers 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-024-09981-4)). JPMorgan's rules, as reported, forbid notes outright. My recommendation: read each firm's instructions first. Where they forbid notes, use none. Where they are silent, the four keywords you write during the prep window, placed directly below the lens, are defensible; full sentences are not. Keep your story list for the minutes before you press start, not for the recording.

### How far questions can be predicted

HireVue's design intent is that real questions are hidden until the interview and every candidate gets the same time to prepare ([HireVue candidate FAQ](https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq)). So you are not meant to know the questions. You are meant to know the types, and those are stable: "why this firm", the CV walk-through, failure, leading a team, pressure, teamwork, conflict and a deal or stock pitch appear in both the November 2023 and September 2026 reported lists ([eFinancialCareers 2023](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/11/hirevue-questions-barclays-deutsche-bank-ubs)) ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)).

On whether exact questions repeat, the accounts conflict. One UK candidate says Bank of America serves "each candidate a different set of questions, even for the same program"; another estimates that about 30% of questions overlapped between BlackRock and another employer; a BlackRock candidate got the same questions for two roles; coaching sites claim some firms ask "the same four every time" ([Canary Wharfian](https://forum.canarywharfian.co.uk/threads/how-to-pass-hirevue-interviews-i-sat-too-many.529/)) ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/asset-management/blackrock-hirevue-interview-tips-and-overview)) ([Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/firms/standard-chartered/hirevue)). The likeliest explanation is that firms set this up differently and you cannot tell which in advance. The main finance forum warns that paid question lists are often fake ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/guide-to-hirevues)).

My recommendation is to predict the skill, not the wording, from five legitimate sources: the latest seasonal list from eFinancialCareers for that firm; Glassdoor interview reviews for that programme from the last 12 months; a university question bank, such as Cambridge's 170-question banking list ([Cambridge Careers](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/files/example_interview_questions_for_banking_and_financial_markets.pdf)); the firm's published values or strengths; and the assumption that there will be one news question about the division you applied to. After every HireVue, write the questions down within the hour. Your own log is the only source you can fully trust. One thing to know about JPMorgan: a submitted video stays valid for six months and can be used for other similar positions you apply to, so the first JPMorgan recording of a season counts more than once ([JPMorgan careers](https://careers.jpmorgan.com/us/en/how-we-hire/prepare-for-an-interview)).

## Getting feedback, including a subject access request

Asking for feedback costs nothing and usually yields little. JPMorgan's reported stance is "Not allowed to give feedback" ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=6851854)). Where firms do send reports, they are automatic summaries of strengths, not a marker's comments: Deloitte states that its feedback report "is not the assessment outcome", and Schroders sends a report within 24 hours of its online stages ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/careers/early-careers/early-careers-assessment.html)) ([Schroders](https://www.schroders.com/en-gb/uk/individual/about-us/careers/graduates/faqs/)). Scores do exist: one candidate was told by a recruiter that their HireVue scores were "well into the 99th percentile" ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7642408)).

The stronger route is a **subject access request**. Under UK data-protection law you can ask any organisation for a copy of the personal data it holds about you. For a job application, guidance written for recruiters says this covers ratings, comments, AI scores, rankings and flags ([Hirevire](https://hirevire.com/articles/gdpr-for-recruiters)). The request goes to the employer, not to HireVue: HireVue says the hiring company is the **data controller**, the organisation legally responsible for your data ([HireVue 2025 Explainability Statement](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)). Send it to the data protection officer's email address in the firm's privacy policy.

Real examples show what comes back. One applicant who sent requests to many firms received a full entrance exam with performance statistics and "amazing" interview notes from one firm, interview feedback from another "despite otherwise having a policy not to", a test pass mark from Goldman Sachs, and the implicit admission from JPMorgan that his videos had not been watched. Replies took "from one day to about 45 days", proof of identity was rarely requested, and recruiters' names were removed. His verdict: "one's more likely to get useful information than not" ([GitHub write-up](https://github.com/Leader-board/OA-and-Interviews/blob/main/Getting%20the%20feedback%20through%20GDPR.md)). In another published case, a candidate's request returned interview scorecards and internal notes showing a unanimous panel "yes" overruled by a director ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/gdpr-sar-exposed-why-a-candidate-with-a-panel-yes-got-rejected/)).

Keep your expectations realistic. You get records, not reasoning that was never written down; if a reviewer only clicked "No", that is all there is. Only one published account involves HireVue records, and it is anonymous and undated. The research found no evidence either way on whether making a request affects later applications to the same firm. I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. Its greatest value to you is the question it answers: was the video rated at all, and was the rejection set before or after you submitted it? A request can say something like this:

> "I am making a subject access request under the UK GDPR for all personal data you hold relating to my application for [programme, reference number, date]. Please include: my scores on any online assessment; any score, tier, ranking or competency rating produced for my recorded video interview, whether by software or by a person; each evaluator's ratings, recommendation and notes, with names removed if necessary; the dates and times at which my video was viewed or rated and at which my application status changed; and whether any decision on my application was made by solely automated means."

The last line matters because the UK regulator, the Information Commissioner's Office, says "Employers need to tell you if automated decisions are being made about your application", and that you can contest such a decision and "request a real person to take a second look" ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/03/here-s-what-jobseekers-need-to-know-about-automated-recruitment-decisions/)).

| (b) Behavioural questions are a tick-box that cannot win the stage, so effort should go to "why firm / why division" | **Contradicted.** | Each question gets its own star rating and the ratings are averaged ([HireVue help centre](https://hvclassichelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43070569356052-OnDemand-Evaluating-an-Interview-On-a-Computer)); HireVue's AI is built on competency questions; JPMorgan's and Morgan Stanley's reported sets are mostly behavioural ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/hirevue-questions-banking)). One forum line says weight falls "mostly on personality and basic knowledge of the firm" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/do-hirevues-even-matter)); another says weighting "depends on your reviewer" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/how-are-hirevue-videos-looked-atrated)). Motivation matters, and often comes first, but a thin behavioural answer lowers your average like any other. |
| (c) Open every answer with a 15-second preview and build it as three reasons, including behavioural answers | **Half right. Supported for motivation and hypothetical answers; contradicted for stories.** | A stated purpose is in the top band of HireVue's scale, which supports a one-sentence headline. But 15 seconds is a sixth of a 90-second answer, taken from the 60% that should be your actions ([MIT careers](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews)). Recruiters respond positively to a real story of one event ([Bangerter et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-014-9350-0)); "three reasons I am a good leader" is the general-claims pattern that scores low. One consulting-prep site makes the same point: opening a leadership story with three reasons "falls flat" ([StrategyCase](https://strategycase.com/the-pyramid-principle-case-interview/)). No experiment has tested opening length. Numbering three *actions* inside a story is fine. |
| (d) Thank the reviewer at the start | **Cannot be settled; nothing supports it.** | No source in the research recommends or tests it. It spends words from a tight budget, adds nothing a transcript-reading system or a marker counting evidence can credit, and delays the first sentence, which is the one most likely to be heard. Skip it. |
| (e) Film each answer 10 to 20 times | **Not supported.** | The nearest figure in the sources is 10 to 20 recorded *questions in total* ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/)). Practice without training did little in the one experiment ([Roulin, Pham & Bourdage 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2023.103912)); a HireVue executive and Wharton both say later takes get worse. Successful candidates do say practise on camera "many times" ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/guide-to-hirevues)), and no source gives a number that preceded a pass. Mark every take, and stop when marks stop rising. |
| (f) Keep two- or three-word notes under the webcam | **Supported on gaze and by one university guide; forbidden at some firms.** | Wharton recommends it and a downward glance was not penalised in an experiment ([Wharton](https://communicationprogram.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/How-to-Look-and-Sound-Your-Best-in-Video-Interviews.pdf)) ([Basch & Melchers 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-024-09981-4)). LSE advises against notes, forum commenters warn against having them in front of you, and JPMorgan's rules as reported forbid notes ([EP Advisory](https://ep-advisory.com/blog/jp-morgan-investment-banking-analyst-application/)). No account was found of anyone passing or failing because of keyword notes. Follow each firm's rules; where they are silent, keywords written in the prep window are defensible. |

For each HireVue after that, the routine has four stages. **On the day the invitation arrives**, read the firm's rules on notes, AI and attempts; note the deadline and whether an AI consent page is mentioned; collect the firm's values or strengths, its latest reported questions and one piece of news about your division; and adapt your three firm-specific answers. **The day before**, run one five-question timed drill and mark it; then stop. **In the hour before**, plug in, silence every device, close every other tab and window, hide your own picture, put the face beside the lens, and use the platform's practice question only to learn what each button does and to check that your audio is clear. **During**, for each question: name the skill, choose the story, write four keywords, start promptly, give the headline first, reach the result before the last 15 seconds, say your planned last sentence, and stop. **Within the hour after**, write down every question, the timings and the date, and add them to your log. Submit early in the window you are given, but never when tired; one candidate's lost application came from rushing through the controls in a panic ([The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7580291)).

### One-page checklist

**Before any HireVue exists**

- [ ] Rejection history table built; each past rejection sorted into video, test, CV, timing or unknown
- [ ] Baseline transcripts marked 1 to 7; a blind rater's marks collected
- [ ] Verdict written down: content, script, lens, signalling, or not the video
- [ ] Six stories mapped to the ten themes, including one failure and one integrity story
- [ ] Every story has a headline sentence, at least three "I" actions, a result and one lesson
- [ ] Three certain answers written, marked 5 or above on paper and cut to keywords
- [ ] One market story, one investment idea, and integrity steps prepared

**When the invitation arrives**

- [ ] Firm's rules on notes, AI and attempts read; deadline noted
- [ ] Firm's values or strengths list found; each one matched to a story
- [ ] "Why this firm" passes the name-swap test: a named person, a checkable fact, your own link
- [ ] One piece of news about your division, with a view
- [ ] One five-question timed drill, marked

**The hour before**

- [ ] Power connected; phone and notifications off; all other tabs and windows closed
- [ ] Self-view hidden; a face or marker beside the lens; camera at eye level
- [ ] Practice question used to learn the buttons, the number of attempts and how submitting works
- [ ] Practice audio played back: clear and loud, no hum

**Each answer**

- [ ] Skill named before the story is chosen
- [ ] First sentence answers the question
- [ ] Situation and task finished within the first third
- [ ] Actions as "I" sentences, including one thing done beyond what was asked
- [ ] Result stated before the last 15 seconds
- [ ] Planned last sentence said, then stop; 75% to 90% of the limit for a story, 50% to 70% for motivation or markets
- [ ] Nothing read aloud; no second window; one re-record only if the result or structure was missed

**Afterwards**

- [ ] Questions, timings and date written down within the hour
- [ ] Date of any rejection logged against the date of submission
- [ ] Feedback requested; a subject access request sent where the cause is unclear

## Conclusion

The advice that failed you (tidy background, smile, keep practising) treats a one-way video interview as a performance to be polished. The evidence describes something closer to an exam marked by a hurried examiner, or by software trained to copy one, with a mark scheme you were never shown, set among so many candidates that an average script is a rejection. Seen that way, your record changes meaning. A first-class economics student who debates competitively and has built real things in two workplaces has both the material and the speaking ability; the missing piece is most likely the translation of that material into the specific, first-person, organised evidence a marker counts, and possibly a habit of reciting on camera what you would say far better from a handful of points. Both can be tested within a week and fixed within a month.

The honest limit is that no part of this can promise a pass. The published figures say most strong candidates are still cut, some videos are never watched, and the same person can score differently on a different day. What you control is narrower and more useful: knowing which of your rejections were about the video, raising the mark of each answer by a band, and finding out through your own records what each firm actually held against you. If the diagnosis shows your transcripts already score well, that is not a disappointing result. It tells you to stop repairing your videos and spend the effort on earlier applications, online tests and the CV screen, where the decision was really being made.
