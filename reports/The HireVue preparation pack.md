# The HireVue preparation pack: what to know for any firm

The questions change every time. What the firm is trying to find out does not.

This pack shows you what sits underneath every question. Then it gives you the short list of things you must be able to say, for any firm.

- **Every question is checking one of eight things.** Learn the eight and no wording can surprise you.
- **Twenty sentences cover all eight.** Fourteen are about the firm and the job. Six are about you.
- **A fact on its own earns nothing.** You have to add what it means, and why that matters to you.
- **One hour for each firm is enough**, if you know where to look.
- **TP ICAP is filled in as the worked example.**

One thing I cannot promise. No pack can guarantee a pass. The biggest firms take fewer than one in a hundred applicants, and no firm publishes how it marks. What this pack can do is make sure no question finds you with nothing to say.

## Part 1: The eight things every firm wants to know

Behind every question is a person with a worry. Your answer is there to settle it.

### 1. Do you know what we do?

The worry: you applied to fifty firms and cannot tell us apart.

### 2. Do you know what this job is?

The worry: you will leave when you find out what the work is really like.

### 3. Why do you want it?

The worry: you want any job, or you just want the name.

### 4. Can you do it?

The worry: you look good on paper and nowhere else.

### 5. What are you like to work with?

The worry: you will be hard work for the team.

### 6. Can we trust you?

The worry: you will hide a mistake.

### 7. Are you interested in our world?

The worry: you do not follow markets and will not keep up.

### 8. Will you stay?

The worry: we are a stepping stone to somewhere else.

### Different words, same eight things

| The question | What it is checking |
|---|---|
| "What makes us stand out?" | 1 and 8 |
| "Why this firm, specifically?" | 1 and 3 |
| "Why broking and not trading?" | 2 and 3 |
| "What do you think you will be doing day to day?" | 2 |
| "Tell us about yourself." | 3 and 4 |
| "Why should we choose you?" | 4 |
| "Tell me about a time you…" | 5, and sometimes 4 |
| "What would you do if…" | 6 |
| "Tell us about something in the news." | 7 |
| "Where do you see yourself in five years?" | 8 |

> **Try it now.** Before you answer any practice question, say the number out loud. "This is number 2." It takes one second and it tells you what the answer is for.

### What your friends told you is half right

They said you need to know what the firm is. That is true.

But it settles only the first two worries. The other six are about you.

This is why hours of research on the firm have not been enough. You were preparing for two of the eight.

## Part 2: A fact is not an answer

This is the part most people miss. It is the reason well-researched answers still fail.

A fact about the firm proves you can read a website. It does not settle any worry.

To make a fact useful, push it through two more steps.

1. **The fact.** What is true.
2. **Which means.** What follows from it.
3. **Which is why I.** How that connects to something you have done or want.

### An example with TP ICAP

Sam is made up. Sam is a final-year student who spent a summer in the office of a small lettings agency. Nothing about Sam is about you.

> **Weak answer.** "TP ICAP is one of the world's largest interdealer brokers. It has four divisions and offices in 28 countries. It does not do proprietary trading."

Three true facts. No worry settled. Anyone who read the website could say it.

> **Strong answer.** "TP ICAP does not trade for itself. It sits between the buyer and the seller. Which means it only earns if both sides trust it enough to come back. Which is why I want it. At the lettings agency I was the person the landlord and the tenant both rang. Getting two sides to agree was the best part of that job."

What changed:

- One fact, not three.
- "Which means" shows Sam understands the business, and has not just read about it.
- "Which is why I" ties it to Sam's own life. Now it is a reason.

> **Remember.** A fact with no "which means" is trivia. A "which means" with no "which is why I" is a lecture. You need all three steps.

**Your version.** Running the client inbox at the asset manager could be your third step for a client-facing firm. So could chasing documents across fifty market openings. Only you know which one you really enjoyed.

## Part 3: The twenty sentences

For each firm, you should be able to say these twenty things. Each one is a sentence or two.

Do not write them out in full. Know the content. Say it in fresh words each time.

### The kind of work: five sentences

1. **What it is.** "This kind of work is [doing what] for [whom]."
2. **How it earns money.** "It gets paid by [whom] for [what]."
3. **Who the clients are, and what they want.**
4. **How it differs from the two nearest jobs.** "Unlike [neighbour], it…"
5. **One thing changing it right now, and what you think about it.**

These settle worries 1, 2 and 7.

Sentence 4 is the one most people do not have. Without it, you cannot answer "why this and not that".

**The test.** Say all five to someone who does not work in finance. If they can repeat it back, you understand it.

### The firm: five sentences

6. **What the firm is, in one sentence.**
7. **Its main parts, and which part you would be in.**
8. **What makes it different from two rivals you can name.** One fact, and what it means.
9. **Its values, in its own words.** Plus one you can prove from your own life.
10. **One recent thing it did or reported.** With the date, and why it matters.

These settle worries 1 and 8.

**The test for sentence 8.** Swap in a rival's name. If the sentence is still true, it is not a difference. Find another.

**A limit on numbers.** Three numbers for each firm is plenty. More than that and you are reciting.

### The job: four sentences

11. **What the job produces, and for whom.**
12. **Three things you would do on a normal day.**
13. **What is hard about it.**
14. **What makes someone good at it.** Use the words in the job advert.

These settle worries 2 and 4.

Sentence 13 matters more than it looks. Saying what is hard about a job shows you have thought about doing it, not just getting it.

### You: six sentences

15. **Who you are and what you are aiming at.**
16. **Why this kind of work.** Where it started in your own life.
17. **Why this firm.** Sentence 8, then "which is why I".
18. **Why this job.** Sentences 11 and 14, matched to two things you have done.
19. **Two strengths with proof, and one weakness with what you are doing about it.**
20. **Where you want to be in a few years, and why this is the place to start.**

These settle worries 3, 4 and 8.

Sentences 15, 16 and 19 are the same for every firm. You only build them once.

### Three more things that are not sentences

- **Six real things that happened to you.** These settle worry 5. The guide called "When your mind goes blank" shows how to build them.
- **Your steps when something goes wrong.** These settle worry 6. Stop it getting worse. Tell your manager straight away. Fix it openly. Work out how it happened.
- **One news story and your view on it.** This settles worry 7.

## Part 4: TP ICAP, filled in

Everything here comes from TP ICAP's own documents unless I say otherwise. Dates are given so you can check for anything newer.

Do not learn these words. Learn what they say.

### The kind of work

**1. What it is.** Broking of this kind means connecting large buyers and sellers who want to trade with each other. TP ICAP says it connects "institutional buyers and sellers across the world's financial, energy, and commodities markets".

**2. How it earns money.** It earns commission on the trades it arranges. It does not bet its own money. The 2025 annual report says: "No proprietary trading. Brokers act solely as intermediaries."

**3. The clients.** Banks, asset managers, hedge funds, large companies, energy producers and trading houses. They want the other side of their trade found quickly, at a good price.

**4. How it differs.** A trader at a bank owns the position and takes the profit or the loss. A broker holds nothing and earns commission for finding the other side. A salesperson at a bank offers that one bank's prices. A broker sits between many banks and funds. The second comparison is my summary, not the firm's wording.

**5. What is changing.** More trading is moving onto screens. TP ICAP's stated view, from its August 2026 results, is that "scale, relationships and execution expertise become more valuable as markets become more complex". You need your own view on whether you agree.

### The firm

**6. What it is.** One of the world's largest interdealer brokers. Its revenue was £2,353 million in 2025.

A caution. TP ICAP's Global Broking division calls itself "the world's largest inter-dealer broker". A rival, BGC, reported similar revenue for 2025. So say "one of the largest".

**7. Its parts.** Four divisions. Global Broking is the biggest, at 58% of 2025 revenue. The others are Energy and Commodities, Liquidnet, and Parameta Solutions. A broking internship in London sits in Global Broking, at 135 Bishopsgate.

**8. What makes it different.** Its main rivals are BGC and Tradition. Here are two things about TP ICAP that you can explain.

- It sells the price data its own brokers produce. That business is Parameta Solutions, and 97% of its income is subscriptions.
- It has Liquidnet, which serves asset managers and not just banks.

Which means it earns from more than commission alone. That reading is mine, built from the firm's figures.

**9. Its values.** Accountability, Adaptability and Authenticity. The firm calls them "Triple-A". Under Accountability its code lists: "Speak up when things are not right."

Some websites still show an older set of four values. Do not use those.

**10. Something recent.** On 3 November 2026 it reported revenue of £1,912 million for the first nine months of the year, up 9%. Global Broking grew 16% in the third quarter, led by Equities and Rates.

### The job

The London advert has closed and I could not read it. These come from two other live TP ICAP adverts, for Hong Kong and New York. Check them against your own invitation and advert.

**11. What it produces.** Matched trades for clients, and commission for the desk. A trainee supports the brokers so the desk runs smoothly.

**12. A normal day.** Entering trades on time. Sorting out queries about trades. Keeping price boards up to date. As an intern, shadowing brokers while they trade and moving between desks.

**13. What is hard.** The adverts call it "a fast paced energetic environment". The annual report says a large part of a broker's pay depends on the revenue they bring in and on their conduct. So results are visible, and so is behaviour. That reading is mine.

**14. What makes someone good at it.** In the firm's words: "sociable and thrive in building long term relationships with clients". "Confident and engaging communicators who… ask thoughtful questions, listen well, and can build rapport". And someone who "has a viewpoint on the markets and economy".

### You

These six are yours to write. Here are two of Sam's, to show the shape.

> **Say it like this.** Sentence 16, why this kind of work: "It started at the lettings agency. I liked being in the middle of two people who both wanted something, and finding the point where they agreed."

> **Say it like this.** Sentence 18, why this job: "The job needs someone who builds relationships and stays calm when it is busy. I've done two years of Saturdays on a supermarket till, and I've kept the same forty society members paying on time as treasurer."

**Your version.** These are possibilities from your own life.

- For "building relationships": the client inbox, or meetings with brokerage firms.
- For "a view on markets": your stock pitch, or your week on a trading floor.
- For "calm when it is fast": debating from an unseen topic with fifteen minutes to prepare.

## Part 5: Building any answer from the sentences

Once you have the twenty, any question is a matter of picking the right ones.

| The question | Use these sentences |
|---|---|
| "What makes us stand out?" | 8, then 17 |
| "Why this firm?" | 8, then 17 |
| "What do you know about us?" | 6, 7 and 10 |
| "Why this kind of work?" | 1 or 2, then 16 |
| "Why this and not trading?" | 4, then 16 |
| "What does someone in this job do?" | 11 and 12 |
| "Why would you be good at it?" | 14, then 18 and 19 |
| "What will you find hardest?" | 13, then 19 |
| "Tell us about yourself." | 15, 16 and 18 |
| "Why should we choose you?" | 14, then 19 |
| "Which of our values matters most to you?" | 9, with your proof |
| "Where do you see yourself?" | 20 |
| "What is happening in our industry?" | 5, or 10 |

### Two worked answers

Sam is speaking. The numbers in brackets show which sentence is being used. You would not say them.

> **Strong answer.** "What makes TP ICAP stand out?" — "What stands out to me is that you sell the price data your own brokers create (8). Which means every trade a broker arranges is worth something twice. Which is why I want to start here (17). I'd be learning a job where the conversations I have turn into something other firms pay for."

> **Strong answer.** "Why broking and not trading?" — "A trader owns the position and takes the profit or loss. A broker holds nothing and earns by finding the other side (4). I want broking because that middle seat is where I've been happiest (16). At the lettings agency I was the one both sides rang."

Notice that both start with the question's own words. Both use one fact, then what it means, then why it matters to Sam.

## Part 6: Where to find it in one hour

Do not read everything. Go to these places, in this order.

1. **The firm's annual report. The first ten pages only.** Look for "at a glance" and "business model". This gives you sentences 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7. About 20 minutes.
2. **The latest results or trading update.** The first page. This gives you sentence 10. About 5 minutes.
3. **The values page or the code of conduct.** This gives you sentence 9. About 5 minutes.
4. **The job advert.** Read it twice. This gives you sentences 11, 12 and 14. About 10 minutes.
5. **One rival's "about us" page.** This gives you sentences 4 and 8. About 10 minutes.
6. **One news search for the kind of work and this year.** This gives you sentence 5. About 10 minutes.

When you have the fourteen, stop. More reading will not earn more marks.

Then spend the next hour saying them out loud, and pushing each fact through "which means" and "which is why I".
