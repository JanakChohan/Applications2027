1. What company runs this? (asymmetry, removed)

Cappfinity — a British assessment company (founded 2005) whose speciality is strengths-based hiring assessments. Your own invite proves it: the assessment link is on apitake.cappassessments.com and the "Candidate Zone" is hsbc.preparationplus.com — both are Cappfinity-owned domains OFFICIAL[1]. (Prep sites that say "HireVue" are describing HSBC's older setup — wrong for your stage.)

Why this matters for how you play it: Cappfinity does not mark most of this like a right/wrong test. It builds a profile — your strengths, judgement, communication — and compares it against a "success profile" for this specific role CORROBORATED[2]. And the success profile is public: the job posting's five traits — curious, team player, problem-solver, detail-focused, analytical — plus HSBC's four values. Everything you record is evidence for or against those. [Deep dive: hsbc-job-simulation-playbook.pdf]

The shape of the sitting CORROBORATED[3][4]: ~15 items across ~4 "tiles" that unlock in order — a camera/mic warm-up, a news-article scenario, a fictional-company analysis, and a final exercise with video answers plus a typed email. 3–5 recorded video questions, ~2 minutes to prepare, up to ~2 minutes to answer, one take each. Not timed overall (~30 minutes typical, per your invite) — but the time you take is recorded and can inform the decision, so steady pace, no marathon pauses. Calculator, pen and paper on the desk (your invite says so — expect some data reading). [Predicted questions, tier by tier: hsbc-question-predictions.pdf]

2. The monitoring and anti-cheating measures — all of them

Cappfinity markets a "multi-layer defence" against cheating OFFICIAL[5]. Here is each layer, what it watches, and — since you're an honest candidate — the innocent behaviour that could accidentally look bad, with the fix. Nothing here is about defeating detection; it's about not tripping it while playing it straight.

Layer What it does Accidental trigger → the fix
Camera-On recording OFFICIAL[5] Records you via webcam during video answers; a human can review the footage Eyes darting off-screen (reading notes), another face or voice in the room, silhouette lighting → sit alone, light from the front, glance at notes only briefly, speak to the lens
Copy-prevention OFFICIAL[5] Blocks/flags pasting into written answers Drafting your email elsewhere and pasting it → type the email live in the box
Time recording CORROBORATED[2] Logs time per item and overall Implausibly instant answers (looks pre-fed) or hour-long gaps mid-tile → natural pace; complete each tile in one sitting; break between tiles
AI-use flagging (Integrity Dashboard) OFFICIAL[5] Flags response patterns "indicative of AI model use" in written and spoken answers Over-polished, generic, boilerplate text — even if you wrote it → your own voice, specific personal examples, natural imperfection
Consistency / authenticity checks CORROBORATED[2] The same strengths are probed multiple ways (including "what would you do" vs "what would you enjoy"); contradictions are flagged Answering personality items as the "ideal banker" instead of as yourself → honest, consistent answers; they beat a faked perfect profile on this platform
Device/connection logging INFERRED — standard for the platform class Session metadata VPN on, switching devices mid-way → home Wi-Fi, no VPN, one laptop start to finish

What HSBC's rules actually permit and prohibit — your invite's AI line, decoded from HSBC's official policy OFFICIAL[6]: AI for support is explicitly fine — research, rehearsing answers, organising your thoughts (all of which you've done, by the book). What's prohibited: using AI to "generate responses during live or recorded interviews", fabricating experience, or submitting anything you "cannot personally explain, validate, or replicate if asked." Practical translation: everything you say on camera and type in the email must be your own words, produced live. The consequence of crossing the line is withdrawal — and Cappfinity keeps integrity records. You have zero need to go near it: your prepared structures are legitimate and better. [Full section: hsbc-job-simulation-playbook.pdf §6]

3. The camera question — yes, look at the lens

Direct answer: look at the camera lens, not at your own face on screen. That's HSBC's own instruction, verbatim: "Look directly at the camera and avoid looking at yourself on screen, imagine the interviewer who will be reviewing this video is there and you are talking to that person" OFFICIAL[7].

The practical set-up (do all of this in the warm-up tile, which you can redo until it's right OFFICIAL[8]): - Camera at eye level — prop the laptop up; looking down into a camera reads badly. - Light in front of you (window or lamp facing you), never behind. - Face and shoulders in frame, about an arm's length back; tidy, neutral background. - Glancing at the on-screen question or your bullet notes is fine — put them right next to the webcam so your eyeline barely moves. Sustained reading is what looks wrong, not a glance. - Notifications off, phone away, door shut, charged or plugged in. - Dress as your invite suggests — smart shirt; it also switches your brain on. - One myth to ignore: claims of "AI facial-expression scoring" trace to the old HireVue setup, not Cappfinity — don't perform expressions; be warm and communicate clearly CORROBORATED-flagged[3].

4. How your video answers are actually assessed

Who watches: Cappfinity's platform offers "AI-assisted scoring… or human-led evaluation" — but HSBC's governing statement is unambiguous: "the AI-based platform does not make any automated decisions… all decisions will be made by our hiring team" OFFICIAL[6][9]. So assume a human reviewer watches you, possibly with algorithmic assistance flagging and structuring — scoring against structured criteria, not vibes.

What they're weighing, in order CORROBORATED/INFERRED[2][6]: 1. Content — did you actually answer the question, with a real, specific example? (The dominant factor.) 2. Structure — a beginning, middle and end; point first. (This is what "communication skills" in your invite means.) 3. Delivery — clarity, steady pace, minimal filler; calm, warm presence. 4. Values and motivation fit — evidence of HSBC's four values and the posting's five traits. 5. Authenticity and consistency — natural, first-person, consistent with your other answers across the assessment.

What sinks people CORROBORATED[3][4]: rambling with no structure · not answering the actual question · scripted-sounding recitation · generic answers with no personal specifics · running past the timer without landing the point · weak set-up (audio/lighting) making everything harder to credit.

5. How to pass — the strategy, condensed to one page

Everything from the library, compressed INFERRED — full versions in the cited docs:

The two-sentence strategy. Be accurate and steady on anything with data; be genuinely, consistently yourself on everything behavioural; and give every video answer a spine — point first, one real example, land the close by ninety seconds. You cannot game a strengths platform, and you don't need to — your preparation advantage is real.

Your five prepared weapons (all built, all legitimate): 1. The 15-second contents section for the motivation question — a named deal (the Calvin Klein supplier programme), one structural term, one number (62% of revenue from multi-country clients). (hsbc-why-from-first-principles.pdf §B2) 2. The decoder for any unpredicted question: which of the five probes is this → pick one story from your bank → 4-beat skeleton. (hsbc-question-playbook.pdf Part 1) 3. The story bank — your true stories (Head Boy, Oakwood pitch, Pictet market openings, Box cross-team plans), each tagged to a value. (hsbc-question-playbook.pdf; CV mapping in hsbc-insight-day-decoded.pdf) 4. The Tier-1 predictions — intro, why-HSBC/programme, new-team, how-you'd-start-the-research, rankings, the email. If you rehearse only six things, these are the six. (hsbc-question-predictions.pdf Part 3) 5. The email formula — acknowledge → triage with a stated "why first" → recommend from the given data → own the next step. Typed live, tight, professional. (hsbc-question-playbook.pdf Part 6)

Per-item tactics: video — 4 bullets in prep, speak within seconds, done by ~1:40; rankings — best and worst first by the values, then the middle, don't re-litigate; personality sliders — fast and honest; email — ~8–10 minutes, one proofread.

Rehearse structures, never scripts. One take + human review + authenticity checks = the candidate who knows the shape and speaks fresh words wins over the one reciting. Record yourself once from bullets tonight; redo the worst answer once; stop.

6. Your 4-day runbook

  • Today: re-read this sheet + skim your Tier-1 predicted answers. Fill anything missing in the story bank. Register on hsbc.preparationplus.com and run its practice material in the real interface.
  • Tomorrow: dress rehearsal — record intro, why-HSBC, new-team and one research-approach answer on your phone, one take each, from bullets. Ten-minute timed email drill, typed. Fix set-up (camera height, front light, audio).
  • Day 3 — SIT IT. Mid-morning, fresh. Quiet room, home Wi-Fi, laptop only, calculator + pen + paper, notifications off. Do the warm-up properly (re-record until sound and framing are right — allowed [8]). Then trust the structure and let it be imperfect — imperfect is what authentic sounds like.
  • Day 4: buffer. Only needed if something went wrong technically — report any genuine failure to emergingtalent.UK@hsbc.com immediately.
