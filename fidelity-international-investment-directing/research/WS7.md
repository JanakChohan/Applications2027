## 1. FINDINGS

### 1a. Who interviews for Fidelity International early careers roles

Fidelity International publishes its own early careers hiring process on its careers site.
It has four stages: online application and CV, online assessments (cognitive and/or
personality, 20 to 30 minutes), first-round video interview, and an assessment day
combining "technical, commercial, and motivational interviews" plus a case study exercise
[S283][Confirmed]. The page does not name job titles for who sits on each stage, only
"a member of our hiring team" and "different members of the team" [S283][Confirmed].

A second Fidelity-facing source, targetjobs, carries direct quotes from two named Fidelity
early-careers talent acquisition managers, "Dave" and "Brian" (surnames not given), who
describe the process as five stages: application, online strengths assessment, pre-recorded
video interview, a strengths-based interview, then the assessment centre [S284][Confirmed].
Dave states the firm has "deliberately moved away from a process where we rely on
competency-based questions" in favour of hypothetical, strengths-based scenarios
[S284][Confirmed]. This is a recruiter-type source describing a recruiter-type stage; it
confirms recruiters/talent acquisition run the early stages, not investment staff.

Candidate-report sources (Glassdoor aggregated snippets) describe a first round as a
"45-minute interview with a senior employee who asks competency questions based on
Fidelity's values and role requirements," and a final round that can run 10am to 2pm,
described by at least one candidate as "disorganised at times, with times changing quickly
without warning" [S280][Reported — single aggregator, individual candidate accounts not
independently verified]. Glassdoor also aggregates a difficulty score of 3/5 and roughly 50%
positive interview experience sentiment for Fidelity International interns generally
[S280][Reported].

Separately, a general (non-Investment-Directing) source on Fidelity's assessment centre
notes that "for some programmes, such as the fixed asset, equity research and multi asset
streams, you'll have more than one interview and these will be motivational and technical
interviews" [S284][Reported — targetjobs summary, not Fidelity primary text]. This implies
investment-facing streams get practitioner interviewers at the assessment stage, on top of
the recruiter-led early stages. Investment Directing is adjacent to, but not identical to,
those streams; whether the same applies is [Inferred] rather than confirmed for this
specific internship.

Putting this together, the credible, sourced picture is:
- Stage 1 (video interview): recruiter / early-careers talent acquisition team. [Confirmed]
- Stage 2 (assessment day): a mix of "commercial" and "technical" interviews plus group and
  case study exercises, run by unnamed "members of the team," which for investment-adjacent
  streams elsewhere in the firm includes people from the business area. [Reported/Inferred]
- A senior investment person appearing at final stage for an internship (as opposed to a
  graduate scheme or experienced hire) is NOT confirmed by any source found. No source states
  that a CIO-level or Investment Director-level person sits on an intern assessment day.
  Treat any senior-investor-interviewer possibility as [Inferred], low confidence, and prepare
  for the more likely case of recruiter + investment-directing-team practitioner (e.g. an
  Investment Director, Associate Investment Director, or Investment Writer/Specialist).

Confidence flag: nothing found is FIL's own documentation naming interviewer seniority by
role band. The "who interviews" picture above is built by triangulating a Fidelity-authored
process page [S283], a targetjobs piece with named recruiter quotes [S284], and Glassdoor
candidate self-reports [S280], none of which is a perfect match for the exact programme.

### 1b. Firm leadership: who is CEO (critical correction to check before interview)

Anne Richards is NOT the current CEO or President of Fidelity International as of September
2026. Multiple trade press sources report she stepped back from the CEO/leadership role
during 2024: she moved to Vice Chair, and Keith Metters was appointed to lead the business,
with the title "President" (not CEO) [S286][Confirmed, corroborated by two outlets in search
snippets: Investment Week and Citywire, though only Investment Week's page content was
fetchable]. The Investment Week headline explicitly reads "Fidelity International replaces
Anne Richards with platform boss as president," dated around 1 March 2024, and states Keith
Metters "takes the helm" [S286][Confirmed]. Wikipedia's summary (itself a secondary,
crowd-edited source, used here only for structural orientation) lists current leadership as
Abigail Johnson (Chairman), Keith Metters (President), and Anne Richards (Vice Chairman)
[S288][Reported — tertiary source, not primary].

Metters previously served as Fidelity International's global head of platform solutions,
joining the international business about four years before his 2024 appointment; he reports
to Abigail Johnson, who chairs Fidelity International's board and is separately CEO of
Fidelity Investments (FMR LLC) in Boston — the OTHER Fidelity [S286][Confirmed].

IDENTITY UNCERTAINTY FLAG: search snippets (not independently fetched in full) referred to
Anne Richards later becoming a "Senior Adviser" in a further step-back around January 2026;
this could not be confirmed against a primary or fetched secondary source in this
workstream and should be treated as [Reported, unverified] — the safe, fully confirmed

top role under an FIL Chairman who is also the Boston CEO.

### 1d. Published house views and thought leadership

**Analyst Survey 2026 ("A power puzzle").** Fidelity International's flagship annual survey
of its own equity and fixed income analysts. The 2026 edition drew on more than 120
analysts and over 20,000 company management meetings, with data captured through early
March 2026, published 4 April 2026 [S291][Confirmed, fetched in full]. Headline findings:
(1) an AI-driven corporate sentiment rebound, with 81% of analysts covering IT and 65%
covering materials reporting moderate-to-significant confidence for the year ahead; (2) AI
infrastructure spending is broadening beyond the largest technology platforms into
materials, industrials and energy, driven by demand for power and commodities for
datacentres; (3) persistent cost pressure — only 8% of analysts expect inflation to ease in
the next 12 months, about half expect it to hold, 40% expect further increases, concentrated
in materials and industrials; (4) a "K-shaped" divide between AI-exposed sectors growing
strongly and consumer-facing sectors under affordability strain; (5) capital expenditure
expected to keep rising, strongest in utilities, energy and IT, to support AI infrastructure
build-out [S291][Confirmed]. Related coverage also flagged emerging-market resilience amid
geopolitical shocks and continued sensitivity to trade-policy shifts, with close to two-
thirds of analysts still factoring tariff risk into their models [S291, corroborating
snippet][Reported].
