# WORKSTREAM 5: LIVE COMMERCIAL AWARENESS
Prepared 2026-09-16. Source-ID range used: S220-S259.

NOTE ON FIRM IDENTITY: this pack repeatedly found that public search results conflate two
separate companies. **Fidelity International (FIL)**, headquartered in Bermuda with its main
hub in London, is the TARGET firm for this internship. **Fidelity Investments / FMR LLC**
(Boston, USA, chaired by Abigail Johnson) is a SEPARATE company that has been independent of
FIL since a 1980s split, though the two still share the "Fidelity" brand and Keith Metters
(FIL's president) worked at the US firm earlier in his career. Wherever a story below is about
the US business (AUM headlines of "$7.8 trillion", "800/1,000 US job cuts", "ETF share classes"
for US mutual funds, "5-day office mandate"), it is FLAGGED AS US-FIRM, NOT FIL, and is
included only for interview colour on the split, not as FIL news. Do not let the candidate
quote US-Fidelity numbers as if they were Fidelity International's.

# 1. FINDINGS

## (a) Fidelity International specifically

**Leadership.** Anne Richards announced on 20 November 2023 that she would step down as CEO of
Fidelity International after five years, moving to vice chair [S220, Confirmed, FETCHED].
Keith Metters, then FIL's global head of platform solutions (and a Fidelity Investments/US
alumnus), was named president with effect from 1 March 2024, reporting to Fidelity chair
Abigail Johnson [S221, Confirmed, FETCHED]. Both of these moves happened before this pack's
main September 2025-September 2026 window, so they are background, not "recent news" — but
they explain the current top-of-house structure the candidate may be asked about. A search
snippet (not independently fetched, so treated with caution) suggested Anne Richards moved from
vice chair to a "senior adviser" role in January 2026 [S222, Reported-unconfirmed — could not
find a primary or fetched article confirming this exact title change; flagged in GAPS].

**China: planned exit from onshore fund business (live, Aug 2026).** Fidelity International is
reportedly weighing withdrawal from its wholly owned China mutual fund business, three years
after launching a Shanghai subsidiary, citing intense domestic competition, repeated management
changes, and persistent difficulty reaching scale [S223, Reported, FETCHED via Private Banker
International, dated 21 August 2026; also carried by Bloomberg same week, PAYWALLED-NOT-READ].
Reported detail: the Shanghai unit's assets peaked at roughly 6 billion yuan a year after
launch, fell about 25% to roughly 4.5 billion yuan ($670m) by end-June 2026; FIL has invested
about $218m in the unit; internal analysis reportedly concluded the business would need at
least $14 billion in assets to break even, against a 2029 internal target that is now far out
of reach; the unit employs close to 100 people and runs 14 retail fund products; no formal
withdrawal application has been filed with Chinese regulators; FIL's public statement was that
"China remains an important market" and there is "no change to report" on strategy [S223,
Reported, FETCHED]. This sits alongside FIL's own house view (see markets section) that it is
overweight China tech in client portfolios — an interesting tension between distribution
strategy (retreating from onshore retail manufacturing) and investment view (still bullish on
Chinese equities) that a sharp candidate can raise.

**Private markets / LTAF.** FIL received FCA approval for the "Fidelity Diversified Private
Assets LTAF" (Long-Term Asset Fund), giving globally diversified exposure across private
equity, private credit, infrastructure, real estate and natural resources, plus a liquidity
sleeve of public assets [S224, Confirmed — corroborated by IPE headline and Portfolio
Adviser/Professional Pensions headlines, though full text of two of these was PAYWALLED-NOT-READ
beyond the opening paragraph]. Coverage indicates FIL planned to integrate this LTAF into its
default DC (workplace pension) growth-phase strategy, building up over roughly three years to a
target allocation of about 15% in private markets assets, explicitly linked to the wider
industry and government push (see Mansion House Accord below) to get DC savers' money into
private assets for better long-term returns [S224/S225, Reported — the fullest detail came from
search-engine synthesis of a paywalled article, so the 15%/three-year figures should be treated
as Reported not Confirmed until the candidate can read the underlying document]. This is
squarely relevant to Investment Directing: LTAFs are hard to explain to clients (illiquid,
notice periods, valuation uncertainty) and are exactly the kind of "new product to meet
investors' demands" the JD references.

**ETFs.** FIL expanded its "Research Enhanced" active ETF range in Europe with two new
investment-grade corporate bond ETFs — a USD Corporate Bond Research Enhanced UCITS ETF (ticker
FBIU) and a EUR Corporate Bond Research Enhanced UCITS ETF (ticker FBIE) — listed on Xetra,
Borsa Italiana and the London Stock Exchange, reported late January 2026 [S226, Reported —
found via ETF Express headline dated 28 Jan 2026, but the article body could not be fetched
(empty on retrieval), so treat as SNIPPET-sourced]. This continues a build-out of FIL's
semi-active, rules-based "Research Enhanced" ETF franchise in Europe, distinct from the pure
index trackers, and shows FIL is trying to hold ground in Europe's fast-growing active-ETF and
ETF-share-class market rather than cede it to Vanguard/BlackRock/JPMorgan.

**UK Adviser Solutions platform.** Fidelity Adviser Solutions (the UK's IFA-facing platform,
run by FIL entity Financial Administration Services Limited, FASL) launched "Flexible Investment
and Retirement Solutions" around October 2025, letting advisers run multiple investment
strategies within a single pension wrapper alongside ISAs and Investment Accounts [S227,
Reported]. Through 2026 the platform has continued running its regular "Platform Clinic"
adviser-education sessions (covering pension planning workflows, cash management, capital gains
reporting) [S227, Reported]. This is the retail/adviser distribution engine that sits alongside
the institutional Investment Directing function — useful context for how FIL reaches UK retail
money.

**Sustainability labelling.** FIL confirmed plans to adopt the FCA's "Sustainability Focus"
label (under the UK Sustainability Disclosure Requirements, SDR) for three UK-domiciled equity
funds: Fidelity Sustainable UK Equity (c.£125m), Fidelity Sustainable Global Equity (c.£463m)
and Fidelity Sustainable European Equity (c.£284m), combined AUM cited as over £865m [S228,
Confirmed, FETCHED from two independent outlets, Investment Week and ESG Today]. IMPORTANT DATE
CAVEAT: both articles I could fetch are dated 18 November 2024 — this decision therefore
pre-dates the brief's main window and should be labelled as background/implementation-in-progress
rather than "new" 2026 news. Ongoing fund disclosures (e.g. the percentage of assets meeting
SDG/EU Taxonomy criteria, reported as 97.00% for the Global Equity fund and 73.26% for the
European Equity fund as of 28 February 2026) show the labels are now live and being reported
against in 2026 [S229, Reported — figures came from a search synthesis of FIL's own SDR
disclosure PDFs, not independently opened, so treat with some caution pending direct reading].

## (b) Asset management industry

**Consolidation.** M&A in asset and wealth management remained active through 2026. Notable
2026 deals cited include Nuveen's reported $13.5bn approach for Schroders and a Trian/General
Catalyst consortium interest in Janus Henderson [S230, Reported — synthesised from multiple
trade outlets, not independently fetched; treat headline deal figures as indicative, not
verified]. A Morgan Stanley/Oliver Wyman forecast is widely cited predicting over 1,500 M&A
deals among asset managers with $1bn+ AUM through 2029, potentially shrinking the number of
global asset managers by roughly 20% [S230, Reported]. Separately, industry commentary notes the
mean management fee on new (2026-vintage) private-markets megafunds fell to a record low of
about 1.57%, with roughly half of megafunds offering fee discounts, reflecting LP bargaining
power [S231, Reported].

**Active vs passive flows (UK).** This is one of the sharpest, best-evidenced trends found.
Fundscape data reported by Money Marketing (fetched, dated 24 July 2026) showed UK platform net
inflows of £4.6bn in Q2 2026, of which passive funds took £3.2bn (70.4%) versus £1.4bn (29.6%)
for active funds — even though passive funds still hold only 34.3% of the total £565.9bn on-
platform assets against 65.7% for active [S232, Confirmed, FETCHED]. In June 2026 alone, passive
vehicles captured £1.7bn of £1.8bn total net inflows, leaving active funds just £45m [S232,
Confirmed, FETCHED]. Separately, LSEG Lipper data cited in search results put UK year-to-date
flows (to end July 2026) at active -£2.26bn against passive +£20.78bn [S233, Reported —
synthesised from search snippets of Lipper's "Everything Flows" reports, not independently
opened]. This is direct, current evidence of the exact pressure Investment Directing teams
exist to fight: making the case for active management (including FIL's own active and
"Research Enhanced" ranges) to clients who can see passive winning the flow war.

**UK industry scale.** The Investment Association's own newsroom (fetched directly, a primary
source) shows UK investment management assets reached a new peak of £11.1 trillion during 2025,
up 11% year-on-year, reported 6 August 2026 [S234, Confirmed, FETCHED]. Retail funds recorded
their strongest single-month inflow since August 2021 in June 2026 (£3.8bn), continuing eight
consecutive months of positive flows as of the August update, extending to nine consecutive
months by the time of a 3 September 2026 update showing £278m net retail sales in July 2026
[S234, Confirmed, FETCHED]. This is useful macro colour: the UK industry itself is growing and
seeing money come back in, even as the mix tilts further to passive.

**UK pension reform: Mansion House Accord.** Seventeen of the UK's largest workplace defined-
contribution pension providers signed the Mansion House Accord on 13 May 2025 (published 14 May
2025), committing to invest at least 10% of DC default funds in private markets by 2030, with
half of that (5% of the total) earmarked for UK assets [S235, Confirmed, FETCHED from
Macfarlanes]. This is reported as aiming to mobilise roughly £50bn of pension capital into
private equity, private credit, infrastructure, real estate and venture capital over five years
[S235, Reported]. It builds on and significantly expands the 2023 "Mansion House Compact," under
which early signatories had committed only to 5% in unlisted equities with no UK-specific
requirement [S235, Confirmed, FETCHED]. This is the single most important UK policy trend
connecting "pension reform" to "why FIL is launching an LTAF" — the Accord is effectively why
demand exists for products like FIL's Diversified Private Assets LTAF.

**ETF share classes (US structural development, not yet UK/EU).** In the US, the SEC has been
granting exemptive relief through 2026 (filings from multiple fund groups, e.g. Lazard, New York
Life, Dimensional) allowing a single mutual fund to offer both a traditional mutual-fund share
class and an ETF share class of the same portfolio — a structure historically patent-protected
by Vanguard until 2023 [S236, Confirmed, FETCHED-level detail from SEC filing index and
etfdb.com]. Dimensional is reported to have launched the first active ETF share class in March
2026 [S236, Reported]. This is a US regulatory/structural story (SEC, not FCA), included because
"ETF share classes" was explicitly asked for in the brief; it is not yet available in the UK or
EU (UCITS rules do not currently permit this structure), but it is a live topic of debate about
whether UCITS should follow — worth knowing as background even though it is not FIL-specific or
UK-specific.

## (c) Markets in 2026 (verified with dates/levels)

**UK equities and gilts.** The FTSE 100 stood at 10,732.39 on 16 September 2026 (up 0.70% on the
day), having reached an all-time intraday/closing high near 10,991 in July 2026, and is up
16.55% year-to-date as of mid-September [S237, Confirmed, FETCHED, Trading Economics]. UK 10-year
gilt yields were around 5.31%-5.39% in mid-September 2026, having been "near 19-year highs"
earlier in the month; 30-year gilt yields were hovering near 5.87%-6%, a level last seen in 1998
[S237/S238, Confirmed, FETCHED, Trading Economics]. This combination — record-high FTSE 100
alongside multi-decade-high long gilt yields — is an important, slightly counter-intuitive story:
equities have kept climbing even as the "risk-free" discount rate embedded in gilts has risen
sharply, which is exactly the kind of tension an Investment Director needs to be able to explain
to a nervous client.

**UK fiscal event.** Chancellor Rachel Reeves's autumn Budget was delivered around 26 November
2025. Coverage (search-synthesised, not independently fetched, so Reported not Confirmed)
describes markets reacting positively: 30-year gilt yields reportedly fell about 11 basis points
to 5.21% and 10-year yields eased to about 4.43% immediately around the Budget, as the OBR's
fiscal headroom estimate came in larger than expected (reported to have risen from £9.9bn to
£21.7bn) [S239, Reported]. Note the apparent contradiction with the September 2026 gilt levels
above (5.3%+ on 10-year, "19-year highs") — this indicates gilts rallied around the November 2025
Budget but yields climbed substantially again over the following nine to ten months into
September 2026, which is itself a useful, tell-able story about how quickly a "relief rally" in
gilts can be given back.

**Bank of England policy.** The Bank Rate stood at 3.75% as of mid-September 2026, having been
held at that level through consecutive Monetary Policy Committee meetings on 18 June 2026 (held,
7-2 vote) and 30 July 2026 (held, 6-3 vote) [S240, Confirmed, FETCHED, Trading Economics]. A
further MPC decision was scheduled for 17 September 2026 — the day after this research was
compiled — widely expected to hold, with the Bank balancing easing headline inflation against
elevated energy prices linked to Middle East tensions [S240, Confirmed for the historical votes;
Reported/forward-looking for the 17 September expectation]. A candidate interviewing in this
window should know there is a live, imminent rate decision.

**AI capex and equity market concentration.** Market commentary widely cites 2026 AI-related
capital expenditure by the largest US hyperscalers surpassing roughly $700bn for data centre
build-out, within a total AI spending figure cited above $2.5 trillion for 2026 (a reported 44%
increase year-on-year) [S241, Reported — synthesised across multiple commentary/analysis sites,
not a single primary source, so treat the precise figures with caution]. Nvidia is widely
reported to have become the first company to reach a $5 trillion market capitalisation during
this period, with trailing twelve-month profit cited around $99bn and net margins around 53%
[S241, Reported]. Commentary flags genuine bubble-risk debate: AI leaders trade on price-to-
sales multiples above 30x, a level historically associated with sharp corrections, though
bulls note (unlike the dot-com era) today's AI leaders are highly profitable [S241, Reported].
This is central "market view" content an Investment Director must be able to discuss, including
skeptically.

**Japan.** The Nikkei 225 has had a strong 2026, closing above 57,650 in February and above
58,583 shortly after, then rising further to close above 68,000 for the first time in June 2026,
taking the index up roughly 33% year-to-date at that point, with commentary attributing the rally
to AI-boom enthusiasm spreading across Asian equities [S242, Reported — synthesised from CNBC/Al
Jazeera/Trading Economics search snippets, not independently fetched in full]. FIL's own Global

directly-quoted, named FIL house view and one of the strongest pieces of material in this pack].
Quaife also described gold as a valuable hedge against market uncertainty and bond-market risk
[S243, Confirmed, FETCHED].

**China equities.** The CSI 300 index and Hong Kong's Hang Seng both rallied hard through 2026:
search-engine-synthesised reporting (Bloomberg/SCMP headlines, not independently fetched, so

**Gold and the dollar.** Gold hit a reported record of about $5,602.22 per troy ounce on 28

## (d) Regulation affecting fund marketing and reporting (UK/EU)

**UK: Consumer Composite Investments (CCI) regime — the KIID/PRIIPs replacement.** The FCA's
policy statement PS25/20, "Supporting informed decision making: Final rules for Consumer
Composite Investments," was first published 19 December 2024 and last updated 9 December 2025
[S246, Confirmed, FETCHED directly from fca.org.uk, a primary source]. The new CCI regime
replaces the PRIIPs/UCITS KIID disclosure framework. Legislation commences 6 April 2026, with an
optional transition period running until full-regime implementation on 8 June 2027 (an 18-month
window) during which CCI manufacturers, including firms using the Overseas Funds Regime, can
choose either the new consumer-facing "product summary" or their existing disclosure format
[S246, Confirmed, FETCHED]. Under the new rules there is no FCA-mandated fixed template or page
limit (unlike PRIIPs' rigid KID), but firms must still cover costs and charges, a 1-10 risk/
return scale using a ten-year volatility methodology, and past performance shown as a long-term
line graph [S247, Reported — detail here came from search-synthesised secondary commentary
(regulationtomorrow.com, fscom, Freshfields, Hogan Lovells headlines), not independently opened
in full]. This is one of the most interview-relevant regulatory facts in the whole pack:
Investment Directing teams produce "marketing material to support sales activity" and "reporting
materials" — precisely the documents this new regime governs — so 6 April 2026 is a real
compliance deadline the team will be living through in 2026-27.

**EU: SFDR review ("SFDR 2.0").** The European Commission published its proposal to overhaul the
Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation on 20 November 2025, aiming to simplify a framework
widely criticised for producing disclosures that are too long and complex and for having become
a de facto (and confusing) product-labelling system, raising greenwashing and mis-selling risk
[S248, Reported — synthesised from search results across IIGCC, STB, EU Parliament legislative-
train and Bloomberg Professional summaries; the underlying EU Commission proposal itself was not
independently opened]. The Council of the EU adopted its negotiating mandate on 24 June 2026 (I
attempted to fetch the Council's own press release directly and it returned a 403 error, so this
date is Reported via secondary sources, not Confirmed by primary fetch) [S248]. The European
Parliament's ECON committee reportedly reached a broad compromise on the file on 10 September
2026 [S248, Reported], with final agreement expected in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027. Key proposed changes:
a new, simpler product categorisation system with minimum criteria, replacing the current
Article 6/8/9 approach, and removal of some entity-level disclosure requirements [S248,
Reported]. For a global manager like FIL that already runs SFDR Article 8/9 funds and UK SDR-
labelled funds side by side, this is a live "watch this space" item — a new EU categorisation
system landing potentially in 2027 will require a fresh mapping exercise across FIL's European
fund range.

# READING LIST — sources to follow, and a 20-minute daily routine

**Every morning (about 20 minutes), in this order:**
1. FT.com "Companies > Financial Services > Asset Management" front page and the daily Lex column
   (5 minutes) — sets the top one or two industry stories of the day and gives an opinionated,
   numbers-led take that models the kind of commentary Investment Directing produces.
2. Reuters "Funds" section (funds/fund-management feed) (3 minutes) — fast, factual wire coverage
   of flows, M&A, and regulatory headlines, good for picking up a story before it is analysed
   elsewhere.
3. Ignites Europe daily email/headlines (3 minutes) — the most FIL-relevant single trade title:
   covers European fund industry moves, launches, closures and distribution in detail.
4. Citywire Selector and/or Citywire Wealth Manager headlines (3 minutes) — UK/European fund
   selector and adviser-facing coverage, strong on people moves and fund launches/closures.
5. Investment Week and Portfolio Adviser front pages (3 minutes combined) — UK retail/adviser
   trade press, good for FCA rule changes and platform news (e.g. Fidelity Adviser Solutions).
6. Quick scan of Financial News London and Investment & Pensions Europe (IPE) headlines (2 minutes)
   — FN London for City-wide asset management/wealth management people and deal news; IPE for
   pensions and institutional investor angles (directly relevant given DC reform and the Mansion
   House Accord).
7. Check the Investment Association newsroom (theia.org/media/press-releases) once a week rather
   than daily — it publishes primary UK flow and AUM statistics roughly monthly and is a genuine
   primary source, useful to cite directly rather than via a trade-press write-up.
8. Check the FCA newsroom (fca.org.uk/news) at least weekly for policy statements (PS), consultation
   papers (CP) and discussion papers (DP) — get the exact reference number and date, as the brief
   for this whole pack requires.

**Weekly (30-60 minutes, e.g. Sunday evening or Monday morning):**
- Bloomberg's asset-management coverage (via terminal access if available, or free headlines) for
  the highest-quality reporting on M&A and firm-specific stories (Bloomberg was frequently the
  first to report FIL's China story in this pack, even though it was PAYWALLED-NOT-READ here).
- Fidelity International's own published market outlooks and podcasts on fidelityinternational.com
  (the firm's own house view — essential for aligning any interview answer with what the actual
  employer is telling clients) and its "Word on the Street" or equivalent client-facing content
  where available.
- Morningstar UK/Europe research and commentary (global.morningstar.com/en-gb) for independent,
  data-led fund analysis, flow reports and manager research — useful for balancing FIL's own
  house view with an outside perspective.
- A named market-view podcast rotation: the FT's "Money Clinic" or "Behind the Money" for
  accessible market/industry explainers; a mainstream markets podcast (e.g. Bloomberg's "Merryn
  Talks Money" or the FT's own weekly markets podcast) for a broader macro view. (Note: specific
  current episode content was not verified in this research pass; the candidate should confirm
  which shows are currently active before citing episode-specific content.)
- Skim EFAMA (European Fund and Asset Management Association) and the Investment Association's
  research/policy pages for the trade-body view on regulation like SFDR and the CCI regime —
  these bodies typically respond formally to consultations and their responses are a good proxy
  for "what the industry thinks," distinct from what regulators propose.

**Why this list, specifically for Investment Directing:** the team's job (per the brief's JD
quotations) is to turn what all of the above outlets report into "core marketing presentations
and reporting materials," "thought leadership," and "the latest internal views on issues." Reading
FT/Lex, Reuters and Ignites daily builds the external news picture; reading FIL's own outlooks and
the FCA/IA primary sources weekly builds the "internal view" and regulatory grounding the intern
will actually be asked to source and synthesise.
