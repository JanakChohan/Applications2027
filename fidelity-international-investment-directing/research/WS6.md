# WORKSTREAM 6: THE FIDELITY INTERNATIONAL EARLY CAREERS SELECTION PROCESS
Investment Directing Summer Internship 2027, London. Compiled 2026-09-16.

# 1. FINDINGS

**Overall shape of the process, as Fidelity International (FIL) itself describes it.** FIL's own
careers site gives a four-stage pipeline for early careers roles: (1) online application form with
CV, (2) online assessments, (3) a video interview, (4) an assessment centre [S260][Confirmed].
The FAQ page separately summarises this as "an application form and CV, online assessment, an
interview, and then an assessment centre," while noting the exact structure "varies by role" [S262]
[Confirmed]. This matches the shared brief's quotation that assessments cover "technical,
commercial, motivational activities, and case study" — the hiring-process page corroborates this
by naming the assessment centre as containing technical, commercial and motivational interviews
plus a case study exercise [S260][Confirmed].

**Stage 1: Application.** A short online form plus CV upload, explicitly designed by FIL to "minimize
completion time" [S260][Confirmed]. No cover letter is described on FIL's own pages. TargetJobs
describes the whole pipeline as "strengths-based" rather than the older competency-based model,
quoting a recruiter saying the firm moved away from "competency-based questions" toward "core
strengths that motivate someone" [S261][Reported, single outlet but consistent with FIL's own FAQ
language about "strengths-based assessment process" [S262][Confirmed]].

**Stage 2: Online assessments.** FIL's own hiring-process page states assessments are triggered
automatically on submission, must be completed "within 5 days," include "cognitive tests and/or
personality questionnaires" that "vary by role," and together should "take no longer than 20-30
minutes" [S260][Confirmed]. Third-party prep sites broadly agree on content but disagree on
supplier and exact structure, which is a real gap:
- practiceaptitudetests.com describes numerical reasoning (tables/charts/graphs, arithmetic,
  percentages, ratios, currency conversion, "10 pieces of material, 8 questions," no hard time
  limit but a 5-day window) and logical reasoning (shape/pattern sequences), with personality
  content varying by role, and does not name an external test publisher [S264][Reported].
- fintest.io separately describes numerical, logical and verbal reasoning as the three cognitive
  components, strengths-based video interview around "45 minutes," plus assessment-centre group
  exercise and case study — again without confidently naming a publisher [S266][Reported].
- JobTestPrep's own commercial page for the Fidelity assessment names a four-part test: a
  Situational Judgement Test ("Opportunities For You"), Numerical Reasoning ("Showcasing your
  capability"), a Personality Assessment ("Getting to Know You More"), and one further unnamed
  section [S265][Reported].
- One initial web-search synthesis attributed the assessment platform to Cappfinity, and another
  attributed the numerical test specifically to SHL, but neither claim survived a direct fetch of
  a primary or clearly authoritative page — the practiceaptitudetests.com page I fetched directly
  explicitly said no publisher is named [S264]. Treat "Cappfinity" and "SHL" as **unconfirmed,
  low-confidence attributions** picked up in secondary summaries, not verified against a fetched
  page [Reported, contradictory, low confidence]. I found no direct FIL statement naming a named
  provider (SHL, Cappfinity, Arctic Shores, HireVue, Pymetrics, Talogy, Sova) for the Investment
  Directing stream specifically. This is a genuine gap (see Section 5).
- No source found describes a "game-based" assessment (à la Arctic Shores/Pymetrics style
  neuroscience games) for FIL early careers. Absence is not proof of absence, but every source
  that goes into detail describes conventional multiple-choice/SJT format, not gamified format
  [Inferred, moderate confidence, from convergence of S264, S265, S266].

**Stage 3: Video/telephone interview.** FIL's hiring-process page calls this the "first meeting,"
covering interest in the programme, developed skills and potential contribution [S260][Confirmed].
TargetJobs adds granularity: a pre-recorded, one-way video interview of "4-5 strengths and
motivation questions," candidates given 5 days to complete it, filmed "in one sitting" (roughly
30 minutes total for the whole interview, i.e. short per-question prep and response windows), plus
in some streams a separate live/real-time interview (Skype/Zoom or face-to-face) that focuses on
hypothetical scenarios rather than "tell me about a time" narratives [S261][Reported, single
outlet]. fintest.io separately describes a strengths-based interview "typically around 45 minutes"
[S266][Reported] — this may describe a different, later-stage interview rather than the first-round
video screen; the two do not obviously reconcile, which I flag rather than resolve by guessing.
Historic Student Room threads (general Fidelity, not confirmed to be current-cycle or Investment
Directing-specific) describe an older-style 30-minute HR telephone interview covering education,
work experience, "why asset management not an investment bank," and basic valuation concepts
[S272][Anecdote, low confidence, unclear vintage — could predate the strengths-based redesign
TargetJobs describes].

**Stage 4: Assessment centre.** Every source that discusses this stage converges on the same core
menu, which corroborates the brief's summary of FIL's own site: technical interview, commercial
interview, motivational interview, group exercise, and case study/technical assessment, done
face-to-face at FIL's offices or virtually [S260][Confirmed]; S262][Confirmed]. FIL's FAQ describes
the assessment centre as combining "some, if not all" of "face-to-face interview, group exercise,
and case study or technical assessment" [S262][Confirmed] — note the hedge "some, if not all,"
meaning the exact combination is role-dependent and FIL does not commit to a fixed formula for
every stream, including Investment Directing specifically. TargetJobs adds "micro-exercises" as
a possible extra component [S261][Reported]. A separate Indeed/Glassdoor-derived search summary
(not independently fetched, so treat as secondary) describes an assessment centre for internship
roles run over Zoom including "a strengths-based interview, technical task, and two presentations"
[S276][Anecdote, low confidence, no outlet or date attached]. No source I found describes a formal
"stock pitch" as a mandatory Investment Directing assessment-centre component, though a Wall Street
Oasis forum snippet on the Investment Directing team (not independently fetched — WSO blocked the
direct fetch with a 403) mentions that Investment Directing interviews can include "markets
questions as well as stock pitches" where "interviewers wanted to see your investment thesis and
pushed back on it" [S271][Anecdote, low confidence — snippet only, role/stream/cycle unconfirmed].
Treat any stock-pitch expectation as possible but unconfirmed for the internship stage specifically
(WSO threads often blend graduate-scheme and internship experiences).

**Assessment centre group exercise and case study, in FIL's own words.** The hiring-process page
gives real detail: the group exercise is scored on how assessors see "individual contributions to
team discussions," rewarding candidates who "articulate ideas clearly while managing time to reach
conclusions" [S260][Confirmed]. On the case study: "Case studies are an opportunity for us to
understand your thought-processes and problem-solving skills. With a relatively short amount of
time on your side, you will be asked to read, digest and summarise a large amount of information,
before making your recommendations" [S260][Confirmed — this is a genuine verbatim quote, see
Section 3]. FIL explicitly frames the case study as being about process, not a "correct answer":
the assessment values problem-solving approach over matching a predetermined solution [S260]
[Confirmed].

**Competency/strengths framework, in FIL's own words.** The brief's quotation — "We do not seek
pre-existing technical skills or experience in our industry, but we do seek an understanding of
what we do and what you would need to do in the role and team you are applying for" — is
corroborated by a further FIL careers-site search snippet, which adds that FIL wants candidates who
are "engaged, smart and intellectually curious," who show "genuine enthusiasm," have done real
research, and who fit behaviours the firm names as being "brave," "bold," "curious," and
"compassionate," on top of core values of "integrity" and "trust" [S262][Confirmed, FIL's own FAQ
page, directly fetched]. This is a **strengths-based**, not competency-based, framework: TargetJobs
is explicit that FIL moved away from classic "tell me about a time" competency questions toward
questions probing what genuinely energises and motivates a candidate, tested via a ranked-response
"strengths assessment" where the advice given is to answer honestly rather than trying to guess the
"correct" strength profile [S261][Reported].

**Timeline for the 2027 cycle — time-critical, verify before relying on it.** FIL's own FAQ page
says early-careers applications typically open "between August and September," vary by programme
and location, run "on a rolling basis," and that registering interest is the way to be notified
when a given cycle opens [S262][Confirmed — this is FIL's general recurring-cycle language, not a
2027-specific announcement]. Multiple secondary listings (Bright Network's "Register Your Interest
Summer Internships 2027" page, and search-engine synthesis of it) indicate that, as of the search
date, FIL had **not yet opened** 2027 Investment Directing applications and was instead running a
"register your interest" pre-registration funnel, with 2027 programmes to be "confirmed in early
autumn 2026" [S269][Reported, secondary/snippet — Bright Network page itself returned HTTP 403 on
direct fetch, so this is snippet-level, not verbatim-read]. Given today's date (2026-09-16) sits
inside that "early autumn 2026" window, this is highly time-sensitive: the candidate should check
careers.fidelityinternational.com directly, today, rather than relying on any date in this pack.
For context, the prior cycle (2026 internship, i.e. one year earlier) closed applications on
"Sunday 30 November [2025] at 11:55pm GMT" per a search synthesis of Bright Network/Prosple listings
[S278][Reported, secondary/snippet, prior cycle only — not confirmation of the 2027 date]. If the
2027 cycle follows the same rough shape, an application window opening in autumn 2026 and closing
around late November 2026 is a reasonable **inference**, not a fact [Inferred, moderate confidence].
FIL's FAQ also confirms summer interns "begin in June each year," matching the brief's "10 weeks
from June 2027" [S262][Confirmed].

**A live and important trap: do not use Fidelity Investments (US) sources for FIL timelines.**
One source found during research, "Fidelity Internship 2027–2028: Deadlines & How to Apply"
(Extern), gives specific dates — "applications open mid-September 2027, close mid-October 2027" —
sourced to jobs.fidelity.com, describes a hybrid onsite-every-other-week model, "no visa
sponsorship," and HireVue video interviews [S274]. **jobs.fidelity.com is the Fidelity
Investments/FMR (Boston, US) careers domain, not careers.fidelityinternational.com.** Per the
brief's firm-identity rule, this source is about the OTHER company and must not be used for the
FIL Investment Directing internship. I flag it here specifically because it is exactly the kind of
plausible-looking, wrongly-attributed source a candidate doing their own research could easily
mix up — the two firms share a brand and history but have been separate since 1980, and their
recruiting calendars, visa policies and platforms are unrelated [Confirmed as a distinct
entity/source-mismatch, per brief's own firm-identity section].

**Virtual experience / Forage.** FIL runs an "Investment Management Virtual Experience Programme"
on the Forage platform: six self-paced hypothetical tasks covering the fundamentals of investing,
how analysts work across asset classes, portfolio construction and fund distribution, framed as a
"day in the life" of an FIL graduate, roughly 5 hours total, free, available 24/7, with a completion
certificate [S263][Confirmed, FIL's own page, directly fetched]. FIL's own page gives no indication
that completing it affects or feeds into the real assessment/selection pipeline — it reads as an
outreach and self-selection tool, not a scored stage [S263][Confirmed — absence of any linkage
claim on FIL's own page]. It is nonetheless a legitimate way for a low-knowledge candidate to
absorb real vocabulary and structure before applying, and completing it is a source of genuine,
truthfully-claimable material for cover letters/interviews ("I completed FIL's Investment
Management Virtual Experience Programme on Forage").

**Anecdotal colour, clearly flagged.** Older Student Room threads (undated relative to this cycle)
describe a chattier first-round call touching on "why asset management not an investment bank" and
basic valuation concepts [S272][Anecdote, low confidence, vintage uncertain]. A Glassdoor summary
(not independently fetched — 403) reports FIL's overall intern interview process as "three stages:
online application with an online assessment, a quick first round interview, and a final round
interview," describes the final round as "can be disorganised at times with schedule changes," and
gives an average "time to hire" for interns of 49 days versus a 32-day company-wide average
[S270][Anecdote, low confidence, aggregated/dated stats, not FIL-verified, likely blends US and
UK entities as "Fidelity International" and "Fidelity Investments" both have Glassdoor pages under
similar names — treat cautiously]. RateMyPlacement/Higherin aggregate ratings put FIL placements
at roughly 4.6/5 with very high satisfaction on support and responsibility [S275][Anecdote, low
confidence — reviews of the internship experience itself, not the selection process].

# 6. INTERVIEW ANGLES

## What this research licenses the candidate to say

Because FIL's own materials are explicit that they want "an understanding of what we do," not
prior technical skill [S262][Confirmed], the candidate's strongest asset is demonstrated, specific
research (this pack), not fabricated experience. The candidate can truthfully say they have
completed FIL's Investment Management Virtual Experience Programme on Forage if they do so before
applying [S263]. They can also accurately describe the case study as testing "thought process and
problem-solving," not a single correct answer — matching FIL's own framing verbatim [S260] — which
should shape how they approach it: narrate reasoning aloud, state assumptions, reach a clear
recommendation even under time pressure, rather than hunting for "the" answer.

## QUESTION BANK — 45+ questions grouped by type

Format per question: **Q** / *tests* / structure / FIL facts to weave in / traps.

### A. Motivational

1. **Why asset management, not investment banking or private equity?**
   *Tests:* genuine interest vs prestige-chasing. *Structure:* contrast the long-term,
   principal-owner mindset of asset management with deal-based advisory work; anchor in a specific
   thing that drew you in (a fund, a market event, a book). *Weave in:* FIL's own framing that it
   wants "genuine curiosity... about investment" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Trap:* generic "I like
   markets" answers with no specific hook; bashing banking as a way to seem virtuous.

2. **Why Fidelity International specifically, and not BlackRock, Schroders, M&G or abrdn?**
   *Tests:* research depth, not just enthusiasm. *Structure:* one differentiator you can defend in
   follow-up (e.g. its private/employee ownership structure, or its Bermuda-headquartered,
   UK-hubbed footprint distinct from US-listed peers) [S273][Reported, single tertiary source].
   *Trap:* naming a fact you cannot defend if pushed — only use what you can explain in one more
   sentence.

3. **Why Investment Directing, rather than a research or portfolio management track?**
   *Tests:* whether you understand the role is commercial/client-facing, not stock-picking.
   *Structure:* reference the JD's own description that Investment Directing "commercialises"
   FIL's investment capabilities and sits between the investment teams and clients [BRIEF JD,
   Reported]; connect to a personal strength in translating complex material for an audience.
   *Trap:* describing the role as if it were portfolio management — a fast disqualifier, since it
   signals you have not read the JD.

4. **Why not equity research?**
   *Tests:* self-awareness about what energises you (client/communication work vs deep single-name
   analysis). *Structure:* acknowledge you respect research, but you are drawn to the breadth
   across "Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic Investing and Real Estate" that
   Investment Directing covers, rather than depth in one asset class [BRIEF JD, Reported].
   *Trap:* implying research is "boring" — disrespects a function you may work alongside.

5. **Why not sales?**
   *Tests:* understanding the line between pure distribution/sales and Investment Directing's
   "content and expertise" positioning. *Structure:* Investment Directing sits closer to the
   investment process (reporting on portfolios, thought leadership) than to a pure relationship/
   sales role [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Trap:* dismissing sales as "less intellectual" — same
   disrespect trap as Q4.

6. **What do you think an Investment Directing intern actually does day to day?**
   *Tests:* whether you absorbed the JD, not the brand. *Structure:* list JD duties verbatim-in-
   spirit: supporting investment directors/specialists, preparing marketing/reporting materials,
   sourcing data and internal views, assisting with project management [BRIEF JD, Reported].
   *Trap:* describing a portfolio-management or trading day instead.

7. **What attracted you to Fidelity's culture specifically?**
   *Tests:* whether you have engaged with FIL's own stated values. *Structure:* reference FIL's
   named behaviours — "brave, bold, curious, compassionate" and values of "integrity" and "trust"
   [S262][Confirmed] — tied to a concrete example of your own that matches one of them.
   *Trap:* reciting the words without a personal example; sounds coached, which is exactly what
   the strengths-based format is designed to catch [S261].

8. **What would you want to learn in ten weeks that you could not learn anywhere else?**
   *Tests:* ambition and realism. *Structure:* something specific to Investment Directing's remit
   (e.g. how a firm translates investment views into client-facing material) rather than generic
   "I want to learn about finance." *Trap:* an answer that would fit any internship at any firm.

### B. Commercial awareness

9. **What is happening in UK or global markets right now that you find interesting?**
   *Tests:* whether you actually read the news, not just company-specific facts. *Structure:*
   pick one theme, state the fact, then a one-line implication for asset managers or clients.
   *Trap:* reciting a headline with no "so what."

10. **How do asset managers make money, and what pressures are on their margins?**
    *Tests:* basic business-model literacy. *Structure:* management fees on AUM, scale economics,
    fee compression from passive investing, and the interviewer may want you to know FIL runs a
    performance-linked "variable management fee" on some funds — I could not verify the current
    detail of this scheme with a fetched primary source this session and flag it as
    **[Inferred/unverified this session — confirm against FIL's own literature before using]**.
    *Trap:* asserting fee-scheme details you have not verified; if raising it, frame as a question
    rather than a stated fact (see Section 6, Questions to Ask, below).

11. **What is Consumer Duty and why does it matter to a firm like Fidelity?**
    *Tests:* regulatory awareness relevant to marketing/client materials, which is core to
    Investment Directing's job of "generating marketing material to support sales activity"
    [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* explain in one sentence that it is an FCA rule requiring
    firms to evidence fair value and clear communication to retail clients, then connect it to why
    marketing material has to be more rigorously reviewed now. *Trap:* I did not source exact FCA
    policy-statement numbers for Consumer Duty in this workstream (out of scope/time) — do not
    quote a PS number unless another workstream in this pack has verified it; keep the answer
    conceptual [Flag: cross-check regulatory workstream before citing a specific PS number].

12. **What is SDR (Sustainability Disclosure Requirements) and why would it affect what
    Investment Directing produces?** *Tests:* same logic as Q11, applied to sustainable-fund
    labelling and marketing claims. *Structure:* explain that SDR restricts what sustainability
    claims can be made in fund marketing, which is directly relevant to a team that writes client-
    facing material. *Trap:* same as Q11 — do not invent specific rule numbers.

13. **Passive investing keeps taking market share from active managers. Why would a client pay
    Fidelity active fees at all?** *Tests:* whether you can construct an active-management defence
    without being defensive. *Structure:* acknowledge the pressure honestly, then argue for
    specific use-cases (less efficient markets, downside protection, income solutions) rather than
    a blanket defence of active management. *Trap:* dismissing passive investing outright — reads
    as naive.

14. **What is one fund or asset class story from the last year you could talk about for two
    minutes?** *Tests:* whether your commercial awareness is current, not stale. *Structure:*
    pick something you can actually sustain follow-up questions on. *Trap:* picking something you
    only half-understand because it "sounds impressive."

15. **How does a rising/falling interest rate environment affect a multi-asset portfolio?**
    *Tests:* basic macro-to-portfolio linkage, relevant since Investment Directing covers Multi
    Asset. *Structure:* rates up → bond prices down, discount rates on equities rise, rotation
    between asset classes; keep to fundamentals, do not over-claim precision. *Trap:* pretending
    to forecast rates — nobody expects that of an intern, and confidently wrong forecasts read
    badly.

16. **Why might a client choose a private, employee-owned asset manager over a listed one?**
    *Tests:* whether you understand FIL's ownership structure and its supposed selling point
    (long-termism, no quarterly-earnings pressure). *Weave in:* FIL is reported as majority
    employee-owned with the Johnson family holding a large minority stake, a structure it has used
    since 1980 [S273][Reported, single tertiary source, not independently verified against a
    primary FIL filing — say so if pushed]. *Trap:* overstating this as fact rather than "as I
    understand it."

### C. Technical / industry

17. **What is the difference between active and passive management?**
    *Tests:* absolute floor-level literacy. *Structure:* active seeks to beat a benchmark via
    manager judgement; passive tracks it at lower cost. *Trap:* getting this wrong is a hard stop
    for a 2/10-knowledge candidate — this must be memorised cold.

18. **What is the difference between equities, fixed income and multi-asset, in one sentence
    each?** *Tests:* whether you understand the asset classes Investment Directing represents
    [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* equities = ownership stakes/growth and risk; fixed income =
    lending/income and capital preservation; multi-asset = blended allocation across both (and
    others) to manage risk/return trade-offs. *Trap:* conflating fixed income with "safe" in all
    cases — high-yield credit is not low-risk.

19. **What does a fund fact sheet typically contain, and why does it matter for Investment
    Directing?** *Tests:* awareness of the actual output the team produces (reporting on
    portfolios) [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* performance, holdings, risk metrics, manager
    commentary — the connective tissue between the investment team and the client. *Trap:*
    describing a prospectus (a different, longer legal document) instead of a fact sheet.

20. **What is Systematic Investing, and how does it differ from traditional active management?**
    *Tests:* awareness of a named group Investment Directing represents [BRIEF JD, Reported].
    *Structure:* rules-based/quantitative, model-driven decisions rather than discretionary
    manager judgement, often used to harvest specific factors. *Trap:* confusing systematic with
    passive — systematic strategies are still actively designed and managed, just rules-driven.

21. **What does Real Estate as an asset class offer investors that listed equities do not?**
    *Tests:* same as Q20, for the other named group [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* income
    yield, diversification, inflation linkage, illiquidity premium/trade-off. *Trap:* forgetting
    the illiquidity downside — a one-sided answer looks unbalanced.

22. **What is diversification, and why does it matter to a multi-asset investor?**
    *Tests:* floor-level literacy again. *Structure:* combining imperfectly correlated assets to
    reduce portfolio volatility for a given expected return. *Trap:* saying diversification
    "guarantees" lower risk — it reduces, not eliminates.

23. **What is a benchmark, and why would a fund be measured against one?**
    *Tests:* understanding of how performance and fees are justified. *Structure:* benchmark as
    reference index; funds are judged (and often priced) relative to whether they beat it net of
    fees. *Trap:* not mentioning "net of fees" — a common giveaway of shallow understanding.

24. **What role does an Investment Director/Investment Specialist play that a Portfolio Manager
    does not?** *Tests:* the exact distinction the whole internship exists to teach. *Structure:*
    PM makes buy/sell decisions; Investment Director/Specialist translates the "why" behind those
    decisions for clients and prospects, often the client-facing voice of the strategy. *Trap:*
    conflating the two roles — this is the single most important distinction to get right for this
    specific internship.

25. **What is thought leadership, in an asset-management context, and why would a firm produce
    it?** *Tests:* understanding one of the team's named responsibilities, "promoting thought
    leadership on topics" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* published views/research used to
    demonstrate expertise, build trust, and support sales indirectly rather than through a direct
    pitch. *Trap:* describing it as pure marketing with no substance — thought leadership needs
    genuine insight to work.

### D. Competency / behavioural (strengths-flavoured)

26. **Tell me about a time you had to explain something complex to someone with less background
    than you.** *Tests:* the core Investment Directing skill — translation for a lay audience.
    *Structure:* brief context, what made it complex, the specific technique you used to simplify
    (analogy, structure, visual), the outcome. *Trap:* over-explaining the complex thing itself
    instead of your communication method.

27. **Describe a situation where you had to work under time pressure to produce something for
    someone else's use.** *Tests:* fit with fast-turnaround marketing/reporting work. *Structure:*
    STAR, but emphasise prioritisation decisions you made under the constraint. *Trap:* choosing an
    example where the pressure was self-inflicted through poor planning — undermines the story.

28. **What energises you most at work or study — and what drains you?** (classic strengths-based
    framing) *Tests:* genuine self-knowledge, since FIL explicitly moved to a strengths model
    [S261][Reported]. *Structure:* answer honestly with a real example rather than reverse-
    engineering the "ideal" answer. *Trap:* naming a strength you cannot back with a specific
    memory — strengths interviewers probe for authenticity, not a rehearsed script [S261].

29. **Tell me about a time you worked in a team where someone was not pulling their weight.**
    *Tests:* group-exercise-relevant collaboration and conflict-handling. *Structure:* focus on
    what you did, not on blaming the other person. *Trap:* a story that is really just a complaint.

30. **Tell me about a time you changed your mind about something after new information came in.**
    *Tests:* intellectual flexibility — directly relevant to investment views changing with data.
    *Structure:* what you believed, what changed it, how you acted differently as a result.
    *Trap:* an answer where you didn't actually change anything, just "considered" the other side.

31. **Give an example of attention to detail mattering in something you produced.** *Tests:* one
    of the JD's named requirements, "attention to detail" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* a
    concrete error you caught or avoided and the consequence of not catching it. *Trap:* a trivial
    example (spelling) with no real stakes attached.

32. **Tell me about a time your creative thinking solved a problem.** *Tests:* another named
    requirement, "creative thinking" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* the constraint that forced
    creativity, the unconventional idea, the result. *Trap:* confusing "creative" with "artistic" —
    they want problem-solving originality, not artistic flair.

33. **How do you prioritise when you have multiple deadlines at once?** *Tests:* fit with
    "assisting with project management tasks" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* a concrete
    prioritisation framework (urgency/impact) plus a real example. *Trap:* a vague "I just make a
    to-do list" with no real example.

34. **Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?** *Tests:*
    coachability — important in a support/intern role. *Structure:* the feedback, your initial
    reaction (be honest if it stung), what you changed. *Trap:* claiming you have never received
    meaningful criticism.

### E. Case-study style

35. **You are given fund performance data for three strategies over the last year and asked to
    recommend which to feature in a client presentation. What do you do first?** *Tests:* the
    exact process FIL describes for its case study — "read, digest and summarise... before making
    your recommendations" [S260][Confirmed]. *Structure:* clarify the audience/objective first,
    then identify the metrics that matter for that audience (not just raw return), then make a
    clear recommendation with a caveat. *Trap:* diving into numbers before establishing the
    objective — FIL's own language emphasises process over instant answers.

36. **A client wants to know why their multi-asset fund underperformed a simple 60/40 benchmark
    this year. How would you structure your answer to them?** *Tests:* translating a technical
    answer for a client audience, the core Investment Directing skill. *Structure:* acknowledge
    the result plainly, explain the strategy's actual objective (which may not be "beat 60/40" in
    all conditions), give one or two concrete drivers, end with forward context. *Trap:* being
    defensive or over-technical for a client audience.

37. **You have 45 minutes to produce a one-page summary of a market event for the sales team to
    use tomorrow. Walk me through how you would spend that time.** *Tests:* time-boxed
    prioritisation matching real marketing-material production [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:*
    allocate explicit minutes to research, drafting, and review; state what you would cut if time
    ran short. *Trap:* an unrealistic plan with no time allocated to review/proofing (attention to
    detail again).

38. **In a group task, your team has 15 minutes left and has not yet reached a shared
    recommendation. What do you do?** *Tests:* the FIL group-exercise scoring criterion of
    "articulating ideas clearly while managing time to reach conclusions" [S260][Confirmed].
    *Structure:* propose a concrete way to converge (e.g. a quick straw poll, time-boxed final
    round) rather than continuing open debate. *Trap:* staying silent and letting the loudest
    voice decide — assessors are explicitly watching individual contribution [S260].

### F. Market view questions

39. **What is one company or fund you have researched recently, and what would make you change
    your view on it?** *Tests:* genuine engagement plus intellectual honesty (falsifiability).
    *Structure:* name it, state your view briefly, then genuinely name a condition that would
    change your mind. *Trap:* a view with no stated way it could be wrong — reads as unconsidered.

40. **What is a macro theme you think is under-appreciated by markets right now?** *Tests:*
    original thinking beyond consensus headlines. *Structure:* state the theme, why you think it
    is under-priced, and the caveat/risk to your own view. *Trap:* just repeating a well-known
    consensus theme as if it were contrarian.

41. **If you had to invest a hypothetical sum for the next five years, how would you think about
    allocating it, and why?** *Tests:* basic asset-allocation reasoning, not a "right" portfolio.
    *Structure:* time horizon, risk tolerance, diversification logic; keep it simple and defensible
    rather than exotic. *Trap:* an overly complex answer using jargon incorrectly — simple and
    correct beats complex and wrong.

### G. Curveballs

42. **If you were CEO of Fidelity International for a day, what would you change?** *Tests:*
    business judgement and tact (do not trash the firm you want to join). *Structure:* pick one
    considered, moderate idea and justify it, rather than a sweeping criticism. *Trap:* an answer
    that implies you think the firm is badly run.

43. **Sell me this pen (or any object in the room).** *Tests:* structured persuasive communication
    under pressure — directly relevant to "generating marketing material to support sales
    activity" [BRIEF JD, Reported]. *Structure:* ask a clarifying question about the "client's"
    need first, then tailor the pitch to that need rather than reciting generic features.
    *Trap:* listing features with no audience framing.

44. **What is a book, podcast or source that changed how you think about markets or investing?**
    *Tests:* whether your interest is self-directed and real, or purely CV-motivated. *Structure:*
    name something specific and be ready to discuss it in depth. *Trap:* naming something
    generic/famous you have not actually engaged with — easy to expose with one follow-up.

45. **What would you do if you strongly disagreed with a senior colleague's investment view in a
    client-facing document you were preparing?** *Tests:* judgement, tact, and understanding of
    your (junior) place in the process. *Structure:* raise it privately and respectfully first,
    defer to the senior view once raised if they maintain it, but show you would not silently
    publish something you believed to be wrong without asking the question. *Trap:* either
    "I'd just do what I'm told" (no initiative) or "I'd refuse to write it" (no judgement about
    seniority/process).

46. **How would you explain what Fidelity International does to your grandparent in under a
    minute?** *Tests:* the exact "translation" skill of Investment Directing, applied to an extreme
    audience. *Structure:* strip out all jargon; use an analogy (e.g. "we look after other
    people's savings and explain how they're invested"). *Trap:* using any technical term at all —
    that is the whole point of the question.

47. **What is something you are bad at, and what have you done about it?** *Tests:* genuine
    self-awareness, again strengths-model-flavoured [S261]. *Structure:* a real, moderate weakness
    plus a concrete, ongoing action taken — not a disguised strength ("I work too hard").
    *Trap:* the classic disguised-strength answer, which strengths-based interviewers are
    specifically trained to see through [S261][Reported].
