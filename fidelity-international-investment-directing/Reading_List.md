## The twenty-minute morning routine

Do this every weekday from now until the interview. It is worth more than any single deep-dive,
because commercial awareness is a habit an interviewer can hear, not a fact you can recite.

1. **FT markets page and Lex** — 5 minutes. What moved, and why.
2. **Reuters funds coverage** — 5 minutes. Flows, launches, people moves.
3. **One UK trade outlet** — 5 minutes. Investment Week, Portfolio Adviser or Citywire.
4. **One thing in depth** — 5 minutes. Better than skimming a fourth source.

**The discipline that makes it count:** for each story, write one sentence on what it means for a firm
like Fidelity International. Fifteen of those sentences is a commercial-awareness answer bank that no
generic preparation can match.

## Read before the interview, in priority order

### 1. Fidelity International's own Analyst Survey 2026, "A power puzzle"
Published 4 April 2026. Draws on 120+ analysts and 20,000+ company meetings `[S291]`.
This is the single highest-value thing to read. It is the house view, it is quotable, and almost no
other candidate will have read it. Key findings are summarised in Part 10.3 of the pack.

### 2. Tom Stevenson's quarterly outlook (fidelity.co.uk)
He is an **Investment Director** at Fidelity Personal Investing — literally the job you are applying
for, done in public `[S295]`. Read two or three. Notice the structure, the hedging, where the caveats
sit and what he does *not* claim. That is the craft of the role.

### 3. A Fidelity fund factsheet and its quarterly commentary
Pick any fund. Read the factsheet alongside the commentary and ask: what story is this positioning
meant to tell? Where are the honest caveats? What has been left out, and is leaving it out still fair?
This is the best possible preparation for the case study.

### 4. A Fidelity Assessment of Value report
UK fund managers must publish an annual public judgement on whether each fund delivers value
`[S78]` `[S349]`. It is the manager marking its own homework in public. Almost nobody reads these.

### 5. The FCA's COBS 4.2 and COBS 4.6 Handbook pages
- COBS 4.2 — fair, clear and not misleading `[S14]`
- COBS 4.6 — past, simulated past and future performance `[S400]`

Twenty minutes on these two pages gives you the strongest technical material in the whole pack.

## Weekly, and the week of any interview

- **The Investment Association newsroom** — UK flow data, monthly.
- **The FCA newsroom** — policy statements and consultations.
- **Ignites Europe** and **Financial News London** — the industry's internal politics. Often paywalled.
- **Investment & Pensions Europe (IPE)** — institutional and pensions angle.
- **Morningstar research** — the active-versus-passive evidence base. Read it even though it is
  uncomfortable; Part 8 of the pack explains why conceding it strengthens your answer.

## Podcasts and longer-form

- **FidelityConnects** — Fidelity's own podcast and webinar platform, where Tom Stevenson appears `[S295]`.
- **FT Unhedged** — short, sceptical, good for forming views rather than collecting facts.
- **Morningstar's investing podcasts** — useful on fund selection, which is how your future clients think.

## What to check the morning of the interview

1. The **leadership page**. Titles change. Part 0 of the pack flags this as the single highest-risk fact.
2. **Market levels**: FTSE 100, UK 10-year gilt yield, Bank Rate.
3. Any **Fidelity International** news in the last fortnight — checking each story is about the right
   firm, not Fidelity Investments in Boston.
4. Whether the **China reporting** in Part 7.1 has developed. It was unresolved at compile date `[S223]`.
