2.1 What actually happened, with the numbers

Your instinct was right, and the detail is better than the headline. But three things in the popular version of this story are imprecise, and getting them right is most of what will make your answer stand out.

The events

Two Blue Owl funds are at the centre of it. Both are non-traded business development companies: US vehicles that lend to mid-sized private companies, sold largely through wealth and adviser channels, offering quarterly buybacks capped at 5% of value[T2][T57].

Three precision points — get these right and you are ahead of the press coverage
  1. The AI quote belongs to one fund, not both. The phrase "heightened market concerns around AI-related disruption to software companies" appears in exactly one filing: OTIC's 8-K exhibit dated 2 April 2026[T3] Confirmed by direct EDGAR full-text search. The flagship credit fund's own letter gives a different explanation: "AI-related disruption, geopolitical risks stemming from the Iran war, and broader negative sentiment"[T4]. Press coverage routinely blends the two. If you attribute the software-disruption line to the big fund, you have repeated an error that a fixed-income person at Fidelity may well spot.
  2. "$36bn fund" is not the right number. The $36bn widely quoted for OCIC is portfolio fair value, which includes borrowed money. The equity NAV that the 5% cap actually applies to is roughly $19bn to $20bn[T1] Inferred from the disclosed $988m paid at a 5% cap. Safest phrasing: describe it as "Blue Owl's flagship private credit fund" rather than attaching a size you would then have to defend.
  3. Requests received are not demand for cash. Because unmet requests are allocated pro-rata, it is rational to ask for more than you want in order to receive the amount you actually need. Some portion of a 40% request figure is strategic inflation, not 40% of investors wanting outInferred. Noticing this is a genuinely analytical observation and very few applicants will make it.

This is not one firm's problem

The single most important thing for your answer's "why it matters" clause: this is systemic within the product category, not idiosyncratic. Ratings agency data cited in trade coverage found that 10 of 16 tracked non-traded business development companies breached their caps, with average requests of 10.3% in Q2 2026 against 5% caps[T21] Reported. Funds run by Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, BlackRock with HPS, and Golub all exceeded their caps in the first half of 2026[T21].

And it crossed the Atlantic and the asset class. Partners Group gated its Global Value fund, an $8.6bn evergreen private equity vehicle, on 3 June 2026, after requests reached about 9.8% against a 5% cap[T22][T63] Reported. Its shares fell sharply on the news, reported at around 17% intraday[T64].

Be careful with the Partners Group comparison

It is a real event and worth citing, but it is smaller, later, and in a different asset class than the Blue Owl episode: private equity rather than private credit, $8.6bn rather than $19bn-plus, 9.8% requests rather than 40%. If you present the two as equivalent you overstate. If you present Partners Group as evidence the strain spread beyond US private credit, you are on solid ground and making a sharper point.

The precedent that tells you how this ends

Blackstone's real estate vehicle BREIT gated redemptions from November 2022 to September 2023, under a 2% monthly and 5% quarterly cap, honouring more than $15bn over the episode before requests normalised and the gate came off[T23] Confirmed from a primary filing.

That matters for your answer because it supplies the honest conclusion: these episodes have historically resolved without the fund failing. The queue clears. What it costs is not usually investors' capital. It is trust, the growth of the product, and the share price of the manager.

Two further details worth knowing because they show what happens to investors who cannot wait for the queue. In February 2026, Saba Capital and Cox Capital launched unsolicited mini-tender offers for three non-traded Blue Owl funds at discounts of 20% to 35%[T26] Reported. That is the price of immediate liquidity when the official exit is rationed, and it is the clearest possible evidence that the stated NAV and the price of getting out now are two different things. Short interest in the manager's own shares reached about 17.6% of free float[T27].
