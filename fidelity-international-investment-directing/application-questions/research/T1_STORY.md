# 1. FINDINGS

## 1.1 What actually happened at Blue Owl, quarter by quarter

Blue Owl runs several "non-traded" (semi-liquid, evergreen) business development companies (BDCs) for private wealth clients. These funds raise money continuously and offer to buy back ("tender for" or "repurchase") a limited slice of shares each quarter — normally capped at 5% of shares outstanding, sometimes stated as 5% of net assets. The two funds under most pressure in 2026 are:
- **OCIC — Blue Owl Credit Income Corp.**, the flagship diversified direct-lending BDC.
- **OTIC — Blue Owl Technology Income Corp.**, a technology/software-lending BDC.
Both are externally managed by affiliates of Blue Owl Capital Inc. (NYSE: OWL) [Confirmed, T4/T6].

A critical structural point, confirmed directly from Blue Owl's own shareholder letters: the "% of shares outstanding" figure is the **repurchase REQUEST received**, not what is actually paid out. Because the funds cap repurchases at 5% of shares outstanding per quarter, when requests exceed 5%, the fund fulfils only a **pro-rata slice** of each shareholder's request — e.g., if 20% of shares are tendered against a 5% cap, roughly one-quarter (5/20) of each holder's request is honoured, not all of it [Confirmed, T4, T6, T8, T10].

**Q4 2025 (baseline, before the spike):** OTIC received repurchase requests equal to 17% of shares outstanding, and Blue Owl chose to raise its cap and satisfy them **in full** (a "one-time increase to accept 15.4% of shares" is referenced in the Q1 2026 letter for OTIC, and OCIC satisfied 100% of a 5.2% request in the period through 31 Dec 2025) [Reported, T46; Confirmed re: OTIC 15.4% figure, T4].

**Q1 2026 (the spike):**
- **OCIC**: repurchase requests = **21.9%** of shares outstanding as of 31 Dec 2025, equal to **$4.2 billion** in dollar terms. Blue Owl fulfilled the standing 5% cap on a pro-rata basis — about **23%** of each tendering shareholder's request was honoured — worth **$988 million**. Portfolio (assets) at fair value: **$36 billion**. Gross capital inflows of ~$872 million meant net outflows of only $116 million, under 1% of NAV [Confirmed, verbatim primary source, T4/T5].
- **OTIC**: repurchase requests = **40.7%** of shares outstanding (measured as of 8 Jan 2026, the expiry of the tender window), representing **$1.2 billion**. The 5% cap was honoured pro rata at roughly **13–14%** of shares tendered, worth **$179 million**. Portfolio at fair value: **$6.2 billion**; but OTIC's own **net asset value (NAV) was approximately $3 billion** as of 31 Dec 2025 — press summaries that call OTIC a "$6.2bn fund" are describing the leveraged loan book, not shareholders' equity [Confirmed, T6/T7].
- Both funds stressed that ~90% of their roughly 90,000 (OCIC) shareholders did not tender at all, and that redemption activity was concentrated in about 1% of holders [Confirmed, verbatim, T4].

**Q2 2026:**
- **OCIC**: requests eased to **18.8%** of shares outstanding (as of 31 Mar 2026), or **$3.6 billion**, down 14% q/q from $4.2bn. The 5% cap was honoured pro rata at **~27%** of shares tendered, worth roughly **$958m–$980m** (the tender offer itself is stated at $958 million; the actual amount accepted, per the SC TO-I/A, was ~$955m across share classes) [Confirmed, T8/T9].
- **OTIC**: requests eased to **38.1%** of shares outstanding (as of 31 Mar 2026) — Blue Owl's own July letter states this was "down from 40.4% last quarter," a figure that differs slightly from the "40.7%" reported for Q1 because of a change in the measurement base date (Q1 letter measured 40.7% as of the 8 Jan 2026 tender-window expiry; the July letter restates the comparable prior-quarter number as 40.4%). Dollar demand: **$1.1 billion**, down from $1.2bn. The 5% cap was honoured pro rata at **~13%** of shares tendered [Confirmed, T10/T11].
- The two Blue Owl funds' Q2 requests (18.8% and 38.1%) are consistent with the brief's early signal of "~19%" and "~38%" — the earlier "40.7%" for OTIC was the Q1, not Q2, figure. **VERIFIED WITH CORRECTION: it is important not to conflate Q1's 40.7% with Q2's 38.1%.**

**Q3 2026 (in progress as of the report date):** OCIC's third repurchase window for 2026 opened 1 September 2026 and closes 30 September 2026; the repurchase price is not yet set and results have not yet been disclosed as of 18 September 2026 [Confirmed, T-website check, see Gaps]. No verified Q3 numbers for OCIC or OTIC could be found as of the research date; treat any "Q3" percentage figures found in secondary aggregator sites with caution (see GAPS).

**Blue Owl Capital Corporation (OBDC)** — the group's flagship, NYSE-**listed** BDC — is a different animal: because it trades on an exchange, it has no quarterly tender/repurchase mechanism and thus no "redemption request" figure. Its relevant 2026 stress signal is its **market price discount to NAV**, not redemptions (see Section on market reaction) [Inferred from structure, T-general knowledge of listed vs non-traded BDCs, consistent with all filings reviewed].

**Blue Owl Capital Corporation II (OBDC II)**, a smaller **non-traded** sister BDC, took the most dramatic action of all: after Cox Capital Partners and Saba Capital Management served notice (17 Feb 2026) of an unsolicited mini-tender to buy OBDC II shares at a 20–35% discount to stated NAV, Blue Owl's board rejected the offer (13 March 2026) and instead **converted OBDC II's liquidity mechanism from quarterly tender offers to mandatory quarterly return-of-capital distributions** — meaning shareholders can no longer request extra redemptions on demand; they simply receive scheduled capital back. The plan included a 30% return-of-capital distribution by 31 March 2026, with further distributions targeting 50%+ of NAV returned to shareholders across 2026 [Reported, T39/T40/T41].

## 1.2 Blue Owl's own explanation — precise attribution

The phrase most often quoted in press coverage — "heightened market concerns around AI-related disruption to software companies" — **is real and verbatim, but it comes specifically from OTIC's shareholder letter, not from OCIC or from Blue Owl Capital Inc. (the parent) generally.** This was confirmed two ways: (1) direct reading of the primary filing, and (2) an SEC EDGAR full-text search for the exact phrase, which returned **exactly one hit**: Blue Owl Technology Income Corp.'s 8-K Exhibit 99.1, filed 2 April 2026 [Confirmed, primary source, T4; search-verified, T12].

Full sentence, verbatim, from OTIC's April 2026 shareholder letter: *"Heightened market concerns around AI-related disruption to software companies have weighed meaningfully on investor perception of software-related credit exposures, despite continued strong performance across OTIC's portfolio companies."* [Confirmed, T4]

OCIC's parallel letter and FAQ use related but **not identical** language, and pair the AI theme with a second, distinct factor: *"The first quarter of 2026 was marked by heightened volatility and uncertainty, driven by concerns about AI-related disruption, geopolitical risks stemming from the Iran war, and broader negative sentiment across private credit."* [Confirmed, verbatim, T6/T7]. So OCIC's own attribution is broader (AI + Iran-war geopolitics + general sentiment), while OTIC's is narrower and specifically about software credit exposure — which makes sense since OTIC lends almost entirely to software companies (64% of its portfolio is software, per the Q2 letter) [Confirmed, T10].

Blue Owl's consistent counter-narrative across both letters is that this is a **sentiment/perception problem, not a credit-quality problem**: *"We continue to observe a meaningful disconnect between the public dialogue on private credit and the underlying trends in our portfolio."* (Identical sentence appears in both the OCIC and OTIC April 2026 letters.) [Confirmed, verbatim, T4/T6]. Both letters report non-accrual rates around 0.2–0.3% of portfolio fair value and continued high-single-digit revenue/EBITDA growth across borrowers, which is offered as evidence against a "credit stress" reading [Confirmed, T4/T6/T8/T10].

On the Q1 2026 earnings call, Co-CEO Marc Lipschultz reiterated the "narrow and concentrated" framing: redemption activity was concentrated among roughly 1% of investors, ~90% of the base did not tender, and OCIC's portfolio repayments ($3bn) ran about 3x ahead of the quarter's redemptions ($1bn) [Reported, single source but company-sourced transcript, T43]. On the Q2 2026 call, Lipschultz noted Blue Owl raised **>$16.5 billion of equity capital across the firm in H1 2026** even through "the most acute headline noise and elevated redemptions," and management argued market commentary on software had become "more balanced," distinguishing businesses vulnerable to AI disruption from mission-critical platforms actively benefiting from AI [Reported, T44].

## 1.3 Partners Group — separate, real, but smaller and later

Partners Group (Swiss-listed, Baar-headquartered, a pioneer of institutional and — more recently — private-wealth "evergreen" structures) did face a genuine gating event, but it happened **later** (June 2026) and in a **different asset class** (private equity, not private credit) and is **materially smaller in absolute terms** than the Blue Owl story:
- **Global Value SICAV**, an ~**$8.6 billion** evergreen private-equity fund, saw Q2 2026 redemption requests of roughly **9.8%** of NAV against a standing **5%-of-NAV-per-quarter** liquidity limit, and the firm activated that 5% gate [Confirmed, T13/T14].
- A separate Delaware-domiciled U.S. evergreen vehicle saw requests of about **6%** of NAV (just above its 5% threshold); three further evergreen funds (~$9.7bn combined) were expected to see Q2 redemptions of 3.5–5% (i.e., mostly within or just at the normal limit) [Reported, T13].
- Partners Group's own explanation: gating is a **structural safety feature, not a distress signal** — "redemption limitations are an indispensable feature of private markets investing to protect [long-term investors]" [Confirmed quote, T14]. CEO David Layton attributed a large share of redemption pressure to investors "coming from Asia and Australia" [Reported, T13].
- OWL's stock fell ~68% peak-to-trough by April 2026; Partners Group's own stock fell **18.2% intraday on 3 June 2026** (the day the gating news broke) and was down roughly 30% year-to-date at that point [Reported, T14].
- Partners Group guided that the evergreen platform could **shave 1–2 percentage points off net AuM growth in H2 2026** and again in FY2027, but it still expects full-year 2026 gross new client demand of **$26–32 billion** and does not expect to add further liquidity restrictions [Confirmed, company press release, T13].

**Do not overstate this**: there is no evidence found of a "Partners Group redemption crisis" comparable in scale to Blue Owl's; it is one flagship evergreen fund gating at a level (9.8% vs 5% cap) that is actually **milder relative to its cap** than what Blue Owl, Apollo, Ares or Blackstone experienced (all of which saw requests at 2–8x their caps). It is a real, dated, sourced event — but it is best characterised as "the private-equity evergreen segment catching the same contagion, on a smaller scale and about two months later," not as a standalone crisis.

## 1.5 The BREIT precedent (2022–2023) — the closest historical analogue

Blackstone Real Estate Income Trust (BREIT) is the template everyone in 2026 coverage references. From **November 2022** through **September 2023** (roughly), BREIT could not meet full redemption demand and paid out only a prorated share each month, under a **2%-of-NAV-per-month / 5%-of-NAV-per-quarter** dual cap. Over that ~15-month period BREIT nonetheless honoured **about $15 billion+ in cumulative liquidity requests** (one 8-K puts cumulative fulfilment at $11.3 billion for Nov 2022–Sept 2023) [Confirmed, primary filing, T17; Reported figure, T-search]. Early investors who queued from the start eventually got back **~97%** of what they had asked to redeem; investors who only started requesting in Q3 2023 had received only **~73%** by that point, illustrating how a persistent queue disadvantages latecomers [Reported, T17-linked search summary]. BREIT's return was weak through the episode (roughly flat to slightly negative, ~0.5% loss for a major share class in 2023) but it did not collapse, and gating was fully lifted once demand normalised. This is the pattern Blue Owl, Apollo, Ares and Blackstone's own BCRED are now replaying: cap enforced, queue managed pro-rata, waiting for sentiment (not fundamentals) to turn.

## 1.6 Market reaction

- **OWL (Blue Owl Capital Inc.) share price**: all-time high **$25.02** (Jan 2025); fell to an all-time low of **$7.95 on 2 April 2026** (the day the Q1 redemption letters hit); closed at a record-low **$8.44 on 6 April 2026**. As of 18 September 2026 it traded around **$10.00** (day range $9.96–$10.40; 52-week range $7.95–$19.29) [Reported, T32/T33; Confirmed live-quote, T45].
- **Credit rating action**: Moody's affirmed OCIC's **Baa2** senior unsecured rating but revised the outlook from **stable to negative** on/around **8 April 2026**, citing OCIC's significantly higher-than-peer Q1 redemption requests; Moody's said it could return to stable if net flows, shareholder concentration, asset quality, leverage and secured-asset levels all improve [Confirmed via multiple aggregator reports of the Moody's release, T33-T36].
- **Short interest**: as of 30 April 2026, roughly **17.6%** of OWL's free float was sold short against a ~$15.2bn market cap — a heavily shorted large-cap for its sector [Reported, single-source, T-search result]. Bloomberg's "The Brink" newsletter flagged Blue Owl as having "moved into short sellers' crosshairs" on **24 February 2026**, i.e., before the Q1 redemption letters, tied to concerns about its software lending book [PAYWALLED-NOT-READ, T37].
- **Activist/opportunistic tender offers**: Saba Capital Management and Cox Capital Partners served notice on **17 February 2026** of intent to launch unsolicited mini-tenders for shares of three non-traded Blue Owl BDCs (OBDC II, OTIC, OCIC) at **20–35% discounts** to stated NAV — effectively betting that the official NAV marks overstate true value. OBDC II's board unanimously recommended shareholders reject the Cox/Saba offer (13 March 2026) [Confirmed via company press release, T39/T40].
- **Litigation exposure**: Girard Sharp LLP announced (from ~27 May 2026, repeated notices through August 2026) an investigation into potential securities claims on behalf of former shareholders of Blue Owl Capital Corp. III and Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp. II who received stock in 2025 mergers into OBDC and Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp (OTF) respectively — noting OBDC's share price down >21% and OTF's down >30% since those mergers [Reported, T42].
- **Listed BDC sector discounts to NAV**: the median price-to-forward-NAV ratio across listed BDCs fell to roughly **0.74x (a ~26% discount)** in late March 2026, the widest since October 2020; some names were quoted near a 50% discount [Reported, T-search synthesis].
- **Distressed-value buyers**: Cox Capital Partners separately launched tender offers in **July 2026** for shares of non-traded BDCs managed by Apollo, Ares and BlackRock's HPS at **15–30%** discounts to end-May NAV — showing the Blue Owl episode was one entry point in a broader opportunistic play across the whole non-traded BDC sector [Reported, T-search synthesis].

# 6. HOW THIS HELPS THE CANDIDATE ANSWER (analysis angles, not drafted prose)

**For Q1 (200 words, theme/story + relevance to an investor + explain to a non-financial client):**

- The clean explanatory device for a non-financial audience is the **queue-at-a-restaurant / oversubscribed-ticket** analogy: these funds promise you a limited "exit window" each quarter (like a raffle for 5 tickets), and when far more than 5 people want out, everyone gets the same fraction of what they asked for — a **pro-rata cut**, not a full return. That directly operationalises the brief's core instruction to distinguish "requests received" from "redemptions honoured," which is the single most testable technical distinction in this whole story.
- The relevance-to-an-investor angle is about **liquidity mismatch**: these vehicles hold illiquid, multi-year loans but offer investors the feeling of near-instant access (monthly subscriptions, quarterly "liquidity"). That mismatch is fine in calm markets and becomes visible exactly when sentiment turns — which is a textbook lesson about how "semi-liquid" or "evergreen" products actually work, and is highly relevant to Fidelity's own push into private markets for DC pension defaults (FutureWise, Mansion House Accord — per the brief's established facts) where ordinary savers will be exposed to exactly this structure.
- A strong answer could note the **sentiment vs. fundamentals** debate explicitly: Blue Owl's own data (non-accruals ~0.2–0.3%, EBITDA growth ~10%+ across borrowers) argues fundamentals are fine, while the redemption queue, Moody's outlook cut, activist discount-tenders (Saba/Cox at 20–35% off NAV) and heavy short interest argue the market doesn't fully believe that. A good answer takes a position on which side is more persuasive, or explains why it's genuinely hard to tell from outside (private marks are self-reported; discount-to-NAV in the *listed* cousins is the best real-time check the market has).
- The **industry-wide table** (Section 1.4) is useful to show this is a structural feature of an asset class (semi-liquid private credit BDCs) reaching maturity/stress simultaneously, not one firm's failure — this elevates the "story" from a single-company anecdote to a macro theme about an entire market segment (now several hundred billion dollars of AUM) tested for the first time since BREIT in 2022–23.
- The BREIT precedent gives the candidate a **forward-looking, "how does it resolve" narrative arc**: gate → prorate → sentiment stabilises over several quarters → gate lifts. That is a much stronger, more sophisticated answer than "there was a crisis," because it shows understanding of how these structures are designed to self-correct.
- Caution: do not claim Partners Group had "the same crisis" — it is a smaller, later, different-asset-class echo. A precise answer would use it to show the phenomenon is **spreading across strategies (credit → private equity)**, not to inflate the scale.

**For Q2 (150 words, £1m allocation short vs long term):**

- This research is less directly about Q2, but it is a genuinely useful **cautionary data point on liquidity terms** for any answer that proposes an allocation to "private credit" or "alternatives" as part of a long-term (b) bucket: it demonstrates concretely why an individual investor's own liquidity needs must be matched to a fund's actual redemption terms (quarterly, capped, pro-rata) rather than to its marketing description ("semi-liquid," "evergreen," "monthly subscriptions"). A candidate who name-checks private credit/BDCs as part of a long-horizon allocation could strengthen the answer by noting this liquidity-mismatch risk explicitly — showing awareness that "long-term" allocations still need an understanding of exit mechanics, which is precisely the kind of client-education point an Investment Directing role would need to make.
