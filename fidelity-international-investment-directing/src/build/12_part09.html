9.1 The process

The structural fact that changes everything: this is strengths-based, not competency-based

Fidelity's own FAQ refers to a "strengths-based assessment process"[S262] Confirmed, and a recruiter is quoted saying the firm moved away from "competency-based questions" toward "core strengths that motivate someone"[S261] Reported.

What this means in practice. A competency interview asks "tell me about a time you worked in a team" and rewards a polished STAR story. A strengths interview asks "what kind of work do you lose track of time doing?" and is designed to catch rehearsal. The questions come faster, they are more varied, and the interviewer is listening for energy and consistency rather than structure.

So prepare differently. Do not memorise six STAR stories. Instead know, honestly: what you are good at, what drains you, what you would choose to do on a free afternoon, and why this specific seat fits those answers. Then have the facts from this pack available to support whatever you say. Strengths format does not excuse you from knowing things. It just means you cannot recite.

Source uncertain — timings and test provider

No source confirms which test publisher Fidelity uses. "Cappfinity" and "SHL" appeared in secondary summaries but did not survive verification and contradicted each other[S264][S265][S266]. Prepare for numerical and logical reasoning plus a personality or situational questionnaire, which every detailed source agrees on, and do not buy a preparation product marketed at a named provider on the strength of that claim.

2027 cycle dates were not confirmed. The prior cycle reportedly closed 30 November 2025[S267] Reported. Programmes were expected to be confirmed in early autumn 2026, which is now. Check the careers site weekly and apply early — if any part of it is rolling, late applications compete for fewer places.

9.3 The question bank

Forty-seven questions. For each: what is really being tested, how to build the answer, which facts from this pack to use, and the trap. These are scaffolds. If you memorise them the strengths format will expose you.

A · Motivation

1Why asset management rather than investment banking or private equity?

Tests genuine interest against prestige-chasing. Build contrast the ownership mindset — you hold the asset for years and answer to the person whose money it is — with deal-based advisory work. Anchor it in one specific thing: a fund, a market event, a book. Trap criticising banking to seem virtuous. You will be working alongside ex-bankers.

2Why Fidelity International and not Schroders, M&G, abrdn or BlackRock?

Tests research depth. Build one differentiator you can defend under one more question. Use private ownership while two named peers are being acquired[S129][S132]; or owning manufacturing and UK distribution together[S78]; or Asia from 1969[S70]. Trap naming a fact you cannot extend by one sentence.

3Why Investment Directing rather than research or portfolio management?

Tests whether you know the role is commercial. Build the JD says the team exists to "commercialise" the investment capabilities; you are drawn to translation and to breadth across five asset classes rather than depth in one name. Trap describing it as portfolio management. This is the single fastest disqualifier in the process.

4Why not equity research?

Build respect for the function, then the honest difference: research goes deep on one name, this seat goes wide across Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic and Real Estate and is judged on whether a client understood and stayed. Trap implying research is dull.

5Why not sales?

Build Investment Directing sits closer to the investment process — reporting on portfolios, thought leadership — while sales owns the relationship and the number. Trap implying sales is less intellectual. They sit ten feet away.

6What do you think an Investment Directing intern actually does?

Build straight from the duty lines: supporting investment directors, preparing presentation and reporting material, sourcing data and internal views from busy people, project support. Add that you expect most of it to be drafting that someone senior marks up. Trap describing a trading floor.

7What do you want to learn in ten weeks?

Build something only this seat offers: how a firm decides what it can honestly claim about a portfolio, and where the line sits between simplifying and misleading. Trap an answer that would fit any internship anywhere.

8Tell me about Fidelity International.

Build thirty seconds, not three minutes. 1969, independent since 1980, private, Bermuda-registered with London its main hub, five investment groups, and unusually it owns UK distribution too. Then stop and let them direct you. Trap reciting the whole of Part 2.

9What attracted you to the culture?

Use the firm's stated purpose, "working together to build better financial futures", which is in the audited accounts rather than only the marketing[S78], and its own line "as a private company we think generationally"[S76]. Pair with one concrete example of your own. Trap reciting values words with no evidence.

10Where do you want to be in five years?

Build a real path: associate investment director, then investment director covering an asset class[S1]. Naming the actual grade structure shows you looked. Trap "a portfolio manager", which says you want a different job.

B · Commercial awareness

11What is happening in the asset management industry right now?

Build three forces with numbers: passive took 70.4% of UK platform net inflows in Q2 2026[S232]; UK industry assets hit a record £11.1trn in 2025[S234]; consolidation, with two close peers in play[S129][S132]. Trap listing trends with no figures or dates.

12If passive keeps winning, what happens to a firm like this?

Build three responses it is visibly making: active ETFs[S226], private markets via the LTAF[S224], and owning distribution so it earns from assets it does not manage[S78]. Trap denying the premise.

13Tell me about a recent news story relevant to us.

Use the China story of 21 August 2026 and the tension with the house investment view[S223]. Trap a Fidelity Investments story from Boston. Check which firm before you open your mouth.

14What is the Mansion House Accord and why does it matter here?

Build signed 13 May 2025, 17 DC providers, about 90% of active DC savers, 10% of default funds into private markets by 2030 with 5% ringfenced for the UK[S235]. Then the link: it is why the LTAF exists. Trap knowing the name but not the mechanism.

15What regulation most affects this team?

Build COBS 4.2's fair, clear and not misleading test[S14], COBS 4.6 on past performance[S400], Consumer Duty's consumer understanding outcome[S401], and the live Consumer Composite Investments transition to June 2027[S224]. Trap saying "MiFID II" and stopping.

16What do you think of the fee pressure in the industry?

Use the fulcrum fee: 0.65% base flexing 0.45% to 0.85%, launched July 2018 after the FCA market study, pulled from open-ended funds May 2021 for lack of demand[S72]. Trap treating fee pressure as purely bad. It also forces firms to say what they are actually for.

17Where do you think markets go from here?

Build a view with a mechanism and a thing that would change your mind. Trap false confidence. "I don't have a strong directional view, but here's the debate I find most interesting and where I lean" is a perfectly good answer from an intern.

18What is one risk to this firm's business model?

Build pick a real one: platform technology spend depressing group margin to roughly 5%[S77][S78]; senior investment turnover[S78]; or dependence on active flows while passive wins. Trap refusing to name one.

19Who are this firm's competitors?

Build segment it: UK active peers (Schroders, M&G, Aberdeen, Baillie Gifford), global scale players (BlackRock, Amundi, JPMAM), and UK platform competitors, which are a different set entirely. Trap naming Fidelity Investments as a competitor without explaining the 1980 split.

20What do you make of AI in this industry?

Use the split in Part 8.4: production is exposed, judgement and accountability are not, and everything produced is a regulated communication someone must sign. Use BCG's projected 25-35% cost reduction[S192].

C · Technical and industry

21What is the difference between AUM and AUA?

Answer managed versus administered. Use the real numbers: £189.5bn and £104.4bn in the UK at end-2024[S78], earning roughly 35 bps and roughly 20 bps respectively. Almost no candidate can do this.

22What is a basis point, and why does the industry use them?

One hundredth of one per cent. Fees are charged on asset value, so revenue moves with markets without an invoice. Hence a market fall is immediately a revenue event.

23What is performance attribution?

Decomposing return against a benchmark into its causes: stock selection, sector or asset allocation, duration, currency. It is the core analytical content of a client review.

24Explain active share and tracking error.

Active share is how much the portfolio differs from the benchmark by holdings. Tracking error is how much the return has differed, annualised. High fees with low active share is the "closet tracker" problem regulators care about.

25What is an OEIC? A unit trust? An investment trust?

See Part 5.1. The key distinction: investment trusts are closed-ended and listed, so the price can differ from the value of the assets, which is why they can use structures open-ended funds cannot — including the surviving fulcrum fee[S72].

26What is an LTAF and what is hard about it?

A UK structure for illiquid private assets in long-term savings[S224]. Hard because notice periods, valuation uncertainty and explaining illiquidity honestly to default-fund members who did not choose it.

27What is a benchmark and why does the choice matter?

The yardstick. It matters because it defines outperformance, and because under COBS 4.6.2R past performance must be shown against an appropriate reference[S400]. Choosing a flattering benchmark is a conduct issue, not a marketing choice.

28What is GIPS?

Global Investment Performance Standards. Composite construction rules that stop a firm showing only its best account[S18]. Frequently a precondition in institutional RFPs.

29What is an RFP and who reads it?

See Part 1.2. Written by you, read by a consultant or a scheme's investment team, scored against competitors, and effectively contractual in tone.

30What does an investment consultant do, and why should you care?

They gatekeep institutional searches through ratings and buy lists[S11]. A downgrade closes more business than any campaign replaces. The CMA ran a full market investigation into their power[S12].

31What is the difference between DB and DC pensions?

Defined benefit promises an income and the employer carries the risk; defined contribution builds a pot and the member carries it. DB is closing and de-risking; DC is where growth and policy attention are.

32Why can't you just show the best five-year period?

The single best technical answer in this pack. COBS 4.6.2R fixes the window: the preceding five years in complete twelve-month periods, not the most prominent feature, with the reference period and source stated and a prominent warning that past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results[S400].

33What is the anti-greenwashing rule?

In force 31 May 2024. Every sustainability claim must be fair, clear and not misleading. Separately the fund naming and marketing rules applied from 2 December 2024[S19].

34What is Consumer Duty, in one sentence?

A requirement to act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers, published 27 July 2022 as PS22/9 and in force for open products from 31 July 2023, with four outcomes including consumer understanding[S401].

35What is inside information, and why does it matter in this seat?

Price-sensitive non-public information. It matters because you sit near people who hold it. Reference the FCA's 2015 thematic review on market abuse in asset management and its wall-crossing good practice[S17].

D · Strengths and behaviour

36What kind of work makes you lose track of time?

Tests whether your stated motivation matches your actual preferences. Build answer honestly, then connect. If the honest answer is "making something complicated make sense to someone", that is this job. If it is "building models alone", say so and explain why you still want this seat.

37What are you not good at?

Build a real weakness plus the specific mechanism you use to manage it. Trap a disguised strength. In a strengths-based process this is the most obviously rehearsed answer and the easiest to spot.

38Tell me about a time you got something wrong.

Build what happened, who you told and how fast, what you changed. Emphasise the disclosure, not the recovery. In a regulated firm, speed of escalation is the competence being tested.

39Describe explaining something technical to someone who did not have the background.

This is the job. Give a real example, and include what you deliberately left out and why leaving it out was still honest. That last part is the whole skill.

40How do you handle competing deadlines from people more senior than you?

Build surface the collision rather than silently choosing; ask which slips; confirm in writing. See the scenario playbook in Part 11.

41Tell me about working with someone difficult.

Trap making them the villain. The signal they want is whether you can be generous about a colleague while still describing a real problem.

42How do you check your work?

Build an actual method, not "I'm detail-oriented". Reading backwards, checking every figure against source, leaving it and rereading, having a named second pair of eyes. Detail orientation is the core requirement of this seat, so assert it with evidence or not at all.

E · Case study and curveballs

43A fund you cover is down 8% against a benchmark down 2%. Draft the client update.

Build get the facts and the attribution first; establish what the manager actually says; state the shortfall plainly at the top rather than burying it; explain the causes; say what would have to change for the thesis to be wrong. Trap leading with market conditions. Clients read that as evasion, and under Consumer Duty's understanding outcome it is a real risk, not just bad style.

44Should we launch this product? Here is the data.

Build demand evidence, capability to run it, whether it can be explained honestly, target market under MiFID II product governance[S16], and cannibalisation of existing funds. Commit to a recommendation.

45Pitch me a stock.

Not confirmed as a standard component for this stream[S268], but prepare one anyway. Two minutes: what it does, why it is mispriced, what would prove you wrong. Trap a company you cannot explain the business model of.

46Sell me this pen.

What it really tests here: whether you ask what the person needs before pitching. That is the honest-selling instinct the whole regulatory framework is built around.

47What haven't I asked you that I should have?

Build have one prepared. The strongest use is the thing that most differentiates you and has not come up — for most readers of this pack, the regulatory understanding in Part 1.5 or the audited-accounts research in Part 2.2.

9.4 Ten questions to ask

Each of these is only askable by someone who did the research. Pick two or three, and adapt them to what you actually heard in the conversation.

  1. "Investment Directing seems to sit under Distribution rather than under the CIO, from what I could find publicly. Does it feel like a commercial function that has to be technically credible, or a technical function that happens to be commercial? I'd imagine that shapes the day quite a lot."
  2. "How does the team decide when a market move is big enough to justify new client commentary, rather than waiting for the scheduled update?"
  3. "With the Consumer Composite Investments regime replacing PRIIPs by June 2027, how much of the team's capacity is going into rebuilding client documentation, and will that still be live next summer?"
  4. "The 2024 accounts described stepping back from manufacturing non-core private assets while the firm was also launching an LTAF for the workplace default. Is the distinction that you'd rather distribute private assets than build them yourselves?"
  5. "Given the firm runs both the funds and the UK platforms, does Investment Directing ever produce material aimed at end investors on the platform, or is the output always intermediated through advisers and institutions?"
  6. "Would an intern support one of the five asset-class groups for the whole ten weeks, or see how the approach differs across them? I'd be curious whether Real Estate works very differently, given it's illiquid."
  7. "Is there a feedback loop from an actual client win or a retained mandate back to the people who wrote the material, or is that connection hard to trace?"
  8. "The careers site says you don't expect technical skills but do expect an understanding of the role. What's the one piece of context you wish interns arrived with that isn't obvious from the outside?"
  9. "How much does the London team work with the Asia offices day to day, given the firm's history there goes back to Tokyo in 1969?"
  10. "When compliance pushes back on a piece of material, what does that conversation usually look like? I'm curious whether it's a negotiation about wording or a harder line than that."
Don't ask this

Anything answered on the first page of the careers site. Anything about salary or hours at first-round stage. And do not ask "what do you enjoy about working here?" — it is not a bad question, it is just the question everyone asks, and it wastes the one slot where you could have shown your research.
