Part 16

What could not be found

Every research pack has holes. Most hide them. These are this one's, listed so that you never state something confidently that rests on nothing, and so you know which questions are genuinely worth asking on the day.

16.1 The blocking constraint

Fidelity International's corporate website, fidelityinternational.com, returned access-denied responses to every automated request made during this research. Its annual report and its MIFIDPRU Pillar 3 disclosure could not be retrieved at all. The careers subdomain was reachable and was used extensively.

The workaround, and it is a good one: the UK subsidiaries are UK companies and must file audited accounts at Companies House. That is where the hard financial numbers in Part 2 come from. But it means this pack has excellent UK data and weak global data, and you should know which is which when you speak.

16.3 Weaker evidence you should treat carefully

  • The active underperformance statistics. The SPIVA and Morningstar figures in Parts 3 and 8 come from trade-press summaries, not the primary reports[S307][S308]. They are widely reported and directionally reliable, but check the current edition before quoting an exact percentage.
  • Two of the four industry views. The BCG and PwC material was read via search summaries because the primary reports were access-blocked[S192][S191]. Attribute them as "BCG has argued" rather than quoting them as verbatim.
  • The GIPS composite mechanism. The primary standards document could not be read; the description in this pack rests on the CFA Institute's educational material[S18].
  • Market levels in Part 7. Compiled on 16 September 2026 and stale by the time you read this.
  • No anti-greenwashing enforcement case exists yet to point to. The rule is new. Do not invent an example.

16.4 The five things to check yourself before any interview

  1. The leadership page. Titles change, and Part 0 flags this as the highest-risk fact in the pack.
  2. Whether the posting has been amended since 16 September 2026, particularly the deadline.
  3. Market levels that morning: FTSE, gilt yields, Bank Rate.
  4. Any Fidelity International news in the preceding fortnight, checking each story is about the right firm.
  5. Whether the China reporting in Part 7.1 has developed, since it was unresolved at compile date.
A closing thought on honesty

The instinct this section is training is the same one the job itself requires. An Investment Director who says "I don't know, I'll check, here's what I can tell you now" is trusted. One who fills gaps with confident approximations is eventually found out, usually by a client with a spreadsheet. The habit that makes you good in the interview is the same habit that makes you good in the seat. That is not a coincidence, and it is worth saying out loud if you get the chance.
