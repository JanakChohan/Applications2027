11.5 The scenario ladder

Twenty-seven scenarios, three for each duty line in the job description, easy to hard. Work down a ladder rather than across, because the hard rung is always the easy rung with the stakes raised, and the answer should not change.

How to use these

Do not memorise the words. For each card, cover the answer and try it yourself using the four-step protocol in Figure 19. Then compare. The value is in noticing which principle the answer reaches for, not in the phrasing.

Duty line 1 · "Representing the Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic Investing and Real Estate groups to prospective and existing clients"

A client asks a question you cannot answer in the meetingEasy
The setup
You are sitting in on a client review. The client asks about the fund's exposure to a sector and nobody in the room has the number.
The trap
Guessing, or producing an approximate number to look useful.
What to say
"I don't want to give you a number from memory on something you may act on. I'll come back to you today with the exact figure as at a stated date." Then actually do it the same day.
Why it works
It shows you understand that a number given verbally in a client meeting is still a communication with a client. Following up the same day converts a non-answer into evidence of reliability.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "They're pushing — they want it now." Hold. "I'd rather be an hour late and right. If it's genuinely urgent I'd step out and call the desk."
The client asks what the manager is buying right nowMedium
The setup
A large prospective client asks, reasonably, whether the manager is adding to a position they have been discussing.
The trap
Answering. It feels helpful and harmless, and it is neither.
What to say
"I can talk about how the manager thinks about that position and the published positioning as at the last reporting date. What I can't do is talk about live trading intentions, because that would be selective disclosure and it's not something we do for any client."
Why it works
It names the boundary and, crucially, frames it as protecting them too. The discipline is the same one the FCA set out in its 2015 thematic review on market abuse in asset management.[S17]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Our other manager tells us." Answer: "Then I'd be a bit concerned on their behalf. I'm happy to be the boring one on this."
You are asked to present a strategy you have doubts aboutHard
The setup
You are covering a fund whose recent performance you privately find hard to defend, in front of a consultant who has done their homework.
The trap
Either overselling it or visibly distancing yourself from your own firm's product.
What to say
"The last two years have been below benchmark, and here's the attribution for why. The manager's case is that the thesis is intact and here's the specific evidence for that. Here's what would tell us the thesis is actually broken."
Why it works
Naming a falsification condition is what separates an honest investment case from marketing. It is also what a consultant is actually listening for.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Do you believe it?" Answer: "My job is to represent the manager's view accurately, and I've pushed them on exactly the question you're asking. What persuaded me was..."

Duty line 2 · "Reporting on portfolios"

The performance number does not tie outEasy
The setup
The figure in your draft factsheet does not match the performance team's official number. It is close, but not the same.
The trap
Assuming yours is a rounding difference and shipping it.
What to say
"I'd stop and reconcile before anything goes further. Any difference has a cause, and I'd want to know whether it's share class, currency, or period before I decide it's immaterial."
Why it works
Everything traceable back to a GIPS-governed composite has to be exact.[S18] "Close enough" is not a category that exists here.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "It's 0.01%." Answer: "Then it'll take five minutes to explain, and I'll know why. If I can't explain a difference I don't know what else it's affecting."
A factsheet error is found after distributionMedium
The setup
A factsheet went out yesterday with a wrong figure. You spotted it. Nobody else has.
The trap
Waiting to see whether anyone notices, or fixing it silently in the next version.
What to say
"I'd tell my manager and compliance immediately, today, before working out the fix. Then: how many clients received it, is it material, does it need a correction notice. I wouldn't quietly correct the next edition."
Why it works
Speed of escalation is the competence being tested. Conduct Rule 3 is about being open with regulators; the internal version is the same instinct.[S350]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You'll look incompetent." Answer: "I'll look worse in three months when a client finds it. And the person who finds their own mistake is more trustworthy than the person who has never reported one."
You are asked to start the track record at a flattering dateHard
The setup
Someone senior suggests the performance chart would look much better starting from the market low rather than the standard period.
The trap
Treating this as a presentational preference to be negotiated.
What to say
"I don't think we can. COBS 4.6.2R requires past performance to cover the preceding five years in complete twelve-month periods, and it can't be the most prominent feature. If we want to show the recovery we'd have to show it alongside the required periods, not instead of them."
Why it works
This is the single strongest technical answer available in the whole playbook, because it is precise, correct, verifiable, and answers a request almost every candidate would simply comply with.[S400]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "It's for an institutional client, so retail rules don't apply." Answer: "That may change which rule applies, and I'd check rather than assume. But the fair, clear and not misleading test in COBS 4.2 doesn't go away."

Duty line 3 · "Promoting thought leadership on topics"

You are asked to write a market commentary in a dayEasy
The setup
A view piece is wanted on a market move, by tomorrow morning.
The trap
Writing something confident and generic that could have been written a year ago.
What to say
"I'd get the house view first rather than invent one — go to the strategist or the PM, get the line to take, and write to that. My value here is clarity, not originality."
Why it works
Interns who invent house views create compliance problems. Interns who go and get the view build relationships with the people who have it.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Nobody's available." Answer: "Then I'd draft from published material we've already approved and flag clearly which parts need a view attached before it can go out."
The interesting angle is the one the firm cannot takeMedium
The setup
The genuinely compelling piece would argue something at odds with the firm's positioning.
The trap
Either writing it anyway, or writing something dishonest.
What to say
"I'd raise it rather than bury it. If our own analysts' evidence points somewhere uncomfortable, that's worth a conversation, not a quiet edit. What we can't do is publish a view the investment team doesn't hold."
Why it works
Thought leadership is a financial promotion under the same COBS 4.2 test as a factsheet.[S14] It is not a free space.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "So you'd just write the safe version?" Answer: "I'd write the honest version of the view we actually hold. Safe and honest aren't the same thing, and the honest one is usually more interesting anyway."
You are asked to comment on a competitor's problemHard
The setup
A rival has had a public failure. Marketing wants a piece implicitly contrasting it with your firm.
The trap
Writing it. It is commercially tempting and reputationally cheap.
What to say
"I'd want to be careful. A piece about what went wrong in general terms and what good practice looks like is defensible. A piece that is really about a named competitor's difficulty is a different thing, and I don't think it's the way we'd want to win."
Why it works
It shows commercial judgement and an understanding that reputation is an asset with a long duration.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You're being squeamish, this is a competitive industry." Answer: "Maybe. My worry is that the client thinks less of us for it, not more. I'd want a second opinion rather than decide that on my own."

Duty line 4 · "Working with product development to create new products to meet investors' demands"

The same RFP question keeps coming upEasy
The setup
You notice the same question appearing in four different RFPs in a month.
The trap
Answering it four times and moving on.
What to say
"I'd log it and raise it. Four clients asking the same thing isn't four questions, it's a signal. That's the kind of thing Product would want, and it's the cheapest research the firm will get all month."
Why it works
It shows you understand the feedback loop the job description describes, and that you would be a useful node in it rather than a pass-through.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Four isn't a trend." Answer: "Agreed, which is why I'd report it as four instances with the wording, not as a trend. Product can decide if it's a pattern."
A client wants a product you are not sure should existMedium
The setup
A large client asks for a variant of an existing fund that would be complex to explain.
The trap
Saying yes because the client is large.
What to say
"I'd take it seriously and ask the hard question early: can we explain this honestly to everyone who'd end up in it? If it needs three paragraphs of caveats to be accurate, that's a problem worth surfacing before we build it, not after."
Why it works
This is MiFID II product governance thinking: a product needs a defined target market and the distributor needs to be able to sell it into that market.[S16] The fulcrum fee is the firm's own cautionary tale about a good idea that was hard to explain.[S72]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "They're threatening to go elsewhere." Answer: "Then that's a decision for people above me, and I'd make sure they had the explainability problem in front of them when they made it."
You are asked whether a fund should be labelled sustainableHard
The setup
There is internal appetite to market a fund on its sustainability characteristics.
The trap
Treating it as a marketing decision.
What to say
"That's not a naming choice, it's a regulatory one. The anti-greenwashing rule has applied since 31 May 2024, and the FCA's naming and marketing rules since 2 December 2024, so the fund has to actually qualify. I'd start with whether it meets the criteria, not with what we'd like to call it."
Why it works
Correct, precisely dated, and it reframes a commercial question as a compliance one — which is exactly the instinct this seat requires.[S19][S20]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Competitors are doing it." Answer: "Then some of them may have a problem coming. I'd rather we didn't."

Duty line 5 · "Generating marketing material to support sales activity"

Sales wants the deck an hour before the meetingEasy
The setup
A salesperson needs a pitchbook for a meeting this afternoon; compliance has not signed it off.
The trap
Sending it and asking forgiveness.
What to say
"I'd send what's already approved and tell them clearly which slides aren't cleared yet and can't be used. Then I'd chase compliance with the actual deadline attached, because they can often prioritise if they know."
Why it works
It solves the real problem — the salesperson needs something usable — without breaking the control. It also shows you treat compliance as a person with a queue rather than an obstacle.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "They used it anyway." Answer: "Then I'd tell my manager the same day. That's not me reporting a colleague, that's a control failure someone needs to know about."
The claim is technically true and clearly misleadingMedium
The setup
A draft says the fund is "top quartile" — true, over a carefully chosen period that is not the standard one.
The trap
Defending it because it is factually accurate.
What to say
"It's true and I still don't think it passes. COBS 4.2 is a fair, clear and not misleading test, not a technically-accurate test, and Consumer Duty adds a consumer understanding requirement on top. If the period is doing the work, a reader is being misled by a true sentence."
Why it works
The distinction between true and not misleading is the most important conceptual point in this whole job, and very few candidates will articulate it.[S14][S401]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Everyone quotes their best quartile." Answer: "Some do. It's still the rule, and it's the kind of thing that looks very bad in hindsight if the fund turns."
You are asked to remove a risk warning because it spoils the pageHard
The setup
A designer or a salesperson wants a warning moved to the back, or shrunk, for the sake of the layout.
The trap
Treating prominence as an aesthetic matter.
What to say
"Prominence is part of the rule, not part of the design. COBS 4.6.2R requires a prominent warning that past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results, and says the performance itself can't be the most prominent feature. Moving it to the back is the thing the rule is specifically about."
Why it works
It demonstrates you know the rule constrains layout, not just words. This is genuinely specialist knowledge for a candidate at this stage.[S400]
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You're being inflexible." Answer: "On this one, yes. I'd happily redesign the page around it — the constraint is the warning's prominence, not where everything else sits."

Intern line 1 · "Support investment directing and investment specialists"

You have finished your work and nobody has given you moreEasy
The setup
It is 3pm in week two and your task is done.
The trap
Sitting quietly, or asking "is there anything I can help with?" into the void.
What to say
"I'd go to someone specific with something specific: 'I've finished the attribution check. I noticed the Q2 factsheets still need the commentary pulled across — shall I start on those?' Asking a narrow question is much easier to say yes to."
Why it works
Interns who make themselves easy to delegate to get given real work. It is the single highest-return behaviour of a ten-week internship.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "What if you're wrong about what's needed?" Answer: "Then they'll tell me, and I've still shown I was paying attention to what the team is doing."
Two people ask you for different things at onceMedium
The setup
Two investment directors, both senior to you, both want something by the end of the day.
The trap
Silently picking one, and letting the other discover it late.
What to say
"I'd tell both immediately rather than choose in private. 'I've got X from you and Y from her, both for five. X looks like two hours. Which should land first?' Then I'd confirm in writing."
Why it works
It moves a prioritisation decision to the people who can actually make it, which is the correct escalation, and it means nobody is surprised.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "They both say theirs is urgent." Answer: "Then I'd ask them to settle it between them, or take it to my manager. I'm not the right person to rank two directors' priorities, and pretending I am would be the real mistake."
You are asked to sit in on a call you think you should not be onHard
The setup
You are invited to a discussion that sounds like it involves price-sensitive information about a listed company.
The trap
Staying because leaving feels rude and presumptuous.
What to say
"I'd ask before the call rather than during it: 'Is this one I should be on?' If something sounds like inside information once I'm there, I'd say so and leave, and tell compliance. Being wall-crossed without realising it is the outcome I'd most want to avoid."
Why it works
The FCA's thematic review identified exactly this failure: people receiving inside information and never formally logging it.[S17] Raising it unprompted is a very strong signal.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You'd really walk out of a meeting?" Answer: "I'd ask first, and I'd rather ask a slightly awkward question than be a person who has to be untangled from a list later."

Intern line 2 · "Prepare core marketing presentations and reporting materials"

Your first draft comes back covered in redEasy
The setup
You get your first piece of work back with more corrections than original text.
The trap
Taking it personally, or apologising at length.
What to say
"I'd read every change and work out the category — is it accuracy, house style, or judgement? Then I'd ask one consolidated question about the judgement ones and not make the style mistakes twice."
Why it works
It shows you treat feedback as information rather than as a verdict, and grouping questions rather than asking sequentially respects the reviewer's time.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Does it bother you?" Answer: "A bit, the first time. But an unmarked first draft in this job would mean nobody checked it, which is worse."
You are asked to produce something you have never seen beforeMedium
The setup
Someone asks for a DDQ response and you do not know what one is.
The trap
Pretending, then producing something wrong.
What to say
"I'd say I haven't done one and ask for a good recent example to work from. In most of this work there's an approved library, so the right first question is 'what's the best previous version of this?' rather than starting from scratch."
Why it works
Understanding that this output is assembled from approved components rather than written fresh is a genuinely inside-baseball insight.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "There's no example." Answer: "Then I'd draft a structure first and get that agreed before writing any content, so we find out early if I've misunderstood the ask."
You realise the approved library itself is out of dateHard
The setup
The standard approved answer you are copying refers to something that has since changed.
The trap
Using it, because it is approved and therefore someone else's problem.
What to say
"I'd flag it rather than use it. Approved means it was checked at a point in time, not that it's still true. If the underlying fact has changed, reusing it makes it a new misstatement with my name on it."
Why it works
This is the difference between following a process and understanding it, and it is precisely the judgement the role is ultimately paid for.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "That's not your call as an intern." Answer: "Agreed, which is why I'd flag it rather than change it. But not mentioning it would be a call too."

Intern line 3 · "Work with internal stakeholders to source key data and obtain the latest internal views"

A portfolio manager has not replied to youEasy
The setup
You need a view from a PM for a deadline tomorrow and two emails have gone unanswered.
The trap
Continuing to email, or escalating immediately.
What to say
"I'd change channel and make it easy to answer: go to the desk or send one short message with the deadline and a suggested answer they can just confirm or correct. Busy people answer yes-or-no questions much faster than open ones."
Why it works
Drafting the answer for someone to approve is the single most useful habit in this job.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "Still nothing." Answer: "Then I'd tell my manager before the deadline, not after, and let them decide whether to push or to change the plan."
You are not sure you understood what the analyst told youMedium
The setup
An analyst explained their view quickly and you are about 70% sure you followed it.
The trap
Writing it up at 70% and hoping.
What to say
"I'd write it up and send it back: 'Here's how I've captured your view — have I got this right?' That's a thirty-second check for them and it protects both of us."
Why it works
A misquoted analyst is a real problem. Confirming back is standard practice and marks you as safe to rely on.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You'll look like you weren't listening." Answer: "Checking a paraphrase reads as careful, not slow. Getting their view wrong in a client document is what actually looks bad."
Two internal sources give you different numbersHard
The setup
The performance system and the PM's own figure disagree.
The trap
Picking the one that suits the story, or averaging them.
What to say
"I'd treat the official performance record as the number that goes in the document, and go back and understand the difference rather than just discarding one. The PM may be looking at a different share class or period — or there may be a genuine error, which someone needs to know about either way."
Why it works
It shows you know which source is authoritative for a client-facing document, and that a discrepancy is information rather than an inconvenience.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "The PM insists theirs is right." Answer: "Then I'd get them and the performance team in the same conversation. I shouldn't be the one adjudicating it, and it shouldn't go out until it's resolved."

Intern line 4 · "Assist with project management tasks as assigned by the team"

You are given work that feels beneath youEasy
The setup
You are asked to update a spreadsheet of meeting follow-ups for a week.
The trap
Visible disappointment, or doing it carelessly.
What to say
"I'd do it properly and then look for the improvement — if I'm doing it by hand for a week, is there a way it doesn't have to be done by hand again? Turning a dull task into a better process is the most useful thing an intern can leave behind."
Why it works
Every senior person has watched an intern be visibly bored by admin. The ones who improve the admin get remembered.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "What if they don't want it changed?" Answer: "Then I'll have learned why, and I've still done the task."
You realise a deadline is going to be missedMedium
The setup
Three days into a two-week project you can see it will not be ready.
The trap
Waiting, hoping, and telling them on the day.
What to say
"I'd say so on day three, not day thirteen. Early is a problem that can be solved with more people or less scope. Late is just a failure. I'd bring a proposal rather than just the bad news."
Why it works
The willingness to deliver bad news early is one of the clearest signals of professional maturity there is.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "You'll look like you can't cope." Answer: "For three days, maybe. On day thirteen I'd have taken the whole team's option to fix it away."
You are asked to cover for someone else's slipHard
The setup
A colleague missed something and suggests presenting the timeline in a way that obscures it.
The trap
Going along with it because you are junior and they are not.
What to say
"I wouldn't misstate the timeline. I'd also not make it about them — I'd focus on where the project actually is and what we do next. If someone asks directly what happened, I'd answer honestly rather than volunteering a colleague's mistake unprompted."
Why it works
It holds the integrity line while showing you are not sanctimonious or eager to expose a colleague, which matters as much.
The twist, and holding it
Twist: "So you'd drop them in it?" Answer: "No. I'd tell the truth about the project. There's a big difference between not covering something up and going out of your way to point at someone."
