The trap in that table: the assessments are not triggered after you apply and sit in a separate window. They have to be finished by 15 November. Two assessments at roughly 40 minutes each is around ninety minutes of focused work. Do not leave them to the final weekend, and note the firm explicitly offers reasonable adjustments if you would benefit from them.

What this pack is, and what it is not

This is a research pack built only from public sources. It is written to take someone with a finance degree and one internship from knowing roughly what an asset manager does to being able to hold a technical conversation about Fidelity International's business model, the Investment Directing function, and the rules that govern what that function is allowed to say to a client.

It is not a script. Every scaffold here is a structure to think inside, not a sentence to recite. Interviewers at asset managers listen to hundreds of these. A memorised answer is audible, and it is worse than a rougher answer that is obviously yours.

Three things this pack does that a normal preparation document does not. It decodes the job description line by line against real, live Fidelity International job postings, so you are working from what the firm actually asks people to do rather than from marketing copy. It gets the regulation right, with rule numbers and dates, because for this specific seat the regulation is the job. And it tells you where the evidence is thin, so you never walk into a room repeating something this pack invented.

The honesty rule this pack follows

Fidelity International is a private company. It does not publish quarterly results, hold earnings calls, or file the kind of disclosure a listed rival does. A large share of what is written about it is estimate or single-source reporting. Where that is true, this pack says so and gives a range rather than inventing a precise number. If you quote an estimate as a fact and an interviewer knows the real figure, you have damaged yourself for the rest of the conversation. Saying "reported at around X, though they do not publish it" is stronger, not weaker.

Source uncertain — how big is Fidelity International?

Do not quote a single number for this. Public sources disagree, and the disagreement is instructive rather than embarrassing. One widely repeated figure is US$1,086bn of total client assets as at 31 December 2025[S402]. A figure attributed to the firm's own Pillar 3 regulatory disclosure is materially smaller and split differently: around US$469bn of assets under management excluding Canada, plus about US$203bn of assets under administration, as at 30 June 2025[S122] Reported.

These are probably not contradictory so much as different measures. Assets under management is money the firm actually invests and charges a management fee on. Assets under administration is money sitting on its platforms that someone else may manage, where the firm earns a thinner platform or administration fee. "Total client assets" bundles them. A firm that owns both an asset manager and a platform, as this one does, has a large gap between the two numbers, and that gap is the whole subject of Part 3.

What to say: "Reported figures range from roughly 470 billion dollars of assets under management to over a trillion of total client assets depending on whether you count administered platform assets. They are private so it is hard to pin down, and the gap between those two numbers is itself the interesting thing about the business." That answer is correct, honest, and demonstrates you understand the business model. A single confident wrong number does none of that.

What they say they are looking for, in their own words

The posting is unusually explicit, and it is worth reading literally: "It's who you are that matters here, rather than what you studied." They want interns who are "analytical, eager to learn and ready to take on a challenge", with "a genuine curiosity and eagerness to learn about investment", "a lively interest in our work and our industry", and "creative thinking and attention to detail"[S403] Confirmed.

Read that list against the job. "Attention to detail" is not filler in a seat where a wrong number in a factsheet is a regulatory matter. "Lively interest in our industry" is why Part 7 exists. And "creative thinking" in a function this constrained means finding a way to make something clear within the rules, not around them. Each of those four phrases is answerable with something specific from this pack.

The nine facts that do most of the work

  1. Fidelity International is not Fidelity Investments. The posting describes a firm "helping clients plan and invest for their futures for over 50 years", from "more than 25 locations", serving "everyone from central banks to private individuals"[S403]. That is the firm you are applying to. It shares a name, a founding family and a history with the Boston business, and they are separate companies. Fidelity International began in 1969 as the overseas arm of the Boston business and became independent in 1980[S402] Confirmed. It is headquartered in Pembroke, Bermuda, and its largest investment hub is London[S402][S44]. Almost every confusing "Fidelity" story you will read online, and a large share of the internship advice, is about the American firm. Getting this wrong in the first minute is the most common way to lose this interview.
  2. It is private, and that is a business model, not a footnote. No quarterly earnings call, no share price to defend, no listed currency to pay for acquisitions with. The consequences run through everything in Part 3.
  3. Investment Directing sits under Distribution, not under the Chief Investment Officer. Public descriptions place the "Client Propositions team comprised of product, solutions and investment directing teams" under the Global Head of Asset Management Distribution[S41] Reported. That single fact reframes the whole role: you are a commercial function that must be technically credible, not an investment function that happens to talk to clients. Most candidates get this backwards.
  4. The industry name for the job is Client Portfolio Manager. Fidelity calls it Investment Director and has a junior grade called Associate Investment Director[S1]. Schroders and Janus Henderson say Client Portfolio Manager, M&G says Investment Specialist, BlackRock says Product Strategist[S2][S3][S4][S5]. Knowing that the seat exists at every serious firm under different names shows you understand the function, not just the advert.
  5. Five investment groups. Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic Investing and Real Estate. The job description names them, and an Investment Director is assigned to one[S21][S1]. Real Estate is the odd one out because it is physical and illiquid, so it reports to clients differently.
  6. Everything this function produces is a regulated communication. The governing rule is COBS 4.2.1R: "A firm must ensure that a communication or a financial promotion is fair, clear and not misleading"[S14] Confirmed. Past performance has its own rules under COBS 4.6.2R, including that it must not be the most prominent feature, must cover the preceding five years in complete twelve-month periods, and must carry a prominent warning that past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results[S400] Confirmed. This is the single highest-leverage technical area for this role.
  7. The rules changed recently and are still changing. Consumer Duty, published 27 July 2022, in force for open products 31 July 2023[S401]. The anti-greenwashing rule from 31 May 2024 and fund naming and marketing rules from 2 December 2024[S19]. And the Consumer Composite Investments regime, which replaces PRIIPs, commenced 6 April 2026 with full implementation by 8 June 2027[S224]. That last one lands during and just after your internship, and it means rewriting client documents.
  8. Active management is under commercial pressure, visibly. UK platform data for the second quarter of 2026 showed passive funds taking 70.4% of net inflows against 29.6% for active[S231] Reported. Do not pretend this is not happening. The interesting answer is what an active house does about it, which is Part 8.
  9. The house view is published and quotable. Fidelity International runs an annual Analyst Survey. The 2026 edition, "A power puzzle", was published 4 April 2026 and draws on its analysts' company meetings[S288]. Reading one piece of the firm's own research and having a view on it is worth more than any amount of general market commentary.
The one fact almost every other source now has wrong

Anne Richards became chief executive of Fidelity International in December 2018, and for six years that was the right answer. It is no longer current. As of September 2026 the reported leadership is Abigail Johnson as Chair, Anne Richards as Vice Chair, and Keith Metters as President[S402][S281] Confirmed. Note also that Abigail Johnson is separately chief executive of Fidelity Investments in Boston, the other firm.

Older preparation articles, forum posts and AI-generated summaries still say "CEO Anne Richards". If you say it to someone who works there, you have told them your research is second-hand and out of date. Check the firm's own leadership page the week of any interview and let that override this pack.
