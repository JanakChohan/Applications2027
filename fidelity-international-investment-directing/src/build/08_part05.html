Part 5

The industry from zero

What the product is, who buys it, how the money is made, and the forces reshaping it in 2026. Written so that someone who has never worked in asset management can follow it, with nothing left undefined.

5.1 What is actually being sold

An asset manager takes money from people who have it and invests it on their behalf, for a fee. That is the whole industry. Everything else is variation on the wrapper, the customer and the fee.

WrapperWhat it is, in plain English
Pooled fundMany investors' money in one pot, managed together. You own units or shares in the pot, not the underlying holdings.
OEIC / unit trustThe two standard UK pooled fund structures. An open-ended investment company issues shares; a unit trust issues units. Practically similar for the investor. "Open-ended" means the fund grows and shrinks as people buy and sell.
SICAV / UCITSThe European equivalents. UCITS is an EU regulatory standard for retail funds that has become a global export label because it is trusted.
Investment trustA closed-ended company listed on the stock exchange whose business is holding investments. Fixed share count, so the price can differ from the value of the assets. Fidelity runs several, including Japan, China Special Situations and Asian Values[S72].
ETFExchange-traded fund. A fund you buy and sell on an exchange like a share, historically index-tracking and cheap. Increasingly also actively managed, which is where Fidelity has been building[S85].
Segregated mandateA single large client's own portfolio, managed to its own rules. Typical for pension schemes. No pooling.
Model portfolioA recipe of funds an adviser applies across many clients at once.
LTAFLong-Term Asset Fund. A UK structure designed to let pension savers hold illiquid private-market assets. Fidelity was authorised for its first in August 2025[S89].

The structural fact worth memorising

UK industry assets hit a record £11.1 trillion in 2025, up 11% on £10.0 trillion in 2024[S150] Confirmed. More importantly for this firm: retail investors now account for roughly 30% of UK-managed assets and have overtaken pension funds at roughly 25% as the largest client group — a reversal of what was, until 2020, roughly an 80/20 institutional-to-retail split[S150][S152] Confirmed.

Why this is the single best industry fact for a Fidelity interview: the client group that is growing fastest is precisely the one Fidelity International reaches directly through its own platform. Connect those two facts out loud and you have made an argument almost nobody else in the room will make.

5.3 The value chain, and where the fee goes

Key terms · the fee vocabulary

AMC is the annual management charge, the manager's own fee. OCF or TER is the total cost of running the fund, including the AMC plus administration and audit, and is the number an investor should actually compare. A performance fee is an extra slice if the fund beats a hurdle. Carried interest is the private-equity version, typically a share of profits above a threshold. The key behavioural point: a fee charged on assets rewards gathering assets; a fee charged on performance rewards taking risk. Firms choose which incentive they want, and regulators watch both.

5.4 What is actually happening to the industry, and four ways it might go

The diagnosis is not disputed. Passive is taking share, fees are compressing, and costs are rising. Passive strategies make up roughly 30% of assets but only about 7% of revenue in one sample, while alternatives are about 18% of assets and about 57% of revenue[S195] Reported. What happens next is disputed, and knowing that there is a genuine disagreement is more impressive than picking a side.

Which view do you find most convincing, and why? The strongest interview move is to notice that view 1 and view 4 are in direct tension: if distribution has genuinely replaced performance as the competitive edge, then a firm that owns its own distribution is structurally advantaged regardless of whether its funds beat the index — which is a remarkably convenient argument for Fidelity International specifically. Say that, then say why you are or are not persuaded by it. Noticing that the argument flatters your prospective employer, and saying so, is a sign of a serious mind.

5.5 The UK policy backdrop you should know

The Mansion House Accord, signed 13 May 2025, committed 17 workplace pension providers, covering around 90% of active DC savers, to invest 10% of their default funds in private markets by 2030, with 5% ringfenced for UK assets[S186] Confirmed. That moves an asset pool of about £252bn today toward a projected £740bn by 2030[S186].

This is the direct commercial reason Fidelity International sought FCA authorisation for an LTAF in August 2025 and folded private assets into its £16.9bn FutureWise workplace default[S89]. Policy created demand; the firm built the vehicle. Being able to draw that line is exactly the "commercial awareness" the assessment tests[S262].
