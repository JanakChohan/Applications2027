10.3 The house view you may be tested against

Fidelity International's flagship publication is its annual Analyst Survey. The 2026 edition, "A power puzzle", was published 4 April 2026, drawing on more than 120 analysts and over 20,000 company management meetings, with data captured through early March 2026[S291] Confirmed.

Headline findings worth knowing:

  • An AI-driven rebound in corporate sentiment: 81% of analysts covering IT and 65% covering materials reported moderate to significant confidence for the year ahead.
  • AI infrastructure spending broadening out of large technology platforms into materials, industrials and energy, driven by demand for power and commodities for datacentres.
  • Persistent cost pressure: only 8% of analysts expect inflation to ease over the next twelve months; about half expect it to hold and 40% expect further increases.
  • A "K-shaped" divide between AI-exposed sectors growing strongly and consumer-facing sectors under affordability strain.

Niamh Brodie-Machura is quoted saying AI investment "is cascading through power and industrial supply chains" and that "detailed fundamental research and active stock selection become increasingly important"[S291] Confirmed.

Your opinion goes here — and note the self-interest

That second quotation is a house that sells fundamental research concluding that fundamental research is becoming more important. It may well be right. It is also exactly what they would conclude. Being able to say "I found the survey persuasive on the capex broadening, and I noticed the conclusion about active stock selection is one the firm has an interest in reaching — though the K-shaped dispersion argument does actually support it" is the kind of remark that gets remembered.
