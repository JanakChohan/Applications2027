7.1 Fidelity International: the four stories that matter

1 · The China tension — the most interesting thing you can raise

On 21 August 2026 it was reported that Fidelity International is weighing withdrawal from its wholly owned China mutual fund business, three years after launching its Shanghai subsidiary, citing domestic competition, management changes and difficulty reaching scale[S223] Reported. Reported detail: assets peaked around 6bn yuan and fell about 25% to roughly 4.5bn yuan by end-June 2026; about $218m invested; internal analysis reportedly concluded the unit needed at least $14bn of assets to break even. No formal application has been filed, and the firm's public line was that "China remains an important market" with "no change to report"[S223].

Why it is the best question in this pack: at the same time, Fidelity's own investment people have been publicly constructive on Chinese equities[S237]. So the firm may be retreating from manufacturing funds in China while remaining bullish on investing in China. Those are not contradictory, but noticing the distinction is exactly the analytical move this job requires.

Say this in the interview — or better, ask it "One thing I found genuinely interesting is the reporting from August that Fidelity might step back from onshore fund manufacturing in China, at the same time as the house view has been constructive on Chinese equities. I read that as a distinction between where you want to build a distribution business and where you want to invest clients' money, which aren't the same decision. Is that a fair reading, or am I missing something?"

Why it works: it is current, it shows you separated two things most people conflate, and ending on a genuine question invites them to teach you, which senior people enjoy. It also protects you — if the reporting is wrong or out of date, you have framed it as a question rather than a claim.

2 · The private assets push

FCA approval for the Fidelity Diversified Private Assets LTAF, spanning private equity, private credit, infrastructure, real estate and natural resources with a liquid sleeve[S224] Confirmed, reportedly to be built into the workplace default's growth phase toward roughly 15% in private markets over about three years[S224][S225] Reported. This is downstream of the Mansion House Accord (Part 5.5). For this team it is a communication problem: illiquid assets, notice periods and valuation uncertainty are hard to explain honestly and are precisely the "new products to meet investors' demands" the job description names.

3 · Active ETFs in Europe

Two new Research Enhanced investment-grade corporate bond ETFs, USD (FBIU) and EUR (FBIE), listed on Xetra, Borsa Italiana and the London Stock Exchange, reported late January 2026[S226] Reported. Semi-active and rules-based rather than pure index tracking. This is how an active house competes in a market flowing to ETFs without pretending to be a passive giant.

4 · Sustainability labels, live and being reported against

Fidelity confirmed plans to adopt the FCA's "Sustainability Focus" label for three UK equity funds with combined assets cited above £865m[S228] Confirmed. Date caveat: the confirming articles are from 18 November 2024, so this is background rather than news; what is current is that the labels are live and being reported against during 2026[S229] Reported.

Don't say this — the trap that catches most candidates

Large amounts of 2026 "Fidelity" news are about Fidelity Investments in Boston: assets of about $7.1trn, roughly 800 US job cuts in May 2026, US ETF share-class launches, US office-attendance mandates[S137][S143]. None of it is your employer. If you walk in with a confident line about "your $7 trillion in assets" or "your recent return-to-office policy", you have identified yourself as someone who searched "Fidelity" and stopped there.

These will be wrong by the time you read this. Check them the morning of any interview. What matters is not the level but being able to say what moved and why.

The three themes to be able to discuss, which will outlast the specific numbers:

  • AI capital spending as the dominant equity market driver, and the question of whether the returns justify the spend. Fidelity's own Analyst Survey 2026, "A power puzzle", published 4 April 2026, is the house view on this and is the single best thing to read before an interview[S288].
  • UK government borrowing costs. Long gilt yields near multi-decade highs matter directly to pension schemes and to anyone selling a bond fund.
  • The shift from rate cuts to "higher for longer", and what that does to the case for bonds against equities, which is the core of any multi-asset conversation.

7.4 What to read, and a twenty-minute morning routine

Every morning, twenty minutes
  1. FT markets page and Lex, five minutes. What moved and why.
  2. Reuters funds coverage, five minutes.
  3. One UK trade outlet, five minutes: Investment Week, Portfolio Adviser or Citywire.
  4. Five minutes on one thing in depth, rather than skimming a fourth source.
Weekly and before any interview
  • The Investment Association newsroom for UK flow data.
  • The FCA newsroom for policy statements and consultations.
  • Fidelity International's own outlooks, the Analyst Survey and its podcast output.
  • Ignites Europe and Financial News London for the industry's internal politics.
  • Morningstar research for the active-versus-passive evidence base.

One discipline worth more than the reading itself: for each story, write one sentence on what it means for a firm like Fidelity International. Fifteen of those sentences is a commercial awareness answer bank that no generic preparation can match.
