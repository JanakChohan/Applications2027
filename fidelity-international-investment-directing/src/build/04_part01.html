The role, decoded

What an Investment Director actually does on a Tuesday, what the internship's duty lines really mean, what binds the job legally, and who sits around you. Built from live Fidelity International job postings rather than from the internship advert.

1.1 What an Investment Director actually is

An Investment Director sits between the people who manage the money and the people who sell it. That is the whole job in one sentence, and almost every other fact about the seat follows from it.

The industry's generic name for this seat is Client Portfolio Manager. Fidelity International calls it Investment Director, and runs a junior grade called Associate Investment Director[S1] Confirmed. Schroders and Janus Henderson use Client Portfolio Manager directly; M&G says Investment Specialist; BlackRock says Product Strategist; Baillie Gifford splits the job in two, pairing a Client Manager who owns the commercial relationship with an Investment Specialist who owns the investment content[S2][S3][S4][S5][S6] Reported.

That the same function exists at every serious firm under six different names tells you it is structural, not a Fidelity quirk. It exists for an economic reason. A fund manager who is excellent at picking stocks is not necessarily good at spending two hours being cross-examined by a pension scheme's investment consultant, and the firm does not want its scarcest and most expensive investment talent doing that all day. Equally, a salesperson who cannot answer a probing question about duration positioning will lose the room. The Investment Director is built to do both: technically credible enough that the portfolio manager trusts them to speak for the strategy, commercially fluent enough to read a client Inferred.

Key term · Client Portfolio Manager (CPM)

A client-facing investment professional who represents a strategy to clients but does not make the buy and sell decisions. They explain the portfolio, they do not run it. One live Fidelity International advert describes the role, in the firm's own words, as a "Portfolio Manager (PM) proxy responsible for client facing activities including investment process, strategy updates, product updates, client pitches, client servicing, prospecting, due-diligence, RFPs and product development"[S47] Confirmed. "PM proxy" is the most precise two-word definition of this job you will find anywhere, and it came from Fidelity, not from this pack.

Say this in the interview "The way I understand it, Investment Directing is the translation layer. The portfolio managers own the decisions, Distribution owns the relationship, and the Investment Director is the person who can stand in front of a client and explain the portfolio at the manager's level of detail without being the manager. One of your own adverts calls it a PM proxy, which I thought was a good description."

Why it works: it shows you found a real job posting, it uses the firm's own phrase, and it gets the reporting line right, which most candidates do not.

1.2 Every duty line, decoded

The internship advert describes the team's job and then the intern's job. Below, each line is taken apart: what it means, who you deal with, what good looks like, and what it quietly tells you about how the firm is built.

Source uncertain — the job description

The 2026 posting's text is reproduced here from search-engine summaries, because the job board hosting it blocks automated access[S21]. The wording is consistent across multiple summaries and matches the language on Fidelity's own careers pages, so it is reliable as substance. Treat it as Reported, not verbatim, and re-read the real 2027 advert when it opens. If you send me the live text I will rebuild this section against it line by line.

Team line 1 · "Representing the Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic Investing and Real Estate groups to prospective and existing clients"

What it means. The investment engine is organised into five asset-class groups, and an Investment Director is assigned to one of them, not to all five. A live advert was scoped specifically to Systematic Investing[S1]. "Representing" means being the person in the room for a prospect meeting, a client review, a consultant call or a conference panel, carrying that group's house view.

Counterparties. Inside: the portfolio managers and analysts in that group, whose views you must reflect rather than invent. Outside: pension schemes, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, wholesale fund selectors, platforms, private banks and wealth managers[S1][S10].

What good looks like. Technical accuracy that would survive the portfolio manager reading it, plus the ability to translate for a non-specialist without flattening it into something misleading. Those two things pull against each other, and managing that tension is the craft of the job.

What it reveals. Real Estate is the odd one out. It is physical and illiquid, valued by appraisal rather than by a daily market price, so it reports to clients on a different rhythm from a daily-priced equity fund[S10]. If you are asked which of the five interests you and you pick Real Estate, be ready to say why the illiquidity changes the communication problem. That is a strong answer.

Team line 2 · "Reporting on portfolios"

What it means. The production line of monthly and quarterly factsheets, commentary, client review packs, and performance attribution. Attribution answers: did we beat the benchmark, by how much, and from what — stock selection, sector allocation, duration, currency.

Counterparties. A performance and attribution team supplies the numbers (Fidelity has such roles; a live Dublin vacancy is titled "Performance & Attribution Lead - Associate Director"[S45]), portfolio managers approve the narrative, and Compliance signs off the wording before it leaves the building.

What good looks like. The numbers tie exactly to the official performance record. You cannot round favourably or quietly drop an ugly period, partly because it is against the rules and partly because a consultant such as Mercer is very likely re-running the same numbers independently[S8][S11].

Team line 3 · "Promoting thought leadership on topics"

What it means. White papers, "viewpoints", webinars, outlook pieces, conference panels. This is not about one fund's last quarter. It keeps the house view visible so that when a client eventually runs a search, the firm is already in mind.

The trap. Candidates assume thought leadership is the fun, unregulated part. It is not. A white paper is a financial promotion in FCA terms just as much as a factsheet is, and clears the same "fair, clear and not misleading" bar[S14].

Team line 4 · "Working with product development to create new products to meet investors' demands"

What it means. There is a distinct Product function, separate from both the investment teams and Distribution. Live Fidelity vacancies include "Product Owner - Equity" and "Product Owner - Multi-Asset"[S43][S44] Confirmed. So the firm splits manage, manufacture and sell three ways. Investment Directing is the channel through which structured client demand — the same RFP question asked eleven times, a consultant's repeated objection, a platform's request for a different share class — flows back into what gets built. One live advert lists "Content for Product launches, reshapes, DDQ, RFPs, perspectives" as a deliverable[S1].

Team line 5 · "Generating marketing material to support sales activity"

What it means. Pitchbooks, RFP and DDQ answers, one-pagers, case studies. Highest volume, most junior-accessible, and almost certainly where most of your ten weeks will go, because drafting a first version needs accuracy and structure rather than years of judgement.

Key terms · RFP and DDQ

A Request for Proposal is a formal document a prospective client or its consultant sends out when it wants to hire a manager, containing dozens to hundreds of questions. A Due Diligence Questionnaire is the same idea aimed at how the firm is run: its controls, its people, its risk processes. Both are answered largely from a maintained library of approved answers, which someone has to keep current. That someone is often the most junior person in the team. It sounds dull and it is genuinely important: the answers are contractual in tone and wrong ones are expensive.

1.3 A worked day in the seat

1.4 What the team is judged on

No firm publishes its internal targets, and none were found for Fidelity[S1]. The structural logic points to three things Inferred:

  • Net flows and asset retention for the strategies covered. Money kept is worth as much as money won, and is cheaper to get.
  • Win rate on RFPs and pitches. The industry treats this as a measurable, improvable skill; vendors sell software on precisely that claim[S13] Reported.
  • Consultant ratings and buy-list status. A manager usually has to be rated by a gatekeeper such as Mercer, WTW or Aon before most institutional searches will consider it[S11]. The UK investment consultants' market was significant enough that the Competition and Markets Authority ran a full market investigation into it[S12].

The sharpest version of the third point, and a good thing to say out loud: a downgrade by one consultant can shut off more new business than any marketing campaign can replace. That is why this function exists at a senior level at all.

Don't say this

"I'd be judged on how persuasive the materials are." No. Persuasion that outruns the evidence is the specific failure mode the rules in 1.5 exist to prevent. The honest version is that you are judged on whether clients stay invested through a bad quarter because the explanation was good and true, not because it was flattering.

1.5 The control dimension: why you cannot just write what sells

This is the single highest-value thing you can raise unprompted, because the naive answer — "I would write compelling material that helps win the mandate" — is exactly what marks a candidate as not having done the reading.

Nearly everything this function produces is, in FCA terms, a communication with a client or a financial promotion. Both are bound by hard rules. Here are the ones that matter, with the numbers and dates, because vague gestures at "compliance" are worth nothing and precise references are worth a great deal.

Say this in the interview "The thing that surprised me reading into the role is that it is not really a marketing job, it is a regulated-communications job. Everything the team produces is a financial promotion under COBS 4.2, so it has to be fair, clear and not misleading. Past performance has its own rules — it can't be the most prominent feature and it has to cover five complete twelve-month periods, so you can't choose a flattering start date. And Consumer Duty added a consumer understanding test on top, so material can be true and still fail. That constraint is what makes the job interesting to me rather than off-putting."

Why it works: three specific, correct references; it reframes a "soft" job as a technical one; and the last sentence answers the motivation question before it is asked.

1.6 Career path from this seat

One rung is confirmed from real postings: Associate Investment Director exists as a distinct grade below Investment Director[S1] Confirmed. A regional Head of Investment Directing, Asia Pacific post is confirmed to exist by a Hong Kong advert naming it as the reporting line[S47] Confirmed. The rest of the ladder — intern, analyst, associate, director, head — follows standard industry convention but was not confirmed by a named Fidelity postingInferred.

The lateral moves this seat opens follow directly from who you work with daily: into Product, into Distribution, or — for those who build the deepest technical grounding — into an investment research seat. The seat is unusually good for optionality because it is one of the few that touches the investment floor, the sales floor and the product function in the same week.

1.7 The team, reconstructed from public evidence

Fidelity International publishes no org chart for this function. What follows was rebuilt from live job adverts, press coverage and public professional profiles. The method is shown so you can judge the evidence yourself, and so you can describe how you did it, which is itself a good answer.

The method · job adverts are an org chart in disguise

A company will not publish its structure, but it will publish a hundred job adverts that each name a reporting line and a set of partner teams. Collect enough of them and the shape appears. Two adverts did most of the work here, and both are quoted below.

Where the function sits — the single most useful structural finding

Public descriptions place "the Client Propositions team comprised of product, solutions and investment directing teams" under the Global Head of Asset Management Distribution, from March 2024[S41][S53] Reported, corroborated across two independent write-ups.

So Investment Directing is owned by Distribution, not by the Chief Investment Officer organisation. Most candidates assume the opposite. It reframes the role as a commercial function that must be technically credible, rather than an investment function that happens to face clients.

But the picture is not clean, and saying so is better than pretending otherwise. Two live adverts point different ways:

An "Investment Director, Fixed Income" role in Hong Kong reports to the "Head of Investment Directing, Asia Pacific", and describes the job as a "Portfolio Manager (PM) proxy responsible for client facing activities including investment process, strategy updates, product updates, client pitches, client servicing, prospecting, due-diligence, RFPs and product development". Live Fidelity International job advert [S47] Confirmed. The same advert states the fixed income business it supports manages "over $US80 billion".
An "Associate Investment Director – Systematic Investing" role in London reports instead to the "Head of Systematic Investing", sits in an "ISS Client Propositions team", and names its key partners as "Multi Asset, Solutions, ETFs, Investment Management, Distribution (PMs, PSG, Stats, PCR, Sales & Marketing, RFP teams)". Live Fidelity International job advert [S48] Confirmed. The same advert says Systematic Investing has grown from roughly $3bn to roughly $30bn of assets in about five years.

The honest reading: Investment Directing is matrixed. It is owned centrally by Distribution, and individual post-holders can be line-managed by the asset-class head they represent. That is normal in asset management, and the public record does not fully resolve it Inferred.

How big is the team?

Source uncertain — this is an estimate, and the method matters more than the number

No source anywhere gives a headcount for Investment Directing. Building one from the structure: five asset-class groups, across roughly two to three regional hubs, at perhaps two to five people per cell, gives a range of very roughly 30 to 80 people globally[S40] Inferred.

Cross-checks: Janus Henderson disclosed a twelve-person Portfolio Construction and Strategy team, though that figure is from 2021 and the scope is not identical[S40]. Fidelity International's UK average headcount was 2,941 in 2024[S78], so this function is a small fraction of one per cent of the firm.

Do not quote the range as fact. If it comes up, give the method and call it a guess. The method is the impressive part.

Where the work splits

  • By asset class. Equities, Fixed Income, Multi Asset, Systematic Investing, Real Estate. Notably, no Real Estate Investment Director title was found, only an "Implementation Director, Real Estate"[S43].
  • By region. An Asia Pacific Investment Directing line is confirmed[S47]. Fidelity describes London as its largest investment hub[S44].
  • By channel. The firm's own Distribution pages name a Global Institutional Team, a Government Institutional Group, retail and wholesale funds, DC and Workplace Savings, Personal Investing and Adviser Solutions[S303].
