## 12.1 Snapshot

Criteria Corp is an American pre-employment testing company, and for most of this guide's audience it is the **odd one out**: unlike SHL, Aon or HireVue, it is **not** a standard fixture of UK front-office finance recruiting. It is included because it is a large, well-known platform whose flagship test — the **CCAT** — a UK finance candidate can genuinely run into, but almost always at the *edges* of the sector: a US private-equity fund's London office, a PE-owned portfolio company, or a US-headquartered trading or fintech firm. The chapter is written to be useful in exactly those cases, and honest about the fact that a candidate applying to a bulge-bracket or elite-boutique graduate scheme is very unlikely to meet it. `[INDEPENDENT + INFERRED — see §12.11]`

Criteria was founded in **2006 in Los Angeles** (now West Hollywood) by Josh Millet, who remains CEO. `[VENDOR]` It is majority-owned by the private-equity firm **Sumeru Equity Partners**, which made a **$56 million majority growth investment in May 2019** (after an earlier 2015 investment from Level Equity). `[VENDOR/INDEPENDENT]` It expanded its product range by acquiring two Australian companies: **Revelian** (game-based assessments, ~March 2020) and **Alcami Interactive** (video interviewing, March 2021), folding both into a single "Criteria" brand in 2022. `[VENDOR]` The platform markets itself as a **self-serve, science-led, low-cost** alternative to the enterprise consultancy model of SHL and Aon — testing that a small company or PE portfolio business can stand up in a day. It claims 4,000+ organisations across 60 countries and 80 million+ assessments delivered. `[VENDOR — marketing figures]`

## 12.2 Why this test exists

Criteria's pitch rests on the same foundation as SHL's and Aon's: **cognitive aptitude (general mental ability, "g") is among the strongest single predictors of job performance and trainability**, and it is far cheaper and more scalable than interviewing. The company positions this as bringing "science, structure and fairness" to employers who are too small for enterprise assessment consulting. `[VENDOR]` The CCAT specifically measures three sub-abilities — **Spatial Reasoning, Verbal Ability, and Math & Logic** — and claims to predict how well someone will "acquire, organise, retain and apply information on the job." `[VENDOR — CCAT InfoBrief]`

The honest evidence picture is the same as elsewhere in this guide, and Criteria's own materials illustrate the tension unusually clearly — because **they publish two different, internally inconsistent validity charts.** One vendor one-pager shows cognitive aptitude predicting performance at **r = 0.50** (echoing the classic Schmidt & Hunter 1998 figure); another Criteria eBook shows it at **r = 0.31**. `[VENDOR — both, contradictory]` The lower figure is, tellingly, close to the **2022 re-analysis by Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens**, which corrected the older meta-analyses for an over-correction and put cognitive-ability operational validity nearer **0.31**. `[INDEPENDENT]` The practical takeaway (developed in Chapter 6.3): a strong CCAT score genuinely helps, but the "4× more predictive than experience" marketing is the optimistic older number; the current best independent estimate is more modest, meaning the test is a real but far-from-decisive signal.

Criteria's own **published psychometrics for the CCAT are, to their credit, primary and specific** `[VENDOR — CCAT InfoBrief]`: a normative sample of **106,740 people** (median raw score **24**, SD **7.0**); internal-consistency reliability of **Cronbach's α = 0.86** (high); construct validity of **r = 0.78** against the Wonderlic and **r = 0.55** against the MRAB; and criterion validity of **r = 0.33–0.44** against job performance in the two published studies. `[VENDOR]` (Note one vendor inconsistency: the CCAT *web page* still quotes an older, smaller norm of 985 people, mean 24.2, SD 8.58 — superseded by the 106,740 figure in the current technical brief. `[VENDOR — both]`)

## 12.3 Why a firm chooses Criteria specifically

Criteria's whole commercial identity is the **anti-SHL/Aon wedge**: self-serve, low-cost, no-consultant testing bought on a flat annual "unlimited testing" subscription. `[VENDOR/INFERRED — exact pricing tiers not public, [UNKNOWN]]` A small PE-backed operating company or a fintech scale-up can adopt it without an assessment-science team. In the US, **"the CCAT" has near-generic brand recognition** as a fast cognitive screen — much as the Wonderlic once did — and it is especially embedded in **private-equity-owned operational hiring**, where a quick, standardised aptitude gate suits the PE playbook of installing consistent metrics across a portfolio. `[INFERRED + CANDIDATE]` Vista Equity Partners is the archetypal heavy user (§12.6, §12.11).

Beyond that, the sales points are **speed** (15-minute CCAT, instant scoring, works on any device), **breadth on one platform** (cognitive + personality + EI + game-based + video + proctoring under one login, with an optional weighted "Talent Signal" composite), and **ATS integrations**. `[VENDOR]` On diversity, Criteria's eBook is unusually candid: it concedes aptitude tests "can have adverse impact" through group mean-score differences, then argues they are lawful in the US if job-related and consistent with business necessity, that many alternatives (education minimums, interviews) have *higher* adverse impact, and it markets its personality and game-based products as lower-adverse-impact options. `[VENDOR]`

Its **reference clients are almost entirely non-finance and US** — names that surface include IBM, Johnson & Johnson, BMW, eBay, and U-Haul, plus the US PE world. `[VENDOR/PREP-VENDOR]` The absence of UK banks from any client list is itself the finding of §12.11.

## 12.4 What the assessment actually is — full mechanics

### CCAT — Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (the flagship)

**50 multiple-choice questions in a hard 15-minute limit** — about **18 seconds per question** — and Criteria states plainly that **"very few people finish all 50 items."** `[VENDOR]` (The candidate lore that "about 1 in 3 finish" is directionally plausible but the precise fraction is `[UNKNOWN]`.) The 50 items interleave the three categories: **Spatial** (odd-one-out, rotation, patterns), **Verbal** (antonyms, analogies, sentence completion, attention to detail), and **Math & Logic** (word problems, number/letter series, syllogisms, tables and graphs), typically with five answer options.

Two mechanics matter more than the rest:

- **No calculator.** The engine provides no calculator or scratch tool; you do the arithmetic mentally (or on your own paper if unproctored and permitted). `[INFERRED — vendor silent, corroborated by prep-vendors]`
- **Dynamic item bank — fixed-length, but NOT fixed-form and NOT adaptive.** This corrects a common assumption. The CCAT draws each candidate's 50 items from "a bank of thousands of individually optimised items, so no two candidates get the exact same set" — but the difficulty and order do **not** react to your answers as an adaptive test would. It is *fixed-length, fixed-time, randomised-content, linear.* `[VENDOR]` The randomisation is a deliberate anti-cheating feature (answer-sharing is near-useless) and it underpins the "Invalid Result" detector in §12.8.

On a disconnection, each sitting has a **Test Event ID**; you return to the candidate portal (oda1.com), re-enter the ID and resume, with the session server-tracked and connectivity logged. `[VENDOR]`

### UCAT — Universal Cognitive Aptitude Test (the more finance-relevant variant)

**40 questions in 20 minutes** (~30 seconds each), deliberately **language-independent** — no verbal or vocabulary content — so it is translation-free and used for international and non-native-English candidates. Its four sub-scores are Logic & Analysis, Numerical Reasoning, Attention to Detail, and Spatial Reasoning. `[VENDOR]` Criteria explicitly lists **Analysts and Accounting/Finance roles** among the "top positions" for the UCAT, which makes it the **more likely Criteria test a finance or analyst candidate would meet internationally.** `[VENDOR]`

### CBST, CAST, and the rest

- **CBST (Criteria Basic Skills Test)** — 40 items / 20 minutes of basic maths, grammar, spelling and attention; an entry-level literacy/numeracy screen, **not** front-office finance calibre. `[VENDOR/PREP-VENDOR]`
- **CAST (Criteria Attention Skills Test)** — measures divided/selective attention and reaction time for vigilance roles (the prompt's "CAB" is a mislabel; Criteria's attention product is CAST). Exact item count/time `[UNKNOWN]`.
- **EPP (Employee Personality Profile)** — **140 prompts, untimed but ~10 minutes**, scoring **12 traits** (Achievement, Assertiveness, Competitiveness, Conscientiousness, Cooperativeness, Extroversion, Managerial, Motivation, Openness, Patience, Self-Confidence, Stress Tolerance) on a Big-Five foundation. Each trait is reported as a **percentile vs a global norm** (not good/bad), and an associated **job family** produces an overall **% match**. `[VENDOR]` The response format (single-stimulus Likert vs forced-choice) is not vendor-confirmed; the "140 prompts, continuous percentile" design strongly implies a **Likert-type agree–disagree** format, not the ipsative forced-choice of SHL's OPQ. `[INFERRED — HIGH confidence; exact scale points [UNKNOWN]]`
- **Emotify** — ability-based emotional-intelligence assessment (~20 minutes), from the Revelian acquisition: three tasks measuring emotional perception (Matching Faces), understanding (Emotional Ties) and management (workplace scenarios). Because it is *ability*-based, there are genuinely better and worse answers. `[VENDOR/PREP-VENDOR]`
- **Revelian Cognify** — a game-based cognitive assessment of up to **6 mini-games (~15–30 min)** — Short Cuts, Grid Lock, Resemble, Numbubbles, Tally Up, Proof It — grouped into problem-solving, numerical and verbal clusters, capturing behavioural *process* telemetry (speed, error patterns, approach) as well as outcomes. Strongest in Australia/NZ. `[PREP-VENDOR]`
- **Alcami** — asynchronous and live **video interviewing** (browser-based, no download); recruiters set questions, candidates self-record, evaluators rate on a five-star scale. Question count, answer time and re-record rules are employer-configured (`[UNKNOWN]` in general). `[VENDOR]` For async-video technique, see Chapter 8.

## 12.5 Is it tailored to the role?

**No at the item level, yes at the interpretation level** — the same pattern as SHL. The CCAT, UCAT and EPP items are universal, not bespoke per employer. Tailoring is a **benchmark overlay**: the CCAT ships with a library of **~46 published "suggested score ranges" by job family** (each a recommended raw-score band computed from 700,000+ CCAT sittings), including **Finance Manager, Financial Services, Analyst, Accounting** and many non-finance families; the employer associates a job family with the test, and the report shows whether you fell within, above or below that band. `[VENDOR]` The EPP works the same way with 12 job-family "success" trait profiles. Employers can also commission **custom score ranges** built from their own incumbents. `[VENDOR]` So, as with SHL, the *same* raw performance can be read as a pass against one job family and a miss against another.

## 12.6 How they screen and filter — scoring, norms, cut-offs, ranking

**Scoring.** The CCAT raw score is the **number correct out of 50** (no evidence of negative marking), converted to a **percentile** against Criteria's global norm — median raw **24 ≈ 50th percentile** — plus three sub-category percentiles. `[VENDOR]` As rough anchors, prep-vendor data (directionally consistent with the vendor median) puts raw **24 ≈ 50th, 31 ≈ 80th, 36 ≈ 92nd, 40 ≈ 97th** percentile. `[PREP-VENDOR]`

**Is there a pass mark?** No universal one — **the employer sets the bar**, as a raw cutoff, a percentile, or Criteria's suggested range for the chosen job family. `[VENDOR]` The recruiter dashboard shows a Candidate Score Report (raw score, percentile, a normal-distribution graphic with the suggested range shaded, the three sub-percentiles, the job-family comparison, and any invalid-result warnings) and an optional weighted **"Talent Signal"** composite across multiple tests. `[VENDOR]`

**Reported employer cut-offs — treat with real caution.** The one widely-repeated figure is that **Vista Equity Partners** weights the CCAT heavily and looks for **"high-40s" raw scores** across its portfolio, with candidates reporting re-sits to clear it. `[CANDIDATE, n≈several — thread titles corroborate; full threads were access-blocked so counts are approximate]` This should be held sceptically: a "high-40s" cutoff is close to a **perfect score on a test the vendor says very few people finish**, which is either folklore, role-specific to a tiny elite, or a misremembering. The exact figure is `[UNKNOWN]`; do not treat it as a target.

**Retake.** Employer-controlled, not fixed by Criteria. Common practice is a **~6-month wait** and a reusable score "badge" valid for about six months; some employers take only the first score, some allow a recruiter-granted retake. Scores do not automatically follow you between unrelated employers unless a badge is reused. `[PREP-VENDOR — generalisation; exact per-employer rules [UNKNOWN]]`

**A note on cheating economics (from Criteria's own eBook).** Criteria recommends employers tell candidates upfront they may be **re-tested on-site**, and reports that when such a policy is disclosed, the share of candidates whose remote and onsite scores diverged suspiciously was **"much less than 2%."** `[VENDOR]` The lesson for you is that an honest remote score you can reproduce under supervision is the safe position — which you have anyway.

**The funnel point.** Where Criteria appears, it is an **early screen** — often the first gate after application, sometimes a deal-breaker at US-PE firms that weight it heavily. Unlike a bulge-bracket UK process (where the CV screen and interviews usually cut hardest — Chapter 6.8), a standalone CCAT at a PE firm can be a genuinely tight, decisive hurdle, precisely because the firm chose a hard, fast cognitive test as its filter. `[INFERRED]`

## 12.7 How to score well — legitimate, specific, actionable

**The CCAT is a speed test before it is a difficulty test.** At ~18 seconds a question with no calculator and most people not finishing, pace management beats raw brilliance. Specifically:

- **Triage and skip-and-return.** Bank the fast verbal and logic points first; never sink 90 seconds into one spatial rotation. A question you can't crack in ~20 seconds is a question to guess and leave.
- **Drill mental arithmetic** — fractions, percentages, ratios, averages, simple algebra — so the no-calculator rule is not a handicap. This is the single highest-leverage preparation.
- **Learn the recurring item types** — antonyms, analogies, number/letter series, odd-one-out, syllogisms, seating/logic puzzles, table/graph reads — so recognition is instant.
- **Practise under a strict 15-minute clock** to build pace tolerance and calibrate when to move on.
- **Never leave blanks at time-up** — there is no evidence of negative marking, so spend the final seconds guessing any unreached items.

**For the UCAT**, the same speed logic applies at 30 seconds a question, weighted toward numerical, spatial and attention (no verbal) — practise data interpretation.

**For the EPP**, answer **authentically and consistently.** The EPP runs faking and consistency detectors (§12.8); answering as your genuine work-self, reading each prompt properly (rushing trips the inconsistency scale), is both the honest and the higher-scoring approach. There are no "right" answers, but there is a right *strategy*: steady authenticity. **For Emotify and Cognify**, a couple of familiarisation runs to remove interface-shock is the main legitimate edge; Emotify rewards genuine emotional reasoning, Cognify rewards fast, accurate fluid reasoning.

**Worked example (CCAT pacing).** *Weak approach:* start at question 1 and grind sequentially, spend two minutes on a hard spatial item at #7, run out of time at #30 having left the easier verbal items at #40–50 untouched. *Strong approach:* move fast, bank every question you can answer in under 20 seconds, flag-and-skip the two or three that resist, and reach the end (or near it) having harvested all the low-cost points — then spend leftover seconds on the flagged items and guess the rest. The score difference is almost entirely pacing, not ability.

## 12.8 Integrity monitoring — what Criteria actually detects

Criteria's integrity model has three layers, and it is comparatively transparent about them.

**Layer 1 — design-based security (always on).** The **dynamic item bank** means no two candidates get identical items, deterring answer-sharing, and **detailed session logging** records time-in-assessment, connectivity and actions. `[VENDOR]`

**Layer 2 — the CCAT "Invalid Result" prior-exposure detector.** The system flags a result as **Invalid** when it judges that a candidate has **seen that CCAT content before**, and it makes that judgement from **speed *and* accuracy** — moving through the test "too quickly and without error" is treated as a sign of prior familiarity, and shows as a warning on the score report. `[VENDOR — CCAT Score Report Guide]` This is important, and its false-positive implication is discussed in §12.9.

**Layer 3 — optional AI proctoring (employer-enabled add-on).** A one-click proctoring option collects three signal types: **image capture** (periodic candidate photos to detect a substitute), **image analysis** (AI flags multiple faces or unauthorised devices), and **screen-and-audio sampling** (periodic screen grabs and ambient-audio snippets to detect second devices or outside help). Notably there is **no browser lockdown and no invasive install** — it "works within the standard assessment experience." The employer receives a **proctoring report of flagged incidents with supporting evidence** and decides what to do; candidates are stated to be **informed of monitoring before they begin** (the explicit consent flow is `[UNKNOWN]`). `[VENDOR — AI Proctoring page]`

**The EPP has its own validity scales.** Three of them: **Self-Enhancing** (faking-good), **Self-Critical** (faking-bad), and **Inconsistent Responding**. Trait scores may be **adjusted** for self-enhancing/critical tendencies, and an **Inconsistent Responding score of 3 or more flags the entire EPP as invalid.** `[VENDOR — EPP Score Report Guide]`

**How flags are actioned:** all of these surface to the **employer**, who decides — auto-reject, a proctored re-test, or ignore. There is **no evidence the candidate is told** they were flagged. `[INFERRED — vendor docs describe employer-facing flags only]`

## 12.9 How candidates commonly trip the system — including honestly

The distinctive false-positive risk here is one you should understand precisely, because it can catch exactly the strong candidate this guide is written for:

- **The CCAT "Invalid Result" heuristic can misfire on a genuinely excellent first-timer.** Because it infers prior exposure from **high speed combined with high accuracy**, a candidate who is simply very good — fast *and* correct on their first legitimate attempt — can pattern-match to "has seen this before." `[INFERRED from the vendor's own description — a real risk]` There is no clean way to "avoid" this without sandbagging (which you should never do); the protection is knowing it exists, sitting the test honestly, and — if flagged and rejected — being ready to request a **supervised re-test** (§12.11), which you would pass identically and which resolves the ambiguity in your favour.
- **EPP Inconsistent Responding (≥3) can flag an honest test-taker** who rushed, misread, has a language barrier, is neurodivergent, or genuinely holds a nuanced, context-dependent self-view. *Mitigation:* read every prompt properly; don't race.
- **EPP Self-Enhancing adjustment** can dock a genuinely confident, high-conscientiousness person answering truthfully. There is nothing to do but answer honestly and accept the model's calibration.
- **AI-proctoring false positives** are the familiar ones (Chapter 6.6): looking away to think, background noise or a family member, a poorly-lit or low-quality webcam, an unused second monitor — read as "multiple faces / unauthorised device / outside help." The absence of browser lockdown means fewer hard blocks, but the image/audio heuristics are probabilistic.
- **A mid-CCAT disconnection**, though recoverable via the Test Event ID, can create log anomalies; screenshot your Event ID if the connection is shaky.

Genuine cheating that is detected: reusing/leaked items (the Invalid Result detector), a proxy test-taker (image capture), and outside help (screen/audio sampling) — none worth the risk, especially given the disclosed on-site re-test.

## 12.10 How to be unambiguously clean

- **Sit the CCAT in one uninterrupted 15-minute block**, alone, in a quiet, well-lit room, on a stable connection, phone away — the test is short, so a single clean window is easy to arrange.
- **Have permitted scratch paper and a pen** ready for mental-maths working (no calculator), and confirm with the recruiter whether the sitting is proctored and whether paper is allowed.
- **Screenshot your Test Event ID** in case of disconnection.
- **On the EPP, don't rush** — reading each prompt is the single best protection against a false Inconsistent-Responding flag.
- **If AI proctoring is on**, front-light your face, keep the frame clear of other people, and disconnect an unused second monitor.
- **Request adjustments in advance** if a timed speeded test disadvantages you (Chapter 6.7). Criteria supports extended time on some tests, but there is **no public candidate-facing adjustments policy** `[UNKNOWN]`, so make the request explicit through the employer, early.

## 12.11 If you are flagged or rejected

Because a US vendor sits behind (usually) a US or US-linked employer, the applicable rights depend on where you and the hiring entity are — but if you are a candidate in the UK/EU, your data-protection rights travel with your data. Full detail in Chapter 6.7; the Criteria-specific points:

- **Ask for a supervised re-test.** This is the highest-value move if you suspect a **CCAT "Invalid Result"** flag caught you as an honest, fast test-taker — a supervised re-sit reproduces your score and resolves the flag. Criteria's own model explicitly contemplates on-site re-testing, so the ask is aligned with how the tool is designed to be used.
- **DSAR (UK GDPR Article 15).** If you're in the UK/EU, request your scores, the norm/job-family benchmark applied, any **integrity/proctoring flags and the Invalid-Result or EPP-validity markers**, any webcam captures, and the decision logic. Note Criteria is a **US company**, so your data is transferred to the US under **Standard Contractual Clauses / the UK IDTA** (whether Criteria self-certifies to the UK Extension of the EU-US Data Privacy Framework is `[UNKNOWN]`); a DSAR still runs through the **employer** as controller. `[VENDOR — DPA; INFERRED]`
- **Automated-decision rights.** If a UK/EU-based employer auto-rejected you solely on a CCAT cutoff, the **Articles 22A–22C safeguards** (UK GDPR as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — Chapter 6.7) let you seek human intervention and contest it. A US employer hiring in the US is outside UK GDPR, so this lever is jurisdiction-dependent.
- **Adjustments (Equality Act 2010 / ADA).** Route through the employer, in advance; a timed speeded CCAT is a clear case for extra time if you have a relevant condition.

**Template — supervised re-test request (Criteria-specific):**
> Subject: Assessment result — request to re-sit under supervision — [name], [role/ref]
> Dear [team], I completed the Criteria (CCAT) assessment for [role]. If my result was flagged (for example as an "invalid result") or fell short, I would welcome the opportunity to **re-sit under supervision / proctored conditions** so you can verify my genuine performance. I sat the test honestly and am confident a supervised re-test would reproduce my score. Thank you.

**Templates for the pre-assessment adjustment request and the DSAR** are in Chapter 6.7 (Templates A and C) and apply here unchanged.

## 12.12 Step-by-step: how to win this assessment

1. **T‑minus 7 days — diagnose.** Sit one full timed CCAT-style practice test (50 questions / 15 minutes) cold. You almost certainly won't finish — that's the point. Note your raw score and where you lost time.
2. **T‑6 to T‑2 — drill for speed.** Daily short sets focused on **mental arithmetic** (percentages, ratios, fractions, averages) and the recurring item types. Keep an error log tagged by cause (too slow / arithmetic slip / misread / didn't reach). Your losses cluster; fix the cluster.
3. **Practise skip-and-return** deliberately until moving on from a hard item feels automatic rather than like defeat.
4. **T‑2 — simulate.** One full timed run in your real environment; confirm you have paper and pen and a quiet 15-minute window.
5. **If a personality/EI/game stage is included**, do one familiarisation run of the EPP/Emotify/Cognify so the interface is not a surprise — but do **not** try to script the EPP; plan only to answer authentically and read each prompt.
6. **Day before / 30 minutes before.** Clear the desk, silence the phone, close other apps, front-light if proctored, water nearby. Screenshot the Test Event ID when you start.
7. **In-test decision rules.** Move fast; bank easy points first; skip-and-return on anything over ~20 seconds; never leave blanks at time-up; on the EPP, read every prompt and answer steadily.
8. **If it's a US-PE process**, recognise the CCAT may be weighted heavily and be a real gate — but ignore the "high-40s" folklore as a target; aim to maximise clean, fast accuracy, not a mythical number.
9. **Debrief.** Note the actual products you were sent (CCAT? UCAT? EPP? Cognify? Alcami?), timings and whether proctoring was on — for next time and to know which chapter techniques applied.
