## 13.1 Snapshot

Talent Q is the assessment business with the best origin story in this guide: it was founded in **2006 by Roger Holdsworth**, the occupational psychologist who co-founded **Saville & Holdsworth Ltd — SHL itself** — and left in 2002 to build what he saw as the next generation: **tests that adapt to the candidate taking them.** `[INDEPENDENT — corroborated corporate history]` Adaptivity was not a feature bolted on later; it was the founding thesis, and it still defines how these tests feel to sit.

The corporate chain since: **Hay Group acquired Talent Q in April 2014** (they had been partners since 2010), and **Korn Ferry acquired Hay Group in 2015** — so today the tests are administered by Korn Ferry, the listed executive-search and organisational-consulting firm, with assessments available in 40+ languages across 125+ countries. `[INDEPENDENT — BusinessWire primary press release; Umbrex]` Unlike SHL or Aon, assessment is one arm of a much larger consulting business — which shapes why firms buy it (§13.3). Candidate-facing materials in the 2025–26 cycle still use the "Korn Ferry Talent Q" name and the Elements/Aspects/Dimensions product names; whether Korn Ferry has internally folded these into its "KF4D"/Korn Ferry Assess platform is not publicly documented. `[INFERRED; UNKNOWN]`

For a UK finance candidate this chapter is directly practical: **Citi runs Talent Q Elements as its online assessment**, and **UBS runs a Korn Ferry-built bespoke "Culture Match" situational judgement test** for its Junior and Graduate Talent programmes — both verified against 2024–26-dated sources. `[PREP-VENDOR — §13.11]` Barclays, Lloyds and RBS/NatWest also appear on Talent Q client lists (with the usual volatility caveats). Each Elements test runs roughly 12–25 minutes; Dimensions about 25 minutes untimed.

## 13.2 Why this test exists

The generic rationale is the same GMA story as Chapters 1 and 2 — cognitive ability as the best cheap predictor of performance, with the honest caveat that the famous r ≈ 0.51 has been revised toward **r ≈ 0.31** by Sackett and colleagues' 2022 re-analysis (Chapter 6.3). `[INDEPENDENT]` What is distinctive here is *why Holdsworth thought a second company was needed after SHL*: **adaptive testing**. A fixed-form test wastes most of its questions confirming what the first few answers already showed; an adaptive test homes in on your actual level, so it can be **shorter** (12–15 questions rather than 24–30) while claiming equal or better measurement precision at the individual's level. `[INDEPENDENT — founding history; psychometric rationale INFERRED, standard IRT logic]` Shorter tests also mean better candidate experience and less exposure of any single item — both of which employers now buy explicitly.

One warning on vendor validity claims: a widely-recycled line says Talent Q management assessments were "96% more precise than the assessment centre average." As stated, this is **garbled marketing with no locatable methodology — treat it as unusable.** `[VENDOR-marketing, uncorroborated]` No independent BPS review or published reliability coefficients were located in this research pass (`[UNKNOWN]` — the BPS reviews of Elements and Dimensions are believed to exist behind the BPS Psychological Testing Centre; a gap logged in the register).

## 13.3 Why a firm chooses Korn Ferry specifically

Four things differentiate the offer. First, **adaptivity plus brevity**: a three-test Elements battery takes under an hour total, against noticeably longer fixed-form batteries — a real candidate-experience and completion-rate argument at volume. Second, the **per-item clock** (§13.4), which Korn Ferry positions as fairer (nobody can hoard time) and more cheat-resistant (little value in looking things up when each item's window is a minute). `[PREP-VENDOR/INFERRED]` Third — and probably decisive for the banks that use it — the **consulting bundle**: Korn Ferry wraps assessment inside its "whole-person" KF4D model (competencies, traits, drivers, experiences), its success-profiles library, leadership consulting and the Hay Group pay-data legacy. A firm already buying Korn Ferry search or consulting gets assessment as an integrated add-on rather than a separate vendor relationship. `[VENDOR/INFERRED]` Fourth, **bespoke builds**: the UBS Culture Match is a custom, values-keyed SJT — evidence that Korn Ferry will write firm-specific instruments, not just resell the library. `[PREP-VENDOR]`

## 13.4 What the assessment actually is — full mechanics

### Elements — the adaptive ability tests (what Citi sends)

The defining mechanic, worth internalising before any numbers: **every question has its own clock.** There is no whole-test timer to manage, no banking time on easy items to spend on hard ones. When an item's window expires, you are moved on. `[PREP-VENDOR — consistent across sources]` The tests are **adaptive**: answer correctly and the next item is harder. `[PREP-VENDOR + the founding design]`

| Test | Items | Per-item clock | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elements Verbal** | **15** | **75 s** for the first question on each passage, **60 s** for subsequent ones | Passage → statements to judge (true/false/cannot-say-style options) |
| **Elements Numerical** | **12** | **90 s** for the first question on each data table, **75 s** for subsequent ones | **4 tables × 3 questions**; multi-step extraction and calculation |
| **Elements Logical** | **12** | **~75 s** per question (~15–23 min) | Abstract/diagrammatic series — find what comes next |

`[PREP-VENDOR — JobTestPrep, PracticeAptitudeTests, GraduatesFirst, AssessmentDay; figures consistent]`

Two further quirks. The **answer-option sets are unusually large** on numerical and verbal items (often eight or more options), which makes blind guessing much weaker than on a 4-option SHL item — elimination before committing matters more here. `[PREP-VENDOR]` And the numerical test's *first-question-per-table* clock is longer precisely because it carries the table-reading load: the design expects you to digest the data set during question one and answer questions two and three faster. Calculator policy is not explicitly documented; calculators are generally understood to be permitted, but confirm with the recruiter. `[INFERRED; UNKNOWN]` Disconnect/resume behaviour is undocumented (`[UNKNOWN]`) — which, with per-item clocks, is a real reason to sit this on your most stable connection (§13.9).

### Aspects — the volume-hire line

**Aspects Ability** covers verbal, numerical and error-checking in short (~12-minute) tests aimed at high-volume and apprentice/entry hiring; **Aspects Styles** is an ~8-minute behavioural questionnaire mapping to **16 competencies**. Question counts are not published. `[PREP-VENDOR — GraduatesFirst; counts [UNKNOWN]]`

### Dimensions — the personality questionnaire

**160 statements**, presented in **blocks of four**. You **rate** each statement from "completely untrue" to "very true" — and here is the distinctive mechanism — **if you give two or more statements in a block the same rating, you must then rank them** against each other. `[PREP-VENDOR — consistent]` This "rate-then-rank" hybrid is a semi-ipsative design: you can *try* to rate everything "very true," but the forced ranking immediately makes you differentiate, which is exactly what defeats uniform fake-good responding (the same family of defence as SHL's forced-choice OPQ — Chapter 6.3). It is **untimed**, typically ~25 minutes, and reports across three domains: **People and Relationships, Tasks and Projects, Drives and Emotions.** Used at later stages and for development as well as selection. `[PREP-VENDOR]`

### Drives, and the leadership tier

**Drives** is the motivation questionnaire in the suite (public detail is thin — `[UNKNOWN]` on counts/timing). Above the early-careers tier sit the **Korn Ferry Assessment of Leadership Potential (KFALP)** and the **KF4D** whole-person framework — executive-level instruments you will not meet in a graduate funnel. `[VENDOR — peripheral here]`

### The UBS Culture Match (bespoke SJT)

What UBS sends its Junior/Graduate Talent applicants is a Korn Ferry-built **situational judgement test**: **18 scenarios**, each with **three possible responses**, from which you pick the **most effective and the least effective**. It is **untimed**, taking roughly 20 minutes, and is keyed to UBS's values and behaviours. `[PREP-VENDOR — AssessmentDay UBS profile, TestHQ, JobTestPrep; 2024–26-dated]` UBS also runs separate cognitive assessments for some junior programmes. `[PREP-VENDOR]`

### A realistic walkthrough (Elements Numerical, Citi-style)

You click through from the invitation, confirm details, and see instructions stating the per-question time limits. The first table appears with question one and a 90-second countdown. You spend the first 20 seconds reading the *table* — headings, units, footnotes — not the answer options, because two more questions on this table are coming. You answer; questions two and three arrive with 75-second clocks each, faster now that you know the table. After three questions the next table loads. Difficulty climbs as you answer well. Twelve questions and roughly a quarter of an hour later, you submit. You will usually see no score; Citi's recruiters see your performance against their benchmark. `[INFERRED from documented mechanics]`

## 13.5 Is it tailored to the role?

The core instruments — Elements, Aspects, Dimensions — are the **same for every buyer**; tailoring happens in the **benchmark** (which norm/benchmark group your performance is compared against, employer-chosen) and in Korn Ferry's **success profiles** overlay that maps results to role and level. `[PREP-VENDOR/INFERRED]` The important exception is the **bespoke SJT tier**: the UBS Culture Match is custom-written and custom-keyed to UBS's values — so for that instrument, the "right answers" are literally defined by the firm, and studying UBS's published values and behaviours is the legitimate, high-leverage preparation (the same logic as Cappfinity's firm-keyed SSTs, Chapter 5). As always, the practical consequence of benchmark-level tailoring: the identical Elements performance can clear one firm's bar and miss another's.

## 13.6 How they screen and filter — scoring, norms, cut-offs, ranking

The honest headline, from the best-available source, is refreshingly plain: **"There is no fixed pass mark for Talent Q assessments. Employers compare your performance to a benchmark or other candidates."** `[PREP-VENDOR — GraduatesFirst FAQ, direct]` The adaptive engine produces an ability estimate (a function of how many *and how hard* the items you answered correctly were — Chapter 6.3 on IRT), which is then compared against the employer's chosen benchmark or norm. Talent Q's traditional reporting used sten-type standardised scales, but the exact current reporting scale, the norm-group catalogue, the recruiter dashboard contents, the retake policy and score portability are all **undocumented in public sources** — `[UNKNOWN]`, logged in the gap register. Assume the usual: employer-set thresholds, no retake within a cycle, scores not portable between firms. `[INFERRED]`

Two scoring consequences of the design are worth spelling out. Because the test is adaptive, **raw "number right" is meaningless to compare between candidates** — a candidate fed harder items can score higher with fewer correct answers. And because each item has its own clock with no carryover, **speed above the per-item requirement buys you nothing** — there is no bonus for answering in 30 seconds what the clock gives you 75 for. The optimal cadence is therefore *use the whole window when you need it, and never exceed it* — a genuinely different optimum from SHL, where saved seconds compound.

**The funnel point.** At Citi the Elements battery is the classic OA gate — necessary to clear, unlikely to be the tightest cut relative to the CV screen and interviews (Chapter 6.8's argument applies). The UBS Culture Match sits even earlier and is best understood as a **values-alignment screen**: untimed, judgement-based, and preparable by studying the firm.

## 13.7 How to score well — legitimate, specific, actionable

**Recalibrate your clock instincts first.** If you have prepared on SHL-style whole-test clocks, the Talent Q optimum is different in three ways:

1. **You cannot bank time, so stop trying to be fast.** Use each item's full window whenever it helps. Rushing to "save time" saves nothing and costs accuracy.
2. **Never dwell past the clock.** When the window ends you are moved on regardless — so commit to an answer inside every window. With large option sets, spend the last ten seconds **eliminating** the implausible options and choosing among survivors rather than blind-guessing.
3. **Front-load the reading on numerical.** The 90-second first question per table is your table-study budget for all three questions. Read headings, units and footnotes *before* attacking question one; questions two and three then run at a comfortable 75 seconds.

**On adaptivity:** start carefully — early answers steer the difficulty path, and (as with all IRT tests) an early careless error costs more than a late one. And when the questions get harder, **that is the test telling you you're doing well.** Candidates who don't know this read rising difficulty as failure and panic; you now can't be one of them.

**Verbal:** strict passage-only discipline — "cannot say" whenever the passage doesn't entail the statement, no outside knowledge (full method in Chapter 6.4). **Logical:** run the dimensions checklist (shape, number, position, shading, orientation) from Chapter 6.4 within each 75-second window.

**Dimensions:** the rate-then-rank design makes uniform fake-good mechanically self-defeating — rate every statement "very true" and the forced ranking makes you differentiate anyway, while inconsistency across 160 items degrades the profile. The winning approach is the one from Chapters 1, 5 and 8: **know the role's competencies, answer as your genuine relevant self, decisively and consistently**, and let the extremes carry your real strengths.

**UBS Culture Match specifically:** read UBS's published values and behaviours ("Pillars, Principles and Behaviors" in current materials) before you sit; answer *both* poles of each scenario deliberately (most effective *and* least effective are separately scored decisions); and apply the SJT keying logic from Chapter 6.4 — ownership and constructive, proportionate action rank high; avoidance and blame rank low.

**Worked example (Elements Numerical pacing).** *Weak:* reads question one's options first, hunts the table reactively, answers at 85 seconds, then meets questions two and three still not understanding the table's structure — misses one to the clock. *Strong:* spends the first 25 seconds of question one mapping the table (units in £'000s, columns are quarters, footnote defines "net"), answers at 70 seconds, then clears questions two and three in 40 seconds each with time to eliminate distractor options. Same maths ability; the difference is spending the long first clock on the *table*, not the question.

## 13.8 Integrity monitoring — what Korn Ferry actually detects

The honest answer: **very little is publicly documented, and the observable posture is low-surveillance.** Typical Talent Q deployments are **unproctored**; no webcam layer, no lockdown browser, and — unlike SHL — **no documented verification re-test model** appears anywhere in the fetched vendor or prep material. `[UNKNOWN — undocumented; absence noted across sources]` The security that exists is **design-based**: adaptive item selection means candidates see different questions at different difficulties (answer-sharing is low-value), and per-item clocks leave little time for looking things up. `[INFERRED]` Whether Dimensions carries social-desirability or consistency scales is plausible but undocumented. `[UNKNOWN]`

For a candidate who has previously been flagged elsewhere, this is one of the friendlier providers in the guide: like Aon and the game-based vendors (Chapter 6.6's "Tier A"), there is essentially **no webcam surface to generate a false flag.** The risks that remain are technical and design-related, not surveillance-related.

## 13.9 How candidates commonly trip the system — including honestly

- **A connection wobble during an item's window is the big one.** With a whole-test clock, a 20-second lag costs 20 seconds; with a per-item clock it can cost the *entire item*, and resume behaviour is undocumented. `[INFERRED; UNKNOWN]` Sit this on your most stable connection, wired if possible, and note the time and item if anything glitches so you can report it immediately.
- **Adaptive anxiety.** Rising difficulty is success, not failure. Candidates who don't know this spiral mid-test.
- **SHL-trained pacing habits** — rushing to bank time that cannot be banked, or dwelling past a clock that will not wait — are honest, well-drilled behaviours that this design punishes.
- **Neurodivergence and the format.** Per-item clocks are unforgiving of processing-speed differences (no buffer to redistribute), and Dimensions' forced ranking can be genuinely difficult if your honest answer is "these are equally true of me." Both are proper grounds for a reasonable-adjustment request — made in advance (§13.10–13.11).
- **Guessing on large option sets** — an honest time-pressure response that is much weaker here than on 4-option tests; eliminate first.

There is no documented evidence of integrity flags, demographic disparities or proctoring false positives specific to Talent Q — consistent with there being little monitoring to misfire. `[UNKNOWN/absence]`

## 13.10 How to be unambiguously clean

The checklist is short because the surveillance is minimal:

- **Best connection you have** — wired if possible; close everything else; the per-item clock makes stability worth more here than on any other provider in this guide.
- **One uninterrupted sitting per test**, quiet room, phone away.
- **Confirm calculator and paper** with the recruiter for Elements Numerical (undocumented policy — get it in writing).
- **Request adjustments in advance** if timed-per-item formats or forced ranking disadvantage you (Equality Act 2010, Chapter 6.7 Template A). Note that *how* extra time is implemented on adaptive per-item clocks is itself undocumented — ask the employer to confirm the arrangement in writing, which both fixes their knowledge under the Act and tells you what to expect on screen. `[UNKNOWN mechanics — the request itself is the protection]`
- **Answer Dimensions authentically and consistently** — the only "integrity" signal plausibly present is internal consistency.

## 13.11 If you are flagged or rejected

A rejection here is almost always a **benchmark outcome, not an integrity flag** — there is little monitoring to be flagged by. The route is the standard one from Chapter 6.7:

- **Feedback query** to the recruiter; some firms share band-level feedback.
- **DSAR (UK GDPR Article 15)** to the **employer** (controller; Korn Ferry processes on their behalf — exact controller/processor split undocumented, so start with the employer): request your **ability estimate/scores and the benchmark or norm group applied**, any **Dimensions profile**, the **Culture Match keying outcome** if applicable, and the **logic of any automated decision**. One month, free.
- **Automated-decision safeguards:** if the sift was solely automated against a benchmark with no human review, invoke **Articles 22A–22C UK GDPR (as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025)** — representations, human intervention, contest (Chapter 6.7, Template B).
- **Adjustments after the fact:** if per-item timing or forced ranking disadvantaged you and no adjustment was in place, a disability-related reconsideration request is the strongest lever — stronger still if you requested in advance and it wasn't actioned.

**Template — pre-assessment adjustment request (Talent Q-specific):**
> Subject: Reasonable adjustment request — [name], [role/ref]
> Dear [team], I've been invited to complete Korn Ferry Talent Q assessments for [role]. I have [condition], which the **per-question time limits** [and/or the forced-ranking format] place me at a substantial disadvantage under. Under the Equality Act 2010 I request [extended per-question time / an alternative format], and I'd be grateful if you could confirm in writing how the adjustment will be applied within the adaptive test before I sit it. I can provide documentation. Thank you.

## 13.12 Step-by-step: how to win this assessment

1. **T‑minus 10 days — learn the format cold.** The per-item clock and adaptivity are the whole game. Sit one Talent Q-style practice test for each Elements module you'll face, specifically to feel the no-banking rhythm.
2. **T‑9 to T‑4 — drill within windows.** Practise numerical table-reads with a 90-second first-question discipline (table first, question second); verbal cannot-say logic at 60–75 seconds; logical series with the dimensions checklist. Error log by cause, per Chapter 6.5.
3. **T‑7 — firm research (UBS route).** If the Culture Match is coming, study UBS's published values/behaviours and map the most/least keying logic onto them.
4. **T‑4 — logistics.** Confirm calculator/paper policy in writing; send any adjustment request now (the adaptive-clock mechanics make early confirmation doubly important).
5. **T‑2 — full simulation** on your real machine and best connection, at your sharpest time of day.
6. **Day of.** Wired or strongest connection; everything else closed; paper and (if confirmed) calculator ready; quiet room.
7. **In-test rules.** Table before question on numericals. Use the full window when needed; commit before it expires, always. Eliminate before guessing. Rising difficulty = winning. On Dimensions: authentic, decisive, consistent; on Culture Match: judge both poles deliberately.
8. **Rolling-deadline note.** Citi and UBS both sift on rolling timelines — ready-and-early beats marginally-sharper-and-late (Chapter 6.8).
9. **Debrief.** Record which modules you were sent, the actual clocks, and whether anything glitched — for the re-sit case and for your next application.
