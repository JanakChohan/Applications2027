## The official process, in Barclays' own words

Barclays publishes its early-careers process on its careers site, and it is worth reading exactly as written because it is shorter than most guides suggest. `[EMPLOYER — search.jobs.barclays/internship-graduate-application, accessed 2026-08-01]`

| Stage | What Barclays says |
|---|---|
| **1. Your Application** | Application form on the careers site, with "hints and tips" resources offered. |
| **2. Initial Assessments** | *"Two short interactive assessments, which will tell us how you might perform in the workplace, and your typical or preferred ways of working."* Completable on **mobile phone or tablet**. **Technical programmes (e.g. Explorer Developer) get an additional technical skills assessment.** |
| **3. Assessment Centre** | In person **or virtual**. Comprises *"two or three stages, including a motivational interview with a member of the leadership team."* Assessors evaluate "relevant strengths, behaviours, and technical skills required for specific roles." |

The programmes Barclays runs: **Internships** (summer *and* off-cycle), **Graduate** programmes (with **Explorer** and **Expert** pathways), the **Discovery Programme** (Barclays' insight/spring-week equivalent, for students within two years of graduating), and **Apprenticeships**. `[EMPLOYER]`

### The discrepancy worth knowing about

**Barclays' official three-stage list does not include a standalone video interview.** Prep vendors near-universally insert a one-way video interview as a fourth stage, and one 2026/27 guide does confirm a video stage exists without naming its vendor. `[PREP-VENDOR]`

This is unresolved, and it matters for planning. `[UNKNOWN]` The most likely readings are that a video component sits *inside* what Barclays calls the assessment centre ("two or three stages"), or that it appears for some programmes and not others. **Plan for the possibility of a video interview, and treat your invitation email as authoritative.**

## What the two interactive assessments actually are

Barclays' own phrasing decomposes neatly: **"how you might perform in the workplace"** = an ability/cognitive measure; **"your typical or preferred ways of working"** = a behavioural/personality/working-style measure. One of each. `[EMPLOYER + INFERRED]`

A current prep-vendor guide (2026/27 cycle) fills in the component detail, listing across the online stages: **Numerical Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, a Values and Mindset assessment, and a Personality assessment**, plus a **coding/computer-science assessment for technical roles** — branded, it says, as **"Business Insight Stage 1" and "Business Insight Stage 2"**, totalling **60–90 minutes**. `[PREP-VENDOR — GraduatesFirst, accessed 2026-08-01]`

**Reconcile these carefully.** Barclays says *two* assessments; the prep vendor lists *five components*. The most plausible reading is that the prep vendor is enumerating **constructs across the whole funnel**, not five standalone tests at stage two — with the reasoning constructs bundled into one interactive cognitive measure and the values/mindset/personality constructs bundled into the second. `[INFERRED]` Do not expect five separate tests; do expect both reasoning and working-style content.

## The vendor question — resolved as far as the evidence allows

Existing research on Barclays pointed two ways: **SHL** and **pymetrics**. Here is the honest resolution.

| Claim | Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Barclays names **no vendor at all** | `[EMPLOYER]` careers page, accessed 2026-08-01 | **Decisive on what is *confirmed*** |
| "Typically delivered via **SHL or Cappfinity**" | `[PREP-VENDOR]` — GraduatesFirst 2026/27 guide, current cycle | Strongest dated third-party attribution |
| SHL listed as Barclays' vendor (cognitive + personality + "mindset") | `[PREP-VENDOR]` — carried in earlier project research | Consistent with the above |
| **pymetrics** as Barclays' vendor | `[PREP-VENDOR]` — single/weak; pymetrics was absorbed into **Harver in 2022**; no dated 2025–26 source found | **Weak — likely stale or a generic-list artefact** |

There is also a suggestive linguistic hint. Barclays' own copy says **"two short *interactive* assessments"** completable on **mobile or tablet** — and SHL's current cognitive line is literally branded **Verify Interactive**, is explicitly mobile-capable, and uses interactive drag-and-drop item formats. `[INFERRED — suggestive, not proof]`

**The honest verdict: the best-evidenced current attribution is SHL, possibly alongside Cappfinity for the values/strengths element. The pymetrics attribution appears outdated. But Barclays confirms nothing, so this cannot be stated as fact.** Your invitation email will name the tool — that is the only authoritative source for *your* assessment, and it outranks this report.

**Practically, does it matter?** Less than you'd think, with one important exception. The reasoning constructs (numerical, verbal, logical) and a working-style questionnaire are prepared for in much the same way whoever supplies them. The exception is **guessing policy**, which differs sharply between vendors: SHL has no negative marking (never leave a blank), while Aon/cut-e deducts for wrong answers (eliminate-then-commit, or leave blank). If your invitation names Aon, switch strategies — see the companion Aon report.

## Selection criteria Barclays states publicly

Barclays says candidates must "share the same key attributes and **Values** as Barclays colleagues throughout our business," and its assessors evaluate "relevant **strengths, behaviours**, and technical skills required for specific roles." `[EMPLOYER]`

That language — values, strengths, behaviours — is exactly what a values/mindset assessment and a competency-keyed assessment centre are built to measure. **Read Barclays' published Values and behaviours before you sit the second assessment**; Chapter 11 turns this into a concrete preparation step.

## Two policies you should know about

**AI tools are banned in interviews.** Barclays states: *"Third-party AI tools are not permitted during your Barclays interview"* — explicitly including transcription bots and meeting assistants — and breach means the **interview is terminated and the application withdrawn**. `[EMPLOYER]` This is a recent (2025-era) addition and it is unusually explicit. Take it literally: no note-taking bots, no meeting assistants running in the background, nothing joining your video call but you.

**Adjustments are actively invited.** Barclays states it welcomes candidates requiring adjustments and **encourages disclosure at application stage**, naming **extra time on assessments** and **offline alternatives to online assessments** among available accommodations, and stating that disclosed information is kept confidential. `[EMPLOYER]` The named "offline alternative" is notable — it is a meaningful option if online proctored testing is a barrier for you. Barclays does not publish a request deadline or a named adjustments contact; a resourcing helpdesk is referenced. `[UNKNOWN]` **Disclose early — the duty to adjust bites once the employer knows.**

## Where the biggest cut happens

**Unknown for Barclays specifically**, and no one should tell you otherwise. Barclays publishes no pass rates; a prep vendor explicitly notes that "Barclays does not publicly share pass rates, as **outcomes vary based on role and competition**" — itself a useful hint that a **rank/top-down sift** is in play rather than a fixed line (Chapter 7). `[PREP-VENDOR]`

What can be said, as sector-level inference rather than Barclays fact: for competitive UK banking programmes the **application/CV screen and the assessment centre typically cut harder than the online stage**, which functions as a screen-out gate. And because Barclays runs **rolling** recruitment — including explicitly off-cycle internships — **applying early is part of your threshold.** `[INFERRED]`
