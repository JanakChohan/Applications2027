# Chapter 5 — Situational Judgement, Video Interviews and Coding

Beyond cognitive ability and personality, SHL sells three further instrument families that a finance applicant may meet: **situational judgement tests**, the **Smart Interview** video range, and **coding/technical assessments**. Each is scored on entirely different logic from the Verify tests, and each rewards different preparation.

## Situational judgement tests

**What they are.** Short workplace scenarios followed by candidate response options, which you either **rate for effectiveness** or **rank**, or where you pick the **most and least effective** action. SHL builds SJTs against its **Universal Competency Framework** and, for larger clients, writes scenarios keyed to the employer's own competencies and values (Chapter 2).

**How they're scored.** Not norm-referenced in the cognitive sense but **keyed**: subject-matter experts at the employer (and/or SHL's IO psychologists) define which responses are effective, and your choices are scored against that key, usually producing competency sub-scores. There is no universal "right answer" — the key encodes *that employer's* view of good judgement, which is why the same answer can score differently at two firms.

**The independent evidence, honestly.** SJTs add only modest incremental validity over cognitive ability and personality — meta-analytic estimates put the increment at roughly **ΔR ≈ 0.03–0.08** — and they are **transparent and coachable**: candidates can often identify the intended answer, and coaching measurably lifts scores. `[INDEPENDENT — SJT review literature]` That is *good news for a prepared candidate*: unlike a cognitive test, the SJT genuinely rewards research.

**How to score well.**
- **Read the employer's published values and competencies first.** This is the single highest-leverage step, because the key is derived from them.
- **The reliable keying pattern:** responses showing **ownership, direct constructive communication, and proportionate escalation** key high. **Avoidance, blame-shifting, and reflexive escalation to a manager as a first move** key low. So does unilateral action that bypasses people who should be involved.
- **Answer both poles** when asked for most *and* least effective — they are separately scored decisions, and candidates routinely answer only the first.
- **Stay consistent across scenarios.** SJT and behavioural instruments run consistency checks; answering one scenario as a diplomat and the next as a steamroller reads as incoherent judgement.
- **Answer as the professional the firm describes, not as your unfiltered self and not cynically.** The middle path — your genuine judgement, calibrated to their stated framework — is both honest and highest-scoring.

## Smart Interview — the video range

SHL's video products come in three flavours:

- **Smart Interview On Demand** — asynchronous, one-way recorded video. You receive preset questions (drawn from SHL's bank or written by the employer) and record answers in your own time. This is the one most graduate candidates meet.
- **Smart Interview Live** — a real-time, structured video interview with a human interviewer, run through SHL's platform.
- **Smart Interview Live Coding** — a live interview with an integrated compiler for technical roles.

**How On Demand is scored.** Employer-configured: responses may be **human-reviewed** by recruiters, **AI-scored**, or both. SHL has offered optional AI scoring of spoken content and — historically — facial expression, voice tonality and body language. `[VENDOR]` **Facial and paralinguistic scoring is contentious across the whole industry**: the dominant video vendor, HireVue, publicly abandoned facial analysis in 2021 after its own data scientist reported that non-verbal signals contributed only about **0.25%** of predictive power relative to answer content. SHL's *current* documented stance on facial/emotion analysis is something this report flags for direct confirmation with the employer rather than asserting. `[UNKNOWN — verify with employer; see Chapter 12]`

The practical implication is unchanged either way: **content is overwhelmingly what is scored**, so preparation should go into substance, not performance.

**How to perform well.** Full method in the companion guide (Chapter 8 there and 6.4); the essentials:
- **Structure every answer** with STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) or CARL (Context, Action, Result, Learning).
- **Lead with a headline sentence** that answers the question directly, then tell the story — reviewers skim and transcript scoring weights early content.
- **Prepare 6–8 competency stories** (teamwork, leadership, resilience, a failure and what you learned, commercial awareness, "why this firm/division"); most questions are recombinations of these.
- **Fill most of the answer window** with substance — a 30-second answer to a two-minute window scores what it deserves.
- **Do the practice questions**, front-light your face, put the camera at eye level, look into the lens, and if you fumble without a re-record, reset in a phrase and continue.

## Coding and technical assessments

For technology, quant and some operations streams, SHL offers **coding assessments** and **Smart Interview Live Coding** (a real-time compiler environment supporting one-to-one, panel and group formats). `[VENDOR]` These are auto-scored on correctness — typically against hidden test cases — often with efficiency (time/space complexity) weighted, and with anti-plagiarism checks standard.

**How to perform well.** Read all constraints before writing; produce the straightforward correct solution *before* optimising; test the boundary cases the hidden tests target (empty input, maximum size, negatives, duplicates); manage the clock across questions rather than perfecting one. And never paste from external sources — similarity detection is routine on coding platforms, and it is one of the few places where a genuine integrity flag is easy to earn.
