# The Aon (cut-e) Assessment Deep-Dive

### Every instrument in the catalogue — what it is, exactly how it is marked, how to win it, and what stops you cheating on it

---

## Why this report exists

Assessment vendors profit from opacity. Aon Assessment Solutions — still known to most candidates by its pre-2017 name, **cut-e** — publishes almost nothing about how its tests are scored, what the pass thresholds are, or how its integrity systems work. Candidates sit tests whose marking rules they are never told, get rejected by algorithms whose logic is confidential, and fill the vacuum with forum folklore and the marketing of practice-test sellers. This report exists to **shrink that information asymmetry** as far as the public record allows — and to be honest, line by line, about where the public record stops.

Two subjects get emphatic priority throughout, because they are the two the vendor documents least and candidates need most:

1. **How the marking actually works** — what earns points, what loses them (Aon's ability tests carry **negative marking**, which most candidates don't know and which inverts the guessing strategy they learned on SHL), how raw performance becomes a percentile, and what "score" even means on a game with no visible score.
2. **The anti-cheating architecture** — what cut-e's design makes impossible, what its optional monitoring detects, what a re-test can expose, and what an honest candidate can be wrongly flagged by.

## Who Aon is (one paragraph)

cut-e was founded in Hamburg in 2002 and acquired by **Aon plc in 2017**, becoming Aon Assessment Solutions. Its design philosophy is distinct in the industry: **very short tests under extreme time pressure**, drawn from **item banks that generate a different test for every candidate**, delivered through its **mapTQ** platform. In UK finance it is a first-rank gatekeeper: **Morgan Stanley** (since ~2019), **Deutsche Bank**, **UBS** (alongside Korn Ferry) and **BNP Paribas** all deploy cut-e instruments in early-careers funnels. `[PREP-VENDOR — corroborated across sources; full mapping in the companion guide]`

## How to read this report

Each instrument gets its own section with the same skeleton: **What it is → How it is marked → How to win → Anti-cheat notes → Quirks and adjustments.** Part IV then consolidates the two priority subjects — the marking rules across all instruments in one table, and the full anti-cheating architecture — plus a comparison of practice resources and a **confidence register** listing every estimate and unknown in one place.

**Evidence tags** appear on every substantive claim: `[VENDOR]` Aon's own claim; `[INDEPENDENT]` peer-reviewed/independent; `[CANDIDATE, n=X]` candidate testimony with a count of agreeing reports; `[PREP-VENDOR]` commercial practice-test sellers — often right on format, commercially biased, and prone to stating confidential figures as fact; `[INFERRED]` reasoning from first principles, shown; `[UNKNOWN]` not publicly available, with the reason and best proxy. **No number in this report is invented.** Where the marking formula or a cut-off is confidential — as most are — you will see `[UNKNOWN]` and a reasoned range, never a fabricated figure. A clearly-labelled estimate is useful; a confident fabrication is worse than useless.

**One boundary:** this report teaches understanding and legitimate preparation. It contains no live test items, no answer keys, and no evasion techniques — the anti-cheating chapter is written so you understand the system and never trip it honestly, not so you can beat it dishonestly.
