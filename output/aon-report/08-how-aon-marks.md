## The five rules this table implies

**1. On the classic ability tests, the marking is +1 / −0.5 / 0 — so guessing policy is everything.** The −0.5 penalty (documented verbatim in Aon's own candidate instructions for numerical and verbal, with negative marking confirmed-but-unquantified for cls and prep-vendor-supported family-wide) is calibrated so that a **blind guess on a three-option item has exactly zero expected value**: ⅓(+1) + ⅔(−0.5) = 0. `[INFERRED — arithmetic from the official formula]` The operating rules that follow:
   - **Eliminate one option and the guess turns profitable** (EV +0.25 between two remaining options). Commit.
   - **Can't eliminate anything? Leave it blank.** Blanks officially score zero — they are a *tool*, not a failure.
   - **Never spray answers at time-out.** On Aon (unlike SHL) the end-of-test scramble to fill every remaining item is score-neutral at best and actively harmful with any option-count above three.
   - Two wrong answers erase one correct one. Accuracy is literally worth double what it looks like.

**2. On adaptive instruments, the penalty is your ceiling, not your total.** The games and lst punish errors by **demoting you to easier, lower-value content** — and recovery is slow ("the more questions you get wrong, the harder it is to recover"). Early accuracy therefore buys more than late speed, everywhere the ladder exists.

**3. On judgement and personality instruments, there are no points to lose — only a profile to fit.** chatAssess is keyed to *the employer's* values; shapes/views/squares/ADEPT-15 match you against role or culture profiles. Maximal answering is either impossible by format (zero-sum allocations, forced-choice pairs) or self-defeating (non-credible saint profiles). The scoring rewards **consistency and authenticity aimed at the role** — which is why the "how to win" advice for these is behavioural, not tactical.

**4. Every score becomes a percentile against a norm group you don't choose.** The mapTQ login flow collects your country, age band, education and occupational area **specifically to place you in a norm group** `[VENDOR-VIA-CLIENT]`; adaptive tests output stable percentile estimates; and the employer then applies its own threshold. **There is no universal pass mark anywhere in the catalogue.** The prep-vendor cut-off folklore (50th–80th percentile depending on competitiveness) remains estimate-grade — see the companion guide's Chapter 2 for the full treatment and the register in Chapter 11 here.

**5. Batteries are multi-hurdle.** A finance deployment typically stacks 3–5 instruments (e.g. Morgan Stanley: numerical + verbal + logical + SJT within 48 hours of application). Each module is normed and thresholded separately in the standard configuration — a stellar numerical does not rescue a failed verbal — so preparation must cover the whole battery, weighted toward your weakest module. `[PREP-VENDOR + INFERRED, per the companion guide]`

## What the recruiter sees

A dashboard of **per-module percentiles**, any SJT competency sub-scores, personality/values profiles against the role model, and — where enabled — proctoring output (Chapter 9). Candidates typically see none of it. Your one lever for transparency after the fact is a **UK GDPR Article 15 subject-access request** through the employer, which can legitimately ask for your raw and scaled scores, the norm group applied, and any threshold outcome — the companion guide's Chapter 6.7 carries the template.

## The honest boundary of this chapter

The −0.5 figure is documented for one client deployment of two tests; whether it is universal or configurable, and every other magnitude in the catalogue, is `[UNKNOWN]` and logged in Chapter 11. Nothing in this chapter is invented; where the table says unknown, that is the state of the public record — and the strategy rules above are chosen to be **robust to every reported variant** of the unknown parameters.
