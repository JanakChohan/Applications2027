# Chapter 3 — Speed & Special-Skills Tests: eql, e3+, mt, tmt, mtu, stm and the Full Catalogue

> ### ⚡ Naming correction that matters
> Candidate folklore often calls Aon's multitasking test "**scales tmt**". **That is wrong.** The vendor catalogue is unambiguous: **scales tmt is "Applied Numeracy / Calculating Capacity"** — a numerical-intensity test — while the **multitasking test is "scales mt"**, with aviation (**mt avi**) and driving (**mt drv**) variants. `[VENDOR catalogue entries — page titles/snippets; JobTestPrep independently agrees]` Any prep material telling you "tmt = multitasking" is mislabelled.

## scales eql — mental arithmetic

**What it is.** Five minutes of pure numeracy: equations with **missing numbers or operators** which you complete to make the equation true ("fill in the gaps to get the correct answer"), with escalating difficulty and an **unlimited task supply** — the classic cut-e "do as many as you can" design. It is the non-game ancestor of the digitChallenge (Chapter 4 — the game is officially "based on scales eql"). `[PREP-VENDOR — AssessmentDay, GraduatesFirst; VENDOR mapping via the smartPredict roster]`

**Marking.** Per-item counts and the penalty magnitude are `[UNKNOWN]`; the family-wide rule (points for correct, deductions for wrong — Chapter 8) is prep-vendor-supported here. Attempt-rate drives the raw score; percentile vs norm follows.

**How to win.** This is a *fluency* test: the preparation is drilling number bonds, times tables, and two reverse-engineering tricks — work backwards from the **units digit** (which candidate digit produces the right final digit?) and use **magnitude bounds** (is the missing operand roughly tens or hundreds?) to reject options without full computation. Accuracy still rules under negative marking: a fast wrong fill is worse than a skipped one.

## scales e3+ — concentration / error-checking

**What it is.** A **two-minute** sustained-attention sprint: rapid compare-and-check error detection. The vendor targets it at "roles requiring concentration and accurate management of data or detail — call-centre or service-centre staff, data entry, administrative roles, quality assurance." `[VENDOR catalogue snippet]` Detailed mechanics beyond this are `[UNKNOWN]` — the product page was unreachable in this pass.

**How to win.** Two minutes leaves no room for a slow start: use the untimed practice phase to lock the comparison method (chunk two-to-three characters at a time, fixed left-to-right rhythm, no backtracking), then hold the rhythm. Perfectionist re-checking is the score-killer on any checking test.

## scales mt — the multitasking test (and its avi/drv variants)

**What it is.** The genuine multitasking instrument: the candidate juggles **simultaneous sub-tasks** — the vendor describes "responding to a signal under time pressure, focused calculation and focused checking" running at once, with **immediate feedback on answers** and **graduated difficulty levels**. `[VENDOR catalogue snippets]` The **avi** variant serves aviation selection (pilot/cadet batteries — pilot-prep sources describe added audio streams and measures like "control stability, correction discipline"), and **drv** serves driving/rail roles. `[VENDOR page titles + PREP-VENDOR pilot-prep sites]`

**Marking.** Duration, item counts and the scoring formula are **`[UNKNOWN]`** — the vendor domains were unreachable (DNS-dead from this research environment) and the archive was blocked; this is the biggest single mechanics gap in the report (see Chapter 11). Given immediate feedback and graduated levels, scoring is plausibly **level-and-accuracy-under-load** rather than simple right-minus-wrong. `[INFERRED]`

**How to win** (from the documented mechanic): in any simultaneous-task design, the **reaction-signal stream is the priority** — a missed signal is unrecoverable, while a paused calculation can be resumed. Let the calculation/checking tasks absorb the slack between signals. And know that the test is *built* to overload you: errors under load are normed against everyone else's errors under the same load, so a feeling of drowning is the design, not your failure. `[INFERRED from mechanics]`

## scales tmt — applied numeracy (calculating capacity)

**What it is.** Business/applied numeracy — "roles which require an intense use of numerical information" — sitting between scales numerical (data reasoning) and eql (pure arithmetic). Used, among others, in aviation batteries alongside nav and cmo. `[VENDOR snippet + PREP-VENDOR]` Timings, items and scoring: `[UNKNOWN]`.

## scales mtu — mechanical-technical understanding

**What it is.** Multiple-choice questions on mechanical/technical drawings — gears, pulleys, forces, circuit-style graphics — over a comparatively generous **15 minutes** (plus ~5 minutes of instructions/practice). Aimed at engineering, manufacturing and apprentice roles. `[PREP-VENDOR — Fintest, Psychometric-Success]` Marking: the family negative-marking statement explicitly includes mechanical (`[PREP-VENDOR]`); magnitude `[UNKNOWN]`. **How to win:** classic mechanical-comprehension prep transfers directly — gear direction alternates through a train, ratios from tooth counts, pulley mechanical advantage, lever arms, pressure — and the time budget rewards care over speed.

## scales stm — short-term memory

**What it is.** Vendor positioning: "roles which require detailed observation," service contexts. The prep-vendor description of the mechanic: **5 minutes, 10 tasks** — images exposed for ~**8 seconds**, then recall which items appeared, with ~7-second gaps; **no pausing**. `[VENDOR snippet + PREP-VENDOR]` Scoring: `[UNKNOWN]`. **How to win:** encode verbally during exposure (name the objects fast — dual-coding beats passive staring) and accept that capacity, not strategy, dominates a pure span task; arrive rested.

## scales verbal (instruct) — understanding basic instructions

**What it is.** A simpler, faster verbal strand for **apprentice and administrative selection**: "simple instructions and instruction manuals… evaluate — as fast as possible — whether a statement referring to the text is correct or incorrect." **Binary correct/incorrect** responses (not True/False/Cannot-Say), **8 minutes of test time** (~12 with instructions), and — notably — the vendor states the test is built by an **item generator at run-time**, so every candidate's test is unique. `[VENDOR catalogue snippet]` Scoring magnitude: `[UNKNOWN]`.

## Anti-cheat and platform notes (all tests in this chapter)

Everything here runs on mapTQ under the platform rules of Chapter 9: unique generated items, session logging with **window-switch telemetry**, no pausing once started, optional webcam proctoring where the employer enables it. The practical honest-candidate rules are unchanged: stable connection, one protected sitting, use the untimed practice phases, and put any adjustment request in writing to the employer before you sit (Aon accommodates case-by-case — extra time for dyslexia and screen magnifiers are cited examples — but publishes no candidate-facing policy `[VENDOR-adjacent, weakly sourced; UNKNOWN mechanics]`).
