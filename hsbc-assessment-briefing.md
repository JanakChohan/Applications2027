# HSBC "Immersive Online Assessment" — Candidate Briefing

**Prepared:** 8 August 2026 · **For:** a candidate preparing legitimately · **Scope:** HSBC graduate / early-careers / experienced-hire early-stage online assessment(s).

This is an evidence-based briefing, not an answer key. Every claim carries a reliability label (see *How to read this briefing*). Where sources disagree, the disagreement is shown rather than resolved silently. Where something could not be verified, that is stated plainly.

> **Golden rule that falls out of the research:** the assessment is a **strengths-based** instrument that cross-checks your answers for authenticity and consistency, plus a **hard cognitive filter** where accuracy matters. The winning strategy is *be accurate on the data questions and genuinely, consistently yourself on the behavioural ones* — not to perform an "ideal banker." This is a research conclusion, not motivational filler; the mechanism is explained in §3–§5.

## Executive summary (one page)

- **Vendor.** The immersive assessment is built for HSBC by **Cappfinity** (formerly Capp / Capp & Co; a UK strengths-assessment firm founded 2005) — **[CORROBORATED]** across 8+ industry sources [15][18][21][23][25][29][31][33]. **Caveat:** no HSBC or Cappfinity primary source that could be read actually *names* HSBC as a Cappfinity client — the attribution is well-corroborated second-hand but **not first-party confirmed** [9][8]. The later **video "Job Simulation"** stage is variously attributed to **Cappfinity or HireVue** — genuinely disputed [18][32].
- **Shape of the process.** **[OFFICIAL]** HSBC runs three stages: (1) online application form (~10–15 min); (2) **online assessment(s)** — "one or two stages", each "around 40 minutes", inside a ~90-minute slot; (3) assessment centre (~3 hours; "Super Day" in the US) — replaced by an online interview for insight/work-experience programmes [1][2].
- **The immersive assessment itself.** **[CORROBORATED]** ~**38 questions across 5 scenario-linked sections**, delivered as clickable "tiles" that unlock sequentially; ~16 cognitive (numerical / verbal / inductive) + ~22 situational-judgement / personality items; **officially untimed but ~50 minutes** in practice; SJT items are **ranked 1–5** [15][21][23][25]. Exact section names and counts are **single-source and cycle-dependent** — do not over-index on them.
- **Scoring.** **[OFFICIAL]** Cappfinity is strengths-based: it scores whether your *authentic, energising pattern of behaviour* fits the role, not whether you picked a "correct" personality answer [7]. **[CORROBORATED]** Cognitive items are **right/wrong, benchmarked against a graduate norm group**, and behave as a **hard filter**; SJT/strengths items are **profile-matched** against HSBC's values with "no single right answer"; **time taken is recorded and factored in** even though the test is "untimed" [19][29][35]. **The exact cut score and weighting are proprietary and undisclosed — a real, verified gap.**
- **The model underneath.** **[CORROBORATED]** The behavioural items map to HSBC's **four current values: We value difference · We succeed together · We take responsibility · We get it done** [38]. Some prep pages still cite the **obsolete pre-2021 triad "Open / Connected / Dependable"** — a dated-source disagreement to ignore [30][35].
- **What separates passes from fails.** **[CORROBORATED as a pattern]** Numerical/data errors are the classic killer; faking a "perfect" values profile backfires because strengths are cross-probed for consistency; the cognitive sections are the ones candidates underestimate [19][33][35].
- **Integrity.** **[OFFICIAL]** Cappfinity markets integrity features — **Camera-On Assessment, Copy Prevention, Time-Recorded Assessment, and an Integrity Dashboard that flags likely AI use** [10]. **[OFFICIAL]** HSBC's Applicant Privacy Notice records video answers, may use them in decisions, uses automated decisioning (with a right to human review), and warns that detected malpractice can lead to refusal of employment; results are retained 12 months [5]. For an honest candidate the risk is *accidental* flags — covered in §6 with how to avoid each.
- **Preparation.** **[OFFICIAL]** The most faithful practice is Cappfinity's own demo and Preparation Hub, plus HSBC's post-application Candidate Hub [11][12]. Paid HSBC-specific packs (JobTestPrep, Graduates First) help most on the SJT/strengths/video dimension; generic aptitude packs (Practice Aptitude Tests, Prepterminal's SHL/Hogan course) help only on numerical/verbal speed [15][28][18][36]. See the separate **`prep-plan.md`** for a two-week schedule.

## Q2 — CONTENT (what each section actually contains)

> All concrete "examples" below are **prep-site reconstructions [INFERRED]**, not verbatim candidate memory, unless explicitly marked as a candidate quote. They illustrate *format and flavour*, not questions you will see.

### Two distinct stages — sources conflate them

- **Stage 1 — Online Immersive Assessment (Cappfinity):** the ~38-question, tile-based, mixed cognitive + SJT test; **generic**, not business-area-specific. **[CORROBORATED]** [15][21][25]
- **Stage 2 — Job Simulation (HireVue/Cappfinity video):** shorter, ~15 items, recorded **video** answers + a **written email** task, **tailored to your business area**; reached only after passing Stage 1. **[CORROBORATED]** [15][20][27]

Because the terms "immersive assessment", "job simulation", "simulation game" and "virtual experience" are used interchangeably, when reading any single report **check whether it describes video answers (Stage 2) or ranking tiles (Stage 1).**

### Stage 1 — section by section

- **SJT sections (Project Kick-Off / Global Engagement, etc.).** **[CORROBORATED]** Present a workplace scenario, then ask you to **rank 5 options 1–5** (drag-and-drop; 1 = most likely, 5 = least likely) [21][23][30].
  - *[INFERRED example, AptitudePrep]* "While you have been diligently working on a project for several weeks, **Kieran**, a new member who recently joined HSBC, has brought forward other suggestions and identified flaws in your work" → "How do you incorporate Kieran's feedback?" → rank 1–5 [23].
  - *[INFERRED example, AptitudePrep]* A team feedback session on displaying client-brochure findings in an app; "only some colleagues are actively participating"; options include "Actively asking quieter members questions to encourage contribution" [24].
- **Cognitive sections (Data Monitoring / Competing Commitments).** **[CORROBORATED]** Multiple-choice numerical/verbal reasoning drawn from **5–6 information tabs** (tables, graphs, pie charts, an email) [15][25].
  - *[INFERRED example, JobTestPrep]* analyse NGO subscriber data across months → "Which of the following NGOs had the second-highest percentage increase in subscribers?" [15].
  - *[INFERRED, TestSolve]* each cognitive item bundles roughly "1 table chart, 2 graphs, 1 pie chart, and an email from a colleague"; question shapes like "Which region showed the largest percentage decline in Q3?" [25].
- **Personality / work-style (Pause and Reflect).** **[CORROBORATED]** Forced-choice **preference scales** — e.g. a slider between "I support on small elements of a variety of tasks" and "I see a task through to completion" [23]. This feeds the strengths/trait profile and the consistency check (see §3, §5).

### Stage 2 — the four micro-exercises

**[INFERRED structure, CORROBORATED that it exists]** [15][16][20][27][30]:

1. **Video set-up** — one practice video question (e.g. "introduce yourself" / "your biggest strength"); framed as an unassessed connection test (**but prep sites warn to treat every item as assessed** [15]).
2. **SJT on a fictitious news article** — e.g. an article about an event (a "G7 Summit" example is cited); video + 1–5 ranking; tests "approach to commencing a research project."
3. **Fictitious company analysis** — ~6 items, personality + SJT.
4. **Further analysis / email task** — ~6 items, video + SJT + a **free-text email reply** (≤ ~3,000 chars) — "respond to an email or report as if you're already on the job." *This 3,000-character email is the single most consistently repeated concrete detail across sources.*

### Values the SJT is scored against

- **[CORROBORATED]** Current SJT rubric = HSBC's four values (see §4). Some sources cite the older triad "Dependable / Open / Connected" — **likely obsolete** [26][30]. *(A 2019 candidate advised mentioning "dependable" as a keyword — see verbatim quotes; this reflects the old values and may no longer apply.)*

## Q3 — SCORING AND RANKING (the priority)

**Up-front verified gap:** Cappfinity treats its scoring algorithm as proprietary. **No source — official or otherwise — discloses the actual cut score, section weightings, or trait-inference formula.** Everything about *mechanism* below is either Cappfinity's published *philosophy* [OFFICIAL] or industry *inference* about how it is operationalised [INFERRED/CORROBORATED].

### The vendor's philosophy dictates the logic

- **[OFFICIAL]** From Cappfinity's 2024 whitepaper *The Scientific Origins of Skills & Strengths* (Edition 8): a strength is "a pre-existing capacity for a particular way of behaving, thinking or feeling that is **authentic and energizing** to the user"; the assessment measures "two or more of the three dimensions of **performance, motivation and use**" [7]. **The scored construct is not "did you pick the correct answer" — it is "does your natural, energising behaviour match what the role rewards."**
- **[OFFICIAL]** Cappfinity explicitly rejects the competency "big-bucket" model ("a descent into mediocrity") in favour of its **80-skill set**, and states "**every Cappfinity skill has been benchmarked against workplace performance over multiple years**", using a **"Futuremark" / "Success Analysis"** method that profiles what success looks like in a role and scores candidates against it — i.e. **criterion validation against the client's own success profile** [7][8].

### How each item type is scored

| Item type | Scoring logic | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Numerical / Verbal (cognitive)** | **Right/wrong, objective**, benchmarked against a **graduate/professional norm group**; no negative marking; unanswered = 0; often **adaptive**. | [CORROBORATED] [19][28][35] |
| **Situational Judgement** | **Ranked/rated** (drag 5 options 1–5), scored against HSBC's behavioural/values model — "**no single correct answer**", but responses closer to the desired pattern score higher. Best described as **behaviourally-anchored + partially ipsative**. | [CORROBORATED] [23][30][35] |
| **Personality profiling** | Self-report preference scales feeding **algorithmic trait inference** into the strengths profile. | [CORROBORATED] [15][20] |
| **Time taken** | **Time-factored** — "untimed" but the time you take "**is taken into account**" alongside accuracy; speed can break ties. | [CORROBORATED] [19][29]; tie-break effect [INFERRED] |

### Cut score, percentile, ranked list?

- **[CORROBORATED]** The **cognitive component is norm-referenced** (percentile vs a graduate norm group) — you compete against other graduates, not an absolute bar [35].
- **[ANECDOTAL/INFERRED]** The **behavioural/strengths component is profile-matched** against the role/intake benchmark: "the same answers can fit one route, such as audit or advisory, better than another" [33].
- **[Verified gap]** The **exact numeric cut score is not published anywhere.**
- **[ANECDOTAL — folklore, treat as such]** Widely repeated but unverifiable figures: only ~10% clear the online gate; HSBC's overall offer rate ~3% [35][43].

### Weighting, carry-forward, human review, benchmarking

- **[ANECDOTAL]** Weighting is undisclosed, but the mechanism implies **cognitive accuracy acts as a hard filter** while strengths/values shape fit ("weak data interpretation can hold back an otherwise strong profile") [33].
- **[INFERRED / weak]** Whether the score **numerically carries forward** is unconfirmed; the immersive stage is best understood as a **gate**, with later stages separately assessed. (Results are *retained* 12 months for reuse across applications — a data-retention fact, not evidence of a rolling score [5].)
- **[CORROBORATED]** **Human review:** the immersive stage is **machine-scored**; human assessors enter later — the Job Simulation free-text/video and the assessment centre are **human-reviewed** [15][20]. HSBC confirms automated decisioning is used, **with a right to human review** [5].
- **[OFFICIAL that the method exists; INFERRED for HSBC specifically]** Cappfinity's Futuremark method is *designed* to benchmark against a client's high performers; that HSBC specifically ran it on its own top performers is plausible but **not independently verified** [7].

### Why the strengths logic matters for scoring

- **[CORROBORATED as philosophy]** Because the same strengths are probed repeatedly in different forms, **coherence is scored, not just content**: "inconsistent or exaggerated answers stand out. A coherent, authentic profile scores better than one that swings around to chase an ideal candidate" [33]. This is the *mechanical* reason that trying to "give HSBC what it wants" backfires.

## Q4 — THE MODEL UNDERNEATH

### HSBC's current values framework (what the behavioural items score against)

**[CORROBORATED]** HSBC's four current values (adopted ~2021, current as of 2026) [38]:

1. **We value difference** — empathy for diversity; actively seek views different from your own; inclusive.
2. **We succeed together** — trust, support and connect with others; collaborate across boundaries.
3. **We take responsibility** — high standards, accountability, good judgement, speaking up.
4. **We get it done** — move steadily toward goals, continuously improve, make clear decisive choices.

- **[DISAGREEMENT to flag]** Several 2026 prep pages still describe the **older triad "Open / Connected / Dependable"** (HSBC's pre-2021 values) — **treat as obsolete**; prepare around the current four [30][35].
- **[OUTLIER — do not rely on]** One site describes an **OPQ32 / SHL "Great 8"** forced-choice model with "Most/Least" ranking [37]. That is inconsistent with a Cappfinity strengths assessment and probably describes HSBC's older SHL-era test or a conflation.

### Mapping each section to the traits it probes (the reasoning, not an answer key)

| Section | Item mix | Competency / value it probes — *why* |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Kick-Off / Creating a Team** | SJT | *We succeed together* + *We get it done* — mobilising a team and setting direction |
| **Global Engagement / Working with Others** | SJT | *We value difference* + *We succeed together* — cross-cultural collaboration and conflict handling |
| **Data Monitoring** | Cognitive + SJT | Cognitive rigour + *We take responsibility* — accurate judgement under ambiguity |
| **Navigating Competing Commitments** | Cognitive + SJT | Prioritisation + *We get it done* — trade-offs under constraint |
| **Pause and Reflect** | SJT + personality | Self-awareness + values coherence — feeds the trait/strengths profile and the **consistency check** |

**[CORROBORATED]** The **Job Simulation** probes the same four values plus **communication and prioritisation under a realistic work scenario**, and is partly human-reviewed [15][20]. **[INFERRED]** The strengths quadrant Cappfinity uses (performance × energy × use) means it is trying to distinguish *realised strengths* (you're good at it **and** it energises you) from *learned behaviours* (good at it but draining) — which is why authenticity, not just competence, is being measured [39].

## Q5 — WHAT SEPARATES PASSES FROM FAILS

### Common failure patterns

- **[CORROBORATED as a pattern; underlying reports second-hand]** **Numerical/data errors are the classic killer** — misreading units, percentage-change slips, rushing data interpretation. The recurring (forum-sourced) warning: "you can't make numerical mistakes as you'll be rejected", while SJT is "more flexible" [43][19][35].
- **[CORROBORATED]** On verbal items, **over-using "Cannot Say"** and **importing outside knowledge** instead of reasoning strictly from the passage [35].
- **[CORROBORATED as philosophy]** **Faking a values profile** — because strengths are cross-probed, an incoherent "ideal candidate" performance is detectable and scores *worse* than an authentic one [33].
- **[ANECDOTAL]** Treating the **set-up/practice video as "not counted"** — prep sites warn every item may be assessed [15].

### Time-management traps

- **[CORROBORATED]** The test is "untimed" **but time is recorded and factored in** — over-deliberating on every item can disadvantage you, and speed breaks ties at equal accuracy. Cited pacing: budget ~50 min; ~80 s per numerical item, ~45–50 s per verbal item [19][35].
- **[ANECDOTAL]** **Review-before-submit is per-subtest only** — don't submit a section prematurely [15].

### Underestimated sections

- **[INFERRED + CORROBORATED]** The **cognitive/data sections** are underestimated because the branding is "immersive/behavioural" — yet accuracy there is what most often ends applications; candidates report **speed** as the hardest aspect [19].
- **[INFERRED]** The **personality/consistency layer** is dismissed as "just a personality quiz" when it materially shapes the strengths-fit score.

### Consistency, and extreme vs moderate responses

- **[CORROBORATED as philosophy]** **Consistency matters and is measured** — cross-probing of the same strengths means an internally consistent, authentic profile scores better than a self-contradictory or exaggerated one [33].
- **[INFERRED — general SJT literature, not an HSBC rule]** Be **decisive and honest, not performatively extreme**: committing clearly to genuine best/worst rankings beats fence-sitting, but artificially maxing every "desirable" trait ("extreme responding") is a known red flag that consistency checks catch [40][41]. **[Verified gap]** No HSBC-specific rule on extreme-vs-moderate is published.

### Retake / reapplication policy & cooling-off

- **[CORROBORATED]** **No retake within the same application cycle** for underperformance — only genuine, documented **technical** failures reported within ~24–48 h are re-issued [33][35].
- **[ANECDOTAL/CORROBORATED]** You may **reapply in the next intake cycle**, and "your previous result does not appear on future applications" [35].
- **[ANECDOTAL / INFERRED — explicit gap]** **Cooling-off:** no official HSBC number is published. Generic industry guidance cited is **~6 months minimum, up to 12 months**; one HSBC-specific anecdote mentioned ~6 months (for a customer-consultant, not graduate, role). **Treat 6–12 months as an estimate and verify with HSBC graduate recruitment** [34].

## Q6 — INTEGRITY & MONITORING (framed for an honest candidate)

> **Purpose:** so an honest candidate understands the rules and avoids **accidental** flags. Nothing here describes how to defeat detection. Where a monitored behaviour is listed, the "avoid" advice is about *not tripping a system while behaving normally*, not evasion.

**Context.** **[INFERRED from take-home format; CORROBORATED it is take-home]** The immersive assessment is best understood as an **unproctored, deadline-based take-home** test (a link, "a set number of days", completed unsupervised) — *except* for the recorded **video** question(s), which use your webcam [15][20].

### What HSBC's own Privacy Notice establishes [OFFICIAL] [5]

- Records "video chats" and "may use these recordings to make decisions on the outcome of your application."
- "May capture additional information about … the **devices or software that you use**"; uses cookies and similar technologies.
- Uses **automated systems** in decisions, **with a right to request human review**.
- Processes data to detect "**fraud, other unlawful or dishonest conduct, malpractice, and other seriously improper conduct**"; verbatim consequence: "**If any of these are detected, you could be refused certain services or employment.**"
- "May pass information to relevant agencies if we think you've given us **false or inaccurate information**."
- Assessment **results are retained 12 months** and reused for future HSBC applications.

### What Cappfinity markets (integrity features)

**[OFFICIAL]** that the features exist; **[ANECDOTAL/INFERRED]** for their mechanics — the feature pages are JavaScript-rendered and could not be read in full [10].

Named features: **Camera-On Assessment**, **Copy Prevention**, **Time-Recorded Assessment**, **Integrity Dashboard** (which surfaces flags to recruiters and "highlights behaviour indicative of AI model use"), and **Image Assessment Stimuli**. **[NOT VERIFIED]** whether HSBC switches Camera-On on for the graduate immersive specifically, or whether it is periodic capture vs continuous recording.

### Monitored behaviour → honest-candidate accidental trigger → how to avoid it

| Monitored | How an honest candidate could accidentally trigger it | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| **Webcam / Camera-On** (video answers) [10][5] | Being off-camera, poorly lit, another person visible/audible; repeatedly looking off-screen (reading notes) can read as external help | Sit alone in a quiet, well-lit room; face the camera; speak to camera (glancing at the on-screen question is fine); run the built-in **video set-up** first [15] |
| **Copy-paste / tab-switch detection** (Copy Prevention, Integrity Dashboard) [10] | Opening another tab to check a definition or a **calculator app**; pasting text drafted elsewhere into a free-text box; notification pop-ups stealing focus | Use only on-screen tools; keep a **physical calculator** on the desk (permitted) rather than an app/tab [32]; **type answers directly**; close other apps and silence notifications |
| **Response-latency / time recording** (Time-Recorded Assessment) [10][19] | Answering *implausibly fast* (complex numerical items in seconds); very long uneven gaps from walking away mid-question | Work at a genuine, steady pace; complete each section in one focused sitting; take breaks *between* sections. There is **no penalty for taking sensible time** — don't rush into errors to look "fast" |
| **IP / device fingerprinting** [5] | Using a **VPN/proxy/Tor** (mismatched or datacentre IP looks evasive); switching devices/networks midway; shared/public Wi-Fi another applicant used | Use your **normal home connection without a VPN**; stay on **one device on one network** start to finish |
| **Social-desirability / consistency scales** (embedded in personality/SJT) [INFERRED] | Giving the "ideal banker" answer to everything → an all-perfect, internally inconsistent profile these scales are built to catch; rushing paired items into contradictions | **Answer honestly and consistently**; read each item; pick what genuinely reflects you |
| **AI / plagiarism detection** (Integrity Dashboard "highlights AI use") [10] | Free-text that reads as AI-generated (generic, over-polished, boilerplate) can be flagged **even if you wrote it**; reading a scripted answer verbatim to camera looks like external prompting | Write in your **own natural voice** with specific personal examples; **speak from bullet notes**, not a full script |

### What the terms prohibit — and the consequences

- **[OFFICIAL/CORROBORATED]** Commonly prohibited: **third-party help / impersonation, using AI tools where disallowed, recording / screenshotting / transcribing content, and sharing or reproducing questions**. Cappfinity keeps an **integrity record**; flags can affect future assessments/roles [10].
- **[OFFICIAL]** Detected malpractice can lead to **refusal of employment** and information being passed to relevant agencies [5].
- **[INFERRED — read this yourself]** The **exact, binding rules for your sitting are on the pre-assessment instruction screen and the declaration/consent box you tick before you start.** A standalone HSBC "assessment honour code" page could not be found; the Applicant Privacy Notice is the strongest official document that could be fully read [5].
