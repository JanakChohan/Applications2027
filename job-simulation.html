For: Janak · Role: Corporate & Institutional Banking (CIB) — Graduate Insight Programme · Stage: Job Simulation (Cappfinity) · Prepared: 10 August 2026.

This is a complete briefing on the stage you were just invited to: the whole process from the moment you click the link, how HSBC and the platform see and score you on their end, how people get screened out, the integrity/anti-cheating measures, exactly what HSBC's AI rules allow, and the full bank of questions you could be asked. It is a preparation guide, not an answer key — and by design it cannot be, because the assessment scores authenticity (see §5).

One-line strategy: this is a strengths-based, authenticity-checked simulation with a human making the final call. You win by being accurate on any data/numerical work, genuinely and consistently yourself on the behavioural/strengths parts, and structured + commercially aware on the video and email. Do not perform an "ideal banker" — the platform is built to catch that.

I did not open your assessment link. Your apitake.cappassessments.com link is a personal, single-use assessment — opening it could consume or corrupt your attempt. Everything here comes from independent research.

2. What's inside — the exercises

CORROBORATED[17][19][22] The Job Simulation is ~15 questions across 4 micro-exercises, ~35 minutes:

  1. Video Set-Up — 1 unscored practice/warm-up video question (camera/mic test + rehearse the format).
  2. SJT on an article — ~2 questions. You read/watch a news article about a fictitious event (a "G7 Summit" scenario is named repeatedly across sources) and respond (video + ranking); assesses how you'd start a research project CORROBORATED[17][28].
  3. Fictitious company analysis — ~6 questions. Details/news about a fictional company; research priorities, decision-making, logical presentation; mixes SJT + personality profiling CORROBORATED[17][28].
  4. Further analysis + email — ~6 questions. Deeper analysis of the same company, framed as presenting findings to potential investors; mixes SJT, video, and the free-text email CORROBORATED[17][19].

Response formats you'll meet: - Recorded video — ~3–5 questions; ≈2 min prep, ≈2 min record each (one source says 30 s / 3 min); no live interviewer; typically no re-record CORROBORATED[22]. - Situational judgement — usually rank 5 options 1 (most likely) → 5 (least likely); some versions rate each option effective→counterproductive, or pick the single best action — prepare for all three CORROBORATED / one ANECDOTAL[23][27]. - Personality / work-style — two-sided preference scales; and a Cappfinity signature: strengths are probed twice — what you'd do vs what you'd most/least enjoy (a "strength" = competence and energy) CORROBORATED[24][25]. - Written email ("free text") — reply to "an email from an HSBC colleague or manager, as if you're already on the job," capped at ≤3,000 characters (aim ~2,500–3,000) CORROBORATED[19][22]. - Numerical / data — reading tables, graphs, pie charts and a colleague's email across several tabs; percentage change, trends, ratios, and integrating two data sources at once (this is why the invite asks for a calculator) CORROBORATED[27][18].

Note on exact names/counts: these come from prep vendors reverse-engineering the test, and HSBC tailors content by business area, so your exact tiles may differ INFERRED. Sources also sometimes blur this Job Simulation with the earlier Immersive Assessment you already passed CORROBORATED disagreement[23].

3. How they see and rank you — the scoring model

The core mechanism OFFICIAL / CORROBORATED[16][25][23]: Cappfinity is a strengths-based publisher. It does not mark most of this like a right/wrong test; it builds a profile and compares it to an employer-defined "success profile" and a standardised norm group. A strength is defined as behaviour that is authentic and energising and drives performance — so it scores whether your natural pattern fits what the CIB role rewards, not whether you guessed a "correct" personality answer OFFICIAL[16].

The four things being measured and combined CORROBORATED[25][23][20]:

Dimension What it is How it's judged
Strengths fit Your preference/behaviour profile vs the strengths this role rewards Profile-matched to the role/intake benchmark — "same answers can fit one division and fail another"
Aptitude accuracy Embedded numerical/verbal/data questions Objective right/wrong; accuracy is a hard filter
Consistency / authenticity The same strengths probed repeatedly Inconsistent or exaggerated answers are penalised; a coherent, authentic profile scores higher
Time taken Latency per item + overall "Both the accuracy of your answers and the time you took… will be taken into account"; speed can break ties

Video and written answers are judged on competency evidence, structure, communication, commercial reasoning and authenticity CORROBORATED[23][25]. Cappfinity's video product offers the employer "AI-assisted scoring… or human-led evaluation when nuance matters most" OFFICIAL[9] — but the governing statement for HSBC is decisive: "The AI-based platform does not make any automated decisions in the hiring process. All decisions will be made by our hiring team" OFFICIAL[1]. So: a human reviews your video and email; AI may help triage/flag, but a person decides.

Pass/fail or ranked? In practice this is a gate against a benchmark between stages (Immersive → Job Simulation → Assessment Centre / Virtual Experience Day) — you clear the threshold or you don't, and the profile also informs later interviews CORROBORATED[23][3]. A single Student Room summary claims "only 10% pass the online job simulation" — treat as folklore, not official ANECDOTAL[41]. Whether HSBC recruiters see a numeric rank is unverified INFERRED.

Marking criteria — what a high vs low response looks like

Higher-scoring CORROBORATED[23][25]: authentic and internally consistent throughout; sound, values-aligned practical judgement on SJTs; accurate numeracy; video answers that are structured (beginning–middle–end), specific, calm and to the point with visible commercial awareness; written answers that are concise, professional, well-structured and grounded in the materials, with the right tone for the recipient.

Lower-scoring / screened-out CORROBORATED[23][25]: inconsistent or exaggerated ("gamed") strengths answers; numerical mistakes; rambling or running out of prep time; generic answers not tied to HSBC values or CIB; weak commercial awareness; not actually answering the question; unprofessional or unclear writing; technical/recording failures. Note a "slow but correct" answer can still be marked down because timing feeds the score CORROBORATED[23].

4. The model underneath — HSBC's values, mapped

OFFICIAL[1][4] You are scored against HSBC's four current values (adopted ~2021, current post-reorg):

  1. We value difference — seek out and respect perspectives different from your own; inclusive collaboration across cultures.
  2. We succeed together — trust, connect and support others; collaborate across boundaries.
  3. We take responsibility — high standards, accountability, sound judgement, speaking up on risk/conduct. Flagged as the most important — fitting for CIB, which lives on trust and risk OFFICIAL[1].
  4. We get it done — decisive, steady progress; continuous improvement.

Ignore the older wording ("Dependable / Open / Connected") that some prep sites still use — it's HSBC's pre-2021 branding CORROBORATED disagreement[31].

How the exercises map to what's being probed (the reasoning, so you can lead with the right behaviour on camera):

Exercise Primarily probes
SJT on the article / research project We get it done + We take responsibility — how you scope and start work under ambiguity
Fictitious company analysis We take responsibility + judgement — prioritisation, sound analysis, ethics
Presenting to investors / email We succeed together + communication — clarity, tone, stakeholder awareness
Personality / strengths items authenticity + consistency — feeds the whole profile
"Working with others" scenarios We value difference + We succeed together — collaboration across cultures/seniority

5. How to get screened out — and how not to

Synthesised from candidate reports and Cappfinity's stated methodology CORROBORATED[23][25]:

  • Numerical errors. The classic killer. Units, percentage change, misreading a chart, rushing. Use the pen/paper/calculator; double-check.
  • Gaming the strengths/SJT. Trying to give the "perfect banker" answer produces an inconsistent profile the consistency check is designed to catch, and it scores worse than honesty. Answer as you genuinely would.
  • Rambling / no structure on video. Use the ~2-min prep to plan a beginning–middle–end; answer the actual question; stop when done.
  • Generic answers. "Why HSBC/CIB" with no specifics, or examples not tied to a value, read as low-effort. Anchor to HSBC's values, CIB's business, and real personal examples.
  • Weak commercial awareness. For CIB, not being able to reference a recent macro/market development is a visible gap (§9).
  • Timing extremes. Very fast (looks pre-prepared) or very slow (timing feeds the score) both hurt — work at a genuine, steady pace.
  • Technical failure. Untested webcam/mic, weak Wi-Fi, leaving the tab. Do the Video Set-Up warm-up and complete each tile in one focused sitting.

Retakes/reapplication: no retake within a cycle for underperformance (only genuine, documented technical failures reported quickly are re-issued); you can reapply in a future cycle CORROBORATED, from the vendor ecosystem[20]. HSBC gives no feedback at this stage ANECDOTAL[39].

6. Integrity & anti-cheating measures (framed so you don't trip them by accident)

Cappfinity markets a "multi-layer defence" (prevention, deterrence, detection) — the title of its own white paper on ChatGPT and assessment integrity OFFICIAL[13]. Confirmed features (existence and headline wording verified; full mechanics not public):

Measure What it does Honest-candidate accidental trigger → how to avoid
Camera-On Assessment OFFICIAL[11] Records you via webcam during the assessment Looking off-screen, long silences, another face/voice in frame → looks like reading a script or getting help. Sit alone, well-lit, look at the camera; glancing at the on-screen question is fine.
Copy Prevention OFFICIAL[11] Restricts copy/paste in written answers Pasting text (even your own notes) → flag. Type your email answer live in the box.
Time-Recorded CORROBORATED[20] Records latency + total time; can feed the decision Implausibly fast, or long unexplained gaps → steady natural pace; one focused sitting per tile.
Integrity Dashboard / AI-use flagging OFFICIAL[12] "Highlights behaviour indicative of AI model use" to recruiters Over-polished, generic, boilerplate writing → can read as AI even if you wrote it. Use your own voice + concrete personal specifics.
Device/IP (typical of the platform class) INFERRED Logs device/connection metadata VPN/proxy, switching devices/networks mid-way → looks evasive. Normal home connection, one device, no VPN.

What HSBC's terms prohibit and the stakes: HSBC's hiring emphasises "Authenticity, integrity, and transparency" and treats fabrication/misrepresentation as grounds to withdraw you; you must be able to "personally explain, validate, or replicate" anything you submit if asked OFFICIAL[1]. Read the on-screen instructions/declaration before you start — those are the binding rules for your sitting. (Per your ask, this briefing does not cover techniques to defeat detection.)

7. The AI rules — read this carefully

Your invite says AI "for support" is fine "but make sure your answers are still true to you." HSBC's Application hints and tips page sets the exact boundary OFFICIAL[1]:

Acceptable AI use: research HSBC, its values and roles · help structure your CV/application "provided the content accurately reflects your own experience and skills" · prepare by "practising answers to common questions" · "assist with organising your thoughts or drafting ideas, ensuring the final submission is genuinely your own."

Unacceptable AI use: "fabricate or exaggerate skills, qualifications, or work experience" · "generate responses during live or recorded interviews" · "misrepresent your motivations, capabilities, or achievements" · submit work you "cannot personally explain, validate, or replicate if asked."

What this means in practice for the Job Simulation: - ✅ Use AI now, to prepare: research CIB and current affairs, rehearse the question bank in §8, structure your "why HSBC/CIB" story, get feedback on practice answers. - ✅ For the written email, using AI to help organise or tidy your thinking is within the rules only if the final wording is genuinely yours and you could reproduce the reasoning — and remember Copy-Prevention means you'll be typing it live, and the Integrity Dashboard flags AI-sounding text, so write in your own voice. - ❌ Do not use AI to generate what you say in the recorded video answers — that's explicitly prohibited and the camera-on + timing + AI-flag layers are the enforcement. - Safe test: could I explain and reproduce this answer, live, if HSBC asked me to? If not, don't submit it.

How to answer well (frameworks — build your own words)

  • Video, any question: use the prep time to jot 3 bullets → structure: point → example (STAR) → link back to HSBC value/CIB. Keep to ~90 s–2 min, look at the camera, one clear message per answer.
  • "Why HSBC / CIB": one genuine personal driver → one specific about HSBC/CIB (the East–West network, the 2024 CIB creation, transaction banking/markets, a recent deal or its Asia strategy) → what you'll contribute. Specific beats generic.
  • Behavioural: STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) — HSBC itself recommends STAR OFFICIAL[1]. Pick examples that each showcase a different value.
  • Strengths items: answer honestly and consistently across the whole test; the effort is in knowing your genuine strengths beforehand, not in guessing.
  • Written email: acknowledge → prioritise/triage → recommend with reasoning from the data → clear next step/ownership. Professional, concise, short paragraphs or bullets, right tone for the recipient. Type it live.

9. CIB context & commercial awareness (so you sound like you belong)

What CIB is CORROBORATED[4][47][48]: In HSBC's 2024 reorganisation (announced 22 Oct 2024, effective 1 Jan 2025 under CEO Georges Elhedery), HSBC moved to four businesses — Hong Kong; UK; Corporate & Institutional Banking; International Wealth & Premier Banking. CIB = the former Global Banking & Markets + Commercial Banking outside the UK & Hong Kong + Western markets, led by Michael Roberts, organised around an Eastern (Asia-Pacific, Middle East) / Western (UK, Europe, Americas) split.

What CIB does OFFICIAL[4]: a "markets-led, financing-focused" wholesale bank spanning Banking (coverage/advisory for large corporates & institutions), Global Trade Solutions (trade & receivables finance), Global Payments Solutions (cross-border payments, cash & liquidity), Markets & Securities Services (FX, rates, credit, equities, custody), and Global Research. Clients: large corporates, financial institutions, governments/public sector, institutional investors.

Commercial-awareness themes to have a view on INFERRED from CIB's mix + current affairs[49]: - Interest rates / monetary policy — net interest income, financing demand, market volatility. - Geopolitics & the East–West split — the whole reorg is a bet on navigating US–China tensions, trade/supply-chain shifts; Asia (esp. Hong Kong/mainland China) vs Western markets. - HSBC's strategy — pivot toward Asia and wealth; exits from some Western retail markets; reported moves to streamline commercial + investment banking to cut costs. - Transaction banking — trade finance and real-time cross-border payments (GTS/GPS). - ESG / sustainable & transition finance — a stated financing and research focus. - Markets — FX and rates volatility, capital-markets/advisory activity.

Prepare 1–2 recent stories you can discuss with a genuine view (what happened, why it matters to HSBC/CIB, the risk and the opportunity). A CIB insight reviewer's advice: "be aware of current affairs so that you can mention them in video interview questions to display that you are sufficiently commercially aware" ANECDOTAL[45].

The programme itself CORROBORATED[3][45]: the Graduate Insight Programme is a short (~≤1 week; CIB version ~4 days) insight/work-experience programme — sessions with MDs and interns, case studies across CIB, networking — and it is a feeder: do well and HSBC fast-tracks you to a virtual experience day for the internship/graduate programme. This Job Simulation is the screening gate into that experience.

10. Your ~10-day prep plan

  • Days 1–2 — Foundations. Read this briefing. Learn HSBC's four values with a personal example for each. Read the CIB careers page and get the reorg/structure straight (§9).
  • Days 3–4 — Commercial awareness. Pick 2 stories (rates, geopolitics/Asia, an HSBC development) and write a "what/why-it-matters/risk/opportunity" note for each. Skim the FT/Bloomberg on HSBC and CIB.
  • Days 5–6 — Strengths + behavioural. List 6–8 genuine strengths with STAR stories, each mapped to a value. Rehearse the §8 behavioural and "why HSBC/CIB" questions out loud.
  • Day 7 — Video practice. Record yourself answering 5–6 §8 prompts in ~2 min each from 3-bullet notes, not a script. Watch back for structure, eye contact, pace. Do the new-team-introduction question (#2) and a macro-event question (#5).
  • Day 8 — Written email + numeracy. Practise a ≤3,000-char "should we invest in X" email (acknowledge→prioritise→recommend→next step), typed live. Drill percentage-change/ratio/table questions with your calculator.
  • Day 9 — Full dry run on the Preparation Plus portal; do the practice questions in the real interface; fix any tech/lighting/audio issues.
  • Day 10 — Sit it when fresh, in a quiet, well-lit room, on your home connection, one device, calculator/pen/paper ready. Do the Video Set-Up warm-up properly. Then submit.
