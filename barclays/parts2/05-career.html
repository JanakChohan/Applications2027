The case for joining this division

  • It is the best-returning business in Barclays — 26.3% RoTE in FY2025, 26.1% in H1 2026, against a target of "greater than 25%". You are not joining a problem child.
  • It is small enough that you are visible. Around 1,300 people in the private bank against 91,065 across the group. On an investment banking analyst class you are one of hundreds; here you are not.
  • It is actively hiring into a stated plan — up to 100 advisers funded in the 2026 cost base. Growth divisions promote faster than mature ones.
  • The strategic priority is real and expensive. Technology spend up more than 75%; a £2.7bn bid tabled for Evelyn Partners. Firms do not bid billions on businesses they intend to run down.
  • The skills compound rather than expire. A private banker's value is a book of relationships and technical judgement built over decades — it grows with age, unlike the stamina-driven early years of an investment banking career.
  • It sits at the intersection of everything. To serve one entrepreneur well you touch the corporate bank, the investment bank, credit, tax and markets. Few seats give you that view of a universal bank.
The case against — know it, and be ready for it

It is slower. Relationships are built over years. If you want to point at a completed transaction after eight weeks, this is the wrong division.

Exit options are narrower than investment banking. There is no conventional private equity path out of wealth management; the route is to other wealth managers, to a family office, or to independence.

Costs are under pressure. A 73% cost:income ratio with costs growing 11% against 2% income growth will not be tolerated indefinitely. The investment has to pay off.

Scale is a live disadvantage. NatWest just bought its way past Barclays on UK AUM. The organic strategy has to work.

Six questions to ask on 14 September

  1. Roughly 58% of the division's income is net interest income rather than fees. As rates come down, how hard is it to grow the fee side fast enough to replace it? — shows you understand the revenue model, not just the brand.
  2. Net new AUM was £1.8bn in the first half, a little below last year, while headline AUM rose on market moves. What are you doing to accelerate genuinely new money? — this is the metric insiders actually watch.
  3. NatWest bought Evelyn and Barclays is growing organically. Does that change the pace you have to move at with the Premier Wealth Management proposition? — current, sharp, and impossible to have got from a careers page.
  4. Around four million Barclays UK customers sit in the £250k–£3m band. What is the actual bottleneck in converting them — is it advisers, technology, or client willingness? — demonstrates you understand the growth plan's mechanics.
  5. The division straddles the ring-fence, with UK wealth inside and international outside. How does that shape what you can offer a client with money in two countries? — almost nobody knows this.
  6. How much of the work is actually investment judgement versus planning, tax and structuring? I want to understand what the job is day to day. — honest, useful to you, and shows you are choosing deliberately.
Your answer to "why wealth management, and why Barclays?"

Wealth management is the best-returning business inside Barclays — 26% return on tangible equity on about 2% of the group's capital — and it's the one they've chosen to invest in, with a hundred new advisers and technology spend up more than 75%. What makes Barclays' version interesting to me specifically is the funnel: roughly four million existing Barclays UK customers have between £250,000 and £3 million to invest, so unlike an independent, Barclays doesn't have to find its clients. It also has a bulge-bracket investment bank, so it's in the room when a founder actually sells their company. I like that it's a business where your value compounds over a career rather than burning out early — and I'd rather join while they're still building it than after it's finished.

One thing to get right

Do not say "assets under management" when you mean total client assets. Barclays has £55.8bn of AUM, £140.6bn of invested assets (AUM plus assets under supervision), and £227.6bn of client assets and liabilities (which adds deposits and lending). Using the right one for the right point is the clearest possible signal that you read the results rather than a summary of them.
