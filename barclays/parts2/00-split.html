Barclays has exactly five operating divisions. Not business areas, not brands — five, each with its own published income, profit and returns. Here they are drawn to scale.

Now the twist — and it is the reason this report exists

Rank the same five divisions by return on the capital they use instead of by revenue, and the order almost exactly reverses. The division that earns the least money is the one that earns the most per pound of shareholder capital risked.

{{RANKROTE}}

Private Bank & Wealth Management produced 4.8% of Barclays' income and did it on 2.2% of the bank's risk-weighted assets — £8.0bn out of £356.8bn. That is why its RoTE is 26.3% while the Investment Bank, five times its size, managed 10.6%. Small division, best economics. Everything in the rest of this report follows from that fact.

Where the wealth business physically sits in the group

One structural detail worth knowing, because it is unusual and it comes up: Barclays is legally split by the UK ring-fence into Barclays Bank UK PLC (retail, protected) and Barclays Bank PLC (international, markets). Four of the five divisions sit cleanly on one side or the other.

Private Bank & Wealth Management is the exception — it straddles the fence. The UK wealth business sits inside the ring-fence; the international private bank, booked in Switzerland, Monaco, the Crown Dependencies and soon Singapore, sits outside it. That is not a quirk of reporting: it means the division operates under two regulatory regimes at once, which is part of why it can serve both a Premier customer in Leeds and a cross-border family office in Geneva.
